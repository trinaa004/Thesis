## 1. Methodological Framework & Observation Dimensions

When evaluating historical facility utilization across a 12-year trajectory (2014–2026), physical presence during past events is impossible. Under environmental design research epistemology (Zeisel, 2006; Groat & Wang, 2013), **Physical Trace Observation** allows the interior designer to systematically decode human spatial behavior, equipment adaptations, circulation barriers, and interior maintenance wear through high-resolution event photography and official textual captions.

For each selected event type, observations are recorded across **five standardized spatial trace dimensions**:
1. **Seating Array & Furniture Layout Traces:** Seating density, parallel linear vs. angled geometries, row pitch, chair push-back clearances, table cluster arrangements, and perimeter dead spaces.
2. **Audio-Visual, Rigging & Utility Traces:** Footprints of ground-supported aluminum crank-stand trusses, loose tripod speaker stands, and trailing electrical cords taped across pedestrian walking paths.
3. **Acoustic & Indoor Environmental Quality (IEQ) Traces:** Unpadded painted upper walls and ceiling, glazed porcelain floor tile reflections, bare envelope flutter echoes, and unconditioned lobby area.
4. **Cultural & Decorative Placemaking Traces:** Reliance on temporary, single-use printed vinyl tarpaulins and tape mountings versus permanent, culturally inspired interior architectural finishes (e.g., Wallcrown dried-tikog-inspired wallpaper, white MCM banig-weave textures, Hadang CNC wood relief carving).
5. **Circulation, Egress & Accessibility Traces:** Main entrance door clearance, lobby holding area crowding, waiter food-service lanes, fire exit portal clearances, and compliance with Batas Pambansa Blg. 344 (BP 344) wheelchair seating integration.

* **Stage Lighting:** The only stage lighting were the floor lamps by the backdrop decor on the stage.
* **Stage Cable Routing:** Cable routing is behind the backdrop decor of the stage, no hazards to be seen.

#### Dimension 3: Acoustic & Indoor Environmental Quality (IEQ) Traces
* **Crowd Noise & Vocal Reverberation:** With over 450 attendees and high acoustic energy from young children, ambient sound pressure levels rise substantially. Speech intelligibility through the PA system might be okay since during this year, the ceiling still had a few fabric ceiling baffles.

* **Stage Scenography:** The stage backdrop consists of a large custom vinyl tarpaulin stretched over a rented wooden frame, framed by rented balloon arches. No permanent Waray or Calbayog cultural design elements or architectural finishes are present.

#### Dimension 5: Circulation, Egress & Accessibility Traces
* **Crushing Aisle Bottleneck:** The entire 500-person assembly relies on a single central aisle measuring approximately 5500 mm in clear width. During the recessional march and post-ceremony dispersal, severe pedestrian gridlock might have occurred at the transition between the Main Hall doors and the Lobby.
* **BP 344 Accessibility Failure:** Zero dedicated wheelchair parking bays are provided within the seating array. PWD grandparents and attendees with strollers might have been forced to park at the far rear perimeter near the loading doors, but none can be observed in the photos.

### Structured Observational Audit (Researcher Personal Notes)

#### Dimension 1: Seating & Furniture Arrangement Traces
* **Observed Spatial Configuration & Table Geometry:** High-density rectangular X-Y grid of linear banquet tables combining standard rectangular venue tables ($450/600 \times 1,800\text{ mm}$) into 10-person communal banquet units (5 seats on each long side). Crucially, the tables are arranged **parallel to the central processional aisle** (running front-to-back toward the stage).
* **Wide Central Processional Aisle:** A dedicated central ceremonial corridor measuring approximately **2,400 to 3,000 mm in clear width** runs straight down the center line of the hall from the main entrance lobby double doors directly to the stage platform. This prominent spine was specifically mandated to allow the religious float (*carroza*) to enter the hall and maneuver with ease, and to provide the ceremonial runway for the Knights of Columbus Honor Guard sword-arch salutes.
* **Table Pitch & Seated Clearance Compression:** Because this 2.4-to-3.0-meter central artery consumed a significant portion of the hall's 24.3-meter width, the remaining banquet tables in the East and West seating banks were forced into extreme lateral compression. Table-to-table pitch is compressed down to roughly **1,400 mm** (far below the 1,800 mm statutory/ergonomic benchmark). When Monobloc chairs are occupied and pushed back during dining, usable service passage between backs of chairs collapses to **300 mm or less ($\le 300\text{ mm}$)**. Guests must tuck themselves in tightly to allow anyone to pass.
* **Side Perimeter Compression:** The wide central aisle requirement simultaneously forced the outermost rows of banquet tables directly against the side perimeter walls, completely eliminating standard perimeter service buffers.

#### Dimension 2: Audio-Visual, Rigging & Utility Traces
* **Perimeter Column T-Bar Lighting Obstructions:** The organizers utilized outsourced stage and ambient lighting mounted on portable aluminum T-bar tripod stands. These T-bar stands were stationed directly next to the structural building columns along the perimeter walls. The splayed tripod legs and vertical masts encroached directly into the narrow perimeter guest and waiter pathways, reducing usable clearance to under 900 mm at column bays and creating severe trip hazards from trailing electrical cables.
* **Front Stage Dais:** The permanent elevated concrete stage (940 mm AFFL) was utilized for the dignitary head table and presentation LED backdrop, but lacked BP 344 ramp access.

#### Dimension 3: Acoustic & Indoor Environmental Quality (IEQ) Traces
* **Bare Ceiling & Lighting Deficits:** The overhead ceiling was kept completely bare, devoid of acoustic draping or decorative fabric swags. The event relied entirely on the existing ceiling high-bay fluorescent light fixtures; however, several lamps were visibly burned out or non-operational, resulting in uneven, dim illumination across several dining clusters.
* **Acoustic Clatter & Vocal Flutter:** With approximately 450 to 500 formal guests dining, conversation levels combined with cutlery/crockery clatter reflected sharply off unbaffled upper gypsum bulkheads and bare porcelain floor tiles, creating elevated background noise.

#### Dimension 4: Cultural & Decorative Placemaking Traces
* **Ceremonial Décor vs. Interior Shell:** Rich formal ceremonial styling, floral centerpieces, sword-arch regalia, and fraternity emblems characterized the event. However, all decorative backdrops relied on temporary rented frames and tape mountings against the painted drywall, with zero permanent Waray or Calbayog cultural design elements embedded in the facility.

#### Dimension 5: Circulation, Egress & Accessibility Traces
* **Severe Catering Waitstaff Collisions:** Catering servers carrying heavy 10-plate banquet trays experienced chronic circulation bottlenecks. Because chair-to-chair gaps were compressed to $\le 300\text{ mm}$, waitstaff frequently brushed against seated diners, were forced to halt until diners tucked their chairs in, or had to divert around tables, significantly slowing dinner service and creating burn/spill risks.
* **Main Entrance / Lobby Portal Encroachment:** The buffet service staging area and registration desks were placed immediately adjacent to the main entrance double doors. Guest queues backed up directly across the threshold into the 129 sqm Lobby, severely congesting the primary ingress and emergency egress portal.
* **BP 344 Universal Accessibility Failure:** Zero dedicated wheelchair companion dining bays were integrated into the rectangular table array. Wheelchair users and mobility-impaired attendees had to be placed at the very head of the central aisle or near door thresholds.

# PART II: COMPOUND & DUAL-ZONE MULTI-USE HYBRID TYPOLOGIES (REPLACING SOLE EXHIBITION)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Methodological Finding & Architectural Reality:**  
> Systematic analysis of the facility's 12-year longitudinal utilization ledger ($N = 127$ events, 2014–2026) and on-site field observations revealed that the Calbayog Cultural Convention Center **never operates as a sole/pure exhibition space**. In actual civic practice, there is no documented record of a standalone exhibition where visitors circulate among display booths in isolation. Every single exhibition, trade fair, or culinary showcase hosted within the 840 sqm interior envelope is **co-programmed with another major activity**—such as a keynote plenary convocation, a classroom training workshop, a live culinary competition with judging ceremonies, or a formal evening banquet gala. Therefore, environmental design research must retire the theoretical fiction of a "sole exhibit" typology and formally evaluate **Compound and Dual-Zone Multi-Use Hybrid Typologies**, where spatial concurrency friction and life-safety hazards under the status quo (Option A) are most severe.

#### Dimension 2: Audio-Visual, Rigging & Utility Traces
* **Outsourced Aluminum Lighting Trusses:** Organizers outsourced heavy aluminum lighting trusses supported by Aluminum Lighting Crank Stand Trusses positioned on the main hall floor plate. These trusses encroached into guest pathways and partially blocked rear sightlines to the stage LED screen.

Additional spotlights were placed directly on top of the air-conditioning floor consoles along Column Grid C pointed toward the ceiling to bounce ambient illumination.

#### Dimension 3: Acoustic & Indoor Environmental Quality (IEQ) Traces
* **Thermal Bipolarity:** In the Main Hall, interior climate was uncomfortably uneven: delegates seated near the floor-mounted AC units experienced chilling drafts (set at 17°C), while delegates seated in the center of the hall felt noticeably warm due to lack of overhead air distribution.
* **Unconditioned Lobby Swelter:** The 129 sqm Lobby lacked dedicated air conditioning, with ambient temperatures exceeding 28°C–30°C. Exhibitors and visitors inside the lobby experienced oppressive heat and humidity.
* **Acoustics:** With ~180 delegates seated around round tables, audio through the outsourced line-array was intelligible with minimal flutter echo, aided by attendee clothing absorption.

#### Dimension 4: Cultural & Decorative Placemaking Traces
* **Commercial Exhibition Stalls:** Municipal trade booths showcased regional products (Basey banig crafts, Catbalogan food items, Daram artisanal goods, and Calbayog specialties), but the physical venue provided zero architectural framework or permanent display tracks, resulting in a disjointed aesthetic of rented pop-up panels and taped signs.

All 200 forum delegates, visitors, and exhibitors were funneled through a single 900 mm active door leaf, creating severe pedestrian gridlock during ingress and mass egress.
* **Exterior Driveway Displacement:** Because the 129 sqm Lobby concourse could not accommodate all participating local government units and food concessionaires, **municipal agro-industrial exhibits and local food/snack concessionaires were physically expelled outside onto the exterior vehicular driveway and passenger drop-off sidewalk** under makeshift pop-up canopies. Exhibitors and visitors were exposed to tropical downpours, vehicular exhaust fumes, and traffic collision hazards along Hinay-la Street.
* **Curtain Obstruction of Side Emergency Exits:** Because interior curtains lacked wall tieback hooks, fabric panels draped loosely across the side sliding emergency doors in the Main Hall; attendees and catering staff had to search blindly through curtain folds to locate the sliding door handles.
