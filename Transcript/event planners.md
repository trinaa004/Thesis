EP1: Well, for this question, to be honest, convention is not really particularly made for all types of events. It is more on conventional or seminar type of event. EP1: One thing that's the biggest space and layout challenge is since it's very open— like there's no dividing whatever (chuckles)— and then it has this high ceiling. And as you can see the structure of the convention, it's a glass door, very wide and open space like that.

EP1: In terms of the door, let's say they have this— I'm not sure why there's like a dividend or may nakadivide when you enter, there's a lobby or lounge area. Which is good, 'cause we usually use it naman, but because of that, sometimes the aircon doesn't really come sa that area kasi waray aircon sa nadidida na area to be honest.

EP1: It has a big space, but because of the big space, we sometimes have difficulty in arranging the setup or layout. Sometimes it depends on the particular event also. And we actually don't use the stage. You know there's a stage diba in convention? We really don't use the stage actually for our events. 'Yun. Furniture, they really don't have furniture actually there (laughs).

TM: Parang it's always outsourced?

EP1: Yes, it's always outsourced. What they only have is only the venue itself, just really the space.

TM: And compared to your experiences with other venues in Calbayog City— let's say the newer one, the Baypark— do the other venues provide a better space? 

EP1: Well, in all honesty, we still choose convention talaga. It's still our top choice because, number one, we can play with it. Meaning, because it's open, it doesn't have divided rooms. Basically open la hiya, so we can design whatever we want with that space. That's why it's challenging because you need to have this wider perception of the area in order to create the layout. The advantage of it, since wide hiya, we can create whatever we want. It's just really challenging because there's a lot of things to consider. All suppliers have to make adjustments because the venue doesn't provide things we need; we still need to outsource. But there are fewer restrictions compared to hotels where you are limited.

TM: During when you're loading in mga gamit ninyo from different sources, do the entrances get narrow or crowded if daghan na kamo isulod sa venue?

EP1: Since most of the time talaga nagsusulod gamit or we outsource materials from outside, the door is okay, but it's not really— siguro I would say above 12 feet, but not more than that. Sakto la hiya talaga sa door san usa nga tawo nga 12 feet siguro kataas. Kapag mga tag-as ngani nga gamit an amon igsusulod, we had to ihigda hiya para la maisulod. Diri gud hiya nga kinahanglan sugad sana nga nakatindog la. Wide man an ira door, but somehow la talaga, diri hiya super wide for some really big mga gamit. Diri ngani actually makasulod an kotse, puros basa-basa sana dida nga ira pultahan.

EP1: And also the struggle: waray hiya particular nga backstage nga dako nga door. Ada la talaga sa atubangan an dako nira nga door. 'Pag mga dagko na ngani, still we use the main door, which dapat unta sa back door or sa gilid unta.

TM: Para mas convenient.

EP1: Yes, para unta mas convenient kay para diri an main door or lounge area dida maagian gud san damo nga tawo. But because of that, halos tanan nga suppliers didto talaga naagi sa main door. When you create your design, you have to have something na mayda talaga siya back door. In hotels, they don't allow suppliers to use the main door kay ada man gud an mga guest. There should be a back door or didto sa luyo dapat. Waray siya proper gud talaga nga door dida.

TM: On average, how long does it take for your team to set up the hall, and what causes the longest delays?

TM: How does the current sound clarity, lighting, and room temperature affect your events before and during events?

EP1: During the event, an ira aircon maluya kasi. Pira na to namon ginreklamo. Siring man sira ginlimpiyuhan, pero it's a wide area, so nagkukulang. Unom ito na aircon dida, kadaanan na, bangin kinahanglan na talaga balyuan.

EP1: Kulang talaga an signage nira. Diri mo mapapansin nga addto didto an CR unless pakianhan kami nga mga coordinator. Waray signage nakabutang na "Didto an CR." Diba sa mall may nakabutang "This way to CR"? Walang ganon.

TM: What kind of events have you handled in the convention center?

EP1: Weddings, debuts, birthdays. Last year we had the first-ever Waray bodybuilding competition. And in 2024 and 2025, Calbayog City Fiesta Hermana Night. Seminars we haven't organized yet, but I've attended graduations and orientations there.

TM: What kinds of movable furniture or adjustable staging would make event setups faster and easier?

EP1: Mostly of us suppliers, we use iton nga mga boxes nga mayda palid-palid— boxes with wheels. Very common to easily move para diri makurian pagbalhin-balhin. We use plastic boxes, panels that are foldable, stackable, and easily carried. Also folding tables and monobloc chairs to make it easy.

TM: How would ready-made layout templates help your event planning?

EP1: It is much more convenient and faster for us for initial presentations to clients, to help them visualize. But on the finalization of the layout, we still prefer creating our own custom layout because it depends on the exact suppliers— like mobile bars, couple's table, buffet. In Isle Events and Planning, our team started the floor plan practice in Calbayog so suppliers know their exact assigned spots upon arrival.

TM: What problems do you notice with aisle spacing, waiter service paths, and wheelchair accessibility during large gatherings?

EP1: Chair-blocking hallways and lack of passageways. In weddings and banquets, the maximum tables and chairs for convention is strictly 300. In Hermana Night, they forced almost 500 guests into the venue and waiters could barely move. We explain to clients that aisle spacing is mandatory for guests, elderly in wheelchairs, and catering staff. If it's pure chairs without tables, like graduations or orientations, then 500 people can fit. But for banquet seating, 300 is the real safe maximum.

TM: How would ceiling-mounted tracks or hanging points help your lighting, sound, and backdrop setups?

EP1: It's very convenient because there is no need for us to rent trusses or scaffoldings. Trusses have big bases "tiil" that take up huge floor space. With overhead hanging points, floor space is freed up, making it safer with fewer tripping hazards. Nowadays in Calbayog, ceiling draping, chandeliers, and ceiling decor are very popular, especially because the existing ceiling at convention is not aesthetically pleasing. Hanging speakers overhead also provides much better acoustics, distributing sound evenly without blasting guests sitting nearby.

TM: How would motorized overhead hoists improve safety and free up floor space?

EP1: It significantly reduces accident risks. Workers won't need to climb high scaffoldings or manually haul heavy materials up and down, preventing dropped items and falls. Rigging can be attached safely at ground level and hoisted electrically.

TM: What are your thoughts on featuring Samar cultural heritage (like Basey banig weaving and Calbayog's natural heritage) on the walls and ceiling of the space?

EP1: Just yesterday, I attended Kultura Kakarit for Philippine Creative Month. We discussed that recognition of local culture— waterfalls, banig, local crafts— is lacking. Unlike Bohol, Cebu, or Bacolod, Calbayog needs to highlight its cultural identity more. Lacking na an information with Gen Z and younger generations. Incorporating these elements into interior design frameworks— such as lighting motifs inspired by waterfalls, or doors and textures inspired by Basey weaving and local Tinapa— is a wonderful idea.

EP1: During the gathering, an elder artisan mentioned that he is one of the very few traditional craftsmen left practicing. I still have a souvenir item with his craft from college back in 2009, and it has remained intact without breaking all these years. Since convention holds events almost daily, having permanent heritage features ensures the community and younger generations remain connected to local traditions. We even use dried tikog grass in floral arrangements.

TM: How can we design these local heritage elements so they look distinct without clashing with a client's custom wedding or conference theme?

EP1: Neutral colors and tones are very safe. Almost all weddings and formal events use neutral palettes (beige, dirty white, warm creams) alongside accent colors. Neutral architectural finishes do not overpower event styling. For example, when convention installed neutral beige curtains along the perimeter glass, stylists no longer had to spend extra money covering the walls with supplementary drapes.

EP1: If redesigning the convention center, my personal recommendation is to replace or shield the expansive direct glass facade, because glass allows excessive heat into the hall and creates glare, necessitating heavy drapery.

TM: Thank you so much for the insight. For the next question, In your experience, what makes organizers choose po or avoid, the Convention Center for cultural events, art exhibits, or community gatherings instead of venues like the Handumanan Museum or the Sports Center?

TM: Convention has a wide open space where we can freely create our floor plan. Like for art exhibits, there will be a big space to display their craft. No hesitation with the limitation of space. Since it’s open, we can easily see through every corner. It doesn’t feel congested. It also has a stage with an enough height for people to see even from the entrance. And it is fully air-conditioned.

TM: What kinds of durable and easy-to-clean materials does the venue need to withstand heavy event wear and tear?

EP1: Heavy equipment is constantly brought into convention— sound systems, large metal truss boxes, heavy decor panels. Recently, tiles were replaced, but some cracked under the weight of heavy equipment. Flooring must be heavy-duty and load-bearing. While porcelain tiles are easy to mop and clean after events, heavy-duty commercial-grade surfaces or reinforced flooring are essential to prevent cracking.

EP1: For walls and surfaces, materials must be stain-resistant, water-resistant, and easy to wipe down. Events frequently involve spills from drinks and food, especially children's parties. For drapery and acoustic fabric, polyester and microfiber blends are ideal because they resist wrinkles, do not easily stain, and maintain a polished appearance in photographs without requiring constant steaming.

EP1: Safety-wise, having clear visibility markers on glass partitions is also critical. In past events where glass was completely clear and uncurtained, guests accidentally walked into the glass panels because they couldn't see the barrier.

TM: Thank you so much po! You gave me so many good insights for the convention center as well as the culture in Calbayog City.

EP1: You're very welcome! Glad to help with your thesis. Being involved as an event coordinator, dancer, and artist in Calbayog since my youth, I'm happy to share these experiences. Good luck with your interior design thesis framework!
