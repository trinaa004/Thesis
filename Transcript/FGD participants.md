# Part 1: Stakeholder Participant Profiles & Livelihood Background Dossier

To ensure rigorous pre-construction validation without physical building demolition or full-scale construction (Tremblay et al., 2010; Zeisel, 2006), the Focus Group Discussion (FGD) panel utilizes a **purposive nested sub-sample of nine ($N = 9$) key stakeholders**. These participants were recruited directly from the Phase 1 empirical survey and interview cohorts who gave verified informed consent for follow-up evaluation. 

The panel purposefully represents the full operational and human lifecycle of the Calbayog Cultural Convention Center: from the municipal division heads who manage venue leases and the professional planners who physically erect stages, to the sound engineers who rig utilities, the cultural advocates who safeguard local heritage, and the civic attendees who sit through multi-hour convocations.

### 3.1 Executive Purpose & Reassurance
* **What is this session?**  
  This is a formal academic Evaluative Focus Group Discussion (FGD) conducted for a Bachelor of Science in Interior Design (BSID) thesis at the University of San Carlos, Cebu City.
* **Why were you invited?**  
  You were purposively selected because of your direct, firsthand professional experience with the Calbayog Cultural Convention Center—whether as a planner who sets up events, an administrative officer who manages the building, a cultural guardian who protects local heritage, or an attendee who spends hours sitting in the hall.
* **Important Reassurance:**  
  **You are NOT being tested.** There are no "right" or "wrong" answers. You are serving as an expert panelist to **stress-test and critically evaluate** the proposed interior design solutions. Your honest critiques, concerns, and suggestions are the primary data that will determine if these interior designs will actually work in the real world.

### 3.2 The Target Facility Envelope & Known Friction Points
To evaluate the proposed designs, you should keep the physical reality of the building in mind:
* **The Space:** The interior comprises an **$840\text{ sqm}$ total floor envelope**, divided into a **$711\text{ sqm}$ Main Plenary Hall** and a **$129\text{ sqm}$ Entrance Lobby / Pre-Function Concourse**. The ceiling height is $5.39\text{ m}$ to the suspended plasterboard ceiling and $6.20\text{ m}$ to the lower roof truss chord. The building has an official maximum life-safety assembly capacity of **$500\text{ occupants}$**.
* **The History:** Built in 2014 by converting an unfinished, open-air municipal bus terminal into an enclosed convention facility.
* **The Documented Friction Points (The Problems You Already Know):**
  1. *Long Setup & Teardown Delays:* Major events take 8 to 12 hours (or up to 2 days) to set up, largely because external suppliers must manually haul aluminum trusses, construct scaffolding, and tape dozens of extension cords to the floor.
  2. *Severe Acoustic Echo:* Acoustic testing revealed an occupied reverberation time of $RT_{60} = 2.53\text{ seconds}$—far exceeding the scientific standard of $0.80\text{ to }1.10\text{ seconds}$—causing spoken words to blur into a muddy cathedral echo.
  3. *Thermal Inefficiency:* The $129\text{ sqm}$ entrance lobby has no dedicated air conditioning, creating a hot, humid holding area ($> 28^\circ\text{C}$), while the hall's six aging split-type ACs struggle to cool the center and rear seating zones during full events.
  4. *Inflexible Concrete Stage:* The permanent $52.5\text{ sqm}$ concrete dais ($940\text{ mm}$ high) cannot be adjusted or moved, wasting prime floor space during trade exhibitions and forcing dance performers down onto the floor.
  5. *Aesthetic Detachment:* The interior lacks local soul, resembling a generic commercial box decorated with temporary, disposable plastic tarpaulins.

### 3.4 The 4 Operational Typologies & 12 Scenarios
The design framework organizes the convention center's 127 historical events into **4 Primary Functional Typologies**, each evaluated across **3 Capacity Scales (Peak, Mid-Scale, and Compact)**:

1. **Typology 1: Plenary Assembly (Theatrical Convocations, Graduations, Civic Summits)**
   - *Scenario 1A (Peak: 450–500 pax):* Maximum civic assembly (e.g., ECCD moving-up ceremonies, charter day). *Focus: Sightlines, aisle flow, door bottlenecks, wheelchair stalls.*
   - *Scenario 1B (Mid: 200–300 pax):* Mid-size assemblies (e.g., Liga ng mga Barangay, Samar Artists conclave). *Focus: Chair alignment stability, camera tripod zones, acoustic intimacy.*
   - *Scenario 1C (Compact: 80–100 pax):* Small commencements and hearings (e.g., TESDA graduation). *Focus: Hall subdivision using mobile screens, elimination of cavernous echo, conditioned lobby holding.*
2. **Typology 2: Classroom / Institutional Training (Workshops, Seminars, DepEd Conclaves)**
   - *Scenario 2A (Full-Hall: 160–200 pax):* Large-scale instructional lectures. *Focus: Central facilitator coaching spine, flip-top nesting tables, ceiling power drops.*
   - *Scenario 2B (Mid-Scale: 80–120 pax):* Interactive capacity-building workshops. *Focus: Collaborative table clusters (6–8 pax), roaming aisles, no tripping over floor cords.*
   - *Scenario 2C (Compact: 30–60 pax):* Focused training sessions. *Focus: Front intimate instructional zone, active lobby registration, acoustic clarity.*
3. **Typology 3: Banquet / Social Celebrations (Civic Galas, Hermana Night, Weddings, Debuts)**
   - *Scenario 3A (Full Gala: 250–300 pax max statutory safe capacity):* Grand awards banquets and weddings. *Focus: Enforcement of the statutory 1.40 sqm/pax building code limit; dual banquet templates (Round Diamond vs. Rectangular Communal with processional aisle); overhead rigging recovering 40 seats.*
   - *Scenario 3B (Mid-Scale: 180–220 pax):* Social dinners and anniversaries. *Focus: 1620–1800 mm catering service corridors, waitstaff tray clearance without bumping chairs.*
   - *Scenario 3C (Compact: 80–150 pax):* Intimate banquets and reunions. *Focus: Central dining cluster, dedicated dance floor clearing, mobile decorative buffet screen.*
4. **Typology 4: Exhibition / Showcase (Trade Fairs, Agro-Industrial Expos, Culinary Cookfests)**
   - *Scenario 4A (Mega Expo: 20–26 Booths):* Commercial trade fairs. *Focus: "Zero-Stage Flat-Floor Mode" (stage struck down to reclaim 52.5 sqm of prime floor space); 2400 mm looped walkways; overhead power drops.*
   - *Scenario 4B (Mid-Scale: 14–18 Booths):* Municipal showcases (e.g., Samar Investment Forum). *Focus: 100% indoor containment of municipal agro-stalls, eliminating outdoor driveway spillover; dual-zone air-conditioned lobby.*
   - *Scenario 4C (Boutique Cookfest: 10–14 Stations):* Culinary competitions (e.g., Tinapa 101 Cookfest). *Focus: Perimeter cooking counters with central radial judging hub; lobby activated as a cultural Tinapa history gallery.*

### 3.5 How to Use the 5-Point Evaluation Rubric
During the Google Meet presentation, you will be asked to score both Option A and Option B for each scenario using a digital Google Form rubric based on a **5-Point Likert Scale**:

| Rating Score | Qualitative Descriptor | Operational & Spatial Meaning |
| :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **5** | **Highly Optimized / Superior** | Layout completely solves the operational problem. Clearances exceed statutory minimums, circulation is effortless, setup is fast, and cultural ambiance is exceptional. |
| **4** | **Acceptable / Functional** | Layout is practical, safe, and meets all standard code requirements. Minor operational adjustments may be needed, but it works smoothly. |
| **3** | **Moderately Deficient** | Layout functions with notable friction. Some aisle pinch points, minor sightline blockage, or noticeable setup delays exist. |
| **2** | **Severe Bottleneck** | Layout causes serious operational failure. Significant crowding, blocked exit avenues, severe sound echo, or excessive setup hours. |
| **1** | **Critically Deficient / Unusable** | Layout violates building/fire safety laws, completely blocks emergency egress, creates severe physical tripping hazards, or produces unacceptable acoustic chaos. |
