Research Respondents
To ensure a well-rounded evaluation of both operational logistics and attendee experience, data will be gathered from two primary respondent groups who have direct experience with the Calbayog City Convention Center:
Event Planners and Organizers
	This group includes individuals who have planned, coordinated, or executed events (e.g., conventions, trade fairs, social galas, exhibitions, or government summits) at the venue. Their expertise will provide critical insights into setup/teardown turnaround times, spatial bottlenecks, FF&E reconfigurability, and ceiling rigging demands.
Event Attendees
	This group includes individuals who have attended events at the convention center. Their feedback will reflect the public user experience, focusing on spatial comfort, circulation, sightlines, acoustics, wayfinding, and cultural ambiance.
Selection Criteria:
To participate in the study, respondents must meet the following criteria:
Respondents must be at least 18 years of agee.
Must have either organized, facilitated, or attended at least one event inside the Calbayog City Convention Center within the past five years.
Must be familiar with the venue's interior layout or the regional cultural context of Samar.
Sampling Method
	 This study will utilize a **purposive sampling method**. Purposive sampling is appropriate for this research as it targets individuals who possess direct, informed experience with the interior envelope of the Calbayog City Convention Center, ensuring high data validity regarding spatial and operational challenges..
For the quantitative phase, the study will target approximately 60–80 participants, selected from an estimated population of 500 individuals who have organized or attended events at the convention center within the past five years. This sample size aligns with recommendations for small to mid-scale mixed-methods studies, where 30–100 participants are considered sufficient for identifying trends and patterns (Creswell & Plano Clark, 2018). The sample will include a balanced mix of:
20–30 event organizers who can offer expertise on logistical and spatial functionality, and


40–50 event attendees who can provide user-focused insights on comfort, layout experience, and cultural ambiance.
Following the survey analysis, the qualitative phase will involve semi-structured interviews with 8–12 purposively selected participants from the initial survey group. This number is sufficient to reach thematic saturation and explore nuanced interpretations of the quantitative findings (Guest, Bunce, & Johnson, 2006). This approach ensures that both general user perceptions and in-depth expert insights are captured, supporting the study’s goal of developing design recommendations that are both user-centered and contextually relevant.
