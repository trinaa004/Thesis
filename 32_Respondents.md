 Research Respondents and Sampling Method

## 1. Research Respondents

To ensure a comprehensive evaluation of both operational venue logistics and overall public attendee experience, data for this study will be gathered from two primary respondent groups possessing direct experience with the Calbayog City Convention Center. The inclusion of these two distinct stakeholder groups ensures that both expert operational requirements and public user needs inform the formulation of the interior design framework.

The first respondent group consists of professional event planners, event coordinators, venue managers, and logistical operators who have organized, managed, or executed events—such as conferences, trade shows, social galas, exhibitions, or municipal summits—inside the convention center. This group represents the operational stakeholders of the venue. Their domain expertise will be leveraged to evaluate operational logistics, setup and teardown turnaround times, spatial bottlenecks, Furniture, Fixtures, and Equipment (FF&E) reconfigurability, and overhead ceiling rigging requirements.

The second respondent group comprises general community members, local residents, and event-goers who have attended one or more gatherings at the convention center. This group represents the primary public users of the space. Their feedback will capture the attendee user experience, focusing on interior spatial comfort, circulation efficiency, seating sightlines, acoustic quality, intuitive wayfinding, and cultural ambiance.

## 2. Selection Criteria

To guarantee data validity and operational relevance, all participants recruited for this study must satisfy explicit inclusion criteria. Specifically, respondents must:
1. Be at least 18 years of age at the time of data collection.
2. Have organized, managed, facilitated, or attended at least one event inside the Calbayog City Convention Center (Main Hall or Foyer) within the past five years.
3. Possess familiarity with the venue's interior space or the regional cultural context of Samar.

### Inclusivity and Physical Accessibility Accommodations

Participant recruitment actively accommodates and encourages the inclusion of individuals with diverse physical accessibility needs, specifically persons with mobility impairments (such as wheelchair users and individuals utilizing assistive mobility devices). The inclusion of participants with physical mobility limitations is essential to critically evaluate the venue's spatial clearances, corridor widths, ramp transitions, integrated accessible seating stalls, and barrier-free circulation against Batas Pambansa Bilang 344 (BP 344 - Accessibility Law) and universal design criteria. To facilitate unhindered participation, research instruments will be administered in physically accessible venue zones, offered via digital formats (Google Forms), or completed with researcher assistance upon request.

## 3. Sampling Method and Sample Size Determination

This study will employ a mixed sampling strategy to recruit participants from the two stakeholder groups, targeting a total quantitative sample size of 115 to 145 respondents alongside a qualitative sample of 8 to 12 key informants.

For the Event Planners and Venue Operators group, a Purposive Expert Sampling method will be applied. Because the total population of professional event organizers actively utilizing this municipal venue is small and localized, attempting to collect a large mass sample is methodologically inappropriate. In specialized facility and MICE venue research, operational parameters are routinely evaluated through a targeted expert panel of 15 to 25 key respondents who possess direct domain expertise (An, Kim, & Hur, 2021; Pembegül, 2009). For small, highly specialized expert populations, purposive expert sampling ensures high data validity and operational relevance without requiring unachievable sample numbers (Etikan et al., 2016; Palinkas et al., 2015).

For the General Public and Event Attendees group, a Purposive Quota Sampling method will be utilized to ensure a balanced demographic cross-section of event-goers across diverse event formats (such as social celebrations, trade exhibitions, civic assemblies, and professional conventions). To determine a statistically defensible sample size for a large public user population (estimated at N = 500 to 1,000+ venue visitors), Slovin's sample size formula:

$$n = \frac{N}{1 + N(e)^2}$$

and Israel’s (1992) sampling guidelines indicate that a sample size of 100 to 120 respondents achieves statistical reliability at a 95% confidence level with an acceptable margin of error ranging from e = 0.08 to 0.09 (Cochran, 1977). In public architectural and facility perception studies, a sample of 100+ public respondents ensures adequate statistical representation for descriptive analysis (Creswell & Creswell, 2018; Roa & Lagamon, 2018).

Following the quantitative survey analysis, a qualitative phase will involve semi-structured, in-depth interviews with 8 to 12 purposively selected participants from the survey pool. Methodological literature confirms that 8 to 12 qualitative interviews are optimal for reaching thematic saturation when examining specialized operational workflows (Braun & Clarke, 2006; Guest et al., 2006).

## 4. Post-Design Validation Focus Group Discussion (FGD) Sub-Sample

Following the completion of the interior design synthesis, an Evaluative Focus Group Discussion (FGD) is conducted to empirically validate the unbuilt design framework, 2D layout typologies, and 3D digital renderings (Tremblay, Hevner, & Berndt, 2010; Zeisel, 2006). 

The validation FGD utilizes a **purposive nested sub-sample of $n = 7 \text{ to } 10$ participants** recruited directly from the Phase 1 respondent pool who have consented to follow-up evaluation. In focus group research methodology, a cohort of 6 to 10 participants represents the optimal group size for complex evaluative inquiries, offering adequate operational diversity while preventing chaotic dialogue and conversational fragmentation (Krueger & Casey, 2014; Stewart & Shamdasani, 2014). The panel intentionally incorporates balanced representation across key stakeholder roles:

1. **Professional Event Planners and Production Coordinators ($n = 3 \text{ to } 4$):** Recruited from the Phase 1 expert pool to critically evaluate layout turnaround feasibility, furniture stacking/mobility, overhead rigging practicality, and setup bottlenecks across Plenary, Classroom, Banquet, and Exhibition configurations.
2. **Venue Facility Operations and Administrative Personnel ($n = 1 \text{ to } 2$):** Recruited from Calbayog Convention Center management/staff to evaluate back-of-house circulation, maintenance durability of proposed materials, egress safety, and daily operational manageability.
3. **General Public Event Attendees and Civic Representatives ($n = 2 \text{ to } 3$):** Recruited from the Phase 1 attendee pool to evaluate attendee seating comfort, sightline clarity to stage/screens, acoustic speech intelligibility, and foyer pre-function circulation.
4. **Local Samar Cultural and Artisan Stakeholder ($n = 1$):** Recruited from the regional cultural informant pool to evaluate the cultural authenticity, appropriateness, and respectful abstraction of Basey *banig* weaving motifs, *tikog* grass textures, and Calbayog waterfall iconography.
