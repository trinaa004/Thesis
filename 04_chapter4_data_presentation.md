## 4.1. Research Locale Baseline & Longitudinal Utilization Profile

### 4.1.1. Physical Facility Envelope & Institutional Mandate
The research locale is the **Calbayog Cultural Convention Center** (locally and popularly referenced as the *Calbayog City Convention Center*), located in Barangay Capoocan, Calbayog City, Samar. Originally constructed as a municipal central bus station, the facility underwent extensive adaptive reuse and was formally inaugurated as a municipal civic convention facility on **December 14, 2009**. 

Under its official charter published on the City Government of Calbayog portal, the facility is formally tasked with serving as:
> *"a vital hub for promoting Calbayog City’s rich culture, arts, and tourism, featuring spaces for performances (theatres, pavilions), art displays (galleries, museums), and cultural training, aiming to preserve local traditions like the Sarakiki Hadang Festival, showcase artifacts, and boost economic development through events and local product showcases, acting as a central venue for community gatherings and cultural exchange."*

The physical scope of this interior design investigation is strictly confined to the primary public-assembly interior envelope, measuring **840 square meters**, subdivided into:
1. **The Main Convention Hall (Zone 2):** **711 square meters** of open, column-free interior floor plate (Grid Lines B to E) designed for up to **500 occupants** in plenary theatrical configuration, featuring a clear ceiling height of **5,390 mm** (5.39 m) to the plasterboard plane (6.20 m to the lower structural truss chord);
2. **The Lobby / Pre-Function Area (Zone 1):** **129 square meters** (Grid Lines A to B) serving as the primary pedestrian reception, registration threshold, and ingress/egress circulation buffer; and
3. **The Elevated Stage Platform (Zone 3 - Baseline Condition):** **52.5 square meters** (11.11 m clear width × 4.20 m depth; Grid Lines E to F) elevated **940 mm AFFL** (accessed via 3 riser steps) above the finished hall floor level in its existing configuration. *(Crucially, while this 52.5 sqm zone exists historically as a static, fixed concrete dais, the proposed interior design framework transforms this into a venue-provided, demountable modular staging system that can be deployed on-demand or completely removed to reclaim 52.5 sqm of continuous flat floor space).*

*(Contextual Boundary Specification: The Back-of-House and Restroom Interface [Zone 4, Grid Lines E to G], encompassing the narrow 955 mm backstage corridor, storage room, electrical room, and administrative offices, is documented strictly as a contextual baseline for problem diagnosis and service circulation analysis, but is excluded from physical interior replanning and 3D modeling deliverables).*

#### Empirical Physical Envelope & Vacant Hall Audit Findings
To establish ground-truth empirical rigor, a physical architectural envelope audit and vacant hall baseline observation were conducted on-site (**CCCC plans and physical site context/01_existing_physical_site_context_and_spatial_audit.md**), uncovering critical operational, acoustic, and life-safety deficits:
1. **Usable Door Leaf Egress Bottleneck (RA 9514 / BP 344):** Although the nominal double-leaf glass doors measure 2000 mm in width, **only one door leaf can be opened**, restricting usable pedestrian entry and emergency egress to just **1000 mm**. Furthermore, there are **no designated Fire Exit doors or illuminated Fire Exit signs**, representing a severe life-safety deficit under the Revised Fire Code of the Philippines (RA 9514).
2. **Loading Ingress Axis & Door Frame Collision Damage:** Field tracking revealed that the perimeter entrances along **Column Grid Line 4 are the most heavily utilized for loading and ingress of outsourced FF&E** (in contrast to Grid Line 1). Due to tight 1000 mm clear door openings, heavy flight cases, sound gear, and banquet tables frequently **bump into and damage door frames and jambs**.
3. **Grid B Glass Walls (3,540 mm Height), Grid 1 & 4 Clerestories, & Upper Gypsum Bulkhead Gap:** Architectural site audits revealed distinct wall envelope conditions across the hall: along Grid Line B, the **glass walls between columns 1B–2B and 3B–4B have a height of 3,540 mm** (from finished floor level up to +3,540 mm AFFL), topped by a **1,850 mm painted gypsum board bulkhead** up to the 5,390 mm ceiling. Along the exterior perimeter walls (Grid Line 1 and Grid Line 4), 2,040 mm sliding glass doors are topped by a **1,500 mm clerestory window band** (reaching +3,540 mm AFFL), which is draped with beige curtains hanging from **+3,640 mm AFFL**. Crucially, the upper vertical envelope—comprising the 1,750 mm exposed gypsum bulkhead above the curtain line on Grids 1 & 4 and the 1,850 mm gypsum bulkhead on Grid B—remains completely unpadded, acting as hard specular boundaries that reflect sound across the upper volume and drive chronic flutter echoes.
4. **Overhead Infrastructure Deficits & Physical Ceiling Damage:** The ceiling plane lacks permanent rigging points, truss hooks, or utility tracks. Event coordinators manually fabricate custom aluminum tie-wire frames and drive temporary anchor hooks into the upper walls and columns, leaving unsightly **puncture holes and wall scars**. Crucially, in **Zone A between Column Grid 3 and 4 (close to Column 3B)**, the ceiling exhibits severe **physical panel failure, leaving the sub-ceiling plenum space exposed**. Additionally, multiple ceiling lighting fixtures are physically missing/vacant in Zone A and the Lobby ceiling planes.
5. **Restroom Wayfinding Deficit & Backstage Cross-Traffic:** The facility features **zero signage indicating restroom locations**. Consequently, attendees are forced to cross the entire length of the hall directly in front of the active stage to access the narrow **955 mm backstage door** (Zone 4), creating prominent visual and acoustic distractions during formal ceremonies, while navigating a dark service corridor congested with heavy AV feeder cables and audio snakes.
6. **Legacy Fixed Concrete Stage Bottleneck (Zone 3):** The existing 52.5 sqm stage is a monolithic, permanent concrete structure elevated at a rigid 940 mm AFFL with immovable concrete side steps. Because it is physically fixed, it permanently locks 52.5 sqm into a static configuration. For flat-floor events (such as trade fairs, consumer expos, large banquets, or floor-level cultural dance performances), the fixed stage acts as unusable dead space that restricts layout flexibility. Furthermore, its static 940 mm elevation creates vertical sightline and ceiling height compression for seated audiences, while its permanent side stairs obstruct scaffolding from sitting flush against the back wall during event styling.

### 4.1.2. Venue Ecosystem Delineation

* **The Calbayog City Sports Center:** A large-scale athletic arena designed for high-density spectator sporting tournaments, mass civic rallies, and the outdoor street-dancing showdowns of the **Hadang Festival (historically and popularly recognized as the Sarakiki-Hadang Festival)**.
* **The Handumanan Museum / Arts Gallery:** A specialized cultural repository dedicated to permanent historical collections, antiquities, and curated visual arts archives.

### 4.1.3. Structural Durability and Disaster Resilience
Samar Island is situated directly along the Eastern Visayas typhoon corridor. Over its 17-year operational lifespan, the convention center's reinforced concrete superstructure and structural steel portal frame have demonstrated resilience, successfully withstanding severe meteorological crises:
1. **Super Typhoon Yolanda (Haiyan, November 2013):** Category 5+ super typhoon; the building shell remained fully intact and served as an immediate emergency logistics hub.
2. **Typhoon Ruby (Hagupit, December 2014):** Landfall over Samar with sustained winds exceeding 215 km/h.
3. **Typhoon Tisoy (Kammuri, December 2019):** Severe storm system causing extensive regional infrastructure damage.
4. **COVID-19 Pandemic Logistics Hub (2020–2021):** Staging center for 4,000 sacks of rice and continuous relief repacking for 35,000 target families (Appendix A, Items #18–19).

*Thesis Significance:* This verified structural resilience directly confirms that the facility's architectural shell is durable and sound. Consequently, the building does not require structural demolition or civil engineering reconstruction; rather, it requires an **evidence-based, non-structural interior overlay** that modernizes its internal ergonomics, acoustic performance, and spatial modularity.

#### 4.2.4.1. Ingress Logistical Frictions, Infrastructure Improvisation, and Setup Marathons
Direct tracking of event preparations across the five shadowed events revealed that the lack of standardized architectural accommodations forces production teams and facility personnel into severe operational workarounds:
1. **The Multi-Day Setup Marathons & Thermal Setup Penalties:** Staging for custom-decorated events routinely turns into exhausting, multi-day labor marathons. During the *Samar Investment Forum*, staging spanned October 10 evening continuously through October 11 until 3:00 am on October 12 (16+ hours). For *Stakeholders' Night*, setup began October 14 at 6:00 pm, continued overnight until 1:00 am, and resumed the next afternoon (12+ hours). Most critically, during the *Wedding Event*, setup spanned three separate days: October 15 evening (8:00 pm – midnight) for truss erection, October 16 afternoon (1:00–10:00 pm) for floral/table dressing, and final morning touches. Compounding this labor, live observation exposed an acute operational penalty: **facility rules strictly forbid turning on the central air conditioning units during setup hours**. During daytime load-in (such as the Wedding at 1:00 pm), indoor temperatures soared past 31°C with severe tropical humidity, causing extreme crew perspiration, physical dehydration, and fatigue. This empirical evidence validates why event planners strongly demand pre-engineered overhead rigging tracks and standardized layout menus to compress setup into under 90 minutes.
2. **Loading Axis Bottlenecks, Ingress Portals, and Door Frame Scars:** Perimeter sliding glass doors along Column Grid Line 4 served as the primary loading access point. However, because the full-length window curtains completely lacked curtain tiebacks or holdbacks, hauling crews were forced to manually knot the fabric or search for makeshift ties to keep the doorways clear, frequently snagging heavy flight cases against drapery. Furthermore, during the Wedding ingress, hauling crews discovered that several perimeter sliding doors were jammed or non-functional, forcing all catering, furniture, and floral supplies through a single active side portal. The door opening was too narrow for large flight cases and modular arches, causing repeated collisions against the aluminum door frames. This explains the physical gouges, scraped paint, and structural frame wear documented during the vacant baseline spatial audit (Chapter 4 Section 4.1).
3. **Scaffolding Risks, Improvised Wire Framing, and Legacy Wall Scars:** Because the 5.39 m ceiling plane lacks permanent unistrut rigging tracks, decorating crews for both the *Wedding* and *Stakeholders' Night* had to haul heavy, multi-tier steel scaffoldings onto the slick ceramic floor plate. Crews improvised overhead framing using flexible aluminum wires and tied fabric swags directly to existing ceiling light fixtures, conduit pipes, and legacy screw anchors left in drywall by previous event crews. This hazardous physical improvisation perpetuates wall puncture scars and risks falling accidents, directly motivating the **proposed perimeter dropped ceiling soffit at +4,685 mm AFFL**, which integrates a continuous **Unistrut P1000 Rigging Channel (Channel B)** equipped with pre-positioned eyebolts, allowing event stylists to clip ceiling fabric swags and banners tool-free and damage-free.
4. **Outsourced Truss Ground Obstructions:** For four of the five shadowed events (*Samar Forum*, *Investor's Gala*, *Stakeholders' Night*, and *Wedding*), the lack of built-in rigging forced organizers to outsource and assemble massive **Aluminum Lighting Crank Stand Trusses** directly on the finished floor. These heavy steel and aluminum bases consumed valuable floor area, blocked guest walking paths, and created visual clutter inside the civic envelope.

#### 4.2.4.2. In-Session Circulation Bottlenecks, Obstructions, and Life-Safety Deficits
Observational shadowing during active event hours documented critical circulation pinch points, stage inadequacies, and life-safety hazards under real crowd conditions:
1. **Lobby Entrance Obstruction & Severe Registration Bottlenecks:** During the *Samar Investment Forum*, one of the two main double-leaf entrance doors connecting the 129 sqm Lobby to the exterior was physically blocked by an exhibition booth, while the remaining door was clogged by the "Palawan Gold" jewelry queue. Similarly, during the *PAGPASAMWAK Caravan*, the event registration counter was positioned directly across the second main double entrance door in the foyer, reducing the entire 300-person ingress flow to a single open leaf. Arriving crowds collided with concessionaire queues and photo booth backdrops, confirming the necessity of the proposed **absolute 2,000 mm door standoff buffer rule** in the Layout Manual, strictly forbidding any display booth or furniture placement within 2 meters of exit portals.
2. **Unconditioned Lobby Holding Area Discomfort:** During both the *Wedding Event* and the *TESDA Graduation*, the 129 sqm Lobby functioned as a pre-event holding area for guests and graduates. Because the lobby envelope lacks mechanical air conditioning, temperatures exceeded 28°C to 30°C. In the Wedding, guests were observed standing in heavy formal attire, visibly sweating and fanning themselves with their hands due to zero seating and zero ventilation. During TESDA, graduates were held in the warm lobby for nearly two hours until 5:40 pm before their processional march. This firsthand observation proves that the lobby cannot function as a dignified pre-function or cultural exhibition threshold without the proposed **two dedicated split-type AC units mounted at Columns 1A and 4A** and mobile waiting lounge seating.
3. **Stage Depth Insufficiency & Spatial Displacement:** A critical architectural deficiency observed during *PAGPASAMWAK* and *Stakeholders' Night* was the inadequacy of the elevated concrete stage (4.20 m depth). Once large LED video walls, floral backdrops, podiums, and stage lighting crank stands were erected, the usable stage depth was compressed to under 1.5 meters. Consequently, in *PAGPASAMWAK*, the stage could not accommodate the cultural production dance number, forcing performers down onto the finished hall floor plate in front of the stage, while key presenters and the guest podium were displaced onto a temporary low floor platform. In *Stakeholders' Night*, VIP head tables had to be relocated to the hall floor plate for the same reason. In the Wedding, decorators boarded up and concealed the entire stage behind drapery because it could not be adapted to a modern ceremonial layout. This validates the proposed **modular, demountable stage system** with variable height decks that can be expanded or stowed into storage.
4. **Catering Squeezes & Waitstaff Collision Risks:** During *PAGPASAMWAK*, buffet tables placed along both perimeter walls generated long, crisscrossing guest queues that squeezed against dining tables. Waitstaff carrying 10-plate banquet trays had to constantly "dodge and weave" through diner chairs, spilling water and risking collisions. This directly substantiates the proposed **1800 mm designated catering service bypass lane** connecting the lobby service portal directly to banquet staging areas.
5. **Substandard Dining Pitch & Livestream Tripod Obstructions:** Measurement of seat-to-seat spacing during the *Samar Investment Forum* revealed substandard clearances of only **700 to 750 mm** between adjacent chairs. Furthermore, media operators set up static camera tripods directly in the center of the 2.0 m longitudinal aisle, forcing diners and waiters to squeeze through an effective width of under 900 mm.
6. **Obscured Emergency Exits & Missing Signage (RA 9514 Violation):** Across all five shadowed events, perimeter sliding glass doors along Grid Lines 1 and 4 remained completely concealed behind full-length draperies hung awkwardly with no tiebacks, and the facility featured **zero illuminated or designated Fire Exit signs**. During egress, attendees were entirely unaware that perimeter sliding doors served as legal exits, funneling entire crowds into the single open lobby door. This empirical evidence directly mandates the proposed framework's **Full-Height Wand-Drawn Acoustic Drapery** housed in the **+4,685 mm dropped soffit pocket**, featuring bi-parting splits, floor-operated baton wands, magnetic wall anchors locking curtains back to preserve a full 2,000 mm clear exit portal, complemented by **illuminated 90-minute battery-backed Fire Exit signage (RA 9514)**.
7. **Stage Sightline Blockages from Ground Truss Towers:** In both the *Wedding* and the *Samar Investment Forum*, vertical aluminum truss uprights placed in the center of the hall plate obstructed the view of the couple / keynote speakers for attendees seated in rear rows (C-value sightline deficit), proving the necessity of overhead ceiling rigging.

#### 4.2.4.3. Acoustic Reverberation and Thermal Comfort Disparities
Tracking environmental performance across the five contrasting gatherings exposed how occupant volume and envelope treatments radically alter the space:
1. **The Acoustic Contrast: Cathedral "Church Echo" vs. Porous Soft Overlays:** A pivotal empirical contrast emerged between the shadowed events. During the *TESDA Graduation Ceremony* (80–100 occupants, zero ceiling drapery, bare porcelain tiles, and exposed upper bulkheads), the cavernous hall generated a **severe, cathedral-like flutter echo** (RT60 > 2.0 s) that muddied speeches into hollow, boomy reverberations. In sharp contrast, during the *Wedding Event* and *Stakeholders' Night*, where event stylists draped hundreds of meters of fabric across the ceiling plane and suspended decorative swags, flutter echoes were completely absorbed. Speech was crisp, intimate, and articulate. This empirical comparison conclusively proves that **the 711 sqm Main Hall requires permanent, porous sound-absorbing overhead and perimeter finishes**—specifically validating the proposed suspended waterfall acoustic ceiling baffles (NRC ≥ 0.85), Wallcrown Atmosphere Digest (UW1062) light beige wallpaper with dried-*tikog*-inspired linear textures, custom staggered light beige fabric-wrapped acoustic panels (NRC ≥ 0.75) with integrated cove lighting, beige acoustic drapery, and A-xet acoustic LVT flooring in Bianco Snow Terrazzo to achieve speech intelligibility without requiring decorators to spend 8+ hours rigging temporary fabrics for every booking.
2. **Microclimate Thermal Stratification:** Across all five events, a persistent, uncomfortable thermal gradient was documented: attendees seated in direct alignment with perimeter wall AC blowers along Grid Lines 1 and 4 experienced freezing drafts (discharge measured at 17°C), while attendees seated in the geometric center of the hall reported stuffy, stagnant air. In the lobby concourse, guests endured unconditioned ambient temperatures exceeding 28°C, confirming the total absence of balanced central air diffusion.

#### 4.2.4.4. Egress Bottlenecks, Rapid Reset Compression, and Lingering Clutter
1. **The 13-Day October Calendar Compression Crucible:** The live observation period captured an extraordinary real-world demonstration of **severe calendar compression**. Across a compressed 13-day window during Calbayog's October Charter festivities (October 5 to October 17, 2026), the convention center hosted five consecutive major functions spanning three contrasting spatial typologies:
   **PAGPASAMWAK Caravan (Oct 5–6) → Samar Forum (Oct 10–12) → Investor's Gala (Oct 12) → TESDA Graduation (Oct 13) → Stakeholders' Night (Oct 14–15) → Wedding Banquet (Oct 15–17)**
   Because the facility lacked standardized modular layout protocols and mobile FF&E dollies, custodial and production crews were pushed into continuous, sleep-deprived setup and teardown marathons.
2. **Single-Door Egress Funnels & Exit Congestion:** Across *PAGPASAMWAK*, *Samar Forum*, and *Stakeholders' Night*, exiting crowds funneled almost exclusively through the single unblocked lobby door, creating slow-moving bottlenecks that took 30 to 45 minutes to clear.
3. **Rapid Same-Day Reset Compression vs. Overnight Decor Abandonment:** During the *Samar Investment Forum*, crews had only 3.5 hours between the 3:00 pm forum adjournment and the 6:30 pm *Investor's Gala* to clear luncheon catering, re-sanitize the floor plate, reset linens, and reconfigure AV. Conversely, for the *Wedding Event*, although loose tables and chairs were stacked and hauled outside in 50 minutes (8:00–8:50 pm, accelerated by a dedicated 3-person sweeping crew), the massive ceiling truss and fabric decor could not be dismantled at night. Decorators were forced to **abandon the ceiling decor overnight**, returning the following morning (October 18, 8:00–11:30 am) to complete teardown. This extended occupancy effectively paralyzed the venue for an additional half-day.
4. **Lingering Logistics Interference Across Bookings:** Due to the total lack of dedicated on-site FF&E storage, trade fair booth tables and staging materials from the *Samar Investment Forum* remained stacked along the lobby concourse until 2:00 pm the following day, physically encroaching into the setup space and graduate assembly queue of the *TESDA Graduation*.

*Scientific Interpretation of Empirical Decay Metrics:*  
The empirical interrupted pink noise decay measurements provide definitive, indisputable proof of the facility's severe acoustic dysfunction:
1. **The 2.53-Second Main Hall Reverberation Plateau:**  
   Across the six main hall listening positions (P-1 to P-6), the empirical sound decay time averaged **2.53 seconds**. The decay duration peaked at the stage front positions (**3.00 seconds** at Nodes P-1 and P-2) where sound energy reflects specularly off the flat concrete stage apron and unpadded proscenium back wall, and remained consistently elevated at **2.17 to 2.33 seconds** across the central and rear audience seating bays.
2. **Comparison with International Standards:**  
   Under international architectural acoustic standards for multipurpose civic assembly halls and speech conventions (ISO 3382-1; ANSI/ASA S12.60; Egan, 2007; Cavanaugh et al., 2010), the recommended optimal reverberation time for clear speech transmission is **RT60 = 0.80 to 1.10 seconds**. The measured vacant decay time of **2.53 seconds** exceeds this international threshold by **more than 130%**. In such an environment, successive spoken syllables linger and collide with subsequent words, causing catastrophic speech masking, vocal fatigue, and severe speech intelligibility loss.
3. **Physical Verification of Flutter Echo Defects:**  
   Concurrently with the interrupted signal cutoff and single-handclap impulse checks (Egan, 2007; Cavanaugh et al., 2010), **flutter echo defects were physically observed and verified across all six Main Hall listening nodes (P-1 through P-6)**. The combination of perfectly parallel, specular boundaries—specifically the 711 sqm glazed ceramic floor plate (α = 0.02) opposing the unbaffled ceiling deck (α = 0.04), alongside bare lateral concrete walls—traps acoustic energy in rapid periodic inter-reflections, generating the audible metallic "buzzing" and "ringing" flutter described by Informants EP4 and EA4. 

   Crucially, physical architectural audits (**CCCC plans and physical site context/01_existing_physical_site_context_and_spatial_audit.md**) clarified the upper envelope dynamics: while the **glass walls between columns 1B–2B and 3B–4B on Grid Line B have a height of 3,540 mm** (topped by a 1,850 mm gypsum bulkhead to the ceiling), and the exterior walls on Grids 1 and 4 have their 2,040 mm doors and 1,500 mm clerestory windows covered by curtains hanging from **+3,640 mm AFFL**, the **unpadded painted gypsum board bulkheads in the upper room volume (from +3,640 mm to +5,390 mm AFFL on Grids 1 & 4, and from +3,540 mm to +5,390 mm on Grid B)** remain completely exposed and hard. These parallel bare gypsum surfaces, opposing the flat plasterboard ceiling and glazed tile floor, reflect high-energy sound across the upper volume, bouncing acoustic waves repeatedly across the hall plate and generating the severe flutter echo defects and decay elongation recorded at Nodes P-3, P-4, and P-6. Conversely, in the 129 sqm Lobby (Nodes P-7 and P-8), flutter echo was *Not Observed*, as the substantially lower ceiling height (3.0 m) and angular foyer partitions allow sound to decay in 1.00 to 1.17 seconds.

### 4.3.4. Actor-Network Theory (ANT) Interpretation of Sensory Friction
Applying **Actor-Network Theory (Latour, 2005)**, the acoustic and visual distress experienced by attendees is understood not as an isolated human perception, but as an aggressive **network friction imposed by non-human actants**:
1. **The Ceramic Tile & Glass Actants:** The physical material properties of the 600 × 600 mm glazed tiles and perimeter glass resist sound wave absorption. They actively bounce acoustic energy back into the room, overpowering the human voice of the speaker (Human Actor) and forcing audio technicians to push PA amplifiers into acoustic feedback squeals.
2. **The Industrial Fluorescent Actant:** Static fluorescent fixtures lack ballast shielding and dimming circuitry, imposing visual glare and micro-flicker on attendees attempting to read presentation slides or educational handouts.
3. **The Improvised Extension Cord Actant:** Because the floor plate lacks flush utility floor boxes or overhead power drops, technicians tape extension cords across walking corridors, acting as physical tripping hazards that restrict human movement.

### 4.4.3. Qualitative Thematic Analysis: Artisanal Synthesis
Transcripts from in-depth key informant interviews conducted with event coordinators, live technical engineers, photo-video production leads, and civic attendees (n = 10, Informants EP1–EP5 and EA1–EA5) were analyzed following Braun & Clarke’s (2006) 6-phase thematic protocol, yielding three core cultural design themes:

#### Theme 1: Regional Samar Weaving Craft (Basey *Banig* & *Tikog* Textures)
> *"I want something innovative that merges our culture. If they have Calbayog City woven things—banig, like modernized weaving—at least it reminds us that there is something you can look back to, na, 'Okay, this is like my culture.' I felt very welcomed because it's like remembering my grandparents."*  
> — **Informant EA5** (Event Attendee & Chamber Orchestra Performer)

> *"Just recently, I attended Kultura Kakarit for Philippine Creative Month. We discussed that recognition of local culture—waterfalls, banig, local crafts—is lacking. Unlike Bohol, Cebu, or Bacolod, Calbayog needs to highlight its cultural identity more... Incorporating these elements into interior design frameworks—such as textures inspired by Basey weaving, tikog grass, and local crafts—is a wonderful idea. An elder artisan mentioned he is one of the very few traditional craftsmen left practicing... Having permanent heritage features ensures the community and younger generations remain connected to local traditions. We even use dried tikog grass in our floral event styling."*  
> — **Informant EP1** (Event Planner, Cultural Dancer & Coordinator)

> *"Mas better, especially if maka-soundproof siya. Kon an acoustic panels ha dingding gamitan hin woven textures o banig, diri la kay magigin matahum, kundi makakadakop pa san echo san tunog."*  
> — **Informant EP3** (Live Audio-Visual & Stage Logistics Engineer)  
> *(English Translation: "It's much better, especially if it provides acoustic soundproofing. If the acoustic wall panels incorporate woven textures or banig patterns, not only does it look beautiful, but it actively catches and traps room echo.")*

*Design Translation (Commercial Material Abstraction vs. Literal Crafts):*  
In strict compliance with commercial public assembly fire codes (PD 1096 & RA 9514) and facility durability requirements, the framework explicitly avoids applying literal, untreated artisanal craft mats directly onto walls (which present severe flame-spread, mold, and dust-accumulation hazards). Instead, regional cultural identity is articulated through engineered, commercial architectural finishes that subtly abstract and resemble local craft:
1. **Stage Perimeter Walls (Main Hall):** Commercial **Wallcrown Atmosphere Digest Wallpaper collection (Code: UW1062)** in light beige, exhibiting subtle vertical linear patterns inspired by dried *tikog* leaves used in Basey mat weaving. Crucially, this neutral wallpaper serves as a seamless, full-height architectural backdrop along the entire eastern perimeter wall (Grid Line F), functioning with equal visual distinction whether the venue is operating in **Stage-Deployed Mode** or in **Zero-Stage Flat-Floor Mode**.
2. **Column Bases (+0.00 to +1,200 mm AFFL):** Durable, impact-resistant **white MCM (Modified Clay Material)** flexible wall cladding textured to resemble traditional *banig* weaving relief.
3. **Upper Column Shafts (+1,200 to +4,685 mm AFFL):** **Flexiflute Primed** flexible fluted panels painted to match an oak wood tone, infusing warm organic texture while maintaining architectural neutrality.
4. **Grid Line B Solid Acoustic Separation Wall (between Columns 1B–2B and 3B–4B):** Demolishing the exposed single-pane glass partitions (which event staff constantly taped over with temporary tarps) and replacing them with a permanent solid acoustic wall. The Main Hall face features custom light beige fabric-wrapped acoustic panels (NRC ≥ 0.75) arranged in a staggered pattern with integrated indirect LED cove lighting for speech clarity and refined visual rhythm.
5. **Flooring & Seamless Spatial Continuity:** Specular porcelain floor tiles are replaced with **commercial acoustic Luxury Vinyl Tile (LVT) with acoustic backing (A-xet Floor Coverings in BIANCO SNOW TERRAZZO)**, dampening footfall impact noise and eliminating specular sound bounce. Crucially, by replacing the legacy fixed concrete stage with a demountable modular staging system, this acoustic LVT flooring runs seamlessly across the entire 711 sqm floor plate all the way to Grid Line F, creating a continuous, uninterrupted surface that unifies the hall and eliminates awkward step-ups when the stage is struck down.

> — **Informant EA4** (Event Attendee & Orchestra Performer)

> *"Calbayog is the 'City of Waterfalls'—Tarangban, Bangon, Mapaso. Lighting motifs and ceiling treatments inspired by falling water provide an elegant, fluid look that elevates the whole venue without looking dated."*  
> — **Informant EP1** (Event Planner & Coordinator)

*Design Translation (Calbayog Waterfall Ceiling Baffles):*  
The cascading fluid drop lines and curvilinear hydraulic geometry of Calbayog's famed waterfalls are translated into suspended acoustic ceiling baffles. Constructed from lightweight, flame-retardant acoustic polyester felt (NRC ≥ 0.85), these undulating curvilinear baffles break up ceiling flutter echo, diffuse high-volume speech reflections, and establish an unmistakable municipal aesthetic signature.

#### Theme 3: Civic Performing Arts (Hadang / CACO Lakub) & The Neutral Finishes Principle

Informant EP1 added that utilizing neutral architectural finishes (warm off-white, light oak, muted beige) prevents event decorators from having to spend tens of thousands of pesos draping perimeter fabrics to mask discordant wall colors, providing an instantly photogenic "as-is" interior that completely resolves EP5's requirement for a non-restrictive styling canvas.

*Design Translation (Hadang Kinesthetics & Lobby Cultural Concourse):*  
The dynamic kinetic rhythm, ceremonial movements, and rooster (*manok*) iconography central to Calbayog's premier *Hadang Festival* are translated into a **custom CNC wood-carved relief feature wall** installed along the pre-function concourse face of the newly converted Grid Line B solid wall (between Columns 1B–2B and 3B–4B in the 129 sqm Lobby). This creates a permanent, dignified cultural landmark for arriving delegates. Concurrently, inside the 711 sqm Main Hall, adhering strictly to a neutral architectural palette (light beige Wallcrown UW1062 wallpaper, natural oak Flexiflute fluting, light beige fabric acoustic panels, and A-xet Bianco Snow Terrazzo LVT) satisfies the visual creative requirement (Informants EP3, EP4, EP5), preventing chromatic color bounce onto bridal gowns and camera lenses while providing a versatile, elegant canvas for any event motif.

The quantitative scenario evaluation demonstrates an overwhelming, statistically significant consensus across all three stakeholder groups: **Option B (Proposed Modular Framework) consistently outperformed Option A (Conventional Status Quo) across every single operational scenario (Overall Composite Mean x̄ = 4.65 vs. x̄ = 2.95)**. 

Triangulating this scoring synthesis with the qualitative interview transcripts validates the concrete spatial mechanics of the proposed framework:
1. **Resolution of Sightline and Stage Constriction (Scenario 1, x̄ = 4.71):**  
   The radial chevron seating arrangement in Option B directly resolves the severe sightline obstructions documented by attendees (EA1, EA3, EA5). By introducing staggered row offsets and radial aisles, the layout guarantees an unobstructed sightline clearance (C ≥ 60 mm), ensuring that rear attendees are not blinded by taller occupants in front. Furthermore, the framework's **venue-provided demountable modular staging system (interlocking platforms with variable heights of 300 mm, 600 mm, or 900 mm)** completely replaces the inflexible legacy concrete stage. This resolves the chronic operational failure reported by Informants EP1, EA1, and EA5, where cultural dance troupes and musical ensembles were previously forced to perform down on the bare floor due to the constricted dimensions of the legacy concrete stage. Moreover, Informant EP5 revealed that the static 940 mm height of the legacy concrete stage creates severe vertical sightline and ceiling height compression (*"mura ni na siya ug part sa ulo sa tawo"*), while its permanent concrete side stairs prevent scaffolding from sitting flush against the back wall during drapery installation. Option B's demountable, height-adjustable modular staging decks allow the stage to be deployed at customized dimensions and heights, equipped with an accessible 1:12 BP 344 ramp, or completely stowed away into storage when an event does not require an elevated platform.
2. **Facilitator Mobility, Cable Hazard Decoupling, and Pod Collaboration (Scenario 2, x̄ = 4.63):**  
   For institutional training sessions and educational workshops (such as the DTI Creative Entrepreneurship Training and DepEd/LGU seminars), Option B's modular collaborative pods (6 to 8 participants per pod utilizing 600 x 1400 mm flip-top mobile nesting tables on lockable casters) earned high praise from attendees (x̄ = 4.75), CACO officers (x̄ = 4.60), and planners (x̄ = 4.50). This configuration directly resolves the severe physical frictions documented during Protocol C trace audits and Informants EP3 and EP4's interviews: instead of continuous rigid lecture desks that compressed row push-back clearance to under 750 mm and forced facilitators to sidestep sideways to assist participants, Option B establishes a generous 1500 mm longitudinal facilitator roaming avenue and 1200 mm cross-aisles. Furthermore, overhead retractable ceiling cord reels drop power and data directly into integrated table wire raceways, completely eliminating the hazardous web of floor extension cables that technicians previously spent 1 to 2 hours taping onto slippery ceramic tiles with duct tape. Acoustically, the surrounding Basey tikog composite wall panels and waterfall ceiling baffles lower occupied reverberation to RT₆₀ ≤ 0.95 s, resolving the hollow, muddy echo that previously obscured trainer instructions, while integrated BP 344 accessible stations at pod ends ensure barrier-free inclusivity for disabled delegates.
3. **Elimination of Banquet Service Bottlenecks & Recovery of 40 Seats (Scenario 3, x̄ = 4.62):**  
   Option B strictly enforces the **300-occupant maximum capacity threshold** articulated by Informant EP1 (*"for banquet seating, 300 is the real safe maximum"*), Informant EP2 (*"kun nadiri san suot, 300 is enough"*), and Informant EP5 (*"convention can only accommodate at least 250 to 300 for a wedding"*). Crucially, Option B's motorized overhead rigging eliminates the four bulky ground truss towers identified by Informant EP5, **instantly reclaiming the floor area of four 10-seater tables (40 guest seats)**. By replacing the chaotic orthogonal grid with staggered diamond table clusters maintaining guaranteed 1800 mm clearances between table perimeters, Option B completely eliminates the hazardous corridor friction described by Informants EP3 and EP4—rendering a comfortable, safe 300-seat banquet layout physically achievable without resorting to emergency catering protocols such as bifurcating waitstaff into non-crossing Left and Right teams.
4. **Decoupling Floor Cable Hazards & Zero-Stage Flat-Floor Space Recovery (Scenario 4, x̄ = 4.52):**  
   For trade exhibitions and culinary showcases, Option B's central island configuration received enthusiastic endorsement from both planners (x̄ = 4.40) and attendees (x̄ = 4.65). Crucially, Option B introduces the **Zero-Stage Flat-Floor Mode**: because trade fairs, artisanal expos, and culinary cookfests require maximum flat floor area rather than an elevated dais, the venue completely demounts the modular staging risers and stows them onto backstage rolling dollies. This **instantly reclaims the entire 52.5 sqm footprint** previously locked by the legacy concrete dais, extending the continuous A-xet Bianco Snow Terrazzo floor plate and accommodating **4 to 6 additional standard $2,000 \times 2,000\text{ mm}$ vendor booths** while establishing generous 2,400 mm arterial walkways. The integration of overhead utility drops directly addresses the primary logistical nightmare voiced by Informants EP3 and EP4 (spending 1 to 2 hours manually taping cables across slick ceramic floor tiles), freeing up continuous pedestrian thoroughfares (2400 mm) and isolating power runs safely overhead. Furthermore, as articulated by Informant EP5, the permanent overhead grid eliminates the 2 to 3 hours required to manually rig temporary tie-wire foundations and 5 hours of drapery hanging, compressing turnaround times significantly.
5. **Economic and Thermal Flexibility via Acoustic Subdivision (Scenario 5, x̄ = 4.77):**  
   Achieving the highest composite score in the rubric, the ceiling-tracked kinetic acoustic partition wall (STC ≥ 45) solves the facility's historic "all-or-nothing" spatial constraint. Facility administrators (EP2) confirmed that booking smaller government workshops (50–100 pax) in the monolithic 711 sqm hall plate currently wastes enormous electrical and cooling energy while turning away simultaneous municipal bookings. Option B enables two independent, fully isolated civic functions to operate concurrently with zero acoustic bleed, maximizing municipal revenue and community accessibility.
