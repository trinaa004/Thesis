### 4.0.1. Theoretical and Analytical Grounding for Qualitative and Observational Analysis

To guarantee that the interpretation of empirical data maintains strict scientific validity and avoids speculative commentary, the qualitative interview transcripts (Informants EP1–EP5, EA1–EA5) and empirical spatial observations (Protocol B: Live Event Lifecycle Shadowing; Protocol C: Archival Physical Trace Analysis) are systematically analyzed through the dual theoretical framework established in Chapter 2 (Section 2.1):

1. **Actor-Network Theory (Latour, 1996, 2005):** Applied to examine event operations as heterogeneous socio-technical networks composed of **Human Actors** (event planners, custodial utility crews, AV engineers, catering staff, and attendees) and **Non-Human Actants** (600 × 600 mm glazed ceramic floor tiles, heavy non-stackable wooden tables, plastic monobloc chairs, unpadded gypsum bulkheads, static 52.5 sqm concrete stage dais, single-leaf sliding doors, and loose electrical extension cords). Rather than attributing setup bottlenecks or sensory discomfort to human user error, ANT diagnoses operational failures as **material network frictions and translation breakdowns** where unyielding physical actants impose severe operational resistance on human workflows.
2. **Environmental Affordance Theory (Gibson, 1979):** Applied to evaluate the functional "action possibilities" that the 840 sqm interior envelope physically offers or denies to its users. The analysis systematically evaluates what the existing architectural shell *affords* (e.g., an unobstructed open standing floor plate) versus what it *fails to afford* (e.g., rapid furniture stowage, safe catering bypass, speech intelligibility, simultaneous dual-event hosting, and dignified PWD access). Crucially, Affordance Theory is utilized to identify the **compensatory coping affordances** developed by local event planners (such as splitting catering teams into isolated room halves, building custom floor decks over the concrete stage, and mandating 2-day client booking windows).
3. **Systematic Coding Mechanics:** These theoretical lenses are operationalized via **Braun & Clarke's (2006) Six-Phase Thematic Analysis** for verbatim interview transcripts and **Zeisel's (2006)** and **Groat & Wang's (2013)** **Physical Trace & Archival Content Analysis** for observational field photography and municipal records.

## 4.1. Research Locale Baseline & Longitudinal Utilization Profile

### 4.1.1. Physical Facility Envelope & Institutional Mandate
The research locale is the **Calbayog Cultural Convention Center** (locally and popularly referenced as the *Calbayog City Convention Center*), located in Barangay Capoocan, Calbayog City, Samar. Originally constructed as a municipal central bus station, the facility underwent extensive adaptive reuse and was formally inaugurated as a municipal civic convention facility on **December 14, 2009**. 

Under its official charter published on the City Government of Calbayog portal, the facility is formally tasked with serving as:
> *"a vital hub for promoting Calbayog City’s rich culture, arts, and tourism, featuring spaces for performances (theatres, pavilions), art displays (galleries, museums), and cultural training, aiming to preserve local traditions like the Sarakiki Hadang Festival, showcase artifacts, and boost economic development through events and local product showcases, acting as a central venue for community gatherings and cultural exchange."*

The physical scope of this interior design investigation is strictly confined to the primary public-assembly interior envelope, measuring **840 square meters**, subdivided into:
1. **The Main Convention Hall (Zone 2):** **711 square meters** of open, column-free interior floor plate (Grid Lines B to E) designed for up to **500 occupants** in plenary theatrical configuration, featuring a finished ceiling plane at **+5.385 m CFL** (5,385 mm clear finished ceiling line, contextually rounded to 5,390 mm plasterboard plane; 6.20 m to the lower structural truss chord);
2. **The Lobby / Pre-Function Area (Zone 1):** **129 square meters** (Grid Lines A to B) serving as the primary pedestrian reception, registration threshold, and ingress/egress circulation buffer; and
3. **The Elevated Stage Platform (Zone 3 - Baseline Condition):** **52.5 square meters** (11.11 m clear width × 4.20 m depth; Grid Lines E to F) elevated **940 mm AFFL** (accessed via 3 riser steps) above the finished hall floor level in its existing configuration. *(Crucially, while this 52.5 sqm zone exists historically as a static, fixed concrete dais, the proposed interior design framework transforms this into a venue-provided, demountable modular staging system that can be deployed on-demand or completely removed to reclaim 52.5 sqm of continuous flat floor space).*

#### Empirical Physical Envelope & Vacant Hall Audit Findings
To establish ground-truth empirical rigor, a physical architectural envelope audit and vacant hall baseline observation were conducted on-site (**CCCC plans and physical site context/01_existing_physical_site_context_and_spatial_audit.md**), uncovering critical operational, acoustic, and life-safety deficits:
1. **Usable Door Leaf Egress Bottleneck (RA 9514 / BP 344):** Although the nominal double-leaf glass doors measure 2000 mm in width, **only one door leaf can be opened**, restricting usable pedestrian entry and emergency egress to just **1000 mm**. Furthermore, there are **no designated Fire Exit doors or illuminated Fire Exit signs**, representing a severe life-safety deficit under the Revised Fire Code of the Philippines (RA 9514).
2. **Loading Ingress Axis & Door Frame Collision Damage:** Field tracking revealed that the perimeter entrances along **Column Grid Line 4 are the most heavily utilized for loading and ingress of outsourced FF&E** (in contrast to Grid Line 1). Due to tight 1000 mm clear door openings, heavy flight cases, sound gear, and banquet tables frequently **bump into and damage door frames and jambs**.
3. **Grid B Glass Walls (3,540 mm Height), Grid 1 & 4 Clerestories, & Upper Gypsum Bulkhead Gap:** Architectural site audits revealed distinct wall envelope conditions across the hall: along Grid Line B, the **glass walls between columns 1B–2B and 3B–4B have a height of 3,540 mm** (from finished floor level up to +3,540 mm AFFL), topped by a **1,850 mm painted gypsum board bulkhead** up to the 5,390 mm ceiling. Along the exterior perimeter walls (Grid Line 1 and Grid Line 4), 2,040 mm sliding glass doors are topped by a **1,500 mm clerestory window band** (reaching +3,540 mm AFFL), which is draped with beige curtains hanging from **+3,640 mm AFFL**. Crucially, the upper vertical envelope—comprising the 1,750 mm exposed gypsum bulkhead above the curtain line on Grids 1 & 4 and the 1,850 mm gypsum bulkhead on Grid B—remains completely unpadded, acting as hard specular boundaries that reflect sound across the upper volume and drive chronic flutter echoes.
4. **Overhead Infrastructure Deficits & Physical Ceiling Damage:** The ceiling plane lacks permanent rigging points, truss hooks, or utility tracks. Event coordinators manually fabricate custom aluminum tie-wire frames and drive temporary anchor hooks into the upper walls and columns, leaving unsightly **puncture holes and wall scars**. Crucially, in **Zone A between Column Grid 3 and 4 (close to Column 3B)**, the ceiling exhibits severe **physical panel failure, leaving the sub-ceiling plenum space exposed**. Additionally, multiple ceiling lighting fixtures are physically missing/vacant in Zone A and the Lobby ceiling planes.
5. **Restroom Wayfinding Deficit & Backstage Cross-Traffic:** The facility features **zero signage indicating restroom locations**. Consequently, attendees are forced to cross the entire length of the hall directly in front of the active stage to access the narrow **955 mm backstage door** (Zone 4), creating prominent visual and acoustic distractions during formal ceremonies, while navigating a dark service corridor congested with heavy AV feeder cables and audio snakes.
6. **Legacy Fixed Concrete Stage Bottleneck (Zone 3):** The existing 52.5 sqm stage is a monolithic, permanent concrete structure elevated at a rigid 940 mm AFFL with immovable concrete side steps. Because it is physically fixed, it permanently locks 52.5 sqm into a static configuration. For flat-floor events (such as trade fairs, consumer expos, large banquets, or floor-level cultural dance performances), the fixed stage acts as unusable dead space that restricts layout flexibility. Furthermore, its static 940 mm elevation creates vertical sightline and ceiling height compression for seated audiences, while its permanent side stairs obstruct scaffolding from sitting flush against the back wall during event styling.

### 4.1.3. Structural Durability and Disaster Resilience
Samar Island is situated directly along the Eastern Visayas typhoon corridor. Over its 17-year operational lifespan, the convention center's reinforced concrete superstructure and structural steel portal frame have demonstrated resilience, successfully withstanding severe meteorological crises:
1. **Super Typhoon Yolanda (Haiyan, November 2013):** Category 5+ super typhoon; the building shell remained fully intact and served as an immediate emergency logistics hub.
2. **Typhoon Ruby (Hagupit, December 2014):** Landfall over Samar with sustained winds exceeding 215 km/h.
3. **Typhoon Tisoy (Kammuri, December 2019):** Severe storm system causing extensive regional infrastructure damage.
4. **COVID-19 Pandemic Logistics Hub (2020–2021):** Staging center for 4,000 sacks of rice and continuous relief repacking for 35,000 target families (Appendix A, Items #18–19).

*Thesis Significance:* This verified structural resilience directly confirms that the facility's architectural shell is durable and sound. Consequently, the building does not require structural demolition or civil engineering reconstruction; rather, it requires an **evidence-based, non-structural interior overlay** that modernizes its internal ergonomics, acoustic performance, and spatial modularity.

### 4.1.4b. Multi-Tier Archival Occupant Estimation Protocol & Stratified Scale Distribution (Addressing SPOP Q2)

A critical methodological challenge in investigating provincial municipal assembly venues is the absence of automated electronic turnstiles or digital ticketing logs. To establish an empirically defensible occupancy profile for all 127 verified events from 2014 to 2026 (**Appendix A**), this study implemented a **Four-Level Archival Triangulation Protocol**, grounded in architectural research methodology (Groat & Wang, 2013), physical trace environment-behavior observation (Zeisel, 2006), and statutory building density standards (PD 1096; RA 9514):

1. **Level 1 — Direct Published Corroboration (Groat & Wang, 2013):**  
   Whenever municipal press releases, official Calbayog City Public Information Office (PIO) bulletins, Department of Education (DepEd) division memoranda, or Philippine Information Agency (PIA) dispatches explicitly stated registration tallies or attendance figures, those verified headcounts were adopted directly. Representative examples include:
   - *Samar-wide Artists Kolektib Conclave (August 2024)*: **295 registered cultural delegates** from across Samar Province.
   - *LGU Civil Mass Wedding / Kasalang Bayan (February 2023)*: **96 couples** (192 principals + immediate families and sponsors, establishing ~300 attendees).
   - *DTI Creative Skills & Entrepreneurship Workshop (November 2025)*: **53 certified participants**.
   - *Early Childhood Care and Development (ECCD) Educators Workshop (April 2025)*: **174 Child Development Teachers** representing 169 barangay day care centers.
   - *Annual Inter-High School Tourism Quiz Challenge (September 2024)*: **17 participating secondary school teams** plus faculty coaches and student cheering delegations (~120 pax).

2. **Level 2 — Photographic Seating Array Audits (Zeisel, 2006):**  
   For events where official bulletins omitted explicit headcounts, systematic visual trace audits were conducted utilizing high-resolution municipal photo archives and press photo albums. Occupant capacities were reconstructed by physically counting monobloc chair rows (*N*~rows~ × *N*~chairs~), lateral seating clusters, and table configurations:
   - High-density plenary events (such as the *CDRRMO Disaster Summit* and *Liga ng mga Barangay General Assemblies*) demonstrated repetitive chair grid arrays of **25 rows with 20 chairs across two flanks (25 × 20 = 500 seats)**, confirming peak capacity.
   - Banquet galas (such as the *Knights of Columbus Council 3288 Hermana Night 2026*) exhibited dense grids of **rectangular linear banquet tables (450/600 × 1800 mm arranged in 10-person communal clusters)** oriented parallel to the central processional aisle to seat ~450–500 diners across the 711 sqm floor plate.

3. **Level 3 — Statutory Architectural Boundary Thresholds (PD 1096 & RA 9514):**  
   All estimated occupant counts were rigorously bounded by the statutory occupant load factors of the **National Building Code of the Philippines (PD 1096, Section 1207)** and the **Revised Fire Code of the Philippines (RA 9514, Section 10.2.8.1)**:
   - *Concentrated Assembly without fixed seats (Plenary / Theatrical)*: 0.65 m²/occupant applied across the 711 sqm Main Hall establishes a theoretical building code limit of 711 / 0.65 = 1,093 occupants; however, the facility's certified architectural design capacity and physical egress envelope cap operational capacity at **500 occupants maximum**.
   - *Unconcentrated Dining Assembly (Banquet / Social)*: Under the National Building Code (PD 1096 Section 1207) and Fire Code (RA 9514), the mandatory dining factor is 1.40 m²/occupant. While raw mathematical division across the unencumbered 711 sqm envelope yields 711 / 1.40 = 507 theoretical diners, deducting the 52.5 sqm fixed stage platform, primary life-safety radial egress avenues (≥ 1500 mm), perimeter circulation buffers (≥ 1800 mm), and central processional aisles leaves a net functional dining footprint of approximately 420 to 450 sqm. This architectural reality establishes that **250 to 300 occupants is the true statutory and ergonomic maximum capacity ceiling** for safe banquet dining in the Calbayog Cultural Convention Center. Client demands attempting to force 450 to 500 diners (as observed in Option A status quo practices) inevitably violate statutory codes—compressing chair clearances to ≤ 300 mm, blocking emergency exits, and creating severe service tray collisions.
   - *Educational / Workshop Assembly (Classroom / Training)*: 1.90 m²/occupant caps classroom table arrangements at 711 / 1.90 ≈ 374 participants; operational practice concentrates classroom workshops between **50 and 250 participants** to accommodate facilitator circulation and training materials.
   - *Exhibition / Trade Space*: 2.80 m²/occupant establishes an operational density limit of 711 / 2.80 ≈ 253 simultaneous patrons within active display corridors.

4. **Level 4 — Empirical Calibration via Live Event Shadowing & Venue Operator Audits:**  
   The archival estimation formula was calibrated against direct on-site physical shadowing of five live contemporary events during field observations (Protocol B; October 2026), where actual door headcounts and seating counts were verified via laser meter measures and handheld tally counters. These observations were cross-referenced with qualitative interviews with five key facility managers and event planners (Informants EP1–EP5), corroborating that municipal venue booking requests consistently group into four distinct functional capacity brackets.

*Analysis & Triangulation:*  
The cross-tabulation in Table 4.1c reveals a pronounced **bimodal occupancy distribution** that directly informs the interior design intervention:
1. **The Peak Convocational Demand (Tier 4: 37.8%, *f* = 48):**  
   Nearly four out of every ten events in archival municipal booking records are nominally classified under peak venue capacity. This tier is overwhelmingly dominated by **Plenary / Theatrical assemblies (*f* = 44, representing 71.0% of all plenary bookings)**. However, rigorous on-site event observation and physical trace audits (e.g., during the landmark *ECCD Centers Moving-Up Ceremony*, Event #64) reveal an essential empirical nuance: while the facility's certified architectural envelope can legally accommodate up to 500 occupants under the National Building Code (PD 1096 / RA 9514), practical seated plenary convocations operate at a realistic peak of **250 to 300 participants**. This finding validates that the 711 sqm Main Hall cannot be permanently subdivided with rigid, non-demountable construction; the entire hall plate must remain capable of operating as a flexible, unified assembly volume with optimized radial sightlines (Sightline C ≥ 60 mm), rapid dual-corridor egress, and overhead utility distribution that eliminates bulky floor equipment.
2. **The Compact Workshop Demand (Tier 1: 28.3%, *f* = 36):**  
   The second-largest operational concentration occurs at the opposite end of the scale: compact assemblies of 50 to 120 participants, dominated by **Classroom / Training workshops (*f* = 24, representing 75.0% of all classroom bookings)** and intimate social functions (*f* = 5). Under current baseline conditions, these small groups are hosted within the undivided, cavernous 711 sqm hall. This mismatch generates severe acoustic flutter echo, spatial intimidation, visual disorientation, and massive thermal conditioning waste—cooling the entire 3,910.5 m³ hall volume with split AC units when only a fraction of the floor is utilized.
3. **The Empirical & Legal Mandate for a Typology-Stratified Capacity Architecture:**  
   Crucially, architectural space planning standards dictate that a single "one-size-fits-all" capacity scale cannot be applied uniformly across disparate event functions. #### 4.1.5.1. The Spatial Reality of Co-Programmed Events: Compound and Dual-Zone Hybrid Typologies

Because the status quo facility lacked zoning adaptability and climate control in the foyer, the secondary program was compromised: display tables encroached upon emergency egress portals, and municipal trade stalls were physically expelled onto the exterior vehicular driveway.

*Analysis of Planner Baseline Diagnostics & Qualitative Triangulation:*  
Grounded in **Actor-Network Theory (Latour, 1996, 2005)** and **Environmental Affordance Theory (Gibson, 1979)**, the survey metrics and qualitative interview narratives from field coordinators, technical operators, and facility managers (n = 5, Informants EP1–EP5) reveal that baseline operational bottlenecks are not generated by human coordinator incompetence. Rather, they represent acute **network translation breakdowns** where rigid, unyielding **non-human actants** (slick ceramic floor tiles, heavy non-stackable furniture, unpadded bulkheads, narrow single-leaf sliding doors, and ground truss lifters) actively resist human actors and **fail to afford** rapid room turnover, safe catering bypass, or intuitive spatial legibility:

1. **The Overhead Utility Void and the "Truss Footprint Penalty" (Item B5, x̄ = 4.42, SD = 0.51):**  
   Scoring the highest agreement in the baseline cluster with exceptional statistical consensus, planners and audio-visual technicians identified the **absence of overhead utility rigging as the primary operational handicap**. Because the 6.20-meter high portal frame lacks ceiling tracks or unistrut suspension channels, technical teams and styling crews face severe operational interdependencies and physical hurdles:
   > *"Kon ha amon gud nga lights and sounds, iton amon pag-set up, mga 1 to 2 hours la man talaga iton para ha bug-os nga sounds ngan lightings.    > *(English Translation: "Ground truss lifters consume enormous floor area due to their wide outrigger bases. #### 4.2.4.1. Ingress Logistical Frictions, Infrastructure Improvisation, and Setup Marathons
Direct tracking of event preparations across the five shadowed events revealed that the lack of standardized architectural accommodations forces production teams and facility personnel into severe operational workarounds:
1. **The Multi-Day Setup Marathons & Thermal Setup Penalties:** Staging for custom-decorated events routinely turns into exhausting, multi-day labor marathons. During the *Samar Investment Forum*, staging spanned October 10 evening continuously through October 11 until 3:00 am on October 12 (16+ hours). For *Stakeholders' Night*, setup began October 14 at 6:00 pm, continued overnight until 1:00 am, and resumed the next afternoon (12+ hours). Most critically, during the *Wedding Event*, setup spanned three separate days: October 15 evening (8:00 pm – midnight) for truss erection, October 16 afternoon (1:00–10:00 pm) for floral/table dressing, and final morning touches. Compounding this labor, live observation exposed an acute operational penalty: **facility rules strictly forbid turning on the central air conditioning units during setup hours**. During daytime load-in (such as the Wedding at 1:00 pm), indoor temperatures soared past 31°C with severe tropical humidity, causing extreme crew perspiration, physical dehydration, and fatigue. This empirical evidence validates why event planners strongly demand pre-engineered overhead rigging tracks and standardized layout menus to compress setup into under 90 minutes.
2. **Loading Axis Bottlenecks, Ingress Portals, and Door Frame Scars:** Perimeter sliding glass doors along Column Grid Line 4 served as the primary loading access point. However, because the full-length window curtains completely lacked curtain tiebacks or holdbacks, hauling crews were forced to manually knot the fabric or search for makeshift ties to keep the doorways clear, frequently snagging heavy flight cases against drapery. Furthermore, during the Wedding ingress, hauling crews discovered that several perimeter sliding doors were jammed or non-functional, forcing all catering, furniture, and floral supplies through a single active side portal. The door opening was too narrow for large flight cases and modular arches, causing repeated collisions against the aluminum door frames. This explains the physical gouges, scraped paint, and structural frame wear documented during the vacant baseline spatial audit (Chapter 4 Section 4.1).
3. **Scaffolding Risks, Improvised Wire Framing, and Legacy Wall Scars:** Because the 5.39 m ceiling plane lacks permanent unistrut rigging tracks, decorating crews for both the *Wedding* and *Stakeholders' Night* had to haul heavy, multi-tier steel scaffoldings onto the slick ceramic floor plate. Crews improvised overhead framing using flexible aluminum wires and tied fabric swags directly to existing ceiling light fixtures, conduit pipes, and legacy screw anchors left in drywall by previous event crews. This hazardous physical improvisation perpetuates wall puncture scars and risks falling accidents, directly motivating the **proposed perimeter dropped ceiling soffit at +4,685 mm AFFL**, which integrates a continuous **Unistrut P1000 Rigging Channel (Channel B)** equipped with pre-positioned eyebolts, allowing event stylists to clip ceiling fabric swags and banners tool-free and damage-free.
4. **Outsourced Truss Ground Obstructions:** For four of the five shadowed events (*Samar Forum*, *Investor's Gala*, *Stakeholders' Night*, and *Wedding*), the lack of built-in rigging forced organizers to outsource and assemble massive **Aluminum Lighting Crank Stand Trusses** directly on the finished floor. These heavy steel and aluminum bases consumed valuable floor area, blocked guest walking paths, and created visual clutter inside the civic envelope.

#### 4.2.4.2. In-Session Circulation Bottlenecks, Obstructions, and Life-Safety Deficits
Observational shadowing during active event hours documented critical circulation pinch points, stage inadequacies, and life-safety hazards under real crowd conditions:
1. **Lobby Entrance Obstruction, Inter-Zonal Spillover & Severe Registration Bottlenecks:** During the *Samar Investment Forum*, the unconditioned thermal state of the 129 sqm Lobby concourse combined with the absence of flexible indoor booth infrastructure forced an acute operational failure: not only was one of the two main double-leaf entrance doors connecting the lobby to the exterior physically blocked by a vendor display table while the remaining door leaf was choked by the "Palawan Gold" jewelry queue, but eight of the fifteen commercial trade fair booths were physically expelled onto the outdoor vehicular driveway apron and unsheltered perimeter sidewalk. This forced delegates and shoppers to navigate live vehicular drop-off traffic in the tropical heat, creating severe safety conflicts and directly violating the mandatory egress and hazard provisions of the Fire Code of the Philippines (RA 9514). Similarly, during the *PAGPASAMWAK Caravan*, the event registration counter was positioned directly across the second main double entrance door in the foyer, reducing the entire 300-person ingress flow to a single open leaf. Arriving crowds collided with concessionaire queues and photo booth backdrops, confirming the necessity of the proposed **absolute 2,000 mm door standoff buffer rule** in the Layout Manual, strictly forbidding any display booth or furniture placement within 2 meters of exit portals, reinforced by the **Dual-Zone Concourse Retrofit (Module 4)** that air-conditions the lobby to contain exhibits safely indoors.
2. **Unconditioned Lobby Holding Area Discomfort:** During both the *Wedding Event* and the *TESDA Graduation*, the 129 sqm Lobby functioned as a pre-event holding area for guests and graduates. Because the lobby envelope lacks mechanical air conditioning, temperatures exceeded 28°C to 30°C. In the Wedding, guests were observed standing in heavy formal attire, visibly sweating and fanning themselves with their hands due to zero seating and zero ventilation. During TESDA, graduates were held in the warm lobby for nearly two hours until 5:40 pm before their processional march. This firsthand observation proves that the lobby cannot function as a dignified pre-function or cultural exhibition threshold without the proposed **two dedicated split-type AC units mounted at Columns 1A and 4A** and mobile waiting lounge seating.
3. **Stage Depth Insufficiency & Spatial Displacement:** A critical architectural deficiency observed during *PAGPASAMWAK* and *Stakeholders' Night* was the inadequacy of the elevated concrete stage (4.20 m depth). Once large LED video walls, floral backdrops, podiums, and stage lighting crank stands were erected, the usable stage depth was compressed to under 1.5 meters. Consequently, in *PAGPASAMWAK*, the stage could not accommodate the cultural production dance number, forcing performers down onto the finished hall floor plate in front of the stage, while key presenters and the guest podium were displaced onto a temporary low floor platform. In *Stakeholders' Night*, VIP head tables had to be relocated to the hall floor plate for the same reason. In the Wedding, decorators boarded up and concealed the entire stage behind drapery because it could not be adapted to a modern ceremonial layout. This validates the proposed **modular, demountable stage system** with variable height decks that can be expanded or stowed into storage.
4. **Catering Squeezes & Waitstaff Collision Risks:** During *PAGPASAMWAK*, buffet tables placed along both perimeter walls generated long, crisscrossing guest queues that squeezed against dining tables. Waitstaff carrying 10-plate banquet trays had to constantly "dodge and weave" through diner chairs, spilling water and risking collisions. This directly substantiates the proposed **1800 mm designated catering service bypass lane** connecting the lobby service portal directly to banquet staging areas.
5. **Substandard Dining Pitch & Livestream Tripod Obstructions:** Measurement of seat-to-seat spacing during the *Samar Investment Forum* revealed substandard clearances of only **700 to 750 mm** between adjacent chairs. Furthermore, media operators set up static camera tripods directly in the center of the 2.0 m longitudinal aisle, forcing diners and waiters to squeeze through an effective width of under 900 mm.
6. **Obscured Emergency Exits & Missing Signage (RA 9514 Violation):** Across all five shadowed events, perimeter sliding glass doors along Grid Lines 1 and 4 remained completely concealed behind full-length draperies hung awkwardly with no tiebacks, and the facility featured **zero illuminated or designated Fire Exit signs**. During egress, attendees were entirely unaware that perimeter sliding doors served as legal exits, funneling entire crowds into the single open lobby door. This empirical evidence directly mandates the proposed framework's **Full-Height Wand-Drawn Acoustic Drapery** housed in the **+4,685 mm dropped soffit pocket**, featuring bi-parting splits, floor-operated baton wands, magnetic wall anchors locking curtains back to preserve a full 2,000 mm clear exit portal, complemented by **illuminated 90-minute battery-backed Fire Exit signage (RA 9514)**.
7. **Stage Sightline Blockages from Ground Truss Towers:** In both the *Wedding* and the *Samar Investment Forum*, vertical aluminum truss uprights placed in the center of the hall plate obstructed the view of the couple / keynote speakers for attendees seated in rear rows (C-value sightline deficit), proving the necessity of overhead ceiling rigging.

#### 4.2.4.3. Acoustic Reverberation and Thermal Comfort Disparities
Tracking environmental performance across the five contrasting gatherings exposed how occupant volume and envelope treatments radically alter the space:
1. **The Acoustic Contrast: Cathedral "Church Echo" vs. Porous Soft Overlays:** A pivotal empirical contrast emerged between the shadowed events. During the *TESDA Graduation Ceremony* (80–100 occupants, zero ceiling drapery, bare porcelain tiles, and exposed upper bulkheads), the cavernous hall generated a **severe, cathedral-like flutter echo** (RT60 > 2.0 s) that muddied speeches into hollow, boomy reverberations. In sharp contrast, during the *Wedding Event* and *Stakeholders' Night*, where event stylists draped hundreds of meters of fabric across the ceiling plane and suspended decorative swags, flutter echoes were completely absorbed. Speech was crisp, intimate, and articulate. This empirical comparison conclusively proves that **the 711 sqm Main Hall requires permanent, porous sound-absorbing overhead and perimeter finishes**—specifically validating the proposed suspended waterfall acoustic ceiling baffles (NRC ≥ 0.85), Wallcrown Atmosphere Digest (UW1062) light beige wallpaper with dried-*tikog*-inspired linear textures, custom staggered light beige fabric-wrapped acoustic panels (NRC ≥ 0.75) with integrated cove lighting, beige acoustic drapery, and A-xet acoustic LVT flooring in Bianco Snow Terrazzo to achieve speech intelligibility without requiring decorators to spend 8+ hours rigging temporary fabrics for every booking.
2. **Microclimate Thermal Stratification:** Across all five events, a persistent, uncomfortable thermal gradient was documented: attendees seated in direct alignment with perimeter wall AC blowers along Grid Lines 1 and 4 experienced freezing drafts (discharge measured at 17°C), while attendees seated in the geometric center of the hall reported stuffy, stagnant air. In the lobby concourse, guests endured unconditioned ambient temperatures exceeding 28°C, confirming the total absence of balanced central air diffusion.

#### 4.2.4.4. Egress Bottlenecks, Rapid Reset Compression, and Lingering Clutter
1. **The 13-Day October Calendar Compression Crucible:** The live observation period captured an extraordinary real-world demonstration of **severe calendar compression**. Across a compressed 13-day window during Calbayog's October Charter festivities (October 5 to October 17, 2026), the convention center hosted five consecutive major functions spanning three contrasting spatial typologies:
   **PAGPASAMWAK Caravan (Oct 5–6) → Samar Forum (Oct 10–12) → Investor's Gala (Oct 12) → TESDA Graduation (Oct 13) → Stakeholders' Night (Oct 14–15) → Wedding Banquet (Oct 15–17)**
   Because the facility lacked standardized modular layout protocols and mobile FF&E dollies, custodial and production crews were pushed into continuous, sleep-deprived setup and teardown marathons.
2. **Single-Door Egress Funnels & Exit Congestion:** Across *PAGPASAMWAK*, *Samar Forum*, and *Stakeholders' Night*, exiting crowds funneled almost exclusively through the single unblocked lobby door, creating slow-moving bottlenecks that took 30 to 45 minutes to clear.
3. **Rapid Same-Day Reset Compression vs. Overnight Decor Abandonment:** During the *Samar Investment Forum*, crews had only 3.5 hours between the 3:00 pm forum adjournment and the 6:30 pm *Investor's Gala* to clear luncheon catering, re-sanitize the floor plate, reset linens, and reconfigure AV. Conversely, for the *Wedding Event*, although loose tables and chairs were stacked and hauled outside in 50 minutes (8:00–8:50 pm, accelerated by a dedicated 3-person sweeping crew), the massive ceiling truss and fabric decor could not be dismantled at night. Decorators were forced to **abandon the ceiling decor overnight**, returning the following morning (October 18, 8:00–11:30 am) to complete teardown. This extended occupancy effectively paralyzed the venue for an additional half-day.
4. **Lingering Logistics Interference Across Bookings:** Due to the total lack of dedicated on-site FF&E storage, trade fair booth tables and staging materials from the *Samar Investment Forum* remained stacked along the lobby concourse until 2:00 pm the following day, physically encroaching into the setup space and graduate assembly queue of the *TESDA Graduation*.

### 4.2.5. Functional Spatial Zoning & Activity Area Analysis: Observational Spatial Distribution Matrix (Addressing SPOP Q1.2)

To synthesize the empirical findings gathered through direct multi-phase event shadowing (**Protocol B**, Section 4.2.4) and secondary physical trace audits (**Protocol C**, Section 4.2.4.5), this section evaluates how the Calbayog Cultural Convention Center’s 840 sqm interior envelope (comprising the 711 sqm Main Hall and 129 sqm Lobby) is operationalized into distinct, interdependent **functional activity sub-zones**. Grounded in **Actor-Network Theory (Latour, 2005)** and **Gibson’s (1979) Affordance Theory**, this spatial taxonomy maps how material actants are geographically distributed across the interior envelope, and evaluates whether the spatial layout provides or denies the necessary functional affordances for each activity zone.

In contemporary facilities planning and environmental design research (Lawson, 2000; Tompkins et al., 2010), a multi-purpose civic hall does not operate as an undifferentiated, homogenous void. Rather, during live event operations, the floor plate dynamically fragments into specialized functional territories:
1. **The Primary Guest Assembly and Dining Plate:** Where audience seating, conference tables, or banquet clusters reside;
2. **The Front-of-House (FOH) Technical Audio-Visual & Production Control Zone:** Housing sound mixing consoles, lighting dimmer boards, video playback switchers, and live camera operators;
3. **The Catering, Buffet Staging & Food Service Dispatch Zone:** Accommodating food service holding warmers, self-service buffet queues, bussing bins, and beverage stations;
4. **The Stage Platform & Ceremonial Presentation Dais:** Serving as the visual and acoustic focal anchor for dignitaries, performers, and keynote speakers;
5. **The Pre-Function Reception, Registration & Ingress Buffer Zone:** Facilitating credential verification, initial crowd filtration, and pedestrian queuing within the 129 sqm lobby; and
6. **The Back-of-House (BOH) & Restroom Service Circulation Axis:** Providing transit for backstage staff, technical power distribution, and sanitation egress.

The primary operational friction documented in the Calbayog Cultural Convention Center is that **these specialized functional zones lack permanent architectural demarcation, dedicated utility drops, and clear circulation buffers**. ### 4.3.3. Objective In-Situ Acoustic Audit & Reverberation Modeling

The empirical investigation encompasses three distinct operational protocols:
1. **Diurnal Passive Envelope Ambient Baseline:** Documenting background sound pressure levels (Lmin, Leq, Lmax) across morning, afternoon, and evening cycles under unconditioned, vacant conditions (HVAC idle).

The empirical interrupted pink noise decay measurements provide definitive, indisputable proof of the facility's severe acoustic dysfunction:
1. **The 2.53-Second Main Hall Reverberation Plateau:**  
   Across the six main hall listening positions (P-1 to P-6), the empirical sound decay time averaged **2.53 seconds**. 2. **Comparison with International Standards:**  
   Under international architectural acoustic standards for multipurpose civic assembly halls and speech conventions (ISO 3382-1; ANSI/ASA S12.60; Egan, 2007; Cavanaugh et al., 2010), the recommended optimal reverberation time for clear speech transmission is **RT60 = 0.80 to 1.10 seconds**. The measured vacant decay time of **2.53 seconds** exceeds this international threshold by **more than 130%**. In such an environment, successive spoken syllables linger and collide with subsequent words, causing catastrophic speech masking, vocal fatigue, and severe speech intelligibility loss.
3. **Physical Verification of Flutter Echo Defects:**  
   Concurrently with the interrupted signal cutoff and single-handclap impulse checks (Egan, 2007; Cavanaugh et al., 2010), **flutter echo defects were physically observed and verified across all six Main Hall listening nodes (P-1 through P-6)**. The combination of perfectly parallel, specular boundaries—specifically the 711 sqm glazed ceramic floor plate (α = 0.02) opposing the unbaffled ceiling deck (α = 0.04), alongside bare lateral concrete walls—traps acoustic energy in rapid periodic inter-reflections, generating the audible metallic "buzzing" and "ringing" flutter described by Informants EP4 and EA4. 

   Crucially, physical architectural audits (**CCCC plans and physical site context/01_existing_physical_site_context_and_spatial_audit.md**) clarified the upper envelope dynamics: while the **glass walls between columns 1B–2B and 3B–4B on Grid Line B have a height of 3,540 mm** (topped by a 1,850 mm gypsum bulkhead to the ceiling), and the exterior walls on Grids 1 and 4 have their 2,040 mm doors and 1,500 mm clerestory windows covered by curtains hanging from **+3,640 mm AFFL**, the **unpadded painted gypsum board bulkheads in the upper room volume (from +3,640 mm to +5,390 mm AFFL on Grids 1 & 4, and from +3,540 mm to +5,390 mm on Grid B)** remain completely exposed and hard. These parallel bare gypsum surfaces, opposing the flat plasterboard ceiling and glazed tile floor, reflect high-energy sound across the upper volume, bouncing acoustic waves repeatedly across the hall plate and generating the severe flutter echo defects and decay elongation recorded at Nodes P-3, P-4, and P-6. Conversely, in the 129 sqm Lobby (Nodes P-7 and P-8), flutter echo was *Not Observed*, as the substantially lower ceiling height (3.0 m) and angular foyer partitions allow sound to decay in 1.00 to 1.17 seconds.

This mathematically validates the empirical findings of Table 4.8c and the survey metrics of Table 4.7, establishing that without engineered acoustic wall and ceiling intervention, the facility cannot achieve the **optimal speech threshold of 0.80 to 1.10 seconds (Egan, 2007; Cavanaugh et al., 2010)**.

### 4.3.4. Actor-Network Theory (ANT) & Affordance Interpretation of Sensory and Spatial Friction
Applying **Actor-Network Theory (Latour, 2005)** and **Gibson’s (1979) Affordance Theory**, the acoustic and visual distress experienced by attendees is understood not as an isolated human perception, but as an aggressive **network friction and denied affordances imposed by non-human actants**:
1. **The Ceramic Tile & Unpadded Bulkhead Actants (Denied Auditory Affordance):** The physical material properties of the 600 × 600 mm glazed tiles, perimeter glass, and unpadded upper gypsum bulkheads actively resist sound wave absorption. They bounce acoustic energy back into the room, generating severe flutter echoes (RT60 > 2.0 s) that **fail to afford speech intelligibility**, overpowering the human voice of the speaker (Human Actor) and forcing audio technicians to push PA amplifiers into acoustic feedback squeals.
2. **The Industrial Fluorescent Actant (Denied Visual Comfort Affordance):** Static fluorescent fixtures lack ballast shielding and dimming circuitry, imposing visual glare and micro-flicker that **fail to afford visual comfort** on attendees attempting to read presentation slides or educational handouts.
3. **The Improvised Extension Cord Actant (Denied Barrier-Free Circulation Affordance):** Because the floor plate lacks flush utility floor boxes or overhead power drops, technicians tape extension cords across walking corridors, acting as physical tripping hazards that **fail to afford safe, barrier-free human movement**.

#### 4.5.2.1. Architectural Specification: Forward-Facing Collaborative Modular Seating Arrangements

To resolve the chronic pedagogical and ergonomic dilemma observed during small-to-midscale departmental training sessions (such as the *DTI Samar Creative Entrepreneurship Training*, Event #3)—where linear classroom rows isolate attendees from group collaboration while traditional banquet round tables force half the room into an uncomfortable 180° neck-twisting posture to view presentations—the framework establishes **Forward-Facing Collaborative Modular Seating Arrangements**:

1. **Physical Formation & Furniture Re-Use:** Rather than demanding expensive specialized furniture, each seating cluster re-layouts two standard venue rectangular tables (450/600 × 1,800 mm) into a perpendicular "T" or collaborative cluster accommodating 6 to 8 participants. The horizontal table functions as the "front wing" while the perpendicular table serves as the "team spine."
2. **Ergonomic Visual Cone & Sightlines:** All participants are seated along the outer flanks facing forward and inward. Maximum head rotation to view the front LED video wall and presenter is strictly constrained to ≤ 30°, fully complying with ergonomic visual comfort standards (Panero & Zelnik, 1979; Grandjean, 1988) and preventing cervical fatigue during multi-hour instructional seminars.
3. **Continuous Facilitator Coaching Spine:** Inter-cluster clear spacing is maintained at 1,200 to 1,500 mm, establishing dedicated longitudinal and transverse roaming lanes. This enables workshop trainers and guest speakers to circulate freely around every group without brushing seated participants or stepping over floor obstacles.
4. **Utility Infrastructure & Peripheral Infill:** Power is supplied tool-free from overhead ceiling retractable cord reels (Module 3) dropping directly into table-mounted modesty wire raceways, completely eliminating floor cable snakes. In hybrid training events requiring product exhibitions, mobile craft display tables (600 × 1,800 mm) are deployed along perimeter walls behind a 1,200 mm display buffer, seamlessly integrating instructional coaching with commercial showcase zones while keeping the newly activated 129 sqm lobby concourse open for registration and hospitality.

#### 4.5.2.2. Architectural Specification: Dual Banquet Table Geometries & Statutory Capacity Ceiling (Templates 3A-1 & 3A-2)

Field investigations and archival trace observations (Section 4.2.4.5) reveal that banquet dining in the Calbayog Cultural Convention Center encompasses two fundamentally distinct spatial geometries driven by event typology and ceremonial traditions, rather than a single uniform round-table layout:

1. **Format A: Round Table Diamond Clusters (Template 3A-1, Max 300 pax):**  
   Tailored for civic galas, awards banquets, and formal wedding receptions (such as the *Stakeholders' Gala Night* and private nuptials). Ten-person round tables (Ø 1800 mm, total seated footprint 3200–) are arranged in a staggered diamond grid. By rotating adjacent table axes, service corridors between table perimeters expand to a guaranteed 1,620 to 1,800 mm (exceeding Neufert's 1,400 mm threshold). This allows waitstaff carrying 10-plate trays to maneuver smoothly behind seated guests without brushing chair backs. Motorized overhead utility battens (Module 3) suspend all stage and wash lighting, **eliminating four bulky ground truss towers and reclaiming 36 sqm of floor area—instantly recovering 40 guest seats (4 full tables)** to safely achieve the maximum 300-occupant ceiling.
2. **Format B: Rectangular Linear Communal Banquet Clusters with Processional Aisle (Template 3A-2, Max 240–280 pax):**  
   Tailored for civic-religious galas, fraternal convocations, and ceremonial banquets (such as the *Knights of Columbus Council 3288 Hermana Night 2026*, Event #118). Rather than round tables, standard venue rectangular tables (450/600 × 1800 mm) are paired end-to-end to form 10-person communal banquet clusters. Crucially, these communal tables are oriented **strictly parallel to a continuous 2,400 to 3,000 mm central ceremonial processional aisle** connecting the main lobby double doors directly to the stage. This wide central avenue accommodates the ceremonial grand entrance of the religious float (*carroza*), religious relics, grand knights, and the traditional sword-arch honors without disrupting seated diners. Rented T-bar light stands previously placed next to perimeter structural columns (which pinched lateral pathways down to < 900 mm) are eliminated through overhead motorized rigging, opening up 1,500 mm unobstructed perimeter service corridors.
3. **The Standards Compliance vs. Client Pax Demand Capacity Ceiling Paradigm:**  
   Under the National Building Code (PD 1096 Section 1207) and the Revised Fire Code of the Philippines (RA 9514 Section 10.2.8.1), unconcentrated dining assembly mandates a minimum occupant load factor of **1.40 m²/occupant**. When applied to the facility's 711 sqm Main Hall:
   - Raw mathematical division (711 / 1.40 = 507 pax) represents a completely unencumbered, wall-to-wall void with zero stage, zero service aisles, and zero egress paths.
   - In actual architectural operations, deducting the 52.5 sqm stage dais, the 2,400–3,000 mm central ceremonial processional aisle or radial service avenues, perimeter life-safety corridors (≥ 1500 mm clear), and waiter bussing buffers leaves a **net usable dining footprint of approximately 420 to 450 sqm**.
   - Dividing this net footprint by the statutory 1.40 m² factor yields a **true statutory and ergonomic maximum capacity ceiling of 250 to 300 occupants**.
   - When event clients demand 450 to 500 occupants (as documented in Option A during *Hermana Night 2026*), event coordinators are forced into dangerous, illegal compromises: table-to-table pitch is collapsed to ~1400 mm, leaving pulled chair clearances under 300 mm, banquet tables are jammed against exit doors, and buffet queues block primary egress portals.
   - **Methodological & Design Significance:** The inability of Option B to accommodate 500 banquet occupants is **not a design limitation, but an empirical architectural finding**: it mathematically and physically proves that the Calbayog Cultural Convention Center is severely undersized for 500 banquet diners. Option B enforces statutory life safety and human dignity by establishing 250–300 pax as the rigid operational ceiling, while utilizing interior design interventions (overhead rigging recovering 40 seats, 129 sqm lobby catering bypass) to maximize legal capacity to its absolute safe physical limit.

*FGD Scoring Synthesis & Cross-Stakeholder Triangulation:*  
The quantitative scenario evaluation demonstrates an overwhelming, statistically significant consensus across all three stakeholder groups: **Option B (Proposed Modular Framework) consistently outperformed Option A (Conventional Status Quo) across all twelve evaluated operational scenarios (Overall Composite Mean x̄ = 4.62 vs. x̄ = 3.06)**. 

Triangulating this scoring synthesis with the qualitative interview transcripts validates the concrete spatial mechanics of the proposed framework:
1. **Resolution of Sightline and Stage Constriction in Plenary Assemblies (Scenarios 1A–1C, x̄ = 4.65 to 4.71):**  
   The radial chevron seating arrangement in Option B directly resolves the severe sightline obstructions documented by attendees (EA1, EA3, EA5). In Peak Mode (Scenario 1A: 250–300 pax), introducing staggered row offsets and radial aisles guarantees an unobstructed sightline clearance (C ≥ 60 mm), ensuring that rear attendees are not blinded by taller occupants in front. In Compact Mode (Scenario 1C: 50–100 pax, observed during the *TESDA Graduation*), Option B deploys mobile Basey *banig* acoustic screens at Column Grid D to partition the front third of the hall, dropping reverberation from RT60 = 2.50 s down to 0.95 s and eliminating the hollow cathedral echo. Furthermore, the framework's **venue-provided demountable modular staging system (interlocking platforms with variable heights of 300 mm, 600 mm, or 900 mm)** completely replaces the inflexible legacy concrete stage. This resolves the chronic operational failure reported by Informants EP1, EA1, and EA5, where cultural dance troupes and musical ensembles were previously forced to perform down on the bare floor due to the constricted dimensions of the legacy concrete stage. Moreover, Informant EP5 revealed that the static 940 mm height of the legacy concrete stage creates severe vertical sightline and ceiling height compression (*"mura ni na siya ug part sa ulo sa tawo"*), while its permanent concrete side stairs prevent scaffolding from sitting flush against the back wall during drapery installation. Option B's demountable, height-adjustable modular staging decks allow the stage to be deployed at customized dimensions and heights, equipped with an accessible 1:12 BP 344 ramp, or completely stowed away into storage when an event does not require an elevated platform.
2. **Facilitator Mobility, Cable Hazard Decoupling, and Collaborative Seating in Workshops (Scenarios 2A–2C, x̄ = 4.61 to 4.66):**  
   For institutional training sessions and educational workshops (such as the DTI Creative Entrepreneurship Training and DepEd/LGU seminars), Option B's collaborative modular seating arrangements (6 to 8 participants per group utilizing 600 × 1400 mm flip-top mobile nesting tables on lockable casters) earned high praise from attendees (x̄ = 4.65 to 4.75), CACO officers (x̄ = 4.60 to 4.70), and planners (x̄ = 4.60 to 4.65). In Full-Hall Training (Scenario 2A: 160–200 pax), Option B directly resolves the severe physical frictions documented during Protocol C trace audits and Informants EP3 and EP4's interviews: instead of continuous rigid lecture desks that compressed row push-back clearance to under 750 mm and forced facilitators to sidestep sideways to assist participants, Option B establishes a generous 1500 mm longitudinal facilitator roaming avenue and 1200 mm cross-aisles. In Compact Mode (Scenario 2C: 30–60 pax), collaborative modular seating clusters near the presentation screen while the 129 sqm Lobby is activated for registration and break buffets. Furthermore, overhead retractable ceiling cord reels drop power and data directly into integrated table wire raceways, completely eliminating the hazardous web of floor extension cables that technicians previously spent 1 to 2 hours taping onto slippery ceramic tiles with duct tape. Acoustically, the surrounding Basey *tikog* composite wall panels and waterfall ceiling baffles lower occupied reverberation to RT60 ≤ 0.95 s, resolving the hollow, muddy echo that previously obscured trainer instructions, while integrated BP 344 accessible stations at table cluster ends ensure barrier-free inclusivity for disabled delegates.
3. **Elimination of Banquet Service Bottlenecks, Dual Geometries & Statutory Capacity Ceiling (Scenarios 3A–3C, x̄ = 4.59 to 4.64):**  
   Option B's banquet framework addresses two critical empirical findings established through live event shadowing and archival trace audits: the coexistence of **dual banquet geometries** and the rigorous enforcement of a **statutory capacity ceiling**:
   - **Dual Banquet Table Geometries (Templates 3A-1 & 3A-2):** Grounded in the distinct spatial traditions of Calbayog events, Option B provides two standardized banquet templates:
     - *Template 3A-1 (Staggered Diamond Round Tables, Max 300 pax):* For social awards and weddings, 25 to 30 ten-person round tables (Ø 1800 mm) are arranged in staggered diamond clusters. This layout guarantees 1,620 to 1,800 mm clear corridors between table perimeters, eliminating the tray collisions described by Informants EP3 and EP4.
     - *Template 3A-2 (Rectangular Linear Communal Clusters with Processional Aisle, Max 240–280 pax):* Grounded directly in the archival audit of *Hermana Night 2026* (Event #118), standard venue rectangular tables (450/600 × 1800 mm) are paired into 10-person communal clusters oriented **strictly parallel to a continuous 2,400 to 3,000 mm central ceremonial processional aisle**. This preserves a grand, unhindered avenue for the religious float (*carroza*) and fraternal sword-arch honors connecting the main entrance double doors directly to the stage dais, while providing 1,500 mm lateral service lanes.
   - **Overhead Rigging Space Recovery (Reclaiming 40 Seats):** In both templates, motorized overhead utility battens (Module 3) eliminate the four heavy aluminum ground truss lifters and perimeter column-mounted T-bar light stands documented in Option A. This **instantly reclaims 36 sqm of prime floor space (the equivalent of four full 10-person tables, or 40 guest seats)** and expands pinched perimeter pathways from < 900 mm back to code-compliant 1,500 mm service avenues.
   - **The Standards Compliance vs. Client Pax Demand (Capacity Ceiling) Paradigm:** The FGD highlighted a fundamental tension between historical client booking demands (~450–500 pax for major civic galas like *Hermana Night*) and statutory life-safety codes. In Option A, accommodating 500 banquet occupants forced catastrophic non-compliance: table pitch collapsed to ~1400 mm, chair push-back clearance was restricted to ≤ 300 mm, banquet tables were jammed against perimeter sliding doors, and buffet queues blocked the main lobby egress doors. By contrast, Option B strictly enforces the statutory occupant load factor of **1.40 m²/occupant mandated by the National Building Code (PD 1096 Section 1207) and Fire Code (RA 9514 Section 10.2.8.1)**. Deducting the stage platform, life-safety radial egress paths (≥ 1500 mm), and catering buffers leaves a net functional dining area of ~420 to 450 sqm, mathematically capping safe dining capacity at **250 to 300 occupants**. Stakeholders unanimously affirmed (x̄ = 4.64) that Option B's inability to seat 500 diners is **not a design deficiency, but an empirical architectural finding**: it proves that the Calbayog Cultural Convention Center's 711 sqm Main Hall is physically and legally undersized for 500 banquet diners. Option B upholds life-safety integrity by establishing 300 pax as the rigid statutory ceiling, while utilizing interior flexibility interventions (overhead rigging recovering 40 seats, conditioned 129 sqm lobby catering bypass) to legally maximize capacity to its absolute safe physical limit. In Intimate Mode (Scenario 3C: 80–150 pax), diamond clusters concentrate in the central zone, opening up a dedicated dance floor and stage clearance, with mobile decorative screens isolating the buffet staging area.
4. **Decoupling Floor Cable Hazards & Zero-Stage Flat-Floor Space Recovery in Exhibitions (Scenarios 4A–4C, x̄ = 4.50 to 4.60):**  
   For trade exhibitions and culinary showcases, Option B's central island configuration received enthusiastic endorsement from both planners (x̄ = 4.50 to 4.60) and attendees (x̄ = 4.50 to 4.65). Crucially, in Mega Trade Mode (Scenario 4A: 20–26 Booths), Option B introduces the **Zero-Stage Flat-Floor Mode**: because trade fairs, artisanal expos, and culinary cookfests require maximum flat floor area rather than an elevated dais, the venue completely demounts the modular staging risers and stows them onto backstage rolling dollies. This **instantly reclaims the entire 52.5 sqm footprint** previously locked by the legacy concrete dais, extending the continuous A-xet Bianco Snow Terrazzo floor plate and accommodating **4 to 6 additional standard 2,000 × 2,000 mm vendor booths** while establishing generous 2,400 mm arterial walkways. In Boutique Cookfests (Scenario 4C: 10–14 Stations, grounded in the *Tinapa 101 Cookfest*), perimeter demonstration stations are paired with a central elevated radial judging hub and organized queue stanchions, eliminating the severe crowd crushes previously documented around a single display table, while the air-conditioned 129 sqm Lobby is transformed into a dedicated cultural gallery showcasing the history of tinapa in Calbayog City and its cultural significance with wand-drawn fabric dividers. The integration of overhead utility drops and ceiling rigging directly addresses the operational bottlenecks voiced by Informants EP3 and EP4—eliminating perimeter cable clutter and avoiding floor crossings, while motorized hoists eliminate the hours lost waiting for manual ceiling decoration and scaffolding setups—freeing up continuous pedestrian thoroughfares (2400 mm) and isolating power runs safely overhead. Furthermore, as articulated by Informant EP5, the permanent overhead grid eliminates the 2 to 3 hours required to manually rig temporary tie-wire foundations and 5 hours of drapery hanging, compressing turnaround times significantly.
