## 4.1. Research Locale Baseline & Longitudinal Utilization Profile

### 4.1.1. Physical Facility Envelope & Institutional Mandate
The research locale is the **Calbayog Cultural Convention Center** (locally and popularly referenced as the *Calbayog City Convention Center*), located in Barangay Capoocan, Calbayog City, Samar. Originally constructed as a municipal central bus station, the facility underwent extensive adaptive reuse and was formally inaugurated as a municipal civic convention facility on **December 14, 2009**. 

Under its official charter published on the City Government of Calbayog portal, the facility is formally tasked with serving as:
> *"a vital hub for promoting Calbayog City’s rich culture, arts, and tourism, featuring spaces for performances (theatres, pavilions), art displays (galleries, museums), and cultural training, aiming to preserve local traditions like the Sarakiki Hadang Festival, showcase artifacts, and boost economic development through events and local product showcases, acting as a central venue for community gatherings and cultural exchange."*

The physical scope of this interior design investigation is strictly confined to the primary public-assembly interior envelope, measuring **840 square meters**, subdivided into:
1. **The Main Convention Hall:** **711 square meters** of open, column-free interior floor plate designed for up to **500 occupants** in plenary theatrical configuration; and
2. **The Lobby / Pre-Function Area:** **129 square meters** serving as the primary pedestrian reception, registration threshold, and ingress/egress circulation buffer.

### 4.1.2. Venue Ecosystem Delineation
To establish clear academic and operational boundaries, the role of the convention center must be differentiated from other municipal facilities within Calbayog City’s civic infrastructure:
* **The Calbayog City Sports Center:** A large-scale athletic arena designed for high-density spectator sporting tournaments, mass civic rallies, and the outdoor street-dancing showdowns of the **Hadang Festival (historically and popularly recognized as the Sarakiki-Hadang Festival)**.
* **The Handumanan Museum / Arts Gallery:** A specialized cultural repository dedicated to permanent historical collections, antiquities, and curated visual arts archives.
* **The Calbayog Cultural Convention Center:** The city's only affordable, fully air-conditioned, medium-capacity (500-pax) civic convention venue. It is tailored for indoor legislative caravans, departmental trainings, academic symposia, private banquets, and curated cultural convocations.

This ecosystem distinction was unequivocally corroborated by operational practitioners who regularly navigate Calbayog's venue constraints:

### 4.1.3. Structural Durability and Disaster Resilience
Samar Island is situated directly along the Eastern Visayas typhoon corridor. Over its 17-year operational lifespan, the convention center's reinforced concrete superstructure and structural steel portal frame have demonstrated resilience, successfully withstanding severe meteorological crises:
1. **Super Typhoon Yolanda (Haiyan, November 2013):** Category 5+ super typhoon; the building shell remained fully intact and served as an immediate emergency logistics hub.
2. **Typhoon Ruby (Hagupit, December 2014):** Landfall over Samar with sustained winds exceeding 215 km/h.
3. **Typhoon Tisoy (Kammuri, December 2019):** Severe storm system causing extensive regional infrastructure damage.
4. **COVID-19 Pandemic Logistics Hub (2020–2021):** Staging center for 4,000 sacks of rice and continuous relief repacking for 35,000 target families (Appendix A, Items #18–19).

*Thesis Significance:* This verified structural resilience directly confirms that the facility's architectural shell is durable and sound. Consequently, the building does not require structural demolition or civil engineering reconstruction; rather, it requires an **evidence-based, non-structural interior overlay** that modernizes its internal ergonomics, acoustic performance, and spatial modularity.

### 4.3.4. Actor-Network Theory (ANT) Interpretation of Sensory Friction
Applying **Actor-Network Theory (Latour, 2005)**, the acoustic and visual distress experienced by attendees is understood not as an isolated human perception, but as an aggressive **network friction imposed by non-human actants**:
1. **The Ceramic Tile & Glass Actants:** The physical material properties of the $600 \times 600\text{ mm}$ glazed tiles and perimeter glass resist sound wave absorption. They actively bounce acoustic energy back into the room, overpowering the human voice of the speaker (Human Actor) and forcing audio technicians to push PA amplifiers into acoustic feedback squeals.
2. **The Industrial Fluorescent Actant:** Static fluorescent fixtures lack ballast shielding and dimming circuitry, imposing visual glare and micro-flicker on attendees attempting to read presentation slides or educational handouts.
3. **The Improvised Extension Cord Actant:** Because the floor plate lacks flush utility floor boxes or overhead power drops, technicians tape extension cords across walking corridors, acting as physical tripping hazards that restrict human movement.

Triangulating this scoring synthesis with the qualitative interview transcripts validates the concrete spatial mechanics of the proposed framework:
1. **Resolution of Sightline and Stage Constriction (Scenario 1, $\bar{x} = 4.71$):**  
   The radial chevron seating arrangement in Option B directly resolves the severe sightline obstructions documented by attendees (EA1, EA3, EA5). By introducing staggered row offsets and radial aisles, the layout guarantees an unobstructed sightline clearance ($C \ge 60\text{ mm}$), ensuring that rear attendees are not blinded by taller occupants in front. Furthermore, the inclusion of modular 300 mm / 600 mm / 900 mm stage extension decks eliminates the chronic operational failure reported by Informants EP1, EA1, and EA5, where cultural dance troupes and musical ensembles were previously forced to perform down on the bare floor due to the constricted dimensions of the legacy concrete stage.
2. **Elimination of Banquet Service Bottlenecks (Scenario 3, $\bar{x} = 4.62$):**  
   Option B strictly enforces the **300-occupant maximum capacity threshold** articulated by Informant EP1 (*"for banquet seating, 300 is the real safe maximum"*) and Informant EP2 (*"kun nadiri san suot, 300 is enough"*). By replacing the chaotic orthogonal grid with staggered diamond table clusters maintaining guaranteed 1800 mm clearances between table perimeters, Option B completely eliminates the hazardous corridor friction described by Informant EP3 and EP4—where waitstaff balancing heavy food trays regularly collided with guest chair pull-backs, photo-video gimbals, and floor-bound wiring.
3. **Decoupling Floor Cable Hazards via Overhead Grids (Scenario 4, $\bar{x} = 4.52$):**  
   For trade exhibitions and culinary showcases, Option B's central island configuration received enthusiastic endorsement from both planners ($\bar{x} = 4.40$) and attendees ($\bar{x} = 4.65$). The integration of overhead utility drops directly addresses the primary logistical nightmare voiced by Informants EP3 and EP4 (spending 1 to 2 hours manually taping cables across slick ceramic floor tiles), freeing up continuous pedestrian thoroughfares ($2400\text{ mm}$) and isolating power runs safely overhead.
4. **Economic and Thermal Flexibility via Acoustic Subdivision (Scenario 5, $\bar{x} = 4.77$):**  
   Achieving the highest composite score in the rubric, the ceiling-tracked kinetic acoustic partition wall ($\text{STC} \ge 45$) solves the facility's historic "all-or-nothing" spatial constraint. Facility administrators (EP2) confirmed that booking smaller government workshops (50–100 pax) in the monolithic 711 sqm hall plate currently wastes enormous electrical and cooling energy while turning away simultaneous municipal bookings. Option B enables two independent, fully isolated civic functions to operate concurrently with zero acoustic bleed, maximizing municipal revenue and community accessibility.
