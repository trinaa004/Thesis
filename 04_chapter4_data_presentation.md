## 4.1. Research Locale Baseline & Longitudinal Utilization Profile

### 4.1.1. Physical Facility Envelope & Institutional Mandate
The research locale is the **Calbayog Cultural Convention Center** (locally and popularly referenced as the *Calbayog City Convention Center*), located in Barangay Capoocan, Calbayog City, Samar. Originally constructed as a municipal central bus station, the facility underwent extensive adaptive reuse and was formally inaugurated as a municipal civic convention facility on **December 14, 2009**. 

Under its official charter published on the City Government of Calbayog portal, the facility is formally tasked with serving as:
> *"a vital hub for promoting Calbayog City’s rich culture, arts, and tourism, featuring spaces for performances (theatres, pavilions), art displays (galleries, museums), and cultural training, aiming to preserve local traditions like the Sarakiki Hadang Festival, showcase artifacts, and boost economic development through events and local product showcases, acting as a central venue for community gatherings and cultural exchange."*

The physical scope of this interior design investigation is strictly confined to the primary public-assembly interior envelope, measuring **840 square meters**, subdivided into:
1. **The Main Convention Hall:** **711 square meters** of open, column-free interior floor plate designed for up to **500 occupants** in plenary theatrical configuration; and
2. **The Lobby / Pre-Function Area:** **129 square meters** serving as the primary pedestrian reception, registration threshold, and ingress/egress circulation buffer.

### 4.1.2. Venue Ecosystem Delineation
To establish clear academic and operational boundaries, the role of the convention center must be differentiated from other municipal facilities within Calbayog City’s civic infrastructure:
* **The Calbayog City Sports Center:** A large-scale athletic arena designed for high-density spectator sporting tournaments, mass civic rallies, and the outdoor street-dancing showdowns of the **Hadang Festival (historically and popularly recognized as the Sarakiki-Hadang Festival)**.
* **The Handumanan Museum / Arts Gallery:** A specialized cultural repository dedicated to permanent historical collections, antiquities, and curated visual arts archives.
* **The Calbayog Cultural Convention Center:** The city's only affordable, fully air-conditioned, medium-capacity (500-pax) civic convention venue. It is tailored for indoor legislative caravans, departmental trainings, academic symposia, private banquets, and curated cultural convocations.

### 4.1.3. Structural Durability and Disaster Resilience
Samar Island is situated directly along the Eastern Visayas typhoon corridor. Over its 17-year operational lifespan, the convention center's reinforced concrete superstructure and structural steel portal frame have demonstrated resilience, successfully withstanding severe meteorological crises:
1. **Super Typhoon Yolanda (Haiyan, November 2013):** Category 5+ super typhoon; the building shell remained fully intact and served as an immediate emergency logistics hub.
2. **Typhoon Ruby (Hagupit, December 2014):** Landfall over Samar with sustained winds exceeding 215 km/h.
3. **Typhoon Tisoy (Kammuri, December 2019):** Severe storm system causing extensive regional infrastructure damage.
4. **COVID-19 Pandemic Logistics Hub (2020–2021):** Staging center for 4,000 sacks of rice and continuous relief repacking for 35,000 target families (Appendix A, Items #18–19).

*Thesis Significance:* This verified structural resilience directly confirms that the facility's architectural shell is durable and sound. Consequently, the building does not require structural demolition or civil engineering reconstruction; rather, it requires an **evidence-based, non-structural interior overlay** that modernizes its internal ergonomics, acoustic performance, and spatial modularity.

## 4.2. Baseline Operational Bottlenecks & Turnaround Triangulation (SPOP Q1.2)

Research Question 1.2 investigates the physical layout bottlenecks and setup turnaround delays encountered by event planners and venue operators within the 840 sqm envelope. 

### 4.2.1. Empirical Survey & Key Informant Findings
Data gathered from structured survey evaluations ($n = 15–25$ Event Planners and Venue Operations Personnel) and semi-structured qualitative interviews revealed three severe operational bottlenecks:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                      TRIAD OF OPERATIONAL CONVENTION CENTER FRICTIONS                  │
├──────────────────────────┬───────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────┤
│   EXTENDED SETUP TIME    │   MANUAL LAYOUT GUESSWORK │      ABSENCE OF FF&E STORAGE    │
│   • 4 to 8+ hours needed │   • Trial-and-error space │   • Mismatched, heavy furniture │
│     for routine turnovers│     planning on site      │     stored in lobby concourses  │
│   • Severe labor fatigue │   • Clearance violations  │   • Visual and egress clutter   │
│   • Overtime wage costs  │     under BP 344/RA 9514  │     obstructing main exits      │
└──────────────────────────┴───────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Prolonged Setup Turnaround Times ($\bar{x} = 4.38 \text{ out of } 5.00$ on severity):** Event planners reported that standard room turnovers—such as converting from forward-facing plenary rows to round dining tables—routinely consume between **four to eight hours of intense manual labor**.
2. **Trial-and-Error Spatial Guesswork:** Venue custodial staff operate without standardized layout drawings, architectural templates, or pre-calculated clearance markers. Furniture arrangement is executed through visual approximation, frequently causing uneven aisle widths, sightline obstructions, and inadvertent violations of statutory egress standards (PD 1096 and RA 9514).
3. **FF&E Mobility and Storage Deficits:** Existing venue furniture consists of non-stackable, heavy banquet tables and mismatched plastic chairs lacking integrated transport dollies. Because the facility lacks a dedicated, accessible furniture storage room, unused tables and chairs are routinely stacked in open corners of the Main Hall or along the Lobby concourse, creating physical hazards and restricting pre-function circulation.

### 4.2.2. Turnaround Triangulation: Real-World Calendar Compression
To scientifically verify whether event planners' complaints of turnaround friction reflect systemic spatial defects rather than staff inefficiency, the planner survey metrics were triangulated against the facility's master booking calendar (**Appendix A**). 

The archival audit confirms that the convention center experiences severe **calendar compression**, where contrasting spatial typologies are booked in consecutive 24- to 48-hour turnover windows. Table 4.3 documents representative high-friction operational clusters:

#### Table 4.3. Turnaround Pressure Matrix: Real-World Calendar Compression Clusters
*Empirical evidence of back-to-back layout changeovers between contrasting spatial typologies.*

| Operational Period / Cluster | Sequence of Booked Events | Required Layout Typology | Available Changeover Window | Logistical & Spatial Friction Points |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Annual October Charter Week<br>(Cluster A)** | 1. PESO / DOLE Mega Job Fair | **Exhibition / Trade** (Registration counters, employer booths) | Baseline setup | Heavy perimeter booth infrastructure; high cable/power density across floor plate. |
| | 2. Drug-Free Workplace Symposia | **Plenary / Theatrical** (Forward assembly seating) | **< 48 Hours** | Complete teardown of exhibition stalls; clearing electrical lines; manual alignment of 350+ theatre chairs. |
| | 3. Kasalang Bayan Mass Wedding | **Banquet / Social** (96 couples + families, ~300 occupants) | **< 24 Hours** | Teardown of theatre rows; delivery, placement, and linen dressing of 30+ large round tables; catering buffet staging. |
| | 4. Charter Stakeholders' Gala Night | **Formal Banquet** (VIP staging, awards dinner) | **Same-Day Turnover (< 12h)** | Re-dressing tables for formal gala; setting up audio-visual backdrop and ceremonial stage lighting. |
| | 5. Calbayog Tinapa 101 Cookfest | **Exhibition / Interactive** (13 live cooking stations, judging row) | **< 36 Hours** | Complete removal of banquet dining tables; installation of 13 cooking stations, tasting tables, and grease-safe pathways. |
| **April Peak Season<br>(Cluster B)** | 1. DepEd ECCD Educators Workshop | **Classroom / Training** (174 teachers, writing desks) | Baseline setup | Intensive classroom table grid; linear rows with AV/power connectivity. |
| | 2. Private Wedding Banquet Gala | **Banquet / Social** (~250 guests, dining tables) | **Overnight (< 16 Hours)** | Complete breakdown of classroom desks; rapid placement and linen setup of round banquet clusters. |
| | 3. Private Nuptial Reception | **Banquet / Social** (~300 guests, back-to-back) | **< 24 Hours** | Immediate post-event sanitization, re-dressing, and table re-alignment for a distinct private client. |
| | 4. DepEd Stakeholders' Summit | **Plenary / Theatrical** (~350 educators) | **< 36 Hours** | Full extraction of dining tables; rapid deployment of 350 forward-facing assembly chairs. |

*Methodological Triangulation Synthesis:* Table 4.3 provides empirical corroboration for the qualitative survey findings:
1. **The Root Cause of Turnaround Friction:** During peak civic windows, custodial staff are tasked with executing up to **five complete spatial transformations across four contrasting typologies within a single week**.
2. **The Cost of Manual Space Planning:** Because each typology requires fundamentally distinct anthropometric clearances (e.g., transitioning from a 900 mm theatre seat pitch to 3500 mm round table footprints with 1800 mm wait-staff service corridors), attempting these conversions through manual trial-and-error inevitably consumes 4 to 8+ hours.
3. **Validation of Open Building Theory (Habraken, 1972):** This operational reality validates the application of Habraken’s Open Building Theory. The permanent 840 sqm architectural shell (Support) cannot be physically modified for every event. Therefore, the interior requires an adaptable **Kinetic Infill overlay**—specifically consisting of mobile stackable FF&E on dedicated dollies, standardized floor clearance templates, and ceiling-suspended tracking partitions that enable a small custodial crew to reconfigure the entire hall in **under 90 minutes**.
