## ABSTRACT

The rapid evolution of the Meetings, Incentives, Conventions, and Exhibitions (MICE) industry demands adaptable regional venues capable of executing swift spatial turnarounds while expressing authentic civic and cultural identity. Officially designated by the City Government of Calbayog as a primary hub for culture, arts, and tourism, the Calbayog Cultural Convention Center operates within an unzoned 840-square-meter interior envelope (711 m² Main Plenary Hall and 129 m² Entrance Lobby) that suffers from severe setup bottlenecks, acoustic speech blur, and spatial inflexibility. Grounded in Actor-Network Theory, Environmental Affordance Theory, and Place Attachment, this study developed evidence-based, non-structural interior design interventions and an operational layout manual to optimize spatial versatility and cultural placemaking without structural alteration. Adopting an Applied Design Research methodology, the study executed a convergent parallel mixed-methods design comprising: (1) an archival and physical audit of 127 verified longitudinal events (2014–2026); (2) structured quantitative surveys administered to professional event planners and AV operators (n = 12, Cronbach's alpha = 0.9402) and civic event attendees (N = 100); (3) semi-structured interviews with key informants (n = 10); (4) multi-phase live shadowing across five representative events; and (5) on-site acoustic sound decay testing across an 8-point spatial grid (ISO 3382-2:2008). 

Empirical baseline findings revealed acute material frictions: 83.3% of venue operators reported setup turnarounds requiring 4 to 12+ hours due to a lack of overhead rigging points (Mean = 4.42), while attendees reported severe environmental dissatisfaction across all Indoor Environmental Quality (IEQ) indicators (Mean = 2.44, Deficient), led by debilitating thermal discomfort (Mean = 2.08) and speech intelligibility degradation (Mean = 2.57). On-site acoustic audits documented a vacant sound decay time of RT60 = 2.53 seconds across the Main Hall (peaking at 3.00 s at the stage apron), exceeding the optimal MICE speech intelligibility threshold (0.80–1.10 s) by +130.0% due to 711 m² of polished ceramic tiles, unbaffled ceilings, and bare upper gypsum bulkheads. Furthermore, historical audit revealed that 100% of facility bookings distribute across four distinct typologies: Plenary (48.8%), Classroom (25.2%), Banquet (13.4%), and Exhibition (12.6%), with over 56.7% packed into the 2025–2026 operational window. 

To resolve these deficits, the study developed evidence-based interior design interventions across four actionable operational modules: (1) standardized, anthropometrically calculated layout typologies bounded by strict life-safety and accessibility codes (PD 1096, RA 9514, BP 344), recovering 30 to 40 seats previously lost to ground truss lifters; (2) culturally responsive acoustic surface treatments abstracting Samar Basey banig geometric weaving, tikog grass textures, Calbayog waterfall fluid forms, and Sarakiki-Hadang proscenium motifs that reduce RT60 to 0.95 seconds; (3) a non-structural overhead utility infrastructure integrating continuous Unistrut P1000 channels, motorized rigging hoists, 3-circuit DALI dimmable scene presets, and perimeter HVAC distribution; and (4) a Dual-Zone Lobby concourse retrofit converting the unconditioned foyer into an 8-stall indoor exhibition concourse. Pre-occupancy evaluative Focus Group Discussion (FGD) validation (N = 8–10 multi-stakeholder panelists) across 12 operational scenarios confirmed that these interior design interventions slash room turnaround times to 1.5–3.0 hours, recover usable capacity, guarantee 100% clear egress, and establish an authentic Samar cultural identity.

**Keywords**: *Evidence-Based Interior Design Interventions, Modular Space Planning, Calbayog Cultural Convention Center, Samar Cultural Heritage, Hadang Festival, MICE Industry, Reverberation Time (RT60), Overhead Rigging Systems, Universal Accessibility (BP 344).*
