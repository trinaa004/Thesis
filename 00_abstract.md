## ABSTRACT

The rapid evolution of the Meetings, Incentives, Conventions, and Exhibitions (MICE) industry demands adaptable civic venues capable of executing swift layout turnarounds while expressing local identity. However, municipal facilities like the Calbayog City Convention Center operate within rigid interior envelopes that cause prolonged setup times and spatial inefficiencies. This study aims to formulate a site-specific, non-structural interior design framework and layout manual for the venue’s 840 square meter Main Hall. Adopting an Applied Design Research and Multi-Phase Spatial Synthesis Framework across four operational phases, the study employs a sequential explanatory mixed-methods methodology. Quantitative data will be gathered from 115 to 145 respondents (15–25 event planners via expert sampling and 100–120 attendees via Slovin's formula quota sampling), complemented by qualitative interviews with 8 to 12 key informants and architectural site mapping. Survey data will be analyzed using descriptive statistics, interview transcripts using Braun and Clarke’s (2006) thematic analysis, and findings triangulated with anthropometric (Panero & Zelnik; Neufert) and accessibility standards (BP 344; ADA). The resulting framework integrates modular furniture typologies (Plenary, Classroom, Banquet, Exhibition), an adaptable overhead ceiling rigging interface, and non-structural surface pattern matrices abstracting Samar Basey *banig* weaving, *tikog* grass, and Calbayog waterfall motifs. The framework is validated through 2D/3D digital spatial clearance simulations (BP 344/ADA), workflow turnaround modeling, and expert practitioner evaluations ($n = 3–5$) using a structured design rubric to substantiate operational viability, turnaround optimization, and regional civic identity.

**Keywords**: *Interior Design Framework, Modular Space Planning, Calbayog City Convention Center, Local Samar Heritage, MICE Industry, Overhead Ceiling Rigging Systems, Universal Accessibility.*
