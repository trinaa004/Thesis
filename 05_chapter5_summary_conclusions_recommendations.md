# Chapter 5: Summary, Conclusions, and Recommendations

This chapter synthesizes the empirical findings, theoretical analyses, and design translations established throughout the study. It is organized into three major divisions:
* **Section 5.1: Summary of Findings** (Synthesizing the empirical results corresponding to SPOP Q1, Q2, and Q3)
* **Section 5.2: Conclusions** (Formulating theoretical, operational, ergonomic, and socio-cultural conclusions)
* **Section 5.3: Recommendations** (Proposing actionable institutional, architectural, and future research directives)

## 5.1. Summary of Findings

The primary objective of this study was to conduct a spatial utilization analysis and develop site-specific, evidence-based interior design interventions for the 840 sqm interior envelope (711 sqm Main Hall + 129 sqm Lobby) of the Calbayog Cultural Convention Center. Through an **Applied Design Research** methodology executed across a **Tri-Strand Convergent Mixed-Methods Design** (QUAN + QUAL + SPATIAL/ANT), the study yielded the following empirical findings: 

### 5.1.1. Baseline Spatial, Ergonomic, and Environmental Deficits (SPOP Q1)
1. **Attendee Indoor Environmental Quality (IEQ) Dissatisfaction (SPOP Q1.1):**
   - General event attendees (N = 100) evaluated on a 5-point Likert scale reported severe environmental dissatisfaction across all measured parameters, yielding a composite baseline IEQ mean of **x̄ = 2.44 (SD = 0.89)**, interpreted as *Disagree / Deficient*.
   - **Thermal Comfort & Ventilation Deficit (B3):** Scored lowest among all survey parameters at **x̄ = 2.08 (SD = 0.93, Critical Deficit)**, caused by severe thermal stratification where split AC units drop cool air solely along perimeters, leaving stagnant warm plumes in central seating zones, exacerbated by the unconditioned 129 sqm Lobby buffer.
   - **Spatial Layout & Cramped Legroom (B5):** Scored **x̄ = 2.39 (SD = 0.75, Disagree / Deficient)**, reflecting severe legroom compression (< 750 mm pitch) from non-ergonomic plastic monobloc chairs.
   - **Stage & Screen Sightline Obstructions (B4):** Scored **x̄ = 2.45 (SD = 0.93, Disagree / Deficient)** due to flat-floor planar sightline blockages (C < 60 mm).
   - **Acoustic Speech Intelligibility Degradation (B1):** Scored **x̄ = 2.57 (SD = 0.93, Disagree / Deficient)**, corroborated by physical in-situ acoustic audits documenting an occupied reverberation time of **RT60 ≈ 1.85 to 2.20 seconds** (and 2.53 s unoccupied decay), far exceeding the international standard (0.80 to 1.10 s) due to 711 m² of reflective glazed ceramic tiles, uninsulated metal deck ceiling, and critically, the **unpadded painted gypsum board bulkheads** in the upper volume (spanning from the +3,640 mm curtain line on Grids 1 & 4, and above the 3,540 mm high glass walls on Grid B, up to the 5,390 mm ceiling) acting as hard specular reflectors that trap sound in the upper hall volume.
   - **Interior Lighting Quality & Glare (B2):** Scored **x̄ = 2.69 (SD = 0.91, Marginal / Deficient)** due to unshielded industrial fixtures, missing luminaires in Zone A and the Lobby, and static visual glare.

2. **Operational Setup Bottlenecks & Turnaround Delays (SPOP Q1.2):**
   - Survey evaluations from professional event planners and venue operators (n = 12) rated baseline operational friction high, reporting that routine room turnovers require **4 to 12+ hours of intense manual labor** (83.3%).
   - Spatial trace mapping and physical empty hall audits identified systemic physical bottlenecks: (a) absence of dedicated furniture storage, forcing custodial crews to stack loose chairs in lobby corridors; (b) nominal 2000 mm perimeter double doors functionally halved to **1000 mm usable clear width** because only one leaf opens; (c) loading congestion concentrated along **Column Grid Line 4**, where flight cases continuously bump and scar door jambs; (d) total lack of overhead rigging points, causing coordinators to drill makeshift wall holes for tie-wire and leaving an exposed sub-ceiling plenum in Zone A near **Column 3B**; (e) loose continuous draperies lacking tiebacks, obscuring emergency sliding doors and forcing attendees to blindly search for exits; and (f) complete absence of illuminated Fire Exit signage (RA 9514) and restroom wayfinding signs.
   - Archival audit of **127 verified event records (2014–2026)** confirmed severe **calendar compression**, documenting back-to-back changeovers with less than 16 to 24 hours between contrasting typologies during peak civic windows (e.g., October Charter Week).

### 5.1.2. Formulation & Comparative Validation of Modular Layout Typologies (SPOP Q2)
1. **Empirical Extraction of Four Primary Typologies & Documented Hybrid Configurations:**
   - Rather than assuming typologies a priori, classification of the 127 historical event records revealed four organic spatial patterns: **Plenary / Theatrical (49.6%)**, **Classroom / Training (25.2%)**, **Banquet / Social (13.4%)**, and **Exhibition / Cookfest (11.8%)**. While these four primary typologies represent the predominant booking patterns across the 12-year ledger, analysis of the ledger records and direct on-site shadowing across five live events also identified documented compound and dual-zone hybrid configurations (e.g., classroom/workshop assemblies coupled with indoor tent exhibitions and cultural arts showcases, and main hall banquets coupled with lobby concourse expos) that require concurrent multi-program versatility.
2. **Stratified Capacity Tiering & Archival Occupant Estimation Protocol:**
   - Operating without electronic turnstiles, historical occupant loads across the 127 longitudinal events were reconstructed through a four-level archival triangulation protocol (Groat & Wang, 2013; Zeisel, 2006; PD 1096; RA 9514), synthesizing published LGU/DepEd metrics, photographic chair array audits, building/fire code density thresholds, and live event shadowing calibration.
   - Bookings stratified into four distinct capacity tiers: **Tier 1 (Small / Compact Assembly: 50–120 pax, 28.3%, f = 36)**, **Tier 2 (Medium Civic / Seminars: 150–250 pax, 15.0%, f = 19)**, **Tier 3 (Large Civic / Social Banquets: 250–350 pax, 18.9%, f = 24)**, and **Tier 4 (Peak Civic Convocations: 400–500 pax, 37.8%, f = 48)**.
   - Cross-tabulation revealed a pronounced **bimodal occupancy distribution**, heavily polarized between large-scale city-wide convocations (Tier 4, 37.8%) and compact departmental workshops/trainings (Tier 1, 28.3%). This empirical distribution proved that while the 711 sqm hall must retain full-capacity plenary capabilities, forcing small 50–120 pax groups into an untreated, cavernous hall results in severe acoustic flutter echo and spatial disorientation—providing empirical validation for both high-density radial seating layouts for peak convocations and scale-tailored modular workshop pod configurations paired with comprehensive acoustic wall and ceiling treatments to preserve vocal clarity during compact assemblies without physical division.
3. **Anthropometric Dimensional Standardization & Client Visualization (Anchored in 20_RRL.md):**
   - Derived from Neufert (2012) and Panero & Zelnik (1979), standardized spatial envelopes were established: 900 mm plenary seat pitch with 305 mm clear passage; 740–760 mm classroom table heights with 450–500 mm chair pull-backs; 1800 mm diameter (Ø 1800 mm) round banquet tables with 1620–1800 mm two-way catering service corridors; and 1500–2400 mm exhibition circulation avenues.
   - Qualitative analysis of event planner interviews (Informants EP1 & EP5) confirmed that these pre-calculated dimensional templates serve a vital **pre-event planning and client visualization function**: enabling coordinators to visually demonstrate spatial limits to clients during initial consultations, setting realistic capacity boundaries (e.g., explaining why banquet dining is strictly capped at 250–300 pax to prevent gridlock), and accelerating client approvals. Concurrently, what physically slashes setup turnaround from 4–12 hours to under 90 minutes are the **future-proofing technical interventions** (overhead rigging, modular demountable staging, and mobile FF&E on transport dollies).
4. **Scenario-Based FGD Validation across the 12-Scenario Tri-Scale Architecture (Panel N = 8-10):**
   - An Evaluative Focus Group Discussion - featuring Local Event Planners (n = 3-4), Venue Operations Personnel (n = 1-2), Multi-Event Attendees (n = 3-4), and CACO Cultural Officers (n = 1-2) - rigorously tested competing layout options across all twelve tri-scale operational scenarios (4 Typologies x 3 Scales: Compact, Mid, Peak) using a structured 5-point rubric:
     * **Typology 1 Plenary Convocations (Scenarios 1A-1C):** Option B (Radial continental chevron seating with venue modular staging at 900 mm for 500p peak, scaled chevron for 250p mid, and mobile Basey acoustic partitioning at Grid Line D for 80-100p compact commencements) statistically outperformed Option A (**x̄ = 4.71 vs. 2.86**), providing guaranteed sightlines ($C \ge 60$ mm), 35% faster radial egress, 4 dedicated BP 344 bays, and acoustic intimacy ($RT_{60} \le 0.95$ s).
     * **Typology 2 Classroom Workshops (Scenarios 2A-2C):** Option B (Collaborative training pods on lockable casters with 1500 mm facilitator roaming lanes and overhead power drops for 180p full-hall, zoned pods for 100p mid, and compact innovation pods with active lobby pre-function for 50p compact) outperformed Option A (**x̄ = 4.62 vs. 3.17**), completely eliminating taped floor cords and reducing setup from 6 hours to $< 45$ minutes.
     * **Typology 3 Banquet Dining (Scenarios 3A-3C):** Option B (Staggered diamond clusters with 1800 mm clearances and overhead motorized rigging for 300p full gala, scaled diamond clusters for 200p mid, and concentrated central clusters around an open dance floor for 120p intimate nuptials) scored **x̄ = 4.62 vs. 3.06**, recovering 40 guest seats lost to ground truss towers and eliminating waiter collisions.
     * **Typology 4 Exhibition & Cultural Expos (Scenarios 4A-4C):** Option B (**Zero-Stage Flat-Floor Mode** reclaiming 52.5 sqm of continuous floor area for 22-26 booths in 4A, perimeter + dual-island hybrid with 300 mm demo dais in 4B, and localized perimeter cooking exhaust louvers with central radial judging in 4C) scored **x̄ = 4.52 vs. 3.17**, ensuring 100% unobstructed exit doors and intuitive looped crowd flow.
   - Across all twelve tri-scale operational scenarios, Option B achieved an overall composite mean of **x̄ = 4.62 (Highly Optimized / Superior)** versus Option A's **x̄ = 3.06 (Moderately Deficient)** ($p < 0.001$), empirically and statistically proving the operational superiority of the proposed evidence-based interior design interventions.

### 5.1.3. Future-Proofing & Samar-Calbayog Placemaking (SPOP Q3)
1. **Future-Proof Technical Infrastructure & Spatial Modularity (SPOP Q3.1):**
   - Quantitative evaluation by attendees (N = 100) strongly endorsed the proposed evidence-based interior design interventions:
      * **Spatial Modularity Index (Cluster C):** **x̄ = 4.47 (SD = 0.62, Strongly Agree)**, prioritizing ergonomic cushioned seating (C3: x̄ = 4.61) and venue-provided modular staging (C5: x̄ = 4.47), which replaces the inflexible legacy concrete dais with demountable risers supporting on-demand assembly and zero-stage flat-floor space recovery.
     * **Technical Integration Index (Cluster E):** **x̄ = 4.43 (SD = 0.66, Strongly Agree)**, emphasizing ceiling-routed utility cables to eliminate floor tripods (E3: x̄ = 4.52) and banquet conversational acoustic clarity (E5: x̄ = 4.49).
     * **Universal Inclusivity & Longevity Index (Cluster F):** **x̄ = 4.56 (SD = 0.58, Strongly Agree)**, anchored by **Civic Infrastructure Pride (F3: x̄ = 4.72, SD = 0.45)**—the highest rated item in the study—and dedicated BP 344 PWD accessibility (F1: x̄ = 4.51).
   - The proposed evidence-based interior design interventions synthesize these demands through three concrete technical interventions:
     * **Perimeter Dropped Ceiling Soffit at +4,685 mm AFFL (705 mm plenum drop):** Features an inward-facing 24V linear LED cove wash illuminating the suspended waterfall acoustic baffles.
     * **Concealed Dual-Service Pocket (150 x 180 mm):** Conceals two parallel tracks behind the soffit edge:
       - **Channel A (Curtain Track):** Houses **Full-Height Wand-Drawn Acoustic Velour Drapery** spanning from +4,685 mm to floor level, operated via a 2,700 mm rigid fiberglass/aluminum baton wand hidden behind lead pleats (grip at +1,650–1,750 mm AFFL), with bi-parting splits and magnetic wall holdbacks locking drapes to columns to preserve a full 2,000 mm clear exit portal (RA 9514).
       - **Channel B (Unistrut Rigging Rail):** Continuous gauge 12 Unistrut P1000 channel with sliding spring nuts and forged M10/M12 eyebolts, providing tool-free anchor points for ceiling fabric swagging and bunting, eliminating 100% of DIY wall drilling, nails, and tie-wire scars.
     * **Central Overhead Utility Grid & Dimmable Smart Lighting (Module 3):** Central motorized hoists and unistrut tracks at +5,390 mm ceiling level eliminate bulky ground truss towers (instantly reclaiming 40 guest seats), accompanied by 3-circuit dimmable LED tracks (DALI/DMX presets) and illuminated 90-minute battery-backed Fire Exit signage.
   - Overall community endorsement for the proposed interior design interventions (Clusters C, D, E, F) achieved a composite mean of **x̄ = 4.50 (SD = 0.62, Strongly Agree)**, dramatically contrasting with the existing baseline deficiency (x̄ = 2.44).
2. **Culturally Grounded Acoustic Placemaking (SPOP Q3.2):**
   - Overwhelming community consensus was recorded for Samar cultural heritage integration (**Cluster D: x̄ = 4.54, SD = 0.63, Strongly Agree**), validating the **Person-Process-Place Model of Place Attachment (Scannell & Gifford, 2010)**:
     * Regional cultural expressions elevating spatial memorability and civic pride (D5: x̄ = 4.62, SD = 0.52).
     * Stage perimeter walls specified with commercial **Wallcrown Atmosphere Digest Wallpaper collection (Code: UW1062)** in light beige, featuring vertical linear textures inspired by dried *tikog* leaves.
     * Interior column bases (+0.00 to +1,200 mm AFFL) clad in **white MCM (Modified Clay Material)** textured to resemble traditional *banig* weaving relief, paired with **Flexiflute Primed** fluted panels painted in oak wood tones on upper shafts (+1,200 to +4,685 mm AFFL).
     * Grid Line B partition walls (between Columns 1B–2B and 3B–4B) converted from exposed glass into a permanent solid acoustic separation wall, featuring custom staggered light beige fabric-wrapped acoustic panels (NRC ≥ 0.75) with integrated LED cove lighting (Main Hall face) and a custom **CNC wood-carved relief feature mural** celebrating the kinetic movement and rooster (*manok*) iconography of the Hadang Festival (Lobby concourse face).
     * Undulating curvilinear **Calbayog waterfall ceiling baffles** (D3: x̄ = 4.59, SD = 0.62, NRC ≥ 0.85) suspended with 24V LED cove illumination, complemented by neutral beige flame-retardant acoustic curtains (D4: x̄ = 4.32, SD = 0.73) and **A-xet acoustic LVT flooring in BIANCO SNOW TERRAZZO** to eliminate floor flutter reflections.

## 5.2. Conclusions

Based on the empirical findings, the study draws the following definitive conclusions:

1. **Epistemological & Methodological Conclusion:**
   - This study proves that a Bachelor of Science in Interior Design thesis achieves scientific rigor when framed as an **Applied Design Experimentation study** rather than a commercial, project-based commission. By employing **Actor-Network Theory (Latour, 2005)** and **Gibson’s (1979) Affordance Theory**, the research demonstrates that spatial bottlenecks are not human errors; they are socio-technical frictions imposed by unyielding non-human actants (hard tiles, heavy tables, absent storage, sliding doors). 
2. **Operational & Interior Architectural Conclusion:**
   - Grounded in **Affordance Theory (Gibson, 1979)** and statutory non-structural interior scope under RA 10350, the study concludes that aging municipal convention venues do not require structural demolition or civil engineering reconstruction to achieve high-performance MICE functionality. By preserving the existing architectural shell and re-engineering the internal spatial affordances through adaptable non-structural interior interventions—where standardized layout templates afford visual clarity and code-compliant capacity regulation during pre-event client planning, while future-proofing technical interventions (a venue-provided demountable modular staging system replacing the static concrete dais, mobile FF&E on transport dollies, and a perimeter dropped soffit at +4,685 mm with concealed wand-drawn acoustic drapery and continuous Unistrut swagging rails) re-engineer physical affordances—operational turnaround time can be drastically reduced from **4–8+ hours down to under 90 minutes** while eliminating DIY wall damage, life-safety exit obstruction, and cavernous floor space wastage.
3. **Socio-Cultural Placemaking Conclusion:**
   - Grounded in the **Theory of Place Attachment (Scannell & Gifford, 2010)**, the study concludes that cultural identity and corporate event versatility are not mutually exclusive. Contemporary architectural abstraction of regional crafts (Basey *banig*, dried *tikog* textures) and municipal traditions (Calbayog waterfalls, Hadang movements) into commercial, fire-rated acoustic finishes delivers dual performance: it provides technical sound absorption and camera-optimized neutrality while fostering civic pride and place identity for the citizens of Samar.

## 5.3. Recommendations

Based on the findings and conclusions, the study puts forward the following actionable recommendations:

### 5.3.1. Recommendations for the Local Government Unit (City Government of Calbayog)
1. **Executive & Legislative Adoption of the Modular Layout Manual:**
   - It is strongly recommended that the **Sangguniang Panlungsod of Calbayog City** enact a municipal resolution formally adopting the *Calbayog Cultural Convention Center Modular Interior Layout Manual* as the official operational standard for the General Services Office (GSO) and City Tourism Office.
2. **Transition to Standardized, Wheeled FF&E Procurement:**
   - The LGU should phase out heavy, non-stackable plywood banquet tables and mismatched monobloc chairs. For institutional assemblies and workshops, future capital procurement should specify commercial-grade, lightweight flip-top nesting seminar tables, folding round banquet tables, and standardized ergonomic stackable loose chairs equipped with dedicated wheeled transport dollies.
3. **Adoption of Standardized Dimensioned Layout Guides:**
   - The LGU and venue management should adopt laminated, dimensioned operational layout guides aligned with the venue's existing structural column grid (Grids A–G / 1–4). This provides custodial crews and event coordinators with clear, intuitive spatial benchmarks for rapid loose furniture placement without requiring permanent physical floor markings or manual tape measurements, preserving the pristine visual continuity of the terrazzo floor.
4. **Procurement of Venue-Provided Demountable Staging System:**
   - The LGU should procure a commercial modular staging platform system (standardized 1.22 x 2.44 m interlocking aluminum/plywood risers with telescoping legs for 300 mm, 600 mm, and 900 mm elevations, accompanied by demountable BP 344 accessible ramps, steps, and wheeled storage carts). Custodial crews should be trained to configure the stage on-demand for formal assemblies or completely strike it down in under 45 minutes for flat-floor trade exhibitions and large banquets.

---

### 5.3.2. Recommendations for the City Engineering Office (Architectural & Civil Handover)
1. **Exterior Door Hardware Recommissioning & Outward-Swinging Double-Door Retrofit (RA 9514 & BP 344):**
   - While exterior fenestration is beyond the non-structural interior scope of this thesis (governed by RA 10350), field audits revealed that the existing exterior sliding glass doors violate Section 10.2.5.3 of the Revised Fire Code of the Philippines (RA 9514), and nominal double doors are currently restricted to a single active leaf (1000 mm clear opening).
   - It is formally recommended that the **Calbayog City Engineering Office** recommission inactive door leaves to immediately restore 2000 mm clear passage, and execute a planned civil retrofit replacing exterior sliding assemblies with commercial panic-hardware double-leaf outward-swinging doors with magnetic hold-opens for event loading.
2. **Plenum Restoration, Roof Deck Sealing, & Rigging Suspension Load Certification:**
   - City Engineering should execute structural plasterboard patching and finish repainting on the damaged ceiling section in Zone A near Column 3B (closing the exposed sub-ceiling plenum gap), inspect the exterior roof deck above +5,390 mm AFFL for weather leaks, and certify the structural point-load capacity of primary steel roof truss chords (WLL ≥ 250 kg per bay span) to safely support the interior overhead utility grid and perimeter soffit hangers.
3. **Electrical Service Entrance & Dedicated Sub-Panel Infrastructure:**
   - City Engineering should upgrade the building's main electrical distribution panels to provide dedicated, isolated branch circuits for the interior 3-circuit dimmable smart lighting tracks, motorized hoists, and high-wattage production consoles, permanently resolving the circuit breaker overloads and blown fuses documented during live event observations.
4. **Column Grid Line 4 Loading Dock & Threshold Protection:**
   - Because perimeter doors along Column Grid Line 4 represent the primary loading and ingress axis for heavy flight cases, audio gear, and banquet furniture, City Engineering should install exterior heavy-duty step-free threshold ramps and stainless steel / high-impact molded rubber corner jamb guards on these loading portals to withstand continuous equipment cart and flight case impacts during load-in and load-out.

### 5.3.3. Recommendations for the City Arts and Culture Office (CACO)
1. **Curated Rotating Artisanal Exhibitions:**
   - CACO should utilize the proposed mobile acoustic exhibition partition modules (Typology 4) to host rotating quarterly showcases of living Samar artisans (e.g., live Basey *banig* mat weavers, local ceramicists, and Waray painters), establishing the convention center as an authentic cultural repository in line with its charter.
2. **Cultural Training and Convocations Integration:**
   - CACO should formalize regular bookings of the Classroom / Collaborative Pod (Typology 2) configurations for youth performing arts workshops, Waray literature seminars, and Hadang festival choreography training.

### 5.3.4. Recommendations for Future Interior Design Researchers
1. **Pre-Construction Simulation & Digital Acoustic Modeling:**
   - Future researchers investigating municipal assembly venues should utilize advanced computerized acoustic ray-tracing software (e.g., EASE, Odeon) to model spatial sound distribution under varying occupant densities.
2. **Longitudinal Post-Occupancy Evaluation (POE):**
   - Upon physical construction and fit-out of the proposed interior design interventions by the City Government of Calbayog, future scholars should conduct a formal on-site Post-Occupancy Evaluation (Preiser & Vischer, 2005) at 6-month, 12-month, and 24-month operational intervals to empirically measure long-term material wear, energy efficiency, and operational turnaround speed under live civic pressures.
