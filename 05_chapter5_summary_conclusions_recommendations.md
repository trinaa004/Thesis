# Chapter 5: Summary, Conclusions, and Recommendations

This chapter synthesizes the empirical findings, theoretical analyses, and design translations established throughout the study. It is organized into three major divisions:
* **Section 5.1: Summary of Findings** (Synthesizing the empirical results corresponding to SPOP Q1, Q2, and Q3)
* **Section 5.2: Conclusions** (Formulating theoretical, operational, ergonomic, and socio-cultural conclusions)

## 5.1. Summary of Findings

The primary objective of this study was to formulate a site-specific, research-based interior design framework and reconfigurable layout manual for the 840 sqm interior envelope (711 sqm Main Hall + 129 sqm Lobby) of the Calbayog Cultural Convention Center. Through an **Applied Design Research** methodology executed across a **Tri-Strand Convergent Mixed-Methods Design** (QUAN + QUAL + SPATIAL/ANT), the study yielded the following empirical findings:

### 5.1.1. Baseline Spatial, Ergonomic, and Environmental Deficits (SPOP Q1)
1. **Attendee Indoor Environmental Quality (IEQ) Dissatisfaction (SPOP Q1.1):**
   - General event attendees (N = 100) evaluated on a 5-point Likert scale reported severe environmental dissatisfaction across all measured parameters, yielding a composite baseline IEQ mean of **x̄ = 2.44 (SD = 0.89)**, interpreted as *Disagree / Deficient*.
   - **Thermal Comfort & Ventilation Deficit (B3):** Scored lowest among all survey parameters at **x̄ = 2.08 (SD = 0.93, Critical Deficit)**, caused by severe thermal stratification where split AC units drop cool air solely along perimeters, leaving stagnant warm plumes in central seating zones, exacerbated by the unconditioned 129 sqm Lobby buffer.
   - **Spatial Layout & Cramped Legroom (B5):** Scored **x̄ = 2.39 (SD = 0.75, Disagree / Deficient)**, reflecting severe legroom compression (< 750 mm pitch) from non-ergonomic plastic monobloc chairs.
   - **Stage & Screen Sightline Obstructions (B4):** Scored **x̄ = 2.45 (SD = 0.93, Disagree / Deficient)** due to flat-floor planar sightline blockages (C < 60 mm).
   - **Acoustic Speech Intelligibility Degradation (B1):** Scored **x̄ = 2.57 (SD = 0.93, Disagree / Deficient)**, corroborated by physical in-situ acoustic audits documenting an occupied reverberation time of **RT60 ≈ 1.85 to 2.20 seconds** (and 2.53 s unoccupied decay), far exceeding the international standard (0.80 to 1.10 s) due to 711 m² of reflective glazed ceramic tiles, uninsulated metal deck ceiling, and critically, the **unpadded painted gypsum board bulkheads** in the upper volume (spanning from the +3,640 mm curtain line on Grids 1 & 4, and above the 3,540 mm high glass walls on Grid B, up to the 5,390 mm ceiling) acting as hard specular reflectors that trap sound in the upper hall volume.
   - **Interior Lighting Quality & Glare (B2):** Scored **x̄ = 2.69 (SD = 0.91, Marginal / Deficient)** due to unshielded industrial fixtures, missing luminaires in Zone A and the Lobby, and static visual glare.

2. **Operational Setup Bottlenecks & Turnaround Delays (SPOP Q1.2):**
   - Survey evaluations from professional event planners and venue operators (n = 12) rated baseline operational friction high, reporting that routine room turnovers require **4 to 12+ hours of intense manual labor** (83.3%).
   - Spatial trace mapping and physical empty hall audits identified systemic physical bottlenecks: (a) absence of dedicated furniture storage, forcing custodial crews to stack loose chairs in lobby corridors; (b) nominal 2000 mm perimeter double doors functionally halved to **1000 mm usable clear width** because only one leaf opens; (c) loading congestion concentrated along **Column Grid Line 4**, where flight cases continuously bump and scar door jambs; (d) total lack of overhead rigging points, causing coordinators to drill makeshift wall holes for tie-wire and leaving an exposed sub-ceiling plenum in Zone A near **Column 3B**; (e) loose continuous draperies lacking tiebacks, obscuring emergency sliding doors and forcing attendees to blindly search for exits; and (f) complete absence of illuminated Fire Exit signage (RA 9514) and restroom wayfinding signs.
   - Archival audit of **129 verified event records (2014–2026)** confirmed severe **calendar compression**, documenting back-to-back changeovers with less than 16 to 24 hours between contrasting typologies during peak civic windows (e.g., October Charter Week).

### 5.1.2. Formulation & Comparative Validation of Modular Layout Typologies (SPOP Q2)
1. **Empirical Extraction of Four Typologies:**
   - Rather than assuming typologies a priori, classification of the 129 historical event records revealed four organic spatial patterns: **Plenary / Theatrical (48.8%)**, **Classroom / Training (25.6%)**, **Banquet / Social (14.0%)**, and **Exhibition / Cookfest (11.6%)**.
2. **Anthropometric Dimensional Standardization (Anchored in 20_RRL.md):**
   - Derived from Neufert (2012) and Panero & Zelnik (1979), standardized spatial envelopes were established: 900 mm plenary seat pitch with 305 mm clear passage; 740–760 mm classroom table heights with 450–500 mm chair pull-backs; 1800 mm diameter (Ø 1800 mm) round banquet tables with 1620–1800 mm two-way catering service corridors; and 1500–2400 mm exhibition circulation avenues.
3. **Scenario-Based FGD Validation (Panel N = 8–10):**
   - An Evaluative Focus Group Discussion—featuring Local Event Planners (n = 3–4), CACO Cultural Officers (n = 1–2), and Multi-Event Attendees (n = 3–4, each having attended ≥ 2 distinct events)—tested competing layout options using a 5-point rubric:
     * **Scenario 1 (Plenary):** Option B (Radial Chevron Angled Seating) statistically outperformed Option A (Linear Rows) (**x̄ = 4.71 vs. 3.02**), providing unobstructed sightlines (C ≥ 60 mm) and faster radial egress.
     * **Scenario 2 (Classroom):** Option B (Modular Workshop Pods) outperformed Option A (Linear Rows) (**x̄ = 4.63 vs. 3.23**), optimizing peer interaction and facilitator mobility.
     * **Scenario 3 (Banquet):** Option B (Staggered Clusters with 2000 mm Perimeter Buffer) scored **x̄ = 4.62 vs. 2.98**, eliminating catering cart collisions.
     * **Scenario 4 (Exhibition):** Option B (Central Island Modules) scored **x̄ = 4.52 vs. 2.99**, providing continuous looped crowd flow and overhead power.
     * **Scenario 5 (Subdivision):** Option B (Operable Acoustic Partition Wall, STC ≥ 45) scored **x̄ = 4.77 vs. 2.51**, validating the feasibility of simultaneous dual-event hosting.

### 5.1.3. Future-Proofing & Samar-Calbayog Placemaking (SPOP Q3)
1. **Future-Proof Technical Infrastructure & Spatial Modularity (SPOP Q3.1):**
   - Quantitative evaluation by attendees (N = 100) strongly endorsed the proposed non-structural technical infill framework:
     * **Spatial Modularity Index (Cluster C):** **x̄ = 4.47 (SD = 0.62, Strongly Agree)**, prioritizing ergonomic cushioned seating (C3: x̄ = 4.61) and modular staging (C5: x̄ = 4.47).
     * **Technical Integration Index (Cluster E):** **x̄ = 4.43 (SD = 0.66, Strongly Agree)**, emphasizing ceiling-routed utility cables to eliminate floor tripods (E3: x̄ = 4.52) and banquet conversational acoustic clarity (E5: x̄ = 4.49).
     * **Universal Inclusivity & Longevity Index (Cluster F):** **x̄ = 4.56 (SD = 0.58, Strongly Agree)**, anchored by **Civic Infrastructure Pride (F3: x̄ = 4.72, SD = 0.45)**—the highest rated item in the study—and dedicated BP 344 PWD accessibility (F1: x̄ = 4.51).
   - The proposed interior design framework synthesizes these demands through three concrete technical interventions:
     * **Perimeter Dropped Ceiling Soffit at +4,685 mm AFFL (705 mm plenum drop):** Features an inward-facing 24V linear LED cove wash illuminating the suspended waterfall acoustic baffles.
     * **Concealed Dual-Service Pocket (150 × 180 mm):** Conceals two parallel tracks behind the soffit edge:
       - **Channel A (Curtain Track):** Houses **Full-Height Wand-Drawn Acoustic Velour Drapery** spanning from +4,685 mm to floor level, operated via a 2,700 mm rigid fiberglass/aluminum baton wand hidden behind lead pleats (grip at +1,650–1,750 mm AFFL), with bi-parting splits and magnetic wall holdbacks locking drapes to columns to preserve a full 2,000 mm clear exit portal (RA 9514).
       - **Channel B (Unistrut Rigging Rail):** Continuous gauge 12 Unistrut P1000 channel with sliding spring nuts and forged M10/M12 eyebolts, providing tool-free anchor points for ceiling fabric swagging and bunting, eliminating 100% of DIY wall drilling, nails, and tie-wire scars.
     * **Central Overhead Utility Grid & Dimmable Smart Lighting (Module 3):** Central motorized hoists and unistrut tracks at +5,390 mm ceiling level eliminate bulky ground truss towers (instantly reclaiming 40 guest seats), accompanied by 3-circuit dimmable LED tracks (DALI/DMX presets) and illuminated 90-minute battery-backed Fire Exit signage.
   - Overall community endorsement for the proposed interior design framework (Clusters C, D, E, F) achieved a composite mean of **x̄ = 4.50 (SD = 0.62, Strongly Agree)**, dramatically contrasting with the existing baseline deficiency (x̄ = 2.44).
2. **Culturally Grounded Acoustic Placemaking (SPOP Q3.2):**
   - Overwhelming community consensus was recorded for Samar cultural heritage integration (**Cluster D: x̄ = 4.54, SD = 0.63, Strongly Agree**), validating the **Person-Process-Place Model of Place Attachment (Scannell & Gifford, 2010)**:
     * Authentic Samar Cultural Identity elevating memorability (D5: x̄ = 4.62, SD = 0.52).
     * Abstracted geometric **Basey *banig* weaving patterns** (*Samat* and *Lara* weaves) on CNC-routed acoustic wall panels (D1: x̄ = 4.61, SD = 0.60, NRC ≥ 0.75) installed across upper bulkheads and side walls.
     * Undulating curvilinear **Calbayog waterfall ceiling baffles** (D3: x̄ = 4.59, SD = 0.62, NRC ≥ 0.85) reducing room reverberation while honoring municipal geography.
     * Tactile natural **tikog grass textures** (D2: x̄ = 4.54, SD = 0.67) and Waray geometric acoustic drapery (D4: x̄ = 4.32, SD = 0.73).
     * Dynamic feature accents derived from the **Hadang / Sarakiki-Hadang Festival**, creating a dignified civic identity that eliminates the need for disposable vinyl tarpaulins.

## 5.2. Conclusions

Based on the empirical findings, the study draws the following definitive conclusions:

1. **Epistemological & Methodological Conclusion:**
   - This study proves that a Bachelor of Science in Interior Design thesis achieves scientific rigor when framed as an **Applied Design Experimentation study** rather than a commercial, project-based commission. By employing **Actor-Network Theory (Latour, 2005)** and **Gibson’s (1979) Affordance Theory**, the research demonstrates that spatial bottlenecks are not human errors; they are socio-technical frictions imposed by unyielding non-human actants (hard tiles, heavy tables, absent storage, sliding doors). 
2. **Operational & Interior Architectural Conclusion:**
   - Grounded in **Affordance Theory (Gibson, 1979)** and statutory non-structural interior scope under RA 10350, the study concludes that aging municipal convention venues do not require structural demolition or civil engineering reconstruction to achieve high-performance MICE functionality. By preserving the existing architectural shell and re-engineering the internal spatial affordances through an adaptable non-structural interior overlay—comprising standardized layout templates, mobile FF&E on transport dollies, a perimeter dropped soffit at +4,685 mm with concealed wand-drawn acoustic drapery and continuous Unistrut swagging rails, and floor alignment markers—operational turnaround time can be drastically reduced from **4–8+ hours down to under 90 minutes** while eliminating DIY wall damage and life-safety exit obstruction.
3. **Socio-Cultural Placemaking Conclusion:**
   - Grounded in the **Theory of Place Attachment (Scannell & Gifford, 2010)**, the study concludes that cultural identity and corporate event versatility are not mutually exclusive. Contemporary geometric abstraction of regional crafts (Basey *banig*) and municipal geography (Calbayog waterfalls) into functional acoustic surfaces delivers dual performance: it provides technical sound absorption while fostering civic pride and place identity for the citizens of Samar.

## 5.3. Recommendations

Based on the findings and conclusions, the study puts forward the following actionable recommendations:

### 5.3.1. Recommendations for the Local Government Unit (City Government of Calbayog)
1. **Executive & Legislative Adoption of the Modular Layout Manual:**
   - It is strongly recommended that the **Sangguniang Panlungsod of Calbayog City** enact a municipal resolution formally adopting the *Calbayog Cultural Convention Center Modular Interior Layout Manual* as the official operational standard for the General Services Office (GSO) and City Tourism Office.
2. **Transition to Standardized, Wheeled FF&E Procurement:**
   - The LGU should phase out heavy, non-stackable plywood banquet tables and mismatched monobloc chairs. Future capital procurement should specify commercial-grade, lightweight flip-top nesting seminar tables, folding round banquet tables, and modular ganging assembly chairs equipped with dedicated wheeled transport dollies.
3. **Implementation of Applied Floor Indicia:**
   - Custodial management should install flush, non-intrusive floor alignment markers (brass dots or contrasting resin joints) corresponding to the pre-calculated layout typologies, enabling utility crews to align seating rows accurately on the first pass without manual tape measurement.

---

### 5.3.2. Recommendations for the City Engineering Office (Architectural & Civil Handover)
1. **Exterior Door Hardware Recommissioning & Outward-Swinging Double-Door Retrofit (RA 9514 & BP 344):**
   - While exterior fenestration is beyond the non-structural interior scope of this thesis (governed by RA 10350), field audits revealed that the existing exterior sliding glass doors violate Section 10.2.5.3 of the Revised Fire Code of the Philippines (RA 9514), and nominal double doors are currently restricted to a single active leaf (1000 mm clear opening).
   - It is formally recommended that the **Calbayog City Engineering Office** recommission inactive door leaves to immediately restore 2000 mm clear passage, and execute a planned civil retrofit replacing exterior sliding assemblies with commercial panic-hardware double-leaf outward-swinging doors with magnetic hold-opens for event loading.
2. **Plenum Restoration, Roof Deck Sealing, & Rigging Suspension Load Certification:**
   - City Engineering should execute structural plasterboard patching and finish repainting on the damaged ceiling section in Zone A near Column 3B (closing the exposed sub-ceiling plenum gap), inspect the exterior roof deck above +5,390 mm AFFL for weather leaks, and certify the structural point-load capacity of primary steel roof truss chords (WLL ≥ 250 kg per bay span) to safely support the interior overhead utility grid and perimeter soffit hangers.
3. **Electrical Service Entrance & Dedicated Sub-Panel Infrastructure:**
   - City Engineering should upgrade the building's main electrical distribution panels to provide dedicated, isolated branch circuits for the interior 3-circuit dimmable smart lighting tracks, motorized hoists, and high-wattage production consoles, permanently resolving the circuit breaker overloads and blown fuses documented during live event observations.
4. **Column Grid Line 4 Loading Dock & Threshold Protection:**
   - Because perimeter doors along Column Grid Line 4 represent the primary loading and ingress axis for heavy flight cases, audio gear, and banquet furniture, City Engineering should install exterior heavy-duty step-free threshold ramps and stainless steel / high-impact molded rubber corner jamb guards on these loading portals to withstand continuous equipment cart and flight case impacts during load-in and load-out.

### 5.3.3. Recommendations for the City Arts and Culture Office (CACO)
1. **Curated Rotating Artisanal Exhibitions:**
   - CACO should utilize the proposed mobile acoustic exhibition partition modules (Typology 4) to host rotating quarterly showcases of living Samar artisans (e.g., live Basey *banig* mat weavers, local ceramicists, and Waray painters), establishing the convention center as an authentic cultural repository in line with its charter.
2. **Cultural Training and Convocations Integration:**
   - CACO should formalize regular bookings of the Classroom (Typology 2) and Dual-Breakout (Scenario 5) configurations for youth performing arts workshops, Waray literature seminars, and Hadang festival choreography training.

---

### 5.3.4. Recommendations for Future Interior Design Researchers
1. **Pre-Construction Simulation & Digital Acoustic Modeling:**
   - Future researchers investigating municipal assembly venues should utilize advanced computerized acoustic ray-tracing software (e.g., EASE, Odeon) to model spatial sound distribution under varying occupant densities.
2. **Longitudinal Post-Occupancy Evaluation (POE):**
   - Upon physical construction and fit-out of the proposed interior framework by the City Government of Calbayog, future scholars should conduct a formal on-site Post-Occupancy Evaluation (Preiser & Vischer, 2005) at 6-month, 12-month, and 24-month operational intervals to empirically measure long-term material wear, energy efficiency, and operational turnaround speed under live civic pressures.
