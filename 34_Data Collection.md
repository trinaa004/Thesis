# Data Collection

Data collection will be executed in a systematic, phased manner following institutional research protocols across seven procedural stages, with field data collection strands (Phases 3, 4, and 5) executed concurrently during the primary fieldwork period, followed by design synthesis and subsequent stakeholder validation:

### Phase 1: Pre-Data Gathering Coordinative Actions & Permissions
Prior to initiating field data collection, formal coordinative actions will be executed following clearance from the University of San Carlos Research Ethics Committee (USC REC):
* **Institutional Permissions & Site Access:** Official transmittal and permission letters will be submitted to the Calbayog City Convention Center management and local municipal authorities to secure administrative site authorization, access to venue event logs, and permission for on-site mapping of the 840 square meter interior envelope (711 sqm Main Hall and 129 sqm Lobby).
* **Expert Participant Outreach:** Formal invitation letters detailing the study's scope, voluntary nature, and time commitment will be transmitted directly to identified event planners, venue operators, and production managers for expert survey and interview recruitment.
* **General Attendee Recruitment:** To recruit general event attendees, call-for-participation posters will be displayed on venue bulletin boards during scheduled public events, supplemented by digital call-for-participation announcements posted on verified local community and civic social media pages.
* **Recruitment Materials & Language Inclusivity:** To prevent linguistic barriers from excluding local community members, grassroots event attendees, or facility end-users, all recruitment materials—including informational flyers, bulletin posters, and preliminary consent notices—will be made available in both English and Waray (the local vernacular of Calbayog City and Samar).
* **Ethical Safeguards & Data Privacy:** All recruitment materials, invitation letters, and transmittal documents will feature official university credentials, researcher contact information, and explicit notifications regarding voluntary participation, informed consent, and safeguards under Republic Act No. 10173 (Data Privacy Act of 2012).

### Phase 2: Pilot Testing & Instrument Reliability
Because the survey questionnaire is a researcher-developed instrument, a pilot test will be administered to 10 qualified experts for the Event Planner Instrument and 10 representative attendees for the Event Attendees Instrument (who will be excluded from the final study sample). This pre-test evaluates item readability, contextual clarity, structural flow, and internal consistency (Cronbach's alpha). Necessary adjustments to item phrasing and scale layout will be implemented based on pilot feedback prior to final survey deployment.

### Phase 3: Quantitative Survey Administration
The survey questionnaire will be administered through both printed self-administered copies and digital survey forms (e.g., Google Forms) to accommodate participants' schedules. Clear instructions will be provided, informed consent will be verified prior to response entry, and completed surveys will be securely collected and encoded.

### Phase 4: Qualitative Semi-Structured Interviews
Semi-structured interviews with key informants will be scheduled at mutually agreed times, either in person in private administrative spaces on-site or via secure digital teleconferencing platforms. Each session will last approximately 20 to 30 minutes. With explicit participant consent, interviews will be audio-recorded and subsequently transcribed verbatim for thematic analysis.

### Phase 5: On-Site Spatial and Dual-Modality Event Lifecycle Observations
Non-participatory field observations and spatial envelope mapping of the 840 square meter interior envelope (711 sqm Main Hall & 129 sqm Lobby) are executed across four structured observational streams:

1. **Non-Event Periods (Vacant Hall Audits):** Dedicated to architectural envelope verification across all four functional zones (documented in **CCCC plans and physical site context/01_existing_physical_site_context_and_spatial_audit.md** and **Existing Baseline Condition Observation_ Empty Hall.pdf**), specifically measuring column clearances, clear ceiling heights (5,390 mm plasterboard plane; 6.20 m truss chord), Grid B glass wall heights (3,540 mm between Columns 1B–2B and 3B–4B), perimeter fenestrations along Grids 1 & 4 (2,040 mm sliding doors topped by a 1,500 mm clerestory band with curtains hung at +3,640 mm AFFL), stage platform elevation (940 mm AFFL via 3 riser steps), nominal vs. usable door openings (2000 mm nominal reduced to 1000 mm usable), primary loading ingress axes (Column Grid Line 4 vs. Grid Line 1), door frame collision damage, ceiling structural condition (documenting damaged panels and exposed sub-ceiling plenum near Column 3B in Zone A), upper column wall puncture scars from improvised wire rigging, missing ceiling luminaires, and auditing life-safety exit signage (RA 9514) and restroom wayfinding.

2. **Primary Direct Live Event Lifecycle Shadowing (Direct Fieldwork, n = 5 contemporary events, October 2026):** Conducted during scheduled live events across contrasting typologies (*PAGPASAMWAK Legislative Caravan*, *Samar Investment Forum & Investor's Gala*, *TESDA Graduation Ceremony*, *Stakeholders' Night*, and *Wedding Banquet Reception*) to observe real-time operational dynamics across three distinct event phases:
   * **Ingress (Load-In & Setup Phase):** Tracking setup turnaround duration, outsourced FF&E delivery and unloading paths, ladder/scaffolding usage, floor cabling hazards, and spatial pinch points during hall preparation.
   * **In-Session (Active Event Execution Phase):** Observing attendee lobby registration flow, crowd movement along main and cross-aisles, waiter tray passage during banquets, visual sightlines to stage/screens, and perceived acoustic clarity and thermal comfort under live occupant loads.
   * **Egress (Teardown & Turnover Phase):** Documenting attendee exiting flow, furniture disassembly, chair stacking, loading door vehicle access, and post-event turnaround duration to return the hall to vacant turnover status. 

3. **Secondary Archival Photographic & Contextual Content Trace Observation (Archival Trace Audits across Representative Exemplar Events):**
   To evaluate the operational realities of historical and recent events that occurred when direct physical shadowing was impossible, a systematic physical trace and visual content audit is executed in accordance with environmental design research methodologies (Zeisel, 2006; Groat & Wang, 2013). The researcher audits high-resolution archival interior event photographs, official municipal press releases, Local Government Unit (LGU) Public Information Office (PIO) records, and verified media captions across representative exemplar events selected from the facility's 12-year longitudinal utilization ledger (N = 127 events, 2014–2026).
   
   Representative exemplar events analyzed include:
   * *Scenario 1 (Classroom / Training | Tier 1/2):* **DTI Samar Creative Entrepreneurship Training** (June 23, 2025; Event #3 in Category 4 / Ledger; 53 creative entrepreneurs + trainers).
   * *Scenario 2 (Plenary Convocations & Graduations | Tier 4):* **ECCD Centers Recognition Rites cum Moving-Up Ceremony** (April 22, 2025; Event #64 in Ledger; citywide child development graduates and families).
   * *Scenario 3 (Social Banquets & Galas | Tier 2/3):* **Knights of Columbus Council 3288 Honor Guard: Hermana Night 2026** (September 6, 2026; Event #118 in Ledger; peak capacity banquet dining with elevated ceremonial dais).
   * *Scenario 4 (Exhibition & Creative Expos | Tier 2/3):* **Annual Calbayog Tinapa 101 Culinary Cookfest** (October 10, 2016 / July 23, 2024 / October 19, 2026; Events #10, #49, #127 in Ledger; multi-station culinary showcase).

   For each exemplar event, the researcher conducts a structured observational evaluation across five spatial trace dimensions recorded in **Transcript/Event Observation/Archival_Photographic_Event_Observations.md**:
   * *a. Seating & Furniture Arrangement Traces:* Physical array patterns (rigid parallel linear rows vs. clusters), Monobloc chair grid spacing, chair push-back clearances, table-to-table pitch, and outer-edge viewing angles to the stage.
   * *b. Audio-Visual, Rigging & Utility Traces:* Placement of heavy aluminum ground truss crank-stands consuming floor seats, audio speakers mounted on loose tripods, and trailing power extension cords taped across aisles.
   * *c. Indoor Environmental Quality & Acoustic Traces:* Unpadded painted gypsum bulkheads and glazed ceramic floor tile surfaces generating specular acoustic reflections and reverberant flutter echo defects.
   * *d. Cultural & Placemaking Expression:* Presence of disposable, single-use printed plastic tarpaulins hung across stage backdrops versus authentic, permanent Samar-Calbayog cultural crafts (Basey *banig*, *tikog* textures).
   * *e. Circulation, Egress & Accessibility Traces:* Door clearances, queuing congestion at the non-air-conditioned 129 sqm Lobby threshold, blocked emergency fire exits, and the presence or complete absence of designated Batas Pambansa Blg. 344 (BP 344) wheelchair stalls.

4. **Physical Acoustic Sound Pressure Level & Reverberation Field Audit Protocol:**
   Physical decibel measurements are logged across the 8 spatial grid points using the Benetech GM1352 sound level meter paired with a stage-mounted JBL PartyBox portable loudspeaker under two operational conditions:
   * **Unoccupied / Vacant Hall Baseline:** Conducted across three distinct diurnal time windows—Morning (8:00 AM – 9:00 AM; capturing morning traffic noise penetration from Inay-la Street), Afternoon (1:00 PM – 2:00 PM; capturing peak thermal solar load and environmental community noise), and Evening (5:00 PM – 6:00 PM; post-traffic baseline). To adhere strictly to Calbayog City LGU administrative policies that prevent operating heavy mechanical HVAC units solely for student research observations, vacant hall readings are logged under the **Passive Architectural Envelope Baseline (HVAC Idle / Off)**. In the unoccupied hall, the stage-mounted JBL PartyBox projects calibrated broadband pink noise (85–90 dBA @ 1m reference) to execute the standardized **interrupted noise decay test (ISO 3382-2:2008 Section 5.2)** across 3 consecutive trials per node, measuring room reverberation decay duration back to the passive background noise floor.
   * **Occupied / Live Event Operations:** Conducted during scheduled events across contrasting typologies (Plenary Summit vs. Social Banquet) during active program sessions with operational event air conditioning and full occupant load. Continuous sound pressure levels (Leq dBA), minimums (Lmin), and maximums (Lmax) are sampled for 2 minutes per node to document operational speech clarity, crowd hum, and background noise under live event conditions.

### Phase 6: Post-Design Stakeholder Validation & Focus Group Discussion (FGD) Execution Protocol
Following the data convergence and interior design synthesis stage (where 2D CADD plans, 3D digital renderings, and material specifications are produced), a structured Evaluative Focus Group Discussion (FGD) was conducted to empirically validate the unbuilt design framework with key stakeholders (Tremblay, Hevner, & Berndt, 2010; Zeisel, 2006). The FGD was administered **synchronously online via Google Meet**, utilizing high-definition video conferencing, digital screen-sharing, and interactive digital evaluation rubrics to accommodate the demanding production schedules and geographical availability of commercial event coordinators, municipal facility administrators, and community stakeholders (Stewart & Shamdasani, 2014). 

To eliminate social desirability bias, prevent chaotic debate, and maintain strict operational rigor (Krueger & Casey, 2014), the 80-minute validation session executes the following six-step procedural protocol:
1. **Participant Outreach & Secondary Ethical Re-Consent:** Formal invitation briefs detailing the unbuilt framework are transmitted to the purposive nested panel (7 to 10, comprising 3–4 event planners, 1–2 venue operations personnel, 2–3 event attendees, and 1 cultural artisan/advocate). Signed secondary informed consent forms covering audio recording and rubric evaluation are secured prior to the session.
2. **Digital Presentation & Multi-Media Setup:** The session is hosted via Google Meet with secure meeting credentials. Each panelist is presented with high-definition digital screen-shares displaying synchronized, side-by-side dimensioned 2D CADD plans, 3D eye-level perspective renderings, reflected ceiling plans, and technical clearance summaries, alongside an individually transmitted digital Design Verification Rubric (accessible via Google Forms / interactive PDF).
3. **Standardized Objective Walkthrough (15–20 minutes):** The researcher presents the 4 modular layout typologies, which were empirically formulated and selected based on the most common event typologies documented from the facility's 12-year longitudinal utilization ledger of events held in the convention center (N = 127 verified events from 2014 to 2026, as presented in Chapter 4, Section 4.1.5, Table 4.2): (1) **Plenary / Theatrical Layout** (accounting for 49.6% of historical bookings, f = 63—the predominant civic assembly configuration for city-wide summits, general assemblies, and cultural convocations); (2) **Classroom / Training Layout** (accounting for 25.2% of historical bookings, f = 32—for institutional seminars, LGU workshops, and educational capacity building); (3) **Banquet / Social Layout** (accounting for 13.4% of historical bookings, f = 17—for formal civic galas, clan reunions, and private wedding banquets); and (4) **Exhibition / Trade / Interactive Layout** (accounting for 11.8% of historical bookings, f = 15—for job fairs, MSME trade expos, culinary cookfests, and medical missions), alongside the **Spatial Subdivision Scenario** (operable acoustic partition wall, STC ≥ 45, enabling dual concurrent events). The walkthrough details wheelchair clearances (BP 344), acoustic NRC specifications, and ceiling track rigging interfaces using a neutral, fact-based script. The researcher explicitly instructs participants that the session is an operational "stress-test" seeking to identify logistical bottlenecks, clearance pinch-points, and material flaws, rather than an aesthetic showcase seeking compliments.
4. **Independent Silent Rubric Scoring (8–10 minutes):** Before any verbal dialogue takes place, participants independently and silently complete the digital 5-point Likert Design Verification Rubric transmitted via Google Forms / interactive digital sheets (evaluating Modularity, Ergonomics, Acoustics, Culture, and Ceiling Grid). This Nominal Group Technique safeguard anchors individual judgment, preventing groupthink, social conformity, and dominant speaker influence in the virtual environment.
5. **Moderated Thematic Discussion (40–45 minutes):** Following Krueger & Casey's (2014) questioning route, the facilitator moderates structured verbal discussion over Google Meet across the core operational themes. To ensure equal voice in the teleconference environment and prevent senior participants from dominating, each discussion block begins with a 90-second round-robin speaking turn (with video enabled and unmuted microphones). The non-structural interior scope boundary is maintained throughout; comments drifting into civil engineering, roof structures, or municipal catering politics are immediately redirected.
6. **Member-Checking Synthesis & Transcription Archiving (10 minutes):** The facilitator orally synthesizes the primary critiques, clearance revisions, and material suggestions on the Google Meet call, allowing participants to confirm the accuracy of the documented feedback (Lincoln & Guba, 1985; Creswell & Creswell, 2018). Completed digital rubrics are electronically retrieved and logged, and the Google Meet cloud audio-video recording is securely archived and transcribed verbatim for thematic coding in accordance with RA 10173.

### Phase 7: Data Privacy, Security, Storage, & Disposal Protocols (RA 10173 Compliance)
To ensure full compliance with the Philippine Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173) and the University of San Carlos Research Ethics Committee (USC-REC) mandates, strict data protection, security, access control, and irreversible disposal protocols are enforced across all collected datasets:

1. **Processing & Usage Timeline:**
   Collected empirical data will be processed exclusively for the duration of the thesis study. All physical survey questionnaires, audio recordings, transcript files, and encoded dataset spreadsheets will be retained for exactly **one (1) year** following final undergraduate thesis defense and degree conferment, after which data disposal will be executed.

2. **Data Structure, Format, Pseudonymization, & Separation:**
   * **Quantitative Dataset Structure:** Survey response data from event planners and attendees will be encoded into Microsoft Excel (.xlsx) spreadsheets using alphanumeric participant codes (e.g., Respondent P-001 for planners, Respondent A-050 for attendees). No participant names, phone numbers, or personal contact details will be stored within response data matrices.
   * **Qualitative Dataset Structure:** Audio recordings and written transcript files will be saved as pseudonymized audio (.m4a/.mp3) and text (.docx/.pdf) files using key informant codes (e.g., Key Informant K-01).
   * **Encryption & Physical Separation:** Digital files and encoded spreadsheets will be encrypted using AES-256 bit encryption algorithms. Signed informed consent forms (ICFs) containing full names and physical signatures will be physically and digitally separated from response files, stored in isolated locked storage to prevent cross-identification. No sensitive personal data (e.g., health status, financial records, or biometrics) is collected.
   * **Time-Bound Anonymization:** The encrypted reference spreadsheet pairing participant real names with their corresponding alphanumeric codes, utilized strictly during data collection and transcription member-checking, will be stored in an isolated, password-protected directory separate from primary response matrices. Within exactly **thirty (30) calendar days** following the final undergraduate thesis defense and formal manuscript approval, this will be permanently destroyed.

3. **Hardware & Location Security (Exclusion of Unsecured Media):**
   * **Physical Hard Copy Storage:** All paper survey questionnaires, signed ICF sheets, and physical observation checklists will be stored in a dedicated locked storage cabinet located at the researcher's primary residence, accessible exclusively by the researcher.
   * **Hardware & Storage Security:** Easily lost or unencrypted portable storage media—such as unencrypted USB flash drives, memory sticks, external thumb drives, or unverified public cloud drives—are **strictly prohibited**. Digital data will be stored exclusively on a primary password-protected, encrypted local laptop hard drive, supplemented by automated encrypted backups on an institutional Google Drive cloud account protected by 2-Factor Authentication (2FA).

4. **Access Control & Data Transfer/Sharing Monitoring:**
   * **Access Rights:** Raw data files, audio recordings, and original survey entry sheets are accessible exclusively by the researcher. Thesis advisers and panel defense examiners will access only anonymized, aggregated statistical summaries and pseudonymized qualitative interview excerpts.
   * **Transfer & Sharing Monitoring:** Data sharing is restricted strictly to aggregated findings in the thesis manuscript and scholarly presentations. If qualitative transcripts are transmitted to key informants for member-checking (transcript verification), files will be sent directly via password-protected PDF attachments over secure email channels managed by the researcher.

5. **Treatment of Incidental Findings & Unforeseen Data:**
   * **General Unsolicited Disclosures:** Any unsolicited or unforeseen data disclosed during qualitative interviews or survey entries (e.g., personal administrative grievances, personnel disputes, or non-interior civil engineering concerns outside the 4-layer thesis scope) will be immediately redacted during transcription, excluded from data analysis, and permanently purged to prevent unintended privacy or confidentiality breaches.
   * **Operational Protocol for Critical Hazards & Public Safety Risks:** In the event that an incidental disclosure or field observation reveals an acute physical hazard—such as severe structural defects, blocked fire egress paths, or critical fire safety violations posing an immediate risk to public life and safety—the researcher will execute an immediate duty-of-care notification. Specifically, the objective physical hazard will be reported formally and anonymously in writing to Calbayog City Convention Center management and municipal facility safety officers without disclosing participant identities, compromising source confidentiality, or attributing statements to any individual. Because external civil structural defects and municipal code enforcement lie strictly outside the non-structural interior scope of this study, such incidental reports will not be incorporated into the research dataset or thesis analysis, thereby safeguarding participant privacy, public safety, and the defined thesis scope boundary.

6. **Irreversible Data Disposal & Destruction Protocols:**
   Data destruction is executed across two distinct, time-bound milestones:
   * **Milestone 1 (Within 30 Days Post-Defense — Linking Key Destruction):** The participant identities with alphanumeric codes will be permanently destroyed within thirty (30) calendar days after the final thesis defense. This immediately and irreversibly renders all remaining analytical spreadsheets, survey datasets, and qualitative transcripts permanently anonymized.
   * **Milestone 2 (Upon Expiration of the 1-Year Retention Window — Full Data Disposal):**
     * **Physical Records:** All paper survey questionnaires, signed consent forms, and physical field notes will be irreversibly destroyed via heavy-duty cross-cut document shredding.
     * **Digital Records:** All electronic spreadsheets, raw audio recordings, transcript files, and cloud backups will be permanently purged, rendering all electronic data permanently unrecoverable.
