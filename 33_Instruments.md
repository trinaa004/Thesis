# Research Instruments

To gather comprehensive diagnostic baseline data and validate the unbuilt design proposal, this study utilizes four complementary research instruments: a researcher-developed survey questionnaire (synthesized from benchmark literature), a semi-structured interview guide, an on-site spatial observation checklist, and a post-design stakeholder evaluation matrix and focus group discussion guide.

To ensure complete participant comprehension across diverse demographic backgrounds in Calbayog City and Samar, research instruments will feature contextualized phrasing in English, supported by bilingual (Waray/Filipino) translations and on-site assistance as needed.

Additionally, to safeguard venue personnel evaluating facility deficiencies, research instruments administered to staff include an explicit confidentiality clause:
> *"Your evaluations of venue deficiencies are strictly confidential, will never be shared with or accessed by supervisors, and will not affect your employment status or performance appraisals."*

## 1. Survey Questionnaire
The primary quantitative instrument will be a researcher-developed structured survey questionnaire. To establish content and construct validity, the survey indicators and facility evaluation dimensions were synthesized from established MICE venue selection and interior facility benchmark literature, specifically An, Kim, & Hur (2021) (Evaluating Convention Center Facility Attributes, Operational Flexibility, and Client Expectations) and Pembegül (2009) (Evaluating Meeting Facility Design Parameters, Seating Styles, and Physical Interior Adaptability).
The questionnaire will utilize a combination of 5-point Likert scales (ranging from 1 = Strongly Disagree / Very Dissatisfied to 5 = Strongly Agree / Very Satisfied), multiple-choice options, and structured open-ended prompts. The survey will be divided into six distinct sections directly mapped to the study's Statement of the Problem (SPOP Q1, Q1.1, Q1.2, Q2, Q3.1, Q3.2):
* Section A: Demographic & Event Profile: Respondent type (planner or attendee), event types organized/attended, and frequency of venue use.
* Section B: Baseline Indoor Environmental Quality (IEQ) & Spatial Operational Challenges (SPOP Q1, Q1.1, Q1.2): Evaluates existing interior envelope limitations, setup turnaround delays, circulation bottlenecks, acoustic echo/reverberation, ambient lighting clarity, and perceived thermal comfort.
* Section C: Flexible FF&E Layout Typologies & Turnaround Efficiency (SPOP Q2): Assesses preferences for reconfigurable seating modules, standardized furniture clusters, convertible staging, and layout configuration speed.
* Section D: Cultural and Natural Heritage Integration & Placemaking (SPOP Q3.2): Gathers feedback on incorporating regional cultural heritage (Samar Basey *banig* weaving patterns, *tikog* grass textures, Waray geometric motifs) and local natural heritage (Calbayog cascading waterfall imagery) into upgraded perimeter acoustic drapery and ceiling acoustic overlays.
* Section E: Overhead Ceiling Plane & Rigging Adaptability (SPOP Q3.1): Measures perceived importance of flexible ceiling track systems, motorized truss hoists, acoustic drapes, and adjustable ambient lighting.
* Section F: Programmatic Parameters & Future-Proof Specifications (SPOP Q2 & Q3.1): Evaluates expectations regarding modular layout typologies (Plenary, Classroom, Banquet, Exhibition), smart technology integration, universal accessibility (BP 344 / ADA), and sustainable acoustic material durability.

## 2. Semi-Structured Interviews
A qualitative semi-structured interview guide will be developed to explore nuanced insights from key event planners and venue operators. The open-ended questions will focus on specific operational workflows, physical challenges encountered during event setup and teardown, desired acoustic and lighting controls, and cultural and natural heritage expression in public venues.

## 3. On-Site Spatial and Event Lifecycle Observation Protocol
The researcher will conduct direct, non-participatory spatial observations within the 840 square meter interior envelope (711 sqm Main Hall and 129 sqm Foyer). This instrument incorporates a two part protocol:

### Part A: Static Architectural Envelope & Physical Facility Checklist
Conducted during non-event and vacant hall periods to record baseline physical dimensions and structural clearances:
1. Physical envelope dimensions, ceiling heights, column grid spacing, and door clearances.
2. Existing FF&E inventory, loading entrance door clear widths, and furniture staging/distribution paths.
3. Overhead ceiling plane finishes, utility infrastructure, and rigging point availability.
4. Physical condition, material wear, acoustic drapery condition, and presence or absence of localized cultural and natural heritage identity elements.

### Part B: Multi-Phase Event Lifecycle Observation Protocol (Ingress, Live Operations, & Egress)
Conducted during scheduled live events held at the convention center across contrasting event typologies (such as plenary conferences, social banquets, and commercial exhibitions) to document real-time operational dynamics across three distinct phases:
1. **Ingress (Load-In & Setup Phase):** Unloading logistics at the loading entrance, outsourced FF&E hauling paths, ladder/scaffolding usage, floor cable management, setup duration/turnaround time, and spatial pinch points during hall preparation.
2. **In-Session (Active Live Event Phase):** Foyer registration flow, attendee circulation patterns, main aisle vs. service lane congestion (waiter paths and server mobility), stage/screen sightline obstructions, live speech intelligibility and room echo, and thermal/ventilation performance under full occupant loads.
3. **Egress (Teardown & Load-Out Phase):** Attendee exit flow and egress dispersal, furniture dismantling, stacking, and removal procedures, loading door vehicle access congestion, and post-event turnover speed.

## 4. Post-Design Stakeholder Evaluation Matrix & Focus Group Discussion (FGD) Guide
To validate the synthesized interior design framework, 2D CADD layout typologies, and 3D visual models following Phase 2, a dual-component validation instrument is deployed during the post-design stakeholder review session (Tremblay, Hevner, & Berndt, 2010; Zeisel, 2006; Sanoff, 2000):

### Part A: Structured Design Verification Rubric (Quantitative Individual Scoring)
A standardized 5-point Likert rubric (1 = Strongly Disagree / Highly Unfeasible to 5 = Strongly Agree / Highly Feasible) administered to each participant for independent, silent completion prior to group deliberation. The rubric evaluates five core dimensions:
1. **Spatial Modularity & Turnaround Viability:** Operational feasibility of the four typologies (Plenary, Banquet, Classroom, Exhibition), FF&E mobility, and storage transfer efficiency.
2. **Anthropometric Clearances & Accessibility:** Adequacy of 1200 mm main aisles, 1620–1800 mm wait-staff service corridors, wheelchair turning bays (BP 344), and foyer crowd dispersals.
3. **Acoustic Performance & Durability:** Perimeter acoustic panel coverage, waterfall ceiling baffle configuration, and maintenance durability of *tikog* grass composite materials.
4. **Cultural & Natural Heritage Resonance & Place Attachment:** Authenticity, civic dignity, and respectful abstraction of Basey *banig* weaves (*Lara*) and Calbayog waterfall fluid mechanics (Scannell & Gifford, 2010).
5. **Overhead Rigging & Utility Adaptability:** Functional effectiveness of ceiling-mounted tracks in eliminating floor cabling and streamlining lighting/audio changeovers.

### Part B: Semi-Structured FGD Discussion Guide (Qualitative Questioning Route)
Anchored on Krueger & Casey’s (2014) questioning route architecture, the discussion guide navigates through four structured stages:
1. **Introductory/Orientation Questions:** Establishing first impressions of the synthesized 2D drawings and 3D perspective renderings compared to the venue's baseline condition.
2. **Transition Questions:** Examining setup labor, crew feasibility, and furniture handling across the four layout typologies.
3. **Core Stress-Testing Questions:** Specifically probing for operational friction, clearance pinch points during 500-occupant peak loads, material maintenance vulnerabilities, cultural and natural heritage representation fidelity, and overhead track practicality.
4. **Closing & Member-Checking Questions:** Eliciting prioritized design refinements and synthesizing group consensus to verify accuracy before finalizing the framework.
