Research Instruments
To gather comprehensive data for this study, data collection will utilize three complementary research instruments: a researcher-developed survey questionnaire (synthesized from benchmark literature), a semi-structured interview guide, and an on-site spatial observation checklist. 

To ensure complete participant comprehension across diverse demographic backgrounds in Calbayog City and Samar, research instruments will feature contextualized phrasing in English, supported by bilingual (Waray/Filipino) translations and on-site assistance as needed.

Survey Questionnaire
The primary quantitative instrument will be a researcher-developed structured survey questionnaire. To establish content and construct validity, the survey indicators and facility evaluation dimensions were synthesized from established MICE venue selection and interior facility benchmark literature, specifically An, Kim, & Hur (2021) (Evaluating Convention Center Facility Attributes, Operational Flexibility, and Client Expectations) and Pembegül (2009) (Evaluating Meeting Facility Design Parameters, Seating Styles, and Physical Interior Adaptability).
The questionnaire will utilize a combination of 5-point Likert scales (ranging from 1 = Strongly Disagree / Very Dissatisfied to 5 = Strongly Agree / Very Satisfied), multiple-choice options, and structured open-ended prompts. The survey will be divided into six distinct sections directly mapped to the study's Statement of the Problem (SPOP Q1–Q6):
Section A: Demographic & Event Profile: Respondent type (planner or attendee), event types organized/attended, and frequency of venue use.
Section B: Existing Spatial & Operational Challenges (SPOP Q1): Evaluates current interior envelope limitations, setup turnaround delays, circulation bottlenecks, acoustic issues, and visual clarity.
Section C: Flexible FF&E Layout Typologies & Turnaround Efficiency (SPOP Q2): Assesses preferences for reconfigurable seating modules, standardized furniture clusters, convertible staging, and layout configuration speed.
Section D: Localized Samar Cultural Integration (SPOP Q3): Gathers feedback on incorporating Samar Basey banig weaving patterns, tikog grass textures, Waray geometric motifs, and Calbayog waterfall imagery into upgraded perimeter acoustic drapery and ceiling acoustic overlays.
Section E: Overhead Ceiling Plane & Rigging Adaptability (SPOP Q4): Measures perceived importance of flexible ceiling track systems, motorized truss hoists, acoustic drapes, and adjustable ambient lighting.
Section F: Programmatic Parameters & Future-Proof Specifications (SPOP Q5 & Q6): Evaluates expectations regarding modular layout typologies (Plenary, Classroom, Banquet, Exhibition), smart technology integration, universal accessibility (BP 344 / ADA), and sustainable acoustic material durability.


Semi-Structured Interviews
A qualitative semi-structured interview guide will be developed to explore nuanced insights from key event planners and venue operators. The open-ended questions will focus on specific operational workflows, physical challenges encountered during event setup and teardown, desired acoustic and lighting controls, and cultural expression in public venues.
On-Site Observation
The researcher will conduct direct, non-participatory spatial observations within the 840 square meter Main Hall. An observation checklist will be used to record:
1. Physical envelope dimensions, column grid spacing, and door clearances.
2. FF&E inventory, storage room accessibility, and setup/teardown movement paths.
3. Pedestrian circulation flow, aisle widths, and back-of-house service lanes.
4. Overhead ceiling plane finishes, utility infrastructure, and rigging point availability.
5. Acoustic and visual environmental quality (perceived speech intelligibility, surface reverberation tendencies, lighting distribution, and sightline clarity).
6. Presence or absence of localized cultural identity elements and surface material condition.
