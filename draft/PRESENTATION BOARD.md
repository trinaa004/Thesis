# Calbayog Cultural Convention Center: Presentation Board

## 1. Background

**The Calbayog Cultural Convention Center is the city's primary air-conditioned civic hall, hosting the majority of municipal assemblies, institutional trainings, and private banquets.** However, its 840-square-meter interior remains aesthetically generic and functionally rigid, requiring laborious 4- to 12-hour turnarounds between contrasting setups.

While metropolitan literature prioritizes massive multi-hall complexes, a critical research gap exists in adapting mid-scale provincial civic venues that must achieve rapid multi-event modularity and localized cultural placemaking within a single shared envelope.

### 2.2 Numbered Spatial Marker Callouts (Direct Presentation Board Cards)

#### [1] Front Entrance Double-Door Throttle (Grid A / Main Entry Portal)
* **Audited Architectural Baseline:** The building's main public entrance along Grid Line A features a nominal 2,000 mm frameless glass double-leaf portal. On-site field audits confirm that **only one door leaf can be unlocked and opened**, restricting the active pedestrian entrance to a narrow **1,000 mm clear opening**. During major events, this remaining portal is further constricted by registration reception tables and vendor display booths placed immediately adjacent to the threshold.
* **Operational Friction & Bottleneck:** Throttle halves code-mandated entry/egress flow, producing acute pedestrian queuing and vestibule gridlock during peak opening surges (up to 500 pax). In an emergency evacuation, this 1,000 mm opening creates a hazardous crush point, violating **RA 9514 (Fire Code of the Philippines)** and **BP 344 (Accessibility Law)** standards for public assembly concourses.
* **Empirical Triangulation:**
  * *Site Audit File:* §2 (Zone 1: Usable Door Egress Bottleneck);
  * *Protocol B Live Shadowing:* *Samar Investment Gala* & *Pagpasamwak* (Event #121, Event #123 — registration tables barricading active leaves);
  * *Interview EP1:* *"Wide man an ira door, but... sakto la hiya talaga sa door san usa nga tawo... tanan nga gamit didi naagi sa atubangan."*

#### [2] Ingress Delivery Collision Axis / Absence of BOH Loading Dock (Grids A to B)
* **Audited Architectural Baseline:** The facility features **no dedicated service loading dock, freight bay, or rear loading ramp** at the stage/backstage perimeter (Grids E–G). 
* **Operational Friction & Bottleneck:** All outsourced event supplies—including heavy iron truss boxes, commercial flight cases, line-array audio cabinets, mobile bars, and catering thermoboxes—are forced to unload at the main vehicular curb and enter through the primary 129 m² guest lobby. This creates severe operational conflict and material damage: heavy wheeled road cases collide with arriving attendees, scar lobby door frames, and tear surface floor treatments. Furthermore, when simultaneous catering setup occurs alongside technical audio rigging, the lobby concourse suffers total circulation gridlock.
* **Empirical Triangulation:**
  * *Site Audit File:* §2 (Zone 1 & Zone 2 loading paths);
  * *Interview EP3 (Audio-Visual Engineer):* *"Waray ka staging area. Pag-unload san mga flight cases, speaker boxes, trusses tikang ha truck, didto dayon naipit ha lobby. Tapos kun durungan pa an catering... magkaka-bunggo na kami."*
  * *Interview EP1:* *"Waray backstage nga dako nga door, so tanan nga gamit didi naagi sa atubangan... In hotels, they don't allow suppliers to use the main door kay ada man gud an mga guest."*

#### [3] Restroom Circulation Detour & Exterior Perimeter Spillover (Grids E to G)
* **Audited Architectural Baseline:** Public male and female restrooms are physically isolated in the rear Back-of-House service block (Zone 4, Grids F–G). The only interior path is an unmarked, severely constricted **955 mm backstage corridor** flanking the stage; **zero directional wayfinding signage** exists in the facility.
* **Operational Friction & Bottleneck:** Attendees seeking restrooms must either walk the entire 32.5-meter length of the hall directly across the front of the active stage dais (causing acute visual and acoustic distractions during formal ceremonies and speeches), or facility staff instruct guests to exit the building through perimeter sliding doors to access exterior toilet portals. This forces attendees to walk along unlit exterior service paths and vehicular driveways, exposing them to tropical rain, extreme humidity, and mud.
* **Empirical Triangulation:**
  * *Site Audit File:* §2 (Zone 4: Restroom Access & Circulation Deficits);
  * *Interview EP1:* *"Kinahanglan mo pa mag-obstacle para la maka-CR, kinahanglan mo pa gumawas. May stage, tas sa luyo may CR. Since an door sa may gilid san stage diri open... maggawas sira sa main door tikadto sa luyo... Waray signage nakabutang na 'Didto an CR.'"*
  * *Protocol B Live Shadowing:* Observed during *PAGPASAMWAK* and *Hermana Night* (attendees wandering backstage or searching outside).

#### [4] Ground Truss Outrigger Footprint Penalty & Sightline Blinding (Grids B to E / Bay 2–3)
* **Audited Architectural Baseline:** The +5.39 m high gypsum ceiling plane completely lacks integrated rigging tracks, unistrut channels, or motorized hoists. To hang stage chandeliers, decorative ceiling swags, or directional moving-head lights, styling crews must erect ground-supported aluminum box trusses with sprawling diagonal outrigger crank bases ("tiil").
* **Operational Friction & Bottleneck:** Ground truss outriggers create a severe **"Truss Footprint Penalty."** Each crank lifter base claims the spatial clearance envelope of an entire 10-seater round banquet table (3,200–3,500 mm operational diameter). Deploying four lifters **permanently eliminates 4 tables (30 to 40 guest seats)** from the hall's floor capacity. Additionally, the vertical vertical uprights create severe visual blockages, cutting off direct line-of-sight to the stage for attendees seated in rear bays (Bays B–C).
* **Empirical Triangulation:**
  * *Chapter 4 Table 4.4:* Item B5 scored **x̄ = 4.42 (Rank 1 Peak Friction)** among venue operators;
  * *Interview EP5 (Event Planner & Stylist):* *"One lifter takes up a space of a 10-seater table... We're going to use four lifters... At least we have saved at least 30 to 40 pax [if lifters are eliminated]... Sumasakop 'yung space 'yung paa ng lifter na 'yan is malapad... Kasi 300 guests tapos may trusses is medyo masikip na."*
  * *Protocol B Live Shadowing:* Observed during *Wedding Event #127* and *Samar Investment Forum* (crank stands obstructing rear sightlines).

#### [5] Surface Taped AV Feeder Cables & Tripping Hazards (Grids D4–E4 to Stage)
* **Audited Architectural Baseline:** The facility's primary technical soundboard and lighting console are stationed along the south wall between Columns D4 and E4. The floor is flat 600 × 600 mm polished porcelain tile with **zero recessed floor trench raceways, flush pop-up boxes, or perimeter cable troughs**.
* **Operational Friction & Bottleneck:** AV technicians and live media crews must manually route heavy main power feeder lines, 32-channel audio snakes, and HDMI/DMX cables across high-traffic floor walkways. Technicians resort to taping cables down with commercial duct tape; however, foot friction repeatedly pulls tape loose, creating hazardous tripping obstacles for guests, waiters, and elderly attendees under dimmed event lighting. Furthermore, loose cables are vulnerable to foot snagging, risking abrupt power disconnections and blown LED panels.
* **Empirical Triangulation:**
  * *Site Audit File:* §2 (Zone 2: AV, Power, & Temporary Rigging Layout);
  * *Interview EP3:* *"An mga kable liwat namon, ginhihimo namon an amon pinaka-makakaya nga diri gud magbalabag ha salog... Pero delikado makasingkod an mga tawo."*
  * *Interview EP5:* *"Mayda kami instance na one of the panel na pumutok an fuse because of that... affected an amon LED wall... that's a red flag for our event."*
  * *Chapter 4 Table 4.3 & §4.2.2:* 33.3% technical operator respondent representation confirming floor cabling hazards.

#### [6] Blocked Perimeter Fire Exits & Blind Drapery Gaps (Column Grids 1 & 4)
* **Audited Architectural Baseline:** The north and south walls (Grids 1 and 4) contain sliding glass perimeter doors (2,000 × 2,040 mm). In the unrenovated baseline, these doors are covered by thick, crude beige curtains hung at +3,640 mm AFFL suspended by temporary tie wires.
* **Operational Friction & Bottleneck:** The existing perimeter drapes **completely lack curtain tiebacks or wand battens**. The continuous fabric masks the location of sliding doors, forcing escaping occupants during egress to blindly rummage through fabric folds to locate door handles. Furthermore, field audits revealed that event coordinators routinely push commercial exhibition tables, registration desks, and buffet warmers directly against these sliding glass portals, physically blocking emergency exit routes. Compounding this danger, the hall contains **zero illuminated Fire Exit signage** above these doors, violating **RA 9514 Section 10.2.5.2**.
* **Empirical Triangulation:**
  * *Site Audit File:* §2 (Zone 2: Acoustic Behavior & Draping Practices / Life-Safety Deficits);
  * *Protocol B Live Shadowing (Samar Investment Gala):* Registration table and "Palawan Gold" trade booth placed directly in front of perimeter exit door;
  * *Protocol B Live Shadowing (Wedding Event #127):* Curtains lacked tiebacks; guests and crew forced to manually knot drapes or blindly search for fabric splits;
  * *Interview EP1:* *"May mga door man sa gilid, upat iton... pero diri la hiya super nao-open gud... waray signage nakabutang."*

### 2.4 Presentation Board Layout & Drafting Specifications

When drafting this diagram for your physical presentation board (AutoCAD, Revit, SketchUp, Photoshop, or InDesign):

1. **Plan Drawing Scale & Line Hierarchy:**
   * **Base Floor Plan:** Scale **1:150** (recommended for standard 20×30 in. or 24×36 in. architectural boards) or **1:200** (for compact multi-drawing composite boards).
   * **Architectural Linework:** Muted grey/monochrome base walls (0.35 mm dark grey for concrete walls, 0.15 mm light grey for interior columns, grid lines, and floor tiles) so that the red circulation graphics stand out with immediate clarity.
2. **Circulation Vector Graphics:**
   * **Primary Ingress Arrows (Solid Red ➜):** Use bold CAD polylines with `HEX #D32F2F` (Crimson Red) with a width of 1.5–2.0 mm on paper. Taper arrowheads cleanly at doorways.
   * **Service/Delivery Ingress Arrows (Dashed Red ⇢):** Use dashed polylines (`HEX #C62828`) with an explicit delivery label pointing from vehicular drop-off through the lobby doors.
   * **Detour Egress Arrows (Dotted Red ⤑):** Use dotted polylines (`HEX #E53935`) tracing through the narrow backstage corridor and curving outward to the exterior building envelope.
3. **Numbered Marker Bubbles:**
   * **Bubble Geometry:** 6.0 mm diameter circular solid red fill (`HEX #B71C1C`) with white bold centered numerals (`1`, `2`, `3`, `4`, `5`, `6`).
   * **Leader Lines:** 0.25 mm solid red leader lines angled at 45° terminating at the exact problem coordinate on the floor plan.
4. **Callout Card Typography Hierarchy:**
   * **Callout Header:** 9–10 pt Bold Sans-Serif (e.g., *Montserrat Bold* / *Helvetica Bold* / *Arial Bold*), colored Dark Red (`#B71C1C`).
   * **Diagnostic Description:** 7.5–8.0 pt Regular Sans-Serif (e.g., *Montserrat Regular* / *Inter*), leading at 10–11 pt.
