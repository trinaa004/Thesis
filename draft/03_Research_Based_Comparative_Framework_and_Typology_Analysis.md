## 1. Executive Summary & Epistemological Pivot: Miss Kirah's Mandate

### 1.1. The Critical Divide: Project-Based vs. Research-Based Thesis
In interior design academic scholarship (particularly within Philippine BS Interior Design degree programs governed by the CHED and PRC Board of Interior Design), there is a decisive structural and epistemological distinction between a **Project-Based Thesis** and a **Research-Based / Applied Experimentation Thesis**:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                         PROJECT-BASED vs. RESEARCH-BASED MATRIX                        │
├──────────────────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│      PROJECT-BASED THESIS (Vocational)   │ RESEARCH-BASED / APPLIED EXPERIMENTATION    │
├──────────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ • Proposes ONE static interior design    │ • Examines MULTIPLE conditions, typologies, │
│   scheme for a single client/space.      │   or operational modes comparatively.       │
│ • Specifies finishes, materials, and 3D  │ • Evaluates significant design elements     │
│   renders without testing alternatives.  │   (furniture, layout, lighting, acoustics)  │
│ • Lacks comparative experimental logic.  │   and their measurable effects on users.    │
│ • "Client commission" approach.          │ • Uses theoretical frameworks (e.g., ANT,   │
│ • Risk: Panel views it as superficial    │   Open Building) to analyze spatial network │
│   styling without scientific rigor.      │ • Proposes and compares tailored design     │
│                                          │   interventions across typologies.          │
└──────────────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Core Strategic Realization:**  
> If this study simply proposes *one* single aesthetic redesign of the 840 sqm hall with nice renders and finishes, it is legally and academically classified as **Project-Based**.  
> 
> To maintain its standing as a **Research-Based / Applied Experimentation Thesis**, the study must adopt a **comparative, multi-typological, and theory-grounded structure**—evaluating the significant design elements across distinct operational modes, analyzing their friction through an established behavioral lens, and developing comparative spatial interventions.

## 3. Methodological Translation: Solving the "One Space" Problem

The immediate structural challenge: **Karsten visited multiple physical buildings (different studios), whereas the Calbayog Cultural Convention Center is ONE physical architectural shell (840 sqm).**

How does a single civic building satisfy the requirement for a comparative, multi-case research study?

### 3.1. The "Chameleon Space" Concept: Intra-Venue Comparative Typology Analysis
A civic convention center is fundamentally a **chameleon interior envelope**. It does not exist as a single, static space. Over a 12-month calendar, that single 840 sqm shell physically transforms into **multiple completely distinct spatial and social realities**:

```
                                [ CALBAYOG CULTURAL CONVENTION CENTER ]
                                       (840 sqm Architectural Shell)
                                                    │
         ┌──────────────────────────┬───────────────┴───────────────┬──────────────────────────┐
         ▼                          ▼                               ▼                          ▼
 [ TYPOLOGY A: PLENARY ]    [ TYPOLOGY B: CLASSROOM ]       [ TYPOLOGY C: BANQUET ]    [ TYPOLOGY D: EXHIBITION ]
 • High-Density Assembly    • Educational / Training        • Social Dining / Galas    • Trade Fairs / Cookfests
 • Forward row seating      • Linear tables with chairs     • 10-pax round tables      • Display booths / stations
 • 350–500 occupants        • 100–200 participants          • 200–350 guests           • Heavy pedestrian loops
 • Stage-dominant sightline • Writing / laptop surfaces     • Wait-staff service lanes • Power/water drop needs
 • Vocal clarity demand     • Facilitator roaming aisles    • Ambient dining mood      • Grease / smoke venting
```

**Therefore, each operational event typology is your "Case Study."**

Instead of comparing Studio A vs. Studio B vs. Studio C (different rooms), you are comparing:
* **Event Typology 1 (Plenary Setup)** vs.
* **Event Typology 2 (Classroom Setup)** vs.
* **Event Typology 3 (Banquet Setup)** vs.
* **Event Typology 4 (Exhibition Setup)**.

## 5. Actor-Network Theory (ANT) in the Convention Center Context

Applying **Actor-Network Theory (ANT)** (Latour, 1996, 2005) provides the theoretical engine for your spatial analysis, directly mirroring Karsten’s theoretical framework:

### 5.1. Actor vs. Actant Mapping Matrix

| Category | Entities Identified in Calbayog Convention Center Fieldwork | Role & Operational Agency in the Space |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Human Actors** | • Event Organizers & Coordinators<br>• Facility Custodial & Utility Crew<br>• Event Attendees / Delegations / Citizens<br>• Keynote Speakers / Performers / Trainers<br>• Catering Staff & Food Service Waiters<br>• Audio-Visual & Sound Technicians<br>• PWD, Elderly, & Vulnerable Occupants | Attempt to execute programs, listen to presentations, dine, facilitate workshops, move carts, and navigate the space safely. |
| **Non-Human Actants (Physical FF&E & Finishes)** | • Plastic Monobloc Stacking Chairs<br>• Heavy Plywood Folding Banquet Tables<br>• Loose Electrical Extension Cords & Gaffer Tape<br>• Hard Ceramic Floor Tiles ($600 \times 600\text{ mm}$)<br>• Perimeter Glass Fenestration & Curtain Walls<br>• Exposed Structural Steel Trusses (6.20 m clear height)<br>• Heavy Sliding Glass Lobby Doors<br>• Disposable Tarpaulins & Staples/Adhesives<br>• Mobile PA Loudspeakers & Tripod Mic Stands | Act upon human actors by imposing physical friction: causing tripping hazards, creating acoustic echoes, blocking wheelchair corridors, causing physical strain during 4-hour setups, and requiring temporary wall stapling. |

### 5.2. Network Friction Points (The Problem Statement Grounded in ANT)
1. **The Translation Failure:** When an event planner envisions an elegant banquet (Human Actor), the non-human actants (heavy non-wheeled tables, narrow doors, lack of on-site storage) resist, forcing the custodial crew (Human Actors) into 6 hours of manual trial-and-error labor.
2. **The Acoustic Discordance:** When a speaker delivers a keynote (Human Actor), the sound waves collide with non-human actants (untreated ceramic tiles, parallel glass walls), returning as high reverberation flutter echo ($RT_{60} > 1.8\text{ s}$) that causes ear fatigue in the audience (Human Actors).
3. **The Circulation Breakdown:** When 300 attendees enter the 129 sqm Lobby (Human Actors), non-human actants (stacked plastic chairs dumped in corners, narrow registration desks, sliding doors) physically restrict movement, creating ingress jams.
