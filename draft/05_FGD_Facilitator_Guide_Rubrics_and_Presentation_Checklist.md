# Part 1: What You Need to Make — Visual Slide Deck & Drawing Specifications

To conduct a valid, scientific evaluative FGD (Tremblay, Hevner, & Berndt, 2010; Zeisel, 2006; Sanoff, 2000), you cannot simply show raw architectural blueprints or decorative 3D glamour shots. You must present **side-by-side comparative stimuli**: showing **Option A (Conventional Status Quo)** versus **Option B (Proposed Modular Reconfigurable Framework)** across each operational scenario.

Prepare a **12-to-15 slide presentation deck** (using PowerPoint, Google Slides, or Canva) exported to a high-resolution PDF for seamless screen sharing over Google Meet.

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                   THE ANATOMY OF AN EVALUATIVE COMPARISON SLIDE                       │
├──────────────────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ LEFT HALF (OPTION A: STATUS QUO)         │ RIGHT HALF (OPTION B: PROPOSED INTERVENTION)│
├──────────────────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ • Scaled 2D CADD Floor Plan              │ • Scaled 2D CADD Floor Plan                 │
│ • Clear dimension lines (aisle widths)   │ • Staggered clearances, BP 344 paths, lanes │
│ • Eye-level 3D render (shows clutter/bad)│ • Eye-level 3D render (shows clear view)    │
│ • Critical metrics: setup time, blindspot│ • Critical metrics: fast changeover, C≥60mm │
└──────────────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```
