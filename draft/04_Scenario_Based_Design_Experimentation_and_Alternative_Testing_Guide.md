```
└──────────────────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **The Golden Rule of Scenario Testing:**  
> You maintain a **consistent, unified material and cultural identity** (Basey *banig* acoustic wall panels, waterfall ceiling baffles, high-durability flooring).  
> 
> What you **vary and test** are the **spatial layouts, furniture arrangements, circulation geometries, and circulation clearances** across contrasting operational scenarios!

### Typology 1: Plenary Assembly & Civic Convocations (49.6%, f = 63)

#### Scenario 1A: Peak Civic Convocations (250-300 Occupants)  -  Maximum Operational Capacity & Life Safety
* *Operational Demand:* Maximum operational plenary seating capacity under PD 1096 Table XII.1 ($0.65\text{ m}^2/\text{pax}$); unobstructed stage sightlines ($C \ge 60\text{ mm}$); rapid emergency egress (RA 9514); dignified wheelchair inclusion (BP 344). *(Note: While statutory building envelope permits 500 occupants theoretically, empirical plenary convocations peak at 250–300 participants).*
* *Empirical Grounding:* Triangulated with the *ECCD Centers Moving-Up Ceremony* (Event #64; verified operational peak at ~250-300 pax) and *Charter Day Civic Assemblies*.
* * **Option A: Traditional Parallel Linear Seating (Status Quo Baseline)**
  - *Layout Description:* 250–300 loose monobloc chairs in straight parallel rows across the front two-thirds of the 711 sqm hall, separated by a central processional aisle; lateral row passage compressed to $< 250\text{ mm}$.
  - *Operational Deficits:* Rear rows suffer severe sightline decay ($C < 25\text{ mm}$ beyond row 6), forcing attendees to stand along side walls; bottlenecks at doors during mass egress; zero dedicated BP 344 wheelchair stalls; 2.5 to 3.5 hours of manual chair placement.
* * **Option B: V-Shaped (Chevron) Theater Row Seating Blocks (Proposed Modular)**
  - *Layout Description:* Theater rows, straight or in a shallow V (chevron) angled at $\le 15^\circ$ toward stage center, in 2 blocks × 12 seats (500 mm) with staggered seats (900 mm pitch; 12 rows = 288 seats, ≈ 290 with the wheelchair bays); 1800 mm central processional aisle and ≈ 3650 mm side aisles leading to perimeter exit doors; 4 dedicated BP 344 wheelchair bays ($760 \times 1220\text{ mm}$), each with a companion seat beside it; no in-hall catering (snack packs at lobby end-wall tables, 2000 mm off the doors).
  - *Operational Advantages:* Sightline clearance $C \ge 60\text{ mm}$ (staggered seats) holds to the last row at 12.4 m from the stage edge (600 mm limit: 13.3 m); wide side aisles keep exit routes clear; stackable loose chairs deployed on rolling dollies and aligned to dimensioned layout guides reduce setup to < 1 hour; 600 mm modular stage (3 × 3 decks, 7.32 × 3.66 m, centred in the stage recess) set back 2.5 m from the first row, with a platform lift in the recess side gap plus stairs (BP 344 allows ramps or platform lifts); house lighting 300–500 lx plus a stage front wash on the overhead grid; flown L/R mains with FOH behind the last row.

#### Scenario 1B: Mid-Scale Civic Assembly (150-200 Occupants)  -  Intermediate Civic Program
* *Operational Demand:* Structured stage visibility; comfortable aisle circulation; prevention of loose chair drifting; integrated media broadcast points.

  - *Operational Deficits:* Unlinked chairs shift and scatter during ingress; media camera tripods and audio snakes obstruct center aisles; vocal sound echoes off unpadded rear drywall; single point-source loudspeaker creates uneven sound field.

  - *Operational Advantages:* Layout-guided loose chairs maintain orderly aisles; overhead audio cabling removes floor trip hazards; Basey *banig* wall panels absorb vocal reflections; guaranteed $\ge 1500$ mm clear egress avenues; < 45-min setup time.

## 6. Panel Defense Script: Defending "Scenario-Based Alternative Testing"

### Question: "How can you claim this is a research-based thesis rather than a subjective interior design proposal?"
> **Verbatim Response:**  
> *"Thank you for that fundamental methodological question. In interior design research (Groat & Wang, 2013; Zeisel, 2006), the defining difference between a commercial, project-based commission and a scientific research thesis is **comparative scenario-based experimentation**.
> 
> In this study, I did not simply present a single, take-it-or-leave-it aesthetic design. Instead, I established a rigorous testing protocol:
> 
> 1. **First, I identified the twelve operational scenarios across three capacity tiers (Compact, Mid-Scale, Peak)** demanded by the facility's 12-year booking history across all four typologies (Plenary, Classroom, Banquet, and Exhibition).
> 2. **Second, for each scenario, I formulated competing spatial layout alternatives**—such as comparing Traditional Linear Seating against V-Shaped (Chevron) Theater Rows for 500-occupant plenary assemblies, and comparing Orthogonal Grids against Zoned Diamond Clusters for banquets.
> 3. **Third, I empirically tested these alternatives in Phase 3** through an Evaluative Focus Group Discussion with 7 to 10 key stakeholders—including event planners, custodial staff, and attendees—using a structured 5-point rubric measuring turnaround speed, anthropometric clearances, sightline C-values, and BP 344 egress compliance.
> 
> The final interior design framework is not based on my personal artistic whim; it is the **empirically validated result of which spatial alternative demonstrated statistically and operationally superior performance under real-world event pressures.**"*
