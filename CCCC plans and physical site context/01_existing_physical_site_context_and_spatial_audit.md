# Calbayog Cultural Convention Center (CCCC)
## Physical Site Context & Existing Spatial Conditions Audit

This document records the verified firsthand physical architectural conditions, materials, spatial dynamics, and operational realities of the Calbayog Cultural Convention Center (CCCC), serving as the foundational ground-truth baseline for the thesis study.

---

## 1. Spatial Scope & Boundary Delineation

* **Primary Interior Scope (Physical & 3D Deliverables)**:
  * **Zone 1: Arrival & Lobby** (129 m², Grids A–B)
  * **Zone 2: Main Plenary Hall** (711 m², Grids B–E)
  * **Zone 3: Elevated Stage Platform** (52.5 m², Grids E–F)
* **Contextual & Analytical Scope (Narrative & Problem Diagnosis Only)**:
  * **Zone 4: Back-of-House / Ancillary Block** (Grids E–G: Storage Room, Electrical Room, Male Toilet, Female Toilet, Office, and Office Hallway). Excluded from 3D modeling and interior replanning deliverables, but documented for circulation, maintenance, life-safety, and qualitative feedback analysis.

---

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Methodological Demarcation: Existing Facility Baseline vs. Proponent's Proposed Design Intervention**  
> The physical architectural dimensions, structural heights (+5,390 mm flat gypsum ceiling), material finishes (600 × 600 mm glossy porcelain tiles, painted concrete, bare upper bulkheads), and operational bottlenecks documented in this audit describe the **EXISTING UNRENOVATED FACILITY BASELINE** (observed on-site, including crude tie-wire drapes hung at +3,640 mm lacking tiebacks, missing exit signs, locked secondary door leaves, and wall puncture scars).  
> In contrast, all specifications regarding the **Perimeter Dropped Ceiling Soffit at +4,685 mm AFFL**, **Inward-Facing Linear 24V LED Cove Lighting**, **Downward Concealed Dual-Service Pocket (150 × 180 mm)**, **Full-Height Wand-Drawn Acoustic Velour Drapery (+4,685 mm to Floor)**, **Concealed Continuous Unistrut P1000 Swagging Rails**, **2,000 mm Door Clearance Buffers**, and **Illuminated Emergency Exit Signage (RA 9514)** constitute the **PROPONENT'S PROPOSED INTERIOR DESIGN FRAMEWORK INTERVENTIONS** formulated under RA 10350 to permanently resolve these audited deficits. They do not exist in the current building shell.

## 2. Detailed Physical & Environmental Conditions by Zone

### Zone 1: Arrival & Lobby (129 m² | Grid Line A to B)
* **Thermal & Environmental Comfort**: 
  * Completely **non-air-conditioned**, resulting in high humidity, poor air circulation, and stuffiness during events.
* **Operational Usage & Traffic Flow**:
  * Functions as a pre-function waiting buffer for attendees prior to main hall entry.
  * Accommodates outsourced commercial concessionaires and food/beverage vendors, generating localized thermal loads, foot-traffic friction, and vestibule congestion.
  * Entry portal from the lobby into the main hall is dimensionally wide and adequate, but lacks directional queue barriers and acoustic isolation.
* **Acoustic, Fenestration, & Life-Safety Profile**:
  * Main exterior entry doors along Grid A are frameless glass doors.
  * **Usable Door Egress Bottleneck**: Nominal double-leaf door width is 2000 mm, but **only one door leaf can be opened**, restricting usable clear pedestrian entry to just **1000 mm**.
  * Severe acoustic vulnerability: street traffic noise from Hinay-La Street penetrates directly into the lobby.
  * Significant acoustic bleed occurs between the non-isolated Lobby and the Main Hall.
  * **Lighting & Maintenance**: Certain ceiling lighting fixtures are visibly missing/vacant in the existing lobby ceiling plane (documented on the RCP).

---

### Zone 2: The Main Plenary Hall (711 m² | Grid Line B to E)
* **Envelope & Architectural Finishes**:
  * **Vertical Envelope**: Almost entirely surrounded by glass fenestrations set in white steel frames. The only solid concrete partition in the primary envelope is the eastern wall flanking the stage.
  * **Doors & Windows**:
    * **Grid Line B Glass Walls (Columns 1B–2B & 3B–4B)**: The glass walls dividing the lobby and main hall between columns 1B & 2B and 3B & 4B have an overall height of **3,540 mm** (extending from finished floor level up to +3,540 mm AFFL). Above these glass walls, the remaining **1,850 mm** up to the 5,390 mm ceiling plane consists of a painted gypsum board bulkhead/spandrel wall.
    * **Grid Line 1 & Grid Line 4 Perimeter Fenestrations (Zone 2 North & South Walls)**: Perimeter sliding glass doors measure 2040 mm in height × 2000 mm width (1000 mm individual leaf width), flanked by lower windows (2040 mm height). Above these doors and windows, a **1,500 mm clerestory window band** spans from +2,040 mm to +3,540 mm AFFL.
  * **Wall Finishes & Rigging Scars**: Painted concrete in a neutral beige tone above window/door headers. Upper concrete column areas exhibit unsightly **puncture holes and wall scars** caused by event coordinators and crews manually driving temporary anchor hooks for aluminum tie-wire drapery frames.
  * **Flooring Finish**: 600 × 600 mm matte white porcelain tiles (highly reflective, non-absorptive surface).
  * **Ceiling Architecture & Physical Defects**:
    * Clear height of **+5.385 m CFL** (5,385 mm clear finished ceiling line, contextually rounded to 5,390 mm) to the flat gypsum/plasterboard plane (6.20 m to lower structural truss chord).
    * Flat, unbaffled painted white ceiling with flush/recessed light fixtures.
    * **Localized Ceiling Failure**: In **Zone A along Column Grid 3 and 4**, specifically close to **Column 3B**, there is severe **physical damage to the ceiling panels, leaving the sub-ceiling plenum space exposed**.
    * **Missing Luminaires**: Certain lighting fixtures are missing/uninstalled in the Zone A ceiling plane.
* **Loading Staging & Life-Safety Realities**:
  * **Primary Loading Axis**: Among the perimeter entrances, the doors along **Column Grid Line 4 are the most heavily utilized for loading and ingress** (compared to Column Grid Line 1).
  * **Door Frame Collision Wear**: Door openings are tight for large flight cases and banquet gear, resulting in equipment frequently bumping into and scarring door frames and jambs.
  * **Critical Life-Safety Deficit (RA 9514)**: **No designated Fire Exit doors exist, nor is there any illuminated Fire Exit signage** within the primary envelope.
  * **Wayfinding Deficit**: **Zero signage exists indicating restroom locations**, disorienting attendees and forcing them to wander backstage.
* **Acoustic Behavior & Draping Practices (Baseline Deficits vs. Proposed Intervention)**:
  * **Existing Baseline Drapery on Grid Lines 1 & 4**: Along the exterior perimeter walls (Grid 1 and Grid 4), thick beige curtains hang crudely at **+3,640 mm AFFL** (100 mm above the clerestory window header) suspended by temporary tie wires, dropping to the floor to cover both the 1,500 mm clerestory window band and the 2,040 mm sliding doors. Live shadowing observed that these curtains lack tiebacks, concealing side doors and forcing attendees to blindly search for splits during exit egress.
  * **Critical Upper Envelope Acoustic Gap**: While the perimeter drapery dampens mid-frequency reflections across the glass doors and clerestories on Grids 1 & 4, the **1,750 mm painted gypsum bulkhead above the curtain line (+3,640 mm to +5,390 mm AFFL)**, together with the **1,850 mm painted gypsum bulkhead above the 3,540 mm glass walls on Grid B**, remains completely unpadded, rigid, and exposed. These opposing hard vertical boundaries, facing the unbaffled ceiling deck (+5,390 mm) and polished porcelain tile floor, act as major specular reflective surfaces that generate flutter echoes and decay elongation in the upper volume.
  * **Reverberation Divergence & Wall Scars**:
    * *Commercial/High-Budget Events*: Event planners install extensive temporary ceiling swagging and fabric wall draping around the perimeter and stage. However, due to the total absence of permanent perimeter rigging points, crews drive nails, drywall screws, and tie-wires directly into concrete columns and gypsum bulkheads, leaving severe permanent wall puncture scars and exposing the plenum near Column 3B.
    * *Standard/Civic LGU Events*: Conducted with bare walls and unpadded ceilings, resulting in severe reverberation and intelligibility loss comparable to an open, untreated church hall.
  * **Proponent's Proposed Design Intervention (Design Translation to Solve Baseline Deficits)**: To permanently eliminate these deficits without structural alteration, the proponent's proposed interior framework introduces perimeter dropped ceiling soffits at **+4,685 mm AFFL** measuring **2.500 m × 32.505 m** along both lateral perimeters, framing a central field of suspended waterfall acoustic ceiling baffles covering **16.075 m × 32.505 m (522.52 m²)** of the Main Hall. This proposed soffit incorporates an inward-facing indirect 24V LED cove washing the proposed suspended waterfall ceiling baffles, while concealing a downward-facing dual-service pocket along the perimeter housing: (a) a concealed track with **Full-Height Wand-Drawn Acoustic Drapery** (+4,685 mm to floor) with 2,700 mm batons, bi-parting door splits, and magnetic wall holdbacks; and (b) a concealed continuous **Unistrut P1000 Rigging Channel** providing tool-free eyebolts for ceiling fabric swagging, completely eliminating DIY wall punctures.
* **AV, Power, & Temporary Rigging Layout**:
  * **Control Hub**: The soundboard, audio mixer, and technical console are stationed along the south wall near the electrical room (between Grid Columns **D4 and E4**).
  * **Lighting & Tripods**: Lighting stands and tripods are stationed along column bays and near the stage perimeter. Cables are dressed along wall perimeters where possible.
  * **Central Ceiling Truss**: For major social banquets, organizers frequently erect a temporary aluminum box truss in the geometric center of the hall to suspend chandeliers, decorative swags, or focal lighting.

### Zone 3: The Elevated Stage Platform (52.5 m² | Grid Line E to F)
* **Dimensions & Elevation**:
  * Stage platform measures 11.11 m in clear width × 4.20 m in depth, elevated **940 mm AFFL** above the hall finished floor level (accessed via 3 riser steps).
  * Finish material is painted concrete.
* **Sightlines & Visual Obstructions**:
  * **Raised Stage Usage**: Offers clear, unobstructed sightlines across the 711 m² hall under standard arrangements.
  * **Truss Obstruction**: When temporary central aluminum box trusses are erected for banquets, the vertical vertical uprights and low-hanging crossbars obstruct stage sightlines for attendees seated in rear bays (Bay B–C).
  * **Floor-Level Performance & Adaptability Bottleneck**: In the baseline facility, the stage is a monolithic, immovable concrete dais. When events require flat floor area (such as trade expos, food festivals, or open banquets), the static stage permanently locks 52.5 m² into unusable dead space. Conversely, when events host cultural dance or musical acts directly on the flat hall floor rather than on the constricted stage, the flat seating plane completely blocks sightlines for rear rows.
* **Backdrop & Proscenium Setup**:
  * Existing backdrop is a plain, flat painted concrete wall.
  * Major events bring in temporary rented modular LED video walls, flanked by temporary pipe-and-drape framing.

---

### Zone 4: Back-of-House Interface & Restrooms (Grid Line E to G)
*(Contextual / Problem Diagnosis Baseline)*
* **Restroom Access & Circulation Deficits**:
  * Attendees must either cross the entire length of the hall directly past the active stage to enter the backstage access doors, or exit the building entirely to reach exterior toilet entrances.
  * Causes prominent visual and acoustic distractions during formal ceremonies, presentations, and cultural performances.
* **Backstage Doorways & Hallway Dimensions**:
  * Backstage access door is notably narrow at only **955 mm** clear width.
  * The interior backstage hallway is severely constricted, creating friction between performers, VIP speakers, and facility personnel.
* **Safety & Operational Hazards**:
  * The backstage access corridor doubles as the service route for AV technicians and sound engineers.
  * When house lights are dimmed for performances, the narrow passage becomes dark and congested with heavy feeder cables, audio snakes, and stage lighting wiring, representing a high-risk tripping hazard.
