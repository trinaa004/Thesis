Data Analysis
The collected quantitative, qualitative, and architectural data will be analyzed using specialized analytical techniques:
Quantitative Data Analysis
Survey data will be encoded, cleaned, and analyzed using Microsoft Excel. Descriptive statistics will be computed, including:
Frequencies and Percentages: To summarize respondent demographics, event frequency, and categorical preferences.
Weighted Means and Standard Deviations: To interpret Likert-scale evaluations regarding spatial challenges, modular flexibility, ceiling track utility, and cultural material preferences.

Qualitative Data Analysis 
The qualitative data from open-ended survey questions and interview transcripts will be analyzed using thematic analysis. This process involves identifying, analyzing, and reporting patterns (themes) within the data. The steps for thematic analysis, as outlined by Braun and Clarke (2006), will be followed:
Familiarization with the Data: The researcher will transcribe and read through all the interview responses and open-ended survey answers multiple times to become thoroughly familiar with the data.
Generating Initial Codes: Initial codes (labels for specific pieces of data) will be generated based on recurring ideas, phrases, or concepts. These codes will be applied systematically to all relevant data.
Searching for Themes: The researcher will examine the codes to identify broader themes or patterns that reflect significant aspects of the participants' experiences and opinions regarding the convention center's interior design.
Reviewing Themes: The identified themes will be reviewed to ensure that they accurately reflect the data. Some themes may be split, combined, or refined at this stage to ensure they are meaningful and represent the full scope of the data.
Defining and Naming Themes: After finalizing the themes, detailed descriptions of each theme will be written, capturing the essence of what each theme represents within the data.
Writing the Report: Finally, the themes will be interpreted in relation to the study’s research questions and objectives, and will be presented in a narrative form, supported by relevant excerpts from the data.
Triangulation of Data
To ensure data validity, a methodological triangulation approach will be used. Empirical survey trends and qualitative interview themes will be cross-referenced with on-site observational mapping, anthropometric standards (Panero & Zelnik, 1979; Neufert, 2012), and accessibility regulations (Batas Pambansa Bilang 344; ADA Standards, 2010).

This synthesized dataset will directly inform the dimensional parameters, layout typologies, ceiling track mechanisms, and surface material specifications for the final non-structural interior design framework.

Phase 4: Framework Validation & Simulation Analysis
Data generated during the Phase 4 validation process will be analyzed using three specialized methods:
1. Spatial Clearance & Regulatory Compliance Verification: Digital 2D/3D CAD models of the four layout typologies will be audited using a binary compliance matrix (Pass/Fail) against mandatory accessibility thresholds (BP 344 1200 mm paths and 1500 mm turning radiuses) and anthropometric clearance standards (Panero & Zelnik, 1979; Neufert, 2012).
2. Logistical Turnaround Comparative Modeling: Time-and-motion transition sequences from the simulated modular workflows will be tabulated and compared against baseline venue setup durations to calculate percentage reductions in turnaround hours.
3. Expert Evaluation Rubric Analysis: Quantitative ratings from the expert practitioner panel ($n = 3 \text{ to } 5$) will be analyzed using weighted means and standard deviations across the five evaluation criteria (Spatial Feasibility, BP 344 Compliance, Acoustic/Ceiling Operability, Cultural Resonance, and Manual Usability) to substantiate design validity and operational feasibility.

Ethical Considerations
This study will adhere strictly to ethical research standards:

Voluntary Participation: All respondents will participate voluntarily and retain the right to withdraw from the study at any point without penalty.
Informed Consent: Participants will receive an Informed Consent Form detailing the study's purpose, scope, methods, and potential benefits prior to survey or interview participation.
Anonymity & Confidentiality: To protect participant privacy, no personal identifying information will be published. Survey responses and interview transcripts will be assigned alphanumeric identifiers.
Data Security: All electronic data, audio recordings, and field notes will be encrypted and stored in password-protected cloud drives accessible only to the researcher, in accordance with Republic Act No. 10173 (Data Privacy Act of 2012). Data will be permanently purged upon final thesis approval.
