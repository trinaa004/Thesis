# Data Analysis

The collected quantitative, qualitative, and architectural data will be analyzed using specialized analytical techniques across the primary diagnostic and post-design validation phases:

## Quantitative Data Analysis
Survey data will be encoded, cleaned, and analyzed using Microsoft Excel. Descriptive statistics will be computed, including:
* **Frequencies and Percentages:** To summarize respondent demographics, event frequency, and categorical preferences.
* **Weighted Means and Standard Deviations:** To interpret Likert-scale evaluations regarding spatial challenges, modular flexibility, ceiling track utility, and cultural and natural heritage integration.

## Qualitative Data Analysis 
The qualitative data from open-ended survey questions and interview transcripts will be analyzed using thematic analysis. This process involves identifying, analyzing, and reporting patterns (themes) within the data. The steps for thematic analysis, as outlined by Braun and Clarke (2006), will be followed:
1. **Familiarization with the Data:** The researcher will transcribe and read through all the interview responses and open-ended survey answers multiple times to become thoroughly familiar with the data.
2. **Generating Initial Codes:** Initial codes (labels for specific pieces of data) will be generated based on recurring ideas, phrases, or concepts. These codes will be applied systematically to all relevant data.
3. **Searching for Themes:** The researcher will examine the codes to identify broader themes or patterns that reflect significant aspects of the participants' experiences and opinions regarding the convention center's interior design.
4. **Reviewing Themes:** The identified themes will be reviewed to ensure that they accurately reflect the data. Some themes may be split, combined, or refined at this stage to ensure they are meaningful and represent the full scope of the data.
5. **Defining and Naming Themes:** After finalizing the themes, detailed descriptions of each theme will be written, capturing the essence of what each theme represents within the data.
6. **Writing the Report:** Finally, the themes will be interpreted in relation to the study’s research questions and objectives, and will be presented in a narrative form, supported by relevant excerpts from the data.

## Post-Design Stakeholder Validation Analysis & Design Refinement
To evaluate the outputs of Phase 2 (the synthesized Interior Design Framework, 2D CADD plans, 3D renderings, and material specifications), the data collected from the Phase 3 Evaluative Focus Group Discussion (FGD) and Design Verification Rubric will be analyzed using a convergent mixed-analysis protocol (Tremblay, Hevner, & Berndt, 2010; Miles, Huberman, & Saldaña, 2014):

1. **Quantitative Rubric Analysis (Descriptive Statistics):**
   Scoring data from the individual 5-point Likert Design Verification Rubrics (7 to 10 panelists) will be encoded into Microsoft Excel. Weighted means (x̄) and standard deviations (SD) will be computed across the five evaluated design dimensions: (a) Spatial Modularity & Turnaround Viability, (b) Anthropometric Clearances & Accessibility, (c) Acoustic Performance & Durability, (d) Cultural & Natural Heritage Resonance & Place Attachment, and (e) Overhead Rigging Adaptability.
   * **Consensus Benchmark:** An overall composite weighted mean of **≥ 3.26 (out of 5.00)** indicates broad stakeholder validation and operational acceptance. Any individual evaluation item scoring **< 2.50** automatically triggers a mandatory operational design refinement in the final CADD layouts or specifications.

2. **Qualitative Thematic Synthesis (Braun & Clarke, 2006):**
   Verbatim audio transcripts from the moderated discussion blocks will be coded and categorized into three thematic categories:
   * *Operational Validation Affirmations:* Specific layout configurations, clearance dimensions, and acoustic materials confirmed by stakeholders to successfully remediate existing bottlenecks.
   * *Operational Friction Points:* Unforeseen circulation risks, furniture stacking conflicts, or maintenance vulnerabilities flagged by practitioners during the walkthrough.
   * *Actionable Design Refinements:* Practical modifications recommended by panelists to optimize real-world functionality.

3. **Problem-to-Design Refinement Traceability Matrix:**
   Qualitative critiques and quantitative rubric outliers will be systematically mapped into a structured Traceability Matrix. This matrix explicitly documents the stakeholder feedback, the corresponding interior design intervention, and the specific drawing sheet or specification section updated (e.g., adjusting banquet service lanes from 1620 mm to 1800 mm or refining lower wall impact protection).

## Triangulation of Data  
To ensure a thorough pre-occupancy validity for the unbuilt design intervention, a methodological triangulation approach is employed across three structured verification channels:
1. **Spatial & Ergonomic Clearance Audits:** Empirical survey trends and qualitative layout preferences are cross-referenced with on-site envelope measurements, anthropometric standards (Panero & Zelnik, 1979; Neufert, 2012), and statutory accessibility regulations (Batas Pambansa Blg. 344; 2010 ADA Standards). This audits and mathematically confirms the dimensional viability of all four typologies (Plenary, Classroom, Banquet, Exhibition), verifying continuous 1200 mm accessible routes, 1500 mm wheelchair turning bays, and designated wait-staff service corridors (1620–1800 mm).
2. **Acoustic Decibel Analysis, Sabine Modeling, and Material Specification:** Empirical Sound Pressure Level (SPL) data logged from the Benetech GM1352 meter across the 8-point spatial grid are compiled into Microsoft Excel. Mean continuous decibels (Mean Leq), minimums (Lmin), and peak levels (Lmax) are analyzed across diurnal times and occupancy states, benchmarked against ANSI/ASA S12.60 and ASHRAE 62.1 Noise Criteria (NC-25 to NC-30) and ASEAN MICE Venue Standards (AMVS). Measured passive background noise floors and stage-projected JBL PartyBox interrupted pink noise reverberation decay durations are triangulated with the theoretical Sabine Reverberation Equation:
   RT₆₀ = (0.161 × V) / A
   where V represents the interior volume (m³; 3,910.5 m³ bounded by the finished ceiling plane at +5.385 m CFL; V ≈ 4,750 m³ total enclosed physical acoustic volume extending to the lower structural roof truss chord at 6.20 m and exposed plenum) and A is the total equivalent absorption (m² Sabine). In the proposed design intervention, suspended waterfall acoustic ceiling baffles cover a central field of 16.075 m × 32.505 m (522.52 m²) of the Main Hall, flanked by perimeter dropped ceiling soffits at +4,685 mm AFFL measuring 2.500 m × 32.505 m on both lateral perimeters housing inward-facing cove illumination and downward concealed drapery pockets. This mathematical modeling demonstrates why the untreated bare ceramic and masonry shell yields excessive reverberation (RT60 > 2.0 s), establishing the empirical necessity for laboratory-certified Noise Reduction Coefficient (NRC) thresholds (Egan, 2007; Cavanaugh et al., 2010)—specifically mandating NRC ≥ 0.75 for Basey *tikog* composite wall panels and NRC ≥ 0.85 for suspended waterfall ceiling baffles to remediate flutter echoes down to the 0.8–1.2s target.
3. **Analog Precedent Triangulation:** Empirical findings from the primary field investigation are systematically cross-referenced with documented post-occupancy evaluations and flexibility benchmarks from analogous public event facilities (Ayuba & Agah, 2018 for modular partition and FF&E flexibility; Baguio BCCC Retrofit, 2022 for provincial municipal LGU hall modernization; and An et al., 2021 for MICE venue reconfigurability).
4. **Longitudinal Facility Utilization, Archival Seating Audits, and Turnaround Triangulation:** Primary questionnaire metrics regarding setup turnaround delays and spatial layout friction are systematically triangulated against the facility's 12-year archival booking ledger (N = 127 verified events, 2014–2026; compiled in **Appendix A**). Because historical civic assemblies operated without electronic turnstile ticketing systems, historical occupant capacity scales were reconstructed through a secondary data estimation procedure synthesizing:
   - *Archival Document Triangulation (Groat & Wang, 2013):* Corroborating explicit attendee, delegate, and participant counts published in official municipal press releases, LGU Public Information Office (PIO) records, Department of Education (DepEd) memoranda, and local news archives (e.g., exactly 295 registered delegates for the *Artists Kolektib*, 96 couples and families for *Kasalang Bayan*, 53 creative entrepreneurs for the *DTI Workshop*, and 13 contestant cooking stations for the *Tinapa 101 Cookfest*).
   - *Physical Trace & Photographic Seating Array Audits (Zeisel, 2006):* Examining high-resolution archival event photography to audit physical furniture arrays, calculating row-by-column chair counts (Nrows × Nseats) and banquet table clusters deployed across the 711 sqm Main Hall floor plate.
   - *Statutory Architectural Capacity Factoring (PD 1096; RA 9514):* Applying occupant load density factors from Section 1004 of the National Building Code of the Philippines (0.65 m²/person for unconcentrated assembly with loose chairs; 1.40 m²/person for banquet dining) and Section 10.2.8.1 of the Fire Code of the Philippines to establish physical upper and lower capacity boundaries for the 711 sqm envelope (maximum 500 occupants for plenary assembly; 250 to 300 occupants for banquet dining).
   - *Stratified Occupancy Tiering:* To reflect operational reality without fabricating unverified single-digit numbers, historical events are organized into four standardized capacity brackets: Tier 1 (50–120 pax), Tier 2 (150–250 pax), Tier 3 (250–350 pax), and Tier 4 (400–500 pax).
   - *Empirical Calibration via Live Event Shadowing:* These archival seating models were directly calibrated and cross-validated against empirical headcounts, furniture layouts, and turnaround durations logged during the researcher's direct on-site physical shadowing across five contemporary live events (n = 5: *TESDA Graduation*, *Samar Investment Forum*, *Private Wedding Reception*, *PAGPASAMWAK Caravan*, and *Stakeholders' Gala Night*) and corroborated through qualitative interviews with veteran venue booking coordinators and event planners (n = 5, Informants EP1–EP5). This empirical corroboration links reported setup delays (4–8+ hours) directly to documented calendar compression clusters (e.g., peak multi-typology turnovers during municipal charter festivities) and mathematically corroborates the community demand distribution across all four modular typologies.

This synthesized dataset directly informs the dimensional parameters, layout typologies, ceiling track mechanisms, and surface material specifications for the final non-structural interior design framework.

Ethical Considerations
This study will adhere strictly to ethical research standards:

Voluntary Participation: All respondents will participate voluntarily and retain the right to withdraw from the study at any point without penalty.
Informed Consent: Participants will receive an Informed Consent Form detailing the study's purpose, scope, methods, and potential benefits prior to survey or interview participation.
Anonymity & Confidentiality: To protect participant privacy, no personal identifying information will be published. Survey responses and interview transcripts will be assigned alphanumeric identifiers.
Data Security: All electronic data, audio recordings, and field notes will be encrypted and stored in password-protected cloud drives accessible only to the researcher, in accordance with Republic Act No. 10173 (Data Privacy Act of 2012). Data will be permanently purged upon final thesis approval.
