Convention or exhibition centers have long been used for large-scale gatherings like conferences, exhibitions, and meetings (Bhandari, 2016). Convention centers are multi-purpose venues that can provide adaptability to cater to different types of events (Fulgar, 2024). Traditionally, these venues were designed with an approach that primarily catered to formal and business-oriented events. However, as the event industry evolves, the need for more flexible and multi-functional spaces that can meet the changing demands of the modern event scene is becoming more and more necessary (Ike et al., 2023). This is further supported by a study by Wee & Anas (2022), which claims that there is growing pressure on outdated event facilities to make improvements that will help modernize the space. 
The rise of the Meeting, Incentive, Convention, and Exhibition (MICE) industry plays a significant role in developing convention centers. Nowadays, event planners search for venues that optimize operational logistics, such as configuration speed and physical adaptability, to seamlessly execute diverse event formats (An, Kim, & Hur, 2021). Furthermore, according to an article by Fakharany (2025), modern convention spaces are moving away from sterile, corporate aesthetics, evolving into experiential environments of discovery and engagement for event-goers. In the contemporary globalized market, this user experience is deeply heightened by grounding global event standards within localized cultural narratives. Consequently, contemporary convention centers serve as primary touchpoints for regional identity and cultural tourism, necessitating interiors that visually and spatially communicate a distinct sense of place. 
However, despite the shifts in demand, a multitude of convention centers still operate within rigid, outdated interior configurations that fail to adapt to the changing expectations of both logistical operators and event-goers. By failing to address these interior dynamic shifts, these spaces are unable to maximize their potential, causing their interior envelopes to function as static, inefficient shells (Etteh, 2016). 
	With this in mind, this study aims to develop a site-specific interior design framework consisting of modular interior typologies and layout standards for the Calbayog City Convention Center. By integrating modularity, localized Samar cultural responsiveness, and future-proof design principles as non-structural interior overlays, the study explores how flexible furniture configurations (FF&E), material surface treatments, and adjustable overhead track systems can optimize the 840 square meter interior envelope (comprising the 711 square meter Main Convention Hall and 129 square meter Foyer area). Ultimately, this study seeks to demonstrate how targeted interior design interventions can resolve the disconnect between a venue's structural shell and its internal operational efficiency, cultivating an adaptable, culturally expressive environment for future-proof event execution.
