Convention or exhibition centers have long been used for large-scale gatherings like conferences, exhibitions, and meetings (Bhandari, 2016). Convention centers are multi-purpose venues that can provide adaptability to cater to different types of events (Fulgar, 2024). Traditionally, these venues were designed with an approach that primarily catered to formal and business-oriented events. However, as the event industry evolves, the need for more flexible and multi-functional spaces that can meet the changing demands of the modern event scene is becoming more and more necessary (Ike et al., 2023). This is further supported by studies by Wee, Kamarulzaman, and Anas (2022) and Ayuba and Agah (2018), which emphasize the growing operational pressure on conventional event facilities to adapt and incorporate flexible, multipurpose interior capabilities. 
The rise of the Meeting, Incentive, Convention, and Exhibition (MICE) industry plays a significant role in developing convention centers. Nowadays, event planners search for venues that optimize operational logistics, such as configuration speed and physical adaptability, to seamlessly execute diverse event formats (An, Kim, & Hur, 2021). Furthermore, modern convention spaces are moving away from sterile, corporate aesthetics, evolving into experiential environments of discovery and engagement for event-goers (Fakharany, 2025). In the contemporary market, this user experience is deeply heightened by grounding global event standards within localized cultural narratives. Consequently, contemporary convention centers serve as primary touchpoints for regional identity and cultural tourism, necessitating interiors that visually and spatially communicate a distinct sense of place. Calbayog City has long established itself as a regional center for culture and the arts in Eastern Visayas. In line with this identity, the local government officially designates its premier indoor facility as the **Calbayog Cultural Convention Center**, chartered to serve as a vital hub for promoting Calbayog City’s rich culture, arts, and tourism, featuring spaces for performances, art displays, cultural training, community assemblies, and celebrating local traditions such as the **Hadang Festival (historically and popularly recognized as the Sarakiki-Hadang Festival)** (City Government of Calbayog, n.d.).
However, despite the shifts in demand and official civic aspirations, a multitude of provincial convention centers still operate within rigid, outdated interior configurations that fail to adapt to the changing expectations of logistical operators, event-goers, and cultural stakeholders. By failing to address these interior dynamic shifts, these spaces are unable to maximize their potential, causing their interior envelopes to function as static, inefficient shells (Etteh, 2016). 
With this in mind, this study aims to develop a site-specific interior design framework consisting of modular interior typologies and layout standards for the **Calbayog Cultural Convention Center** (locally referenced as the Calbayog City Convention Center). By integrating modularity, localized Samar & Calbayog cultural responsiveness, and future-proof design principles as non-structural interior overlays, the study explores how flexible furniture configurations (FF&E), material surface treatments, and adjustable overhead track systems can optimize the 840 square meter interior envelope (comprising the 711 square meter Main Convention Hall and 129 square meter Lobby area). Ultimately, this study seeks to demonstrate how targeted interior design interventions can resolve the disconnect between a venue's shell and its operational efficiency, cultivating an adaptable, culturally expressive environment capable of effortlessly accommodating civic assemblies, corporate seminars, curated cultural gatherings, weddings, birthdays, and other events.
