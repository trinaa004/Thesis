# Chapter 4: Data Presentation, Analysis, and Triangulation

This chapter presents, analyzes, and interprets the empirical data gathered in accordance with the study's **Applied Design Research** methodology. The findings are systematically structured to address the Statement of the Problem (SPOP Q1–Q3) established in Chapter 1, synthesizing four empirical investigative pillars:
1. **A 12-Year Longitudinal Venue Utilization Audit (N = 127 verified events from 2014 to 2026; compiled in Appendix A):** Reconstructing real-world booking trajectories, capacity scale distributions, and operational typologies to ground interior interventions in historical community demand (**Addressing SPOP Q2**).
2. **An Expert Operational Survey & Diagnostic Field Interviews (n = 12, Cronbach's alpha = 0.9402; Informants EP1–EP5):** Evaluating logistical turnaround bottlenecks, changeover pressures, FF&E storage deficits, and spatial frictions encountered by event planners and technical crews (**Addressing SPOP Q1.2**).
3. **A Public Venue Attendee Survey & Qualitative User Interviews (N = 100, Cronbach's alpha = 0.9469; Informants EA1–EA5):** Measuring community perceptions of baseline indoor environmental quality (IEQ), acoustic speech intelligibility, thermal comfort, seating ergonomics, and demand for Samar cultural placemaking (**Addressing SPOP Q1.1 & SPOP Q3.2**).
4. **Physical In-Situ Environmental & Observational Audits:** Comprising an 8-point spatial acoustic audit (diurnal ambient noise floors, ISO 3382-2 pink noise decay testing, live event acoustic stress tests, and Sabine RT60 modeling), direct live event lifecycle shadowing (Protocol B), and archival physical trace analyses (Protocol C).

The chapter culminates in **Section 4.5**, where empirical diagnostic metrics are synthesized into four standardized modular layout typologies across a tri-scale capacity architecture, validated through a pre-occupancy **Evaluative Focus Group Discussion (FGD)** employing scenario-based alternative testing (Option A vs. Option B) across 13 operational scenarios.

### 4.0.1. Theoretical and Analytical Grounding for Qualitative and Observational Analysis
To guarantee scientific rigor and guard against speculative interpretation, qualitative narratives and observational data are examined through the dual theoretical framework established in Chapter 2:
1. **Actor-Network Theory (Latour, 1996, 2005):** Operational logistics are conceptualized as heterogeneous socio-technical networks where **Human Actors** (planners, custodial crews, AV technicians, caterers, attendees) interact with **Non-Human Actants** (glazed ceramic floor tiles, heavy non-stackable wooden tables, unpadded gypsum bulkheads, monolithic 52.5 sqm concrete stage dais, 1000 mm door leaves, and loose electrical cables). Baseline operational failures are diagnosed as *material network frictions and translation breakdowns* where unyielding physical actants impose severe operational resistance on human workflows.
2. **Environmental Affordance Theory (Gibson, 1979):** Applied to evaluate the functional action possibilities the 840 sqm interior envelope physically offers or denies. Crucially, this lens distinguishes between the *cognitive and communicative planning affordances* of standardized layout templates (enabling planners to visualize capacity limits for non-expert clients) and the *physical affordances* of technical infill systems (overhead utility rigging, demountable modular staging, and mobile FF&E dollies) that physically compress changeover turnaround times from 4–12 hours down to under 90 minutes.
3. **Analytical Mechanics:** Qualitative transcripts are coded using **Braun & Clarke's (2006)** six-phase thematic analysis, while field photography and archival records are evaluated through **Zeisel's (2006)** and **Groat & Wang's (2013)** physical trace methodology.

#### Empirical Physical Envelope & Baseline Audit Findings
Architectural site surveys and vacant hall audits revealed six critical spatial, life-safety, and acoustic deficits:
1. **Single-Leaf Egress Bottleneck (RA 9514 / BP 344):** While primary double-leaf entrance doors nominally measure 2000 mm, only one leaf can be opened, restricting usable pedestrian flow to **1000 mm clear**. The hall completely lacks illuminated exit signage and designated emergency fire exits.
2. **Loading Ingress Axis & Door Frame Collision Damage:** Service loading along Column Grid Line 4 suffers severe vehicular and door congestion. Narrow 1000 mm clear door openings cause heavy flight cases, mobile sound gear, and banquet furniture to repeatedly impact and damage door jambs and frames.
3. **Acoustic Clerestory & Gypsum Bulkhead Reflectance:** Along Grid Line B, 3,540 mm glass walls are topped by an unpadded 1,850 mm painted gypsum bulkhead up to the 5,390 mm ceiling. Along exterior walls (Grids 1 and 4), 2,040 mm sliding glass doors are topped by a 1,500 mm clerestory window band with lightweight curtains hanging from +3,640 mm AFFL, leaving a 1,750 mm unpadded upper gypsum bulkhead. These hard, specular vertical surfaces trap sound in the upper volume and drive chronic flutter echoes.
4. **Overhead Rigging Void & Physical Ceiling Panel Failure:** The ceiling plane lacks permanent unistrut rigging tracks or truss suspension points. Organizers drive ad-hoc tie-wires and nails into walls, leaving unsightly puncture scars. Crucially, near Column 3B (Grid 3–4), the ceiling exhibits physical panel failure, exposing the sub-ceiling plenum space.
5. **Restroom Wayfinding Deficit & Backstage Cross-Traffic:** With zero signage indicating restroom facilities, attendees are forced to cross the active presentation area to reach the narrow 955 mm backstage corridor (Zone 4), creating visual distractions while navigating tripping hazards from taped audio snakes and power feeders.
6. **Legacy Monolithic Concrete Stage Bottleneck:** The static 52.5 sqm concrete dais elevated at +940 mm permanently consumes 7.4% of the hall plate. It cannot be cleared for flat-floor trade fairs or culinary cookfests, while its height compresses vertical sightlines and causes dancers and VIP tables to spill onto the main floor plate.

#### Qualitative Thematic Analysis: Actor-Network Frictions & Affordance Breakdown
Triangulating the survey scores with in-depth interviews (Informants EP1–EP5) reveals four core operational breakdowns:
1. **The Overhead Utility Void and the "Truss Footprint Penalty" (Item B5, x̄ = 4.42, SD = 0.51):**  
   Scoring highest in the baseline cluster, the absence of ceiling rigging emerged as the primary operational handicap. Because the 6.20 m portal roof trusses lack unistrut channels, technical crews must erect heavy ground-supported aluminum crank-stand trusses on the floor plate:
   * **Floor Area Penalty:** Four ground truss towers consume **36.0 sqm of prime floor plate** (including safety ballast envelopes), directly displacing four 10-person round banquet tables—a net loss of **40 guest seats**.
   * **Visual Sightline Deprivation:** Ground vertical truss masts create prominent visual obstructions for rear audience seating rows.
   * **Cabling Hazards:** Informant EP3 noted: *"Our lights and sounds setup takes only 1–2 hours. The longest delay is waiting for styling decorators to finish manual ceiling drapery and scaffolds. Because there are no ceiling tracks, we have to route cables all along the floor perimeters to prevent tripping."*
2. **Setup Marathons and Physical Strain (Item B2, x̄ = 3.92, SD = 0.67):**  
   Without pre-calculated layout guides, setups devolve into trial-and-error manual labor. Informants EP2 and EP5 explained that decorators must hang up to **9 full rolls of cloth (25–30 panels)** across bare walls to mask unpadded concrete and suppress flutter echoes. This manual draping marathon consumes **1.5 full working days (over 12 hours)**, causing intense physical fatigue and forcing multi-day venue rentals.
3. **Loading Bottlenecks & Service Collisions (Item B3, x̄ = 3.58, SD = 0.51):**  
   Loading along Column Grid Line 4 suffers severe spatial friction. Narrow 1000 mm door leaves force service crews carrying 10-plate banquet trays, flight cases, and heavy wooden furniture into single-file bottlenecks, resulting in repeated door frame collision damage and unconditioned lobby chaos.
4. **The Planning vs. Physical Turnaround Paradigm (Gibson, 1979; Items B2 & C1):**  
   Crucially, qualitative analysis of veteran planners (EP1, EP5) established an essential theoretical distinction: **ergonomic layout templates and furniture density clusters do not physically slash on-site setup times; rather, their primary affordance operates during the pre-event client planning and capacity visualization phase.**
   * *Cognitive Planning Affordance (Templates):* Informant EP1 (Isle Events) testified that templates enable planners to visually enforce realistic limits: *"In weddings and banquets, the maximum tables and chairs is strictly 300. When 500 people were forced into the hall for Hermana Night, waiters could barely move. Having pre-calculated layout templates allows us to prove to clients why 300 is the real safe maximum."*
   * *Physical Turnaround Affordance (Technical Infill):* What **physically compresses setup turnaround from 4–12 hours down to under 90 minutes** are the proposed **non-structural technical infill systems (Modules 1, 3, & 4)**: motorized ceiling rigging tracks (eliminating ground truss towers), demountable modular staging platforms (reclaiming 52.5 sqm of continuous flat floor plate), mobile FF&E on transport dollies, and the conditioned lobby concourse.

### 4.4.3. Qualitative Thematic Analysis: Artisanal Synthesis
Grounded in Braun & Clarke's (2006) thematic framework, qualitative synthesis generated three actionable design themes that harmonize regional heritage with modern civic functionality:

#### Theme 1: Regional Samar Weaving Craft (Basey Banig & Tikog Textures)
The traditional reed mat (*banig*) weaving of Basey, Samar—celebrated for its intricate geometric linework—serves as the primary tactile and visual motif. Rather than hanging literal woven mats that could fray or harbor dust, the mathematical geometry of **Samat** (diamond and chevron rhythms) and **Lara** (interlocking warp-and-weft patterns) is abstracted into:
1. Custom fabric-wrapped acoustic wall absorption panels (NRC >= 0.75) mounted along perimeter concrete walls.
2. Micro-perforated natural oak acoustic paneling (Flexiflute) and flexible column cladding on Column Grids B and C.
3. Laser-etched cultural identification plaques embedded at eye level (1500 mm AFFL) explaining the weaving heritage of Basey artisans to visiting delegates.

#### Theme 2: Calbayog "City of Waterfalls" Linework
Calbayog is nationally renowned as Samar's "City of Waterfalls," home to iconic natural landmarks including Bangon Falls, Tarangban Falls, and Pan-as Falls. The cascading kinetic energy and fluid contours of these waterfalls are abstracted into the overhead plane:
1. **Undulating Acoustic Ceiling Baffles:** Suspended continuous thermoformed PET felt baffles (NRC >= 0.85) contouring longitudinally across the 711 sqm Main Hall.
2. **Visual & Acoustic Optimization:** These cascading ceiling baffles obscure the industrial portal roof deck, integrate recessed smart LED lighting lines, and physically trap rising mid-to-high frequency flutter reflections.

#### Theme 3: Civic Performing Arts (Hadang / CACO Lakub) & The Neutral Finishes Principle
The vibrant ceremonial movements, rooster (*manok*) iconography, and rhythmic drumming of the annual *Hadang Festival* and CACO *Lakub* dances inspire the venue's focal features:
1. **The Photographer's Neutrality Mandate:** Crucially, interviews with visual production leads and event stylists (Informants EP3, EP4, EP5) established an essential design rule: **avoid saturated, vibrant paint colors on primary hall walls**. Informant EP4 explained: *"Camera lenses pick up chromatic reflection. If the walls are brightly colored, that color bounces directly onto the bride's white gown and guests' faces, turning them green or reddish on camera! Primary walls must remain neutral earth tones, light oak, and warm beige, so they coordinate flawlessly with any event theme."*
2. **Permanent Hadang Cultural Landmark:** To celebrate Hadang without causing camera color spill, cultural iconography is concentrated in the **129 sqm Lobby concourse face of Grid Line B** (between Columns 1B–2B and 3B–4B) in the form of a **monumental custom CNC wood-carved relief feature mural**. Arriving delegates encounter an inspiring cultural landmark, while the Main Hall remains an elegant, neutral architectural canvas (warm beige Wallcrown UW1062 wallpaper, light oak paneling, and A-xet Bianco Snow Terrazzo LVT).

#### Architectural Sightline & Stage Clearance Ruleset
Stage heights are mathematically governed by the flat-floor sightline clearance formula (Neufert, 2012):
> **C = [(Stage Height + 1.50 m - 1.15 m) x Row Pitch] / Distance to Presenter >= 60 mm (with staggered seating)**

This check proves that a **300 mm dais** provides passing sightlines up to 8.8 m for theater seating (0.9 m pitch) and up to 38.0 m for cabaret seating (1.4 m pitch), confirming that 300 mm is optimal for compact commencements (Scenario 1C), full-hall cabaret training (Scenario 2A), and hybrid showcases (Scenarios 4A–4D). Peak convocations (Scenario 1A: 12 rows extending to 12.4 m) utilize a **600 mm stage** (passing checks up to 13.3 m). Universal accessibility is delivered via a **movable vertical platform lift on lockable casters (1.5 x 1.5 m; BP 344 compliant)** stationed in the 1.89 m recess side gap, serving both 300 mm and 600 mm elevations without consuming linear hall floor plate for sprawling ramps.

#### Dual Banquet Table Geometries & Safe Capacity Ceiling
Banquet dining encompasses two distinct spatial configurations:
1. **Format A (Round Banquet Tables, Template 3A-1; Max 300 pax):** 30 ten-person round tables (O 1800 mm) at 3.6 m centers (5 across x 6 rows) maintain a guaranteed **1620 to 1800 mm service corridor** (exceeding Neufert's 1400 mm standard), allowing waitstaff carrying 10-plate trays to circulate smoothly without brushing diner chair backs. Suspending lighting from motorized ceiling hoists **reclaims 36 sqm of floor plate, recovering 40 guest seats (4 full tables)** to safely achieve the 300-pax ceiling.
2. **Format B (Rectangular Communal Clusters, Template 3A-2; Max 240–280 pax):** Linear communal tables (450/600 x 1800 mm units arranged in 10-pax clusters) oriented parallel to a **2400–3000 mm central ceremonial processional aisle**, specifically designed for religious galas (*Hermana Night*), fiesta banquets, and civic weddings requiring solemn processional marches.
3. **The 300-Pax Banquet Capacity Ceiling:** Triangulating PD 1096 Table XII.1 (1.40 sqm/pax for dining) and RA 9514 life-safety egress proves that after deducting stage, buffet, and perimeter avenues, the 711 sqm Main Hall provides ~420–450 sqm of net dining space. Holding 1620–1800 mm service lanes across this area caps safe dining capacity at **250 to 300 occupants**. Past events attempting 500 banquet guests (e.g. Hermana Night) represent severe, dangerous code violations.
