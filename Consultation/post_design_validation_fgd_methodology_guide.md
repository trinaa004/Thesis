## 1. Executive Summary & Context: The Coordinator-Adviser Consensus

In applied interior design and architectural research where the final thesis deliverable is an unbuilt, evidence-based spatial proposal rather than a physically constructed building, establishing pre-construction validity is a foundational requirement. While theoretical calculations (such as anthropometric clearance audits and laboratory-certified Noise Reduction Coefficient [NRC] ratings) provide technical justification, your Research Coordinator and Thesis Adviser have correctly emphasized that **human operational validity** must also be demonstrated.

To bridge this requirement, this study introduces a formal **Post-Design Focus Group Discussion (FGD) Stakeholder Validation Stage**. Conducted after the completion of the physical design deliverables (the Interior Design Framework, 2D layout drawings, and 3D visual models), this stage reconvenes a targeted, purposive sub-sample of previously surveyed or interviewed key stakeholders (professional event planners, venue facility operators, attendee representatives, and cultural advocates). 

Through a structured visual presentation, independent rubric evaluation, and moderated thematic discussion, this validation phase stress-tests the proposed interior interventions against real-world logistical workflows, spatial clearances, cultural authenticity, and acoustic practicality.

## 3. Addressing Question A: How to Conduct the FGD Without Being "Messy" or "Too Biased"

Focus group discussions evaluating creative or architectural outputs face two common pitfalls:
1. **The "Messy" Pitfall (Disorganization & Scope Creep):** Participants drift into non-interior debates (civil structural repairs, parking lot politics, catering food menus, municipal budget squabbles) or talk over one another without reaching actionable conclusions.
2. **The "Too Biased" Pitfall (Groupthink & Polite Acquiescence):** Participants exhibit social desirability bias (praising the student's attractive 3D models out of courtesy: *"Napakaganda, perfect na ito!"*), or a dominant senior participant (e.g., a senior venue manager) intimidates younger event coordinators into silence.

To prevent both pitfalls, the validation FGD must follow a **structured, evidence-based methodological protocol**:

### A. Anti-Bias Protocols: Ensuring Objective, Unfiltered Evaluation

1. **Pre-Discussion Independent Silent Scoring (Nominal Group Technique Element):**
   - *The Mechanism:* Before any open verbal discussion begins, participants spend 8–10 minutes reviewing the design boards and completing an individual, anonymous **Design Verification Rubric**.
   - *Why It Eliminates Bias:* It captures each participant's unfiltered, independent judgment before group discussion begins. It completely neutralizes "anchoring bias" (where the first speaker sets the tone) and "social conformity bias" (where junior planners hesitate to disagree with senior colleagues).

2. **Facilitator Neutrality Script & Objective Framing:**
   - *The Mechanism:* The researcher must never present the project as an "artist showcasing finished art," but rather as an **investigator stress-testing an operational hypothesis**.
   - *Scripted Facilitator Anchor:*
     > *"Thank you for being here. Today, I am not presenting this design to ask for compliments or approval. This is an applied interior design framework intended to solve real operational bottlenecks in Calbayog City. My goal today is to **stress-test this proposal**. I need you to actively look for flaws, circulation pinch-points, unrealistic turnaround steps, or material concerns. Finding potential problems today is what will make this framework viable in the real world. Your critical, honest feedback is the most valuable contribution you can provide."*

3. **Structured Round-Robin Turn-Taking (Eliminating Dominant Speaker Bias):**
   - In accordance with Krueger & Casey (2014), each discussion block begins with a quick round-robin where every participant receives exactly 60 to 90 seconds of uninterrupted floor time to share their initial reaction before open floor deliberation begins.
   - If a participant attempts to dominate, the facilitator politely intervenes: *"Thank you, [Name], that’s a critical insight on the staging. Let's make sure we hear from [Coordinator B] regarding how the service lanes work for catering staff before we proceed."*

4. **Multi-Stakeholder Triangulation (Balanced Composition):**
   - The focus group is intentionally composed of diverse stakeholder perspectives (operational, facility management, and user/community), ensuring that no single special interest dictates the outcome.

### B. Anti-Mess Protocols: Ensuring Laser-Focused Operational Rigor

1. **Strict Non-Structural Scope Boundary Anchor:**
   - At the opening minute of the session, the boundary slide is projected:
     > *"Scope Reminder: This study addresses the interior envelope of the 840 sqm Main Hall and Foyer. We are evaluating interior layout typologies, furniture reconfigurability, acoustic surface claddings, and overhead track adaptability. Matters regarding exterior civil engineering, roof truss replacements, outdoor parking, or municipal catering operations are strictly outside our design scope."*
   - Any participant straying into outside topics is immediately and gently redirected back to the interior modules.

2. **Side-by-Side Dual Stimulus Presentation (2D Plans + 3D Renderings):**
   - *The Danger:* Showing only technical 2D blueprints confuses non-technical participants; showing only 3D renderings leads to superficial aesthetic commentary on colors rather than functional evaluation of aisle widths and turnaround times.
   - *The Solution:* Every slide presents a synchronized split-screen:
     - **Left Side:** Dimensioned 2D CADD Layout (showing aisle clearances, wheelchair turning radiuses, service lanes, and egress arrows).
     - **Right Side:** Photorealistic Eye-Level 3D Perspective (showing how the space feels to an occupant or worker).
     - **Bottom Banner:** Quantitative Metrics (Occupant capacity, turnaround setup sequence, and acoustic NRC ratings).

3. **Time-Boxed 4-Block Modular Agenda (80-Minute Session):**
   - To avoid participant fatigue and conversational drift, the FGD is strictly partitioned into four 15-minute operational blocks, preceded by an orientation and concluded with member-checking:

## 4. Addressing Question B: Authoritative Academic Precedents & Sources

Your thesis committee and coordinator require that this methodology is firmly anchored in recognized academic and design literature. The proposed post-design FGD validation is supported by the following peer-reviewed, seminal sources:

### A. Seminal Precedents in Design Artifact & Framework Validation

1. **Tremblay, M. C., Hevner, A. R., & Berndt, D. J. (2010). Focus groups for artifact refinement and evaluation in design research. *Communications of the Association for Information Systems*, 26(1), Article 27.**
   - *Why It Directly Validates Your Study:* This is the landmark peer-reviewed methodological paper establishing the use of focus groups in applied design research. Tremblay et al. distinguish between *Exploratory Focus Groups* (used to define problems prior to design) and **Evaluative Focus Groups (EFGs)** (used post-design to rigorously evaluate the utility, feasibility, and quality of design artifacts and frameworks with expert practitioners). This directly mirrors your methodology.

2. **Zeisel, J. (2006). *Inquiry by design: Environment/behavior/neuroscience in architecture, interiors, landscape, and planning*. W. W. Norton & Company.**
   - *Why It Directly Validates Your Study:* Zeisel establishes the foundational environmental design cycle: *Empirical Programming $\rightarrow$ Design Synthesis $\rightarrow$ Focused Design Review & Simulation Testing*. Zeisel explicitly validates holding focused stakeholder review sessions using unbuilt drawings and visual simulations to test spatial fit and operational efficiency before physical execution.

3. **Sanoff, H. (2000). *Community participation methods in design and planning*. John Wiley & Sons.**
   - *Why It Directly Validates Your Study:* Sanoff details structured participatory design evaluation methods where unbuilt architectural and interior plans are presented to stakeholder groups alongside visual rating matrices, allowing users to score and discuss spatial proposals systematically.

4. **Preiser, W. F. E., & Vischer, J. C. (Eds.). (2005). *Assessing building performance*. Elsevier / Architectural Press.**
   - *Why It Directly Validates Your Study:* Preiser and Vischer define **Pre-Occupancy Evaluation (PrOE)**. They demonstrate that when actual post-occupancy testing of an unbuilt facility is impossible, researchers must conduct pre-occupancy predictive evaluations through expert simulation reviews, clearance cross-audits, and stakeholder focus sessions.

### B. Qualitative Methodological Rigor & Anti-Bias Foundations

5. **Krueger, R. A., & Casey, M. A. (2014). *Focus groups: A practical guide for applied research* (5th ed.). SAGE Publications.**
   - *Why It Directly Validates Your Study:* The premier international authority on focus group design. Provides explicit protocols for participant recruitment, question routing (transitioning from broad to critical questions), facilitator neutrality, mitigating dominant participants, and ensuring data saturation without researcher bias.

6. **Lincoln, Y. S., & Guba, E. G. (1985). *Naturalistic inquiry*. SAGE Publications. / Creswell, J. W., & Creswell, J. D. (2018). *Research design*. SAGE.**
   - *Why It Directly Validates Your Study:* Establishes **Member Checking (Participant Validation)** as the gold standard for qualitative trustworthiness and credibility. By reconvening interviewees and survey respondents to verify whether the synthesized spatial design faithfully interprets and solves their articulated problems, the researcher guarantees qualitative validity.

7. **Stewart, D. W., & Shamdasani, P. N. (2014). *Focus groups: Theory and practice* (3rd ed.). SAGE Publications.**
   - *Why It Directly Validates Your Study:* Establishes standard participant sizing rules for specialized focus groups. Confirms that for focused facility and operational evaluations, small groups of **6 to 10 participants** optimize interactive discussion depth while preventing conversational splintering and chaos.

### Instrument 4B: Semi-Structured FGD Discussion Guide (Questioning Route)

Following Krueger & Casey (2014), the discussion follows a structured 4-phase questioning sequence:

#### Phase 1: Introductory & Orientation Questions (5 minutes)
- *"Having reviewed the 2D floor plans and 3D renderings of the proposed interior framework, what is your overall first impression regarding how this compares to the current condition of the convention center?"*

#### Phase 2: Transition Questions: Operational Feasibility (10 minutes)
- *"Looking specifically at the setup procedures for the four layout typologies (Plenary, Banquet, Classroom, Exhibition), how realistic is the proposed furniture handling and mobile partition deployment for your typical crew sizes?"*

#### Phase 3: Core Key Questions: Stress-Testing & Critical Scrutiny (30 minutes)
- **Modularity & Logistics:** *"Where do you foresee potential physical bottlenecks or operational friction when transitioning between a daytime conference (Plenary) and an evening gala (Banquet) within this envelope?"*
- **Circulation & Accessibility:** *"From your experience managing crowd movements or attending events, are the 1200 mm aisles and designated service corridors realistic during peak capacity (500 occupants)?"*
- **Acoustics & Materials:** *"Examining the proposed Basey tikog composite wall panels and ceiling baffles, what are your practical thoughts regarding their operational durability, cleanability, and acoustic dampening capabilities?"*
- **Cultural Identity:** *"Do the abstracted weaving linework and waterfall baffle forms genuinely capture Calbayog and Samar pride, or do you feel any elements are culturally misaligned or over-abstracted?"*
- **Overhead Utility Grid:** *"How effectively does the proposed overhead track-and-rigging system address your past challenges with lighting, staging banners, and audio cabling?"*

#### Phase 4: Ending & Member-Checking Questions (10 minutes)
- *"If you could change or refine one specific interior element, clearance dimension, or furniture feature before this framework is finalized, what would it be?"*
- *"Summary Synthesis: [The facilitator reads back the primary concerns and suggestions noted during the session]. Did I accurately capture the essence of our discussion today, or is there any important detail we missed?"*

## 8. Data Analysis Techniques for the Validation Output

To be integrated into **`35_Data Analysis.md`**:

Data collected from the post-design focus group discussion is analyzed using a convergent mixed qualitative-quantitative synthesis:

### 1. Quantitative Rubric Analysis (Descriptive Statistics)
- Individual rubric scores from the $n = 6 \text{ to } 10$ participants are encoded into Microsoft Excel.
- Weighted means ($\bar{x}$) and standard deviations ($SD$) are computed for each design dimension (Modularity, Ergonomics, Acoustics, Cultural Identity, Overhead Grid).
- **Consensus Benchmark:** An overall weighted mean score of **$\ge 3.26$ (out of 5.00)** indicates high stakeholder validation and operational acceptance. Any individual indicator scoring **$< 2.50$** triggers an automatic mandatory design refinement.

### 2. Qualitative Thematic Analysis (Braun & Clarke, 2006)
- Verbatim transcripts of the discussion are coded following the 6-phase thematic analysis framework.
- Qualitative excerpts are categorized into:
  - **Operational Validation Affirmations:** Documenting specific features confirmed to solve turnaround bottlenecks.
  - **Identified Operational Friction Points:** Documenting stakeholder-flagged clearance risks or workflow issues.
  - **Actionable Design Refinements:** Specific, non-structural modifications integrated into the final framework revision.

### 3. Problem-to-Design Refinement Traceability Matrix
The findings directly populate a Chapter 4/5 closing matrix proving how stakeholder feedback actively refined the final interior proposal:

| Stakeholder Group | Identified Friction / Critique | Proposed Operational Solution | Final Design Drawing / Spec Adjustment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *Event Planners* | Wait-staff service lane in Banquet layout felt tight near buffet tables. | Increased service corridor clearance from 1620 mm to 1800 mm. | Updated Sheet ID-103 (Banquet CADD Plan) with widened 1.8m service lanes. |
| *Venue Operations* | Tikog grass texture at lower wall levels might suffer impact from chair backs. | Integrated a durable 900 mm solid wood wainscot bumper before tikog acoustic cladding. | Modified Wall Section Detail Sheet ID-401 showing protective wainscot datum. |
| *Attendees / Public* | Requested clear visual signage from foyer into plenary hall. | Added bilingual (English/Waray) woven-pattern wayfinding panel at foyer entrance. | Incorporated entrance portal wayfinding detail on Sheet ID-202. |
