## 1. Executive Summary & Epistemological Grounding

### The Core Defense Anchor
> *"In Interior Design and Environmental Space Planning, layout configurations (aisle widths, table pitches, seat rows, egress avenues, and HVAC loads) cannot exist in an abstract vacuum without occupant numbers. Removing the occupant scale would invalidate the life-safety and ergonomic foundation of the entire thesis. The occupant numbers in our historical ledger are not arbitrary assumptions; they are secondary archival estimates derived from published municipal records and photographic seating audits, bounded by statutory building code limits, and calibrated against five live shadowed events."*

### Why We NEVER Remove the Pax Scale:
1. **Life Safety Compliance (RA 9514 & BP 344):** Under the Fire Code of the Philippines and Accessibility Law, minimum egress widths ($1200\text{ mm}$ to $1800\text{ mm}$) and wheelchair parking bays are legally dictated by total occupant load. Without pax, no layout can be certified as compliant.
2. **Turnaround Labor Modeling:** The core problem statement of this thesis focuses on operational turnaround bottlenecks. Setting up $80$ loose chairs for a small workshop takes $30\text{ minutes}$, whereas aligning $450$ Monobloc chairs for a plenary assembly or dragging heavy steel-leg tables for a $300\text{-pax}$ banquet takes $5\text{ to }12\text{ hours}$. Without occupant scales, turnaround time cannot be scientifically calculated or modeled.
3. **Acoustic Volume & Absorption:** Room acoustic calculations ($RT_{60} = \frac{0.161V}{A}$) depend on human absorption factors (a standing or seated person provides approximately $0.40\text{ to }0.46\text{ m}^2$ Sabine of sound absorption). An empty hall echoes at $2.53\text{ s}$, while a 500-pax assembly naturally dampens high frequencies. Occupancy scale is an essential physical variable.

## 4. Word-for-Word Oral Defense Scripts

### Script 1: "How did you know the number of attendees for past events if you weren't there? Isn't that just an assumption?"

> **Candidate Response:**  
> *"Thank you for raising that important methodological point, esteemed panelist. 
> 
> These numbers are not arbitrary assumptions. Because the convention center operates as a public municipal facility without electronic turnstile gates, I employed a secondary archival triangulation methodology established in architectural research (Groat & Wang, 2013; Zeisel, 2006).
> 
> First, for a large portion of our 127 documented events, official municipal press releases, LGU Public Information Office bulletins, and local news archives explicitly published participant headcounts - such as exactly 295 registered delegates for the Artists Kolektib, 96 couples and their families for Kasalang Bayan, 53 entrepreneurs for DTI training, 174 teachers for ECCD workshops, and 13 cooking teams for the Tinapa 101 Cookfest.
> 
> Second, for events without an explicit published number, I audited official high-resolution photo archives following Zeisel's (2006) physical trace observation principles, counting the actual furniture arrays - the chair rows and banquet tables set up across the 711 square meter floor plate.
> 
> Third, these numbers are strictly bounded by building code parameters: under the National Building Code (PD 1096) and Fire Code (RA 9514), the 711 square meter hall has an engineered physical capacity limit of 500 occupants for assembly with loose chairs and 250 to 300 occupants for dining tables. 
> 
> Finally, I directly calibrated these archival estimates through on-site physical shadowing of five live contemporary events in 2026, ranging from an 80-pax graduation to a 300-pax legislative assembly, corroborated by interviews with the venue coordinators. Therefore, these figures represent an empirically grounded, code-compliant baseline for our interior space planning."*

---

### Script 2: "Why didn't you just remove the pax column if you don't have turnstile ticket counts?"

> **Candidate Response:**  
> *"Thank you, esteemed panelist. In interior design, removing occupant load would make it impossible to conduct valid space planning. 
> 
> Under the National Building Code (PD 1096), Fire Code (RA 9514), and Accessibility Law (BP 344), every layout element - such as our 1500 mm radial aisles, emergency door capacities, and wheelchair parking bays - is legally governed by occupant load. 
> 
> Furthermore, the central problem statement of this thesis is turnaround time and labor efficiency. The labor required to set up 80 nesting chairs for a 50-pax workshop is completely different from deploying 450 chairs for a 500-pax convocation or hauling heavy dining tables for a 300-pax banquet. 
> 
> If we removed occupant scale, our layout templates would exist only in an abstract, unmeasurable vacuum. By maintaining standardized capacity tiers, our interior design framework provides the LGU with concrete, code-compliant operational guidance."*

---

### Script 3: "Why did you establish a separate tri-scale capacity tier for each typology instead of one generic 50-to-500 scale?"

> **Candidate Response:**  
> *"Thank you, esteemed panelist. That touches on a critical insight emphasized during our thesis consultations: **each spatial typology has fundamentally different operational density limits and scaling dynamics.** 
> 
> A single monolithic capacity bracket like '50 to 120 pax' or '400 to 500 pax' does not translate equally across typologies:
> 1. In **Plenary Convocations**, 500 occupants fill the hall with dense theater chairs ($0.65\text{ m}^2/\text{pax}$ under PD 1096), requiring radial continental sightlines and wide egress avenues. Conversely, a compact commencement of 80 to 100 occupants (like the TESDA graduation we shadowed) leaves 75% of the hall empty, creating severe acoustic reverberation ($RT_{60} > 2.50\text{ s}$).
> 2. In **Classroom Workshops**, 200 participants is the absolute ergonomic maximum for the 711 sqm floor plate because each participant requires a writing desk and chair ($1.50\text{ m}^2/\text{pax}$), while a 50-pax creative workshop requires intimate breakout pods and active lobby reception.
> 3. In **Banquet Dining**, code mandates $1.40\text{ m}^2/\text{pax}$, setting an ergonomic ceiling of 250 to 300 occupants (30 round tables). When Hermana Night attempted to pack 500 diners, emergency exit aisles were illegally blocked.
> 4. In **Exhibitions**, the metric is not chair count, but vendor booth footprints ($2000 \times 2000\text{ mm}$) and pedestrian flow volumes.
> 
> Therefore, by establishing a **Typology-Stratified Tri-Scale Architecture (Compact, Mid-Scale, Peak)** across all four typologies, we created twelve precise operational scenarios. This allows the LGU to select the exact spatial layout, acoustic zoning, and staging elevation engineered specifically for the scale of their scheduled event."*

### Script 4: "Did Zeisel or Groat & Wang write an exact 'Occupant Load Protocol' for convention centers?"

> **Candidate Response:**  
> *"No, sir/ma'am. Neither Zeisel nor Groat & Wang published a specific commercial protocol under that title. 
> 
> Rather, what I did was synthesize their established methodologies to address a real-world municipal data constraint:
> - I adapted Groat & Wang's (2013) principles of archival document research and multi-source corroboration to verify municipal event records;
> - I applied Zeisel's (2006) physical trace and photographic observation methods to visually audit furniture layouts and seating densities from photo archives; and
> - I synthesized these observational methods with statutory occupant load factors from PD 1096 and RA 9514.
> 
> It is a researcher-synthesized procedure grounded in recognized literature, rather than an off-the-shelf checklist."*
