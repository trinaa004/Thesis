# Research Design & Data Analysis In a Nutshell

**Official Working Title:**  
*An Interior Design Framework for the Calbayog City Convention Center: Harmonizing Spatial Modularity, Local Samar Heritage, and Future-Proof Event Adaptability*  
**Degree Program:** Bachelor of Science in Interior Design  
**Research Locale:** Calbayog City Convention Center (Brgy. Capoocan, Calbayog City, Samar)  
**Spatial Scope:** 840 square meters (711 sqm Main Convention Hall + 129 sqm Foyer / Pre-function Zone)  
**Structural Scope Boundary:** Strictly non-structural interior infill overlay  

---

## 1. Executive Summary: Methodology at a Glance

> **The 1-Minute Pitch:**  
> *"This study utilizes an **Applied Design Research & Multi-Phase Spatial Synthesis Framework** executed through a **Sequential Explanatory Mixed-Methods Design** ($\text{QUAN} \rightarrow \text{qual} \rightarrow \text{Spatial Synthesis}$).  
> 
> Rather than relying on guesswork, the research first quantitatively diagnoses baseline indoor environmental quality (IEQ) and setup turnaround bottlenecks from **115 to 145 respondents** (15–25 expert event planners and 100–120 public event-goers). It then qualitatively explores operational root causes and Samar cultural craft narratives through **8 to 12 in-depth key informant interviews** analyzed via Braun & Clarke's (2006) thematic analysis.  
> 
> These empirical findings directly translate into **four standardized modular layout typologies** and **cultural acoustic surface overlays**. Because this is an unbuilt undergraduate thesis, validity is scientifically established prior to construction using a **3-Pillar Pre-Occupancy Predictive Verification Framework** (clearance audits, acoustic NRC ratings, and analog facility precedent transfer)."*

## 2. The 3-Phase Sequential Workflow

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ PHASE 1: QUANTITATIVE DIAGNOSTIC BASELINE (QUAN)                                      │
│ • Instrument: Structured Likert-scale questionnaires (Survey Form)                     │
│ • Sample: N = 115 to 145 respondents                                                   │
│   ├─ Group 1: Event Planners & Venue Operators (n = 15–25, Purposive Expert Sampling)  │
│   └─ Group 2: Public Event Attendees (n = 100–120, Purposive Quota via Slovin's Formula)│
│ • Focus: Turnaround delays, layout bottlenecks, IEQ satisfaction (lighting/acoustics)   │
│ • Analysis: Descriptive statistics (frequencies, percentages, weighted means, SD)      │
└───────────────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                                            │ Explains & Expands Upon Quantitative Gaps
                                            ▼
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ PHASE 2: QUALITATIVE ROOT-CAUSE & CULTURAL ABSTRACTION (qual)                         │
│ • Instrument: Semi-structured interview guides                                         │
│ • Sample: n = 8 to 12 key informants (purposively selected from survey pool)           │
│ • Focus: Operational setup root causes, utility fatigue, Samar Basey banig weaving,    │
│   tikog grass textures, and Calbayog waterfall geometry                                │
│ • Analysis: 6-Step Thematic Analysis (Braun & Clarke, 2006) for thematic saturation    │
└───────────────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                                            │ Directly Informs Physical Design Parameters
                                            ▼
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ PHASE 3: SPATIAL SYNTHESIS & PRE-OCCUPANCY PREDICTIVE VERIFICATION                     │
│ • Physical Design Outcome: 4 Modular Layout Typologies (Plenary, Banquet, Classroom,   │
│   Exhibition) + Acoustic Wall/Ceiling Overlays + Overhead Utility Grid Track           │
│ • Pre-Occupancy Predictive Verification (3 Pillars):                                   │
│   1. Spatial & Geometric Clearance Audits (PD 1096 Sec. 1207 Table XII.1; BP 344)     │
│   2. Acoustic Material NRC Specifications (NRC ≥ 0.75 wall panels, ≥ 0.85 baffles)     │
│   3. Analog Precedent Triangulation (Baguio BCCC LGU Retrofit; Ayuba & Agah, 2018)    │

```

## 3. Sampling Matrix & Statistical Justification

| Respondent Group | Target Sample ($n$) | Sampling Technique | Inclusion & Screening Criteria | Theoretical & Methodological Justification |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Group 1: Event Planners & Venue Operators** | **$15 \text{ to } 25$** | Purposive Expert Sampling | $\ge 18$ yrs old; has organized, managed, or coordinated an event in the venue within the last 5 years. | Highly specialized operational population. In MICE facility research, expert panels of 15–25 respondents provide high operational validity without requiring unachievable sample sizes (An et al., 2021; Pembegül, 2009; Palinkas et al., 2015). |
| **Group 2: Public Event Attendees** | **$100 \text{ to } 120$** | Purposive Quota Sampling | $\ge 18$ yrs old; has attended at least one conference, social event, or expo in the venue within the last 5 years. | Calculated from a venue visitor population ($N = 500\text{--}1000+$) using **Slovin’s Formula** at a 95% Confidence Level ($e = 0.08\text{--}0.09$) and Israel (1992) standards for descriptive perception studies. |
| **Total Quantitative Sample ($N$)** | **$115 \text{ to } 145$** | Mixed Quantitative | Combined survey sample. | Ensures a dual-perspective evaluation balancing active logistical needs with passive attendee comfort. |
| **Qualitative Key Informants** | **$8 \text{ to } 12$** | Purposive Sub-sampling | Survey participants with deep operational or local cultural knowledge. | Thematic saturation literature confirms that 8 to 12 in-depth interviews uncover over 80% of core operational patterns and themes (Guest et al., 2006; Braun & Clarke, 2006). |

### Slovin's Formula Calculation
$$n = \frac{N}{1 + N(e)^2}$$
Where:
- $N = 500 \text{ to } 1,000+$ (Estimated attendee pool per major event cycle)
- $e = 0.08 \text{ to } 0.09$ (Margin of error at 95% confidence level)
- Result: **$n \approx 100 \text{ to } 120$ respondents**.

## 4. Data Analysis Techniques & Interpretation Protocols

### A. Quantitative Analysis (Descriptive Statistics)
Survey data will be encoded, cleaned, and statistically computed in Microsoft Excel using:
1. **Frequencies and Percentages:** Summarizes demographic profiles, event attendance typologies, and categorical FF&E preferences.
2. **Weighted Means ($\bar{x}$) and Standard Deviations ($SD$):** Measures the central tendency and dispersion of Likert-scale evaluations regarding IEQ satisfaction, setup turnaround friction, and modular layout needs.

#### 4-Point Likert Scale Verbal Interpretation Scale
*(Prevents central tendency bias / neutral fence-sitting)*:

| Scale / Weight | Mean Score Range | Level of Agreement | Level of Satisfaction / Performance |
| :---: | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| **4** | $3.26 - 4.00$ | Strongly Agree (SA) | Very Satisfied / Highly Efficient |
| **3** | $2.51 - 3.25$ | Agree (A) | Satisfied / Moderately Efficient |
| **2** | $1.76 - 2.50$ | Disagree (D) | Dissatisfied / Inefficient (Problem Area) |
| **1** | $1.00 - 1.75$ | Strongly Disagree (SD) | Very Dissatisfied / Severe Bottleneck |

---

### B. Qualitative Analysis (Braun & Clarke's 6-Phase Thematic Analysis)
Interview audio recordings and open-ended text are analyzed following Braun & Clarke (2006):

```
[ Phase 1: Familiarization ] ──► Verbatim transcription and active reading of all transcripts.
[ Phase 2: Initial Coding   ] ──► Generating inductive labels (e.g., "manual tape measuring", "echo").
[ Phase 3: Searching Themes ] ──► Grouping codes into broader conceptual clusters.
[ Phase 4: Reviewing Themes ] ──► Checking candidate themes against the full qualitative dataset.
[ Phase 5: Defining Themes  ] ──► Formulating precise thematic narratives and operational definitions.
[ Phase 6: Reporting & Synthesis]► Integrating qualitative excerpts into spatial design criteria.
```

### C. Triangulation & Pre-Occupancy Predictive Verification (Approach B)

Because an undergraduate thesis produces an unbuilt interior design framework rather than a physically constructed building, conducting an on-site Post-Occupancy Evaluation (POE) is physically impossible during the thesis timeframe (Preiser & Vischer, 2005). Pre-construction validity is established through **Three-Pillar Triangulation**:

| Triangulation Pillar | Methodological Execution | Regulatory / Academic Anchor | Design Impact |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pillar 1: Spatial & Clearance Audits** | Mathematically auditing proposed CADD layouts against anthropometric standards. | **PD 1096 Sec. 1207 Table XII.1; BP 344; RA 9514; Neufert (2012); Panero & Zelnik (1979)** | Validates that all 4 typologies maintain 1200 mm wheelchair paths, 1620–1800 mm service lanes, 900 mm row pitches, and $\le 45\text{ m}$ egress. |
| **Pillar 2: Acoustic NRC Material Specifications** | Establishing laboratory-certified sound absorption thresholds. | **Egan (2007); Cavanaugh et al. (2010); Chojnacki et al. (2024)** | Mandates NRC $\ge 0.75$ for *banig* wall panels and $\ge 0.85$ for waterfall ceiling baffles to predictively ensure speech clarity. |
| **Pillar 3: Analog Precedent Triangulation** | Cross-referencing operational metrics with documented municipal facility retrofits. | **Baguio BCCC Retrofit (2022); Ayuba & Agah (2018); An et al. (2021)** | Validates that modular layout manuals and cultural surface overlays successfully reduce changeover times in provincial LGU halls. |

## 5. Ethical Considerations (RA 10173 Compliance)

1. **Voluntary Participation & Right to Withdraw:** Participation is 100% voluntary; respondents may withdraw at any moment without penalty or loss of benefits.
2. **Informed Consent:** All participants sign an Informed Consent Agreement detailing the academic purpose, scope, and procedures of the research prior to participation.
3. **Anonymity & Confidentiality:** Alphanumeric coding (e.g., `EP-01`, `AG-05`) is used in all publications and presentations to prevent personal identification.
4. **Data Privacy Act Compliance (RA 10173):** All physical survey sheets and digital audio recordings are stored in encrypted, password-protected cloud drives accessible exclusively to the researcher, and will be permanently destroyed upon final institutional acceptance of the thesis.

## 6. Panel Defense "Cheat Sheet": Verbatim Speaking Scripts

### Question 1: "Why did you use a Mixed-Methods design for an interior design thesis? Why not just draw the layouts?"
> **Verbatim Response:**  
> *"Interior design is both a technical science and a human-centered art. A purely qualitative study would lack the statistical rigor needed to quantify turnaround delays and IEQ satisfaction. Conversely, purely quantitative numbers cannot explain the operational root causes of setup friction, nor can numbers capture the cultural nuances of Samar’s weaving heritage.  
> 
> By using a **Sequential Explanatory Mixed-Methods Design ($\text{QUAN} \rightarrow \text{qual}$)**, the quantitative survey establishes the exact baseline deficits, while the qualitative interviews explain why those problems occur and how local artisans translate Basey banig and Calbayog waterfalls into interior surfaces. This ensures the resulting layout manual is grounded in empirical human evidence rather than subjective aesthetic assumptions."*

---

### Question 2: "Why is your Event Planner sample size only 15 to 25? Isn't that too small statistically?"
> **Verbatim Response:**  
> *"In research methodology (Etikan et al., 2016; Palinkas et al., 2015), sampling technique must match population characteristics. Professional event planners and venue coordinators who have managed operations inside the Calbayog City Convention Center represent a **small, highly specialized expert population**, not a mass consumer demographic.  
> 
> Established literature in MICE venue research (An et al., 2021; Pembegül, 2009) confirms that expert purposive samples of 15 to 25 respondents provide deep, high-fidelity operational data without diluting validity through unqualified respondents. Meanwhile, to ensure broad statistical reliability for general public perceptions, we use **Purposive Quota Sampling for 100 to 120 event attendees**, calculated via Slovin’s formula at a 95% confidence level."*

---

### Question 3: "How can you claim your design works if you haven't done a Post-Occupancy Evaluation (POE) on the finished building?"
> **Verbatim Response:**  
> *"As established by Preiser and Vischer (2005), conducting a real-world, on-site Post-Occupancy Evaluation requires a physically constructed, occupied facility, which is beyond the physical and temporal scope of an unbuilt undergraduate thesis.  
> 
> Instead, this study establishes pre-construction validity through **Pre-Occupancy Predictive Verification across three rigorous pillars**:  
> 1. **Spatial & Clearance Audits:** Mathematically proving that every aisle, row pitch, and service corridor complies with the National Building Code (PD 1096 Sec. 1207 Table XII.1) and Accessibility Law (BP 344);  
> 2. **Acoustic Material Performance Specifications:** Mandating laboratory-certified NRC ratings ($\ge 0.75$ for wall panels, $\ge 0.85$ for ceiling baffles) to guarantee acoustic control based on established architectural physics (Egan, 2007); and  
> 3. **Analog Precedent Triangulation:** Benchmarking against verified municipal LGU retrofits, specifically the Baguio Convention Center modernization.  
> 
> Furthermore, formal on-site Post-Occupancy Evaluation is systematically structured as a primary **Chapter 5 recommendation** for the City Government of Calbayog upon physical project implementation."*
