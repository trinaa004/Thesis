### A. The Epistemological Misalignment in Building Performance Evaluation (BPE)
* **Preiser, Rabinowitz, and White (1988)** and **Preiser and Vischer (2005)** in their seminal works on *Building Performance Evaluation (BPE)* establish that the building lifecycle consists of distinct evaluative phases:
  1. *Planning / Programming (Needs Assessment)*: Users identify functional deficits and spatial needs.
  2. *Design Review (Pre-Construction)*: Evaluation is conducted against normative codes, empirical literature, and expert peer review.
  3. *Post-Occupancy Evaluation (POE)*: Occupants evaluate the space **after** it has been built and inhabited for a meaningful period.
* **The Methodological Error:** Attempting to conduct occupant satisfaction testing during the design phase (Phase 3) using 2D floor plans or 3D renderings commits an epistemological category error. Lay users cannot perceive acoustic reverberation times ($RT_{60}$), service clearance dynamics, or thermal gradients from paper drawings.

### B. The "Layperson vs. Professional" Validity Problem
* **Groat and Wang (2013)** (*Architectural Research Methods*) and **Zeisel (2006)** (*Inquiry by Design*) explain that non-designers evaluate visual representations through **subjective aesthetic preference** (*"I prefer a different color," "I don't like modern patterns"*) rather than functional, ergonomic, or statutory performance.
* Re-polling lay attendees on technical drawings introduces **uncontrolled subjective bias** that dilutes code compliance (BP 344) and empirical spatial standards (Panero & Zelnik, 1979).

### C. Evidence-Based Design (EBD) Methodology
* **Linda L. Nussbaumer (2009)** (*Evidence-Based Design for Interior Designers*) and **Hamilton and Watkins (2008)** define interior design research as a **hypothesis-driven process**:
  1. The survey and interview data gather **diagnostic evidence** (identifying spatial bottlenecks).
  2. The designer translates this evidence into spatial solutions using a **Criteria Matrix** (Karlen, 2009; Peña & Parshall, 2012).
  3. The design's internal validity is established by its **direct, auditable correspondence** to verified research and regulatory standards, not by a popular vote.

#### Part B: Detailed Evidence-Based Design (EBD) Domain Dossiers

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ DOMAIN 1: OPERATIONAL TURNAROUND DELAYS & SPATIAL RECONFIGURABILITY                              │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```
* **Diagnosed Spatial Bottleneck *(Chapter 3 Empirical Data)*:**
  * **Prolonged Setup & Teardown Turnaround:** Events require an average of 4 to 6 hours for conversion due to manual trial-and-error furniture placement.
  * **Staff Logistical Strain:** Significant physical exhaustion and spatial confusion for venue crews during back-to-back bookings *(Event Planners & Operators, $n=15–25$)*.
* **Proposed Non-Structural Interior Design Intervention:**
  * **Standardized Modular Layout Manual:** Pre-calculated, anthropometrically verified layout templates across four typologies: *Plenary, Classroom, Banquet, and Exhibition*.
  * **Visual Layout Management:** Color-coded floor registration markers embedded into floor finishes to eliminate manual re-measuring.
  * **High-Mobility FF&E:** Stackable, folding furniture paired with customized transport dollies and designated perimeter storage niches.
* **Supporting Empirical Literature *(Substantive Findings)*:**
  * **An, Kim, & Hur (2021):** Empirically prove that venue adaptability and meeting room flexibility are primary determinants of event facility competitiveness and operational efficiency.
  * **Ike et al. (2023):** Document that pre-engineered modular furniture clusters and flexible partitions minimize spatial downtime and operational changeover labor.
* **Normative Codes & Technical Standards:**
  * **Tompkins, White, Bozer, & Tanchoco (2010) (*Facilities Planning*, 4th ed.):** Lean layout principles, systematic layout planning (SLP), and visual management markers.
  * **Neufert (2012) & Panero & Zelnik (1979):** Dynamic service lane clearances (1620–1800 mm between banquet tables) and circulation aisle buffers (400–450 mm).

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ DOMAIN 2: SEVERE ACOUSTIC DISTORTION, ECHOES & SPEECH INTELLIGIBILITY                            │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```
* **Diagnosed Spatial Bottleneck *(Chapter 3 Empirical Data)*:**
  * **Severe Flutter Echoes & Reverberation:** Hard, reflective parallel walls cause harsh sound reflections and uncontrolled reverberance.
  * **Poor Speech Intelligibility:** Attendees and presenters report distorted audio and vocal fatigue during plenary assemblies *(Event-Goers & Planners, $N=115–145$)*.
* **Proposed Non-Structural Interior Design Intervention:**
  * **Suspended Curved Ceiling Baffles:** Overhead sound-absorbing baffles arranged in a fluid waterfall motif to disrupt specular flutter echoes between ceiling and floor.
  * **Acoustic Wall Paneling Envelope:** Fabric-wrapped porous wall panels integrating natural *tikog* reed textures with high-density acoustic cores.
* **Supporting Empirical Literature *(Substantive Findings)*:**
  * **Cairoli (2020):** Demonstrates that curved ceiling baffles and targeted wall absorption eliminate flutter echoes and stabilize $RT_{60}$ within optimal speech ranges in multipurpose halls.
  * **Chojnacki et al. (2023, 2024):** Prove that sustainable porous plant-fiber and wood composite panels provide high sound absorption ($\alpha \ge 0.70–0.85$) at speech frequencies (500–2000 Hz).
* **Normative Codes & Acoustic Performance Benchmarks (Approach B — Material NRC Specification):**
  * **Egan (2007) & Cavanaugh et al. (2010):** Establish that a 500-seat civic assembly hall requires controlled reverberation ($RT_{60} = 0.8–1.1\text{ s}$) at speech frequencies (500–2000 Hz) to ensure high speech intelligibility ($STI \ge 0.60$).
  * **Evidence-Based Material NRC Thresholds:** Rather than conducting complex in-situ sound wave engineering on an unbuilt proposal, the interior design framework establishes mandatory laboratory-certified Noise Reduction Coefficient (NRC) benchmarks (ASTM C423):
    * **Suspended Waterfall Baffles:** High-density porous core with fire-retardant fabric wrap achieving **NRC ≥ 0.85** to disrupt specular flutter echoes between ceiling and floor.
    * **Tikog Composite Wall Panels:** Perforated acoustic core faced with natural Basey *tikog* reed weave achieving **NRC ≥ 0.75** (Chojnacki et al., 2023) to eliminate lateral wall flutter echoes.
    * **Upholstered Seating Clusters:** High-density acoustic foam seating providing consistent absorption (~0.30 metric sabins/seat) across fluctuating crowd capacities.

### Strategy 2: Defining the Official Expert Validators (The Professional Panel)

In professional licensure fields (Interior Design, Architecture), the validity of a design intervention before physical construction is established through **Expert Peer Review / Panel Examination** (Groat & Wang, 2013; Zeisel, 2006):

1. **The Survey Respondents ($N=115–145$)** act as the **Diagnostic Baseline**: They provide the *problem definition* (SPOP 1).
2. **The Empirical Literature & Normative Standards** provide the **Design Rules**: They govern the *formulation of solutions* (SPOP 2 & 3).
3. **The Defense Panel of Examiners (Licensed Interior Designers / Registered Architects)** serves as the **Official Technical Validation Body**: They evaluate whether the proposed design framework accurately synthesizes the empirical evidence, complies with building codes (BP 344, National Building Code), and achieves professional spatial viability.

### Strategy 3: Defining the Undergraduate Research Boundary (Diagnostic Intake vs. Future POE)

To safeguard the feasibility and academic timeline of a Bachelor of Science in Interior Design (BSID) thesis, the methodological boundaries must be explicitly articulated:

* **The Diagnostic Role of Venue Operators in Chapter 3:**
  The Venue Administrator and event planners serve as **Key Informants during Phase 1 (Empirical Diagnosis)**. Their operational bottlenecks, setup labor constraints, and rigging hazards are fully documented in the initial baseline data collection (SPOP 1). 
* **Why Post-Design External Member-Checking is Inappropriate:**
  Requiring a secondary, post-design evaluation from the Venue Administrator after the drawings are rendered creates an unnecessary, non-standard research cycle that delays graduation. Just like public attendees, administrative personnel cannot legally or technically validate building code compliance, fire egress widths, structural rigging loads, or acoustic Sabine reverberation calculations.
* **The Post-Occupancy Evaluation (POE) Distinction:**
  In accordance with **Preiser and Vischer (2005)**, empirical evaluation of occupant satisfaction with a physical environment can only take place during **Phase 5 (Post-Occupancy)**. Because an undergraduate thesis produces a design *proposal* and non-structural masterplan rather than a constructed facility, POE is formally designated under **Chapter 5: Recommendations for Future Research / Institutional Implementation**. This assigns longitudinal performance evaluation to the City Government of Calbayog once constructed, requiring **zero post-design fieldwork or data gathering by the undergraduate researcher**.
