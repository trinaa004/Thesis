## 2. Theoretical Foundations

### A. Open Building Theory (Habraken, 1972)
* **Core Premise**: Separates the building into distinct levels of permanence:
  * **Support (Fixed Base Building)**: Immovable structural shell, columns, perimeter walls, and roof structure.
  * **Infill (Fit-out / Interior Overlay)**: Kinetic, adaptable interior, modular FF&E, suspended track systems, and acoustic drapery.
* **Application to Calbayog**: The existing 840 sqm interior envelope (711 sqm Main Hall and 129 sqm Foyer) remains structurally untouched (preserving municipal capital), while the interior infill is treated as a flexible overlay that can be rearranged in under 60–90 minutes.

### B. Theory of Place Attachment (Scannell & Gifford, 2010)
* **Core Premise**: Explains human bonding to physical environments through the **Person-Process-Place (PPP)** Tripartite Model:
  * **Person**: Individual memories and collective Waray/Samarnon cultural identity.
  * **Process**: Affective, cognitive, and behavioral mechanisms that build emotional attachment and spatial memory.
  * **Place**: Physical interior characteristics (materials, geometries, acoustic intimacy, lighting) that embody local meaning.
* **Application to Calbayog**: Transforms an anonymous "box" into a culturally expressive civic landmark that strengthens community belonging.

## 3. Spatial Modularity, Flexibility, and Future-Proofing

* **The Modularity Continuum**:
  * **Modularity**: The segmented physical architecture that localizes changes (*Hashemian, 2005*).
  * **Flexibility**: The immediate, day-to-day reconfiguration of spaces for differing event typologies (*Ike et al., 2023; Barr, 2023*).
  * **Adaptability / Future-Proofing**: Long-term resilience to shifting technological standards, acoustic needs, and demographic changes without structural renovations (*DesignHorizons, 2024; Dong et al., 2024*).
* **Acoustic Adaptation**: Utilizing variable passive acoustic systems—such as hybrid absorptive-diffusive wooden panels (*Chojnacki et al., 2023, 2024*) and movable curtains (*Cairoli, 2020; Habibi, 2025*)—to adjust reverberation time between speech-heavy assemblies and musical/cultural performances.

## 5. Ceiling Infrastructure & Overhead Rigging

* **Freeing the Floor Plane**: Traditional staging clutters the floor with wiring and speaker stands. Suspending dynamic lighting, acoustic drapes, and projection screens from overhead modular tracks frees the ground plane for fluid pedestrian circulation (*Phantos, 2025; AV Alliance, 2024*).
* **Aesthetic & MEP Concealment**: Overhead baffles conceal mechanical/electrical ducts while functioning as sculpted acoustic elements (*Syma, 2023; Cat, 2023*).

## 6. Cultural Precedents & Samar Heritage Translation

### A. Precedent Hierarchy
1. **National Landmark Benchmark**: *Philippine International Convention Center (PICC)* (Leandro Locsin) — Demonstrates how civic architecture acts as a national cultural monument (*Fulgar, 2024*).
2. **International Placemaking Precedents**: 
   * *Albuquerque Convention Center (New Mexico)* — Living museum featuring indigenous art and multicultural programming (*ASM Global, 2024*).
   * *Coex Convention Centre (Heatherwick Studio, Seoul)* — Public "Cabinet of Curiosities" connecting interior event zones to urban landscape (*Fakharany, 2025*).
3. **Provincial Philippine LGU Direct Benchmark**:
   * ***Baguio Convention & Cultural Center (BCCC) Retrofit*** — A municipal 1977 concrete hall upgraded via non-structural acoustic paneling, Cordilleran *halipan* motifs, and modular seating without costly structural demolition (*DPWH, 2021; City of Baguio, 2019*).

### B. Regional Samar Cultural Vocabulary
* **Basey *Banig* Weaving (*Tikog* Grass / *Fimbristylis umbellaris*)**: Abstracting traditional Samat and Lara geometric weave lines into acoustic wall panels and textile screens (*Del Monte et al., 2022; Garado et al., 2026; Wagas, 2016*).
* **Calbayog "City of Waterfalls" Topography**: Translating cascading waterfall fluid mechanics (Tarangban, Bangon, Mapaso) into organic acoustic ceiling baffles and lighting ribbons.

There is an empirical deficit in non-structural interior design frameworks, pre-calculated layout manuals, and cultural abstraction protocols specifically tailored for mid-scale (840 sqm interior envelope: 711 sqm Main Hall + 129 sqm Foyer) provincial municipal LGU venues operating under fiscal constraints.**
