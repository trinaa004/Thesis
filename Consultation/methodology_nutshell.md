## 1. Methodological Framework Architecture

This study adopts an **Applied Design Research & Multi-Phase Spatial Synthesis Framework** (Archer, 1995; Groat & Wang, 2013) executed through a **Sequential Explanatory Mixed-Methods Design** (Creswell & Creswell, 2018). It systematically transitions from empirical field diagnosis to design synthesis and multi-tier validation.

## 2. Four Operational Research Phases

| Research Phase | Focus & Methodological Scope | Primary Output / Deliverable |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Phase 1: Quantitative Ergonomic & Dimensional Modeling** | • Venue & attendee structured perception surveys ($N = 115–145$).<br>• Mathematical mapping of the 840 sqm Main Hall envelope.<br>• Calculation of static/dynamic anthropometric clearances.<br>• BP 344 & ADA universal accessibility clearance integration. | Anthropometrically validated dimensional envelope, seating pitch standards, and corridor clearance rulesets. |
| **Phase 2: Qualitative Cultural Abstraction & Pattern Language** | • Documentation of Basey *banig* (Samat/Lara weaves) and *tikog* grass (*Fimbristylis umbellaris*).<br>• Abstraction of Calbayog waterfall geometries.<br>• Computational surface pattern translation (*Wagas, 2016; Del Monte, 2021*). | Replicable Cultural Pattern Matrix for laser-cut acoustic wall panels, drapery, and ambient ceiling baffles. |
| **Phase 3: Spatial Synthesis & Framework Formulation** | • Integration of Phase 1 dimensional rules and Phase 2 cultural overlays.<br>• Design of kinetic overhead ceiling tracks and rigging interfaces.<br>• Formulation of standardized modular FF&E layouts and zoning. | Comprehensive Interior Design Framework & Practical Layout Standard Manual. |
| **Phase 4: Framework Validation & Simulation Verification** | • **Tier 1**: CAD 2D/3D accessibility & sightline simulation (BP 344 Pass/Fail compliance).<br>• **Tier 2**: Time-and-motion turnaround comparative workflow modeling. | Empirically and computationally validated operational interior design framework. |

## 3. Research Environment & Locale Context

* **Venue**: Calbayog City Convention Center (Main Convention Hall)
* **Location**: Barangay Capoocan, Calbayog City, Samar, Philippines
* **Spatial Footprint**: **840 square meters**
* **Maximum Occupancy**: Up to 500 occupants
* **Historical Context**: Originally established as a municipal bus terminal, renovated on December 14, 2009 into a convention center.
* **Current Operational Challenges**: Rigid interior envelope, long setup/teardown durations, acoustic reverberation/flutter echoes, and absence of localized cultural identity.

## 4. Respondents, Population & Sampling Matrix

### A. Inclusion Criteria
All participants must:
1. Be at least 18 years old.
2. Have organized, operated, or attended at least one event in the Main Hall within the last 5 years.
3. Possess familiarity with the venue or Samar’s regional cultural context.

### B. Sampling Matrix Breakdown

| Stakeholder Group | Target Sample Size ($n$) | Sampling Technique | Rationale & Methodological Source | Focus of Inquiry |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Group 1: Event Planners, Coordinators & Venue Operators** | **$15–25$ respondents** | **Purposive Expert Sampling** | Highly specialized, small local population; expert knowledge required (*An et al., 2021; Pembegül, 2009; Etikan et al., 2016*). | Turnaround bottlenecks, FF&E reconfigurability, loading staging, overhead rigging needs. |
| **Group 2: General Public & Event Attendees** | **$100–120$ respondents** | **Purposive Quota Sampling** | Slovin’s formula ($N = 500–1000+$, 95% Conf., $e = 0.08–0.09$) ensuring cross-sectional representation (*Cochran, 1977; Israel, 1992*). | Spatial comfort, seating pitch, sightlines, acoustics, wayfinding, cultural ambiance. |
| **Qualitative Key Informants** | **$8–12$ participants** | **Purposive Sub-sampling** | Optimal sample size for thematic saturation in operational workflows (*Braun & Clarke, 2006; Guest et al., 2006*). | In-depth setup pain points, lighting control preferences, cultural identity narratives. |
| **TOTAL SAMPLE ($N$)** | **$115–145$ respondents** | **Mixed Sampling Strategy** | Ensures high statistical validity and qualitative operational depth. | Comprehensive operational & experiential baseline. |

## 6. Data Collection Protocols & Ethical Safeguards (RA 10173)

Data collection is executed across **six sequential phases**:

1. **Phase 1: Institutional Approvals & Site Access**: USC Research Ethics Committee (USC-REC) clearance, formal transmittal letters to Calbayog LGU and convention center administration.
2. **Phase 2: Pilot Testing ($n = 20$)**: 10 event planners and 10 attendees pre-test instruments for readability and Cronbach's alpha internal consistency (pilot participants excluded from final sample).
3. **Phase 3: Quantitative Survey Administration**: Administered via printed forms and secure digital forms (Google Forms) with bilingual (English/Waray) support.
4. **Phase 4: Qualitative Interviews**: 20–30 minute semi-structured interviews conducted on-site or via secure teleconferencing; recorded and transcribed verbatim.
5. **Phase 5: Spatial Observation & Mapping**: Non-participatory field audits during live event setups (observing turnaround friction) and vacant periods (dimensional mapping).
6. **Phase 6: Data Privacy, Security & Disposal (RA 10173 Compliance)**:
   * **Pseudonymization**: Alphanumeric coding (e.g., `Respondent P-001`, `Key Informant K-01`).
   * **Storage & Encryption**: AES-256 bit encryption, password-protected hardware, 2FA cloud backup; unencrypted flash drives strictly prohibited.
   * **Retention & Destruction**: Retained for exactly **1 year post-defense**, followed by irreversible cross-cut paper shredding and permanent digital file purging.

## 7. Data Analysis Plan

### A. Quantitative Statistical Analysis
* **Software**: Microsoft Excel.
* **Descriptive Statistics**:
  * *Frequencies and Percentages*: Participant demographics, event frequency, layout preferences.
  * *Weighted Means ($\bar{x}$) and Standard Deviations ($SD$)*: Likert evaluations of spatial bottlenecks, acoustic clarity, FF&E reconfigurability, and cultural resonance.

### B. Qualitative Thematic Analysis (Braun & Clarke, 2006)
Analyzed through a rigorous **6-step thematic protocol**:
1. *Familiarization with Data* (Verbatim transcription and repeated reading)
2. *Generating Initial Codes* (Systematic coding of operational friction and spatial needs)
3. *Searching for Themes* (Clustering codes into overarching operational/cultural themes)
4. *Reviewing Themes* (Cross-checking themes against the full qualitative dataset)
5. *Defining and Naming Themes* (Formulating clear thematic definitions)
6. *Producing the Narrative Report* (Integrating thematic insights with data excerpts)

### C. Triangulation & Phase 4 Validation Metrics
* **Triangulation**: Cross-referencing survey statistical means, qualitative themes, on-site physical measurements, and ergonomic standards (Panero & Zelnik; Neufert; BP 344).
* **Validation Protocols**:
  * *Spatial Compliance Matrix*: Binary Pass/Fail audit for continuous 1200 mm wheelchair aisles and 1500 mm turning radiuses in CAD models.
  * *Time-and-Motion Modeling*: Mathematical comparison of baseline setup hours vs. standardized modular manual workflows to quantify turnaround reduction.
