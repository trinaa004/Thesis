### 1. Academic Track Differentiation: Why Your Adviser Said This
In university design departments (BSID / BS Architecture), undergraduate theses are divided into two distinct academic traditions:

| Evaluation Dimension | Project-Based Track ("Design Intervention") | Applied Experimentation Track (YOUR THESIS) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **The Primary "Juice"** | Creative, decorative styling, elaborate physical detailing, high-concept aesthetics, render showcase. | **The Empirical Analysis:** Baseline diagnostics, spatial clearance metrics, acoustic calculations, and data results. |
| **Role of Design Drawings** | The final end-product and chief judging criterion. | The **spatial testbed / proof-of-concept** demonstrating how data solves physical bottlenecks. |
| **Deliverable Weight** | 80% Creative Execution / 20% Research Documentation. | **60% Field Data, Spatial Analysis & Methodology / 40% Evidence-Based Spatial Layouts & Recommendations.** |
| **Panel Expectation if Titled "Intervention"** | Panel expects an exhaustive, bespoke creative showcase. | N/A (Using "Intervention" risks misclassification into the Project-Based track). |

# Chapter 1: Research Framework

## Statement of the Problem
This study aims to formulate a site-specific interior design framework for the Calbayog City Convention Center that integrates spatial adaptability and Samar heritage integration for flexible event environments, situated at Inay-la St., Brgy. Capoocan, Calbayog City, Samar.

Specifically, the study addresses the following research questions:

1. What is the current baseline condition of the Calbayog City Convention Center's Main Hall and Lobby in terms of indoor environmental quality (IEQ) and spatial operational efficiency?
   1.1. What is the level of attendee satisfaction regarding existing interior lighting, acoustics, and thermal comfort?
   1.2. What setup turnaround delays and spatial layout bottlenecks do event planners and venue operators encounter within the 840 sqm interior envelope?
2. How can modular interior zoning, reconfigurable loose FF&E, and standardized layout typologies (Plenary, Classroom, Banquet, Exhibition) be formulated to maximize spatial capacity, configuration speed, and universal accessibility?
3. How can the interior environment of the Calbayog City Convention Center be designed to balance long-term spatial adaptability with localized cultural identity?
   3.1. What interior material specifications, acoustic overlays, and overhead ceiling track systems can be proposed to support multi-scale event versatility?
   3.2. In what ways can localized Samar heritage (Basey *banig* weaving geometry, *tikog* grass textures) and Calbayog natural motifs (waterfall fluid geometries) be integrated into interior surface treatments to cultivate a distinct sense of place without compromising event neutrality?

---

## Research Objectives
This study aims to develop a site-specific interior design framework consisting of modular layout typologies and evidence-based spatial design recommendations that adapt to the diverse event demands of the Calbayog City Convention Center, integrating spatial adaptability and culturally-responsive interior treatments to enhance spatial efficiency, acoustic comfort, and user experience for both event planners and venue attendees. Specifically, it seeks:

1. To evaluate the baseline indoor environmental quality (IEQ) and spatial operational conditions of the Calbayog City Convention Center's Main Hall and Lobby.
   1.1. To measure attendee satisfaction with existing interior lighting, acoustic performance, and thermal comfort.
   1.2. To identify physical layout bottlenecks and setup turnaround delays experienced by event planners and venue operators during live event operations.
2. To formulate standardized modular layout typologies (Plenary, Classroom, Banquet, Exhibition) and reconfigurable FF&E arrangements that optimize spatial capacity, accelerate turnaround speed, and ensure universal accessibility compliance.
3. To establish an interior design framework that balances operational adaptability with local civic identity.
   3.1. To propose interior material specifications, acoustic wall and ceiling assemblies, and a non-structural overhead ceiling track-and-rigging interface to support multi-scale event versatility.
   3.2. To synthesize culturally-responsive interior surface treatments—abstracting Basey *banig* weaving patterns, *tikog* grass textures, and Calbayog waterfall fluid forms—to establish localized place attachment without hindering functional flexibility.

---

## Significance of the Study
The results of this study will benefit the following stakeholders:

* **Event Planners and Venue Operators:** This study provides a practical spatial reference by offering pre-calculated modular layout templates and ergonomic clearance standards. This directly minimizes setup and teardown turnaround times, eliminates manual trial-and-error space planning, and streamlines operational logistics within the hall.
* **Event-Goers and Visitors:** This study enhances the interior user experience through optimized circulation zoning, intuitive egress paths, and acoustically controlled surfaces. Furthermore, integrating authentic Samar cultural motifs provides a sensory connection to regional heritage and a dignified civic atmosphere.
* **The Local Government Unit (LGU) of Calbayog City:** By establishing an evidence-based interior modernization proposal, the municipality is provided with a functional, high-performance civic venue capable of hosting diverse institutional, educational, and commercial MICE gatherings, maximizing the utility of existing public infrastructure without costly structural expansion.
* **The Local Community of Calbayog City:** The study reinforces the convention center as an accessible, culturally grounded public asset that honors local weaving traditions and natural landmarks, fostering community pride and supporting regional tourism.
* **Interior Design Professionals and Future Researchers:** This study contributes to the body of knowledge on applied interior design experimentation within adaptive-reuse civic facilities, providing a replicable model for balancing strict statutory compliance (BP 344, PD 1096, RA 9514), operational flexibility, and vernacular cultural abstraction.

## Scope and Delimitations of the Study

### Scope of the Study
The scope of this study is strictly confined to the formulation of a non-structural interior design framework and evidence-based spatial layout recommendations for the primary interior envelope (Main Convention Hall and Lobby) of the Calbayog City Convention Center, located at Inay-la St., Brgy. Capoocan, Calbayog City, Samar. Specifically, the study includes:

* Designing a standardized menu of four repeatable interior layout templates (Plenary, Classroom, Banquet, and Exhibition configurations) optimized for the specific dimensions and occupant capacity of the venue.
* Formulating ergonomic furniture density arrangements and loose FF&E layout clusters to maximize floor plate utility and minimize setup/teardown turnaround times for utility staff.
* Establishing evidence-based spatial layout recommendations and clearance matrices in Chapter 5—incorporating statutory clearance standards (Batas Pambansa Bilang 344, Presidential Decree 1096, and Republic Act 9514)—to eliminate guesswork in furniture spacing and aisle clearances.
* Integrating localized Samar heritage elements—specifically abstracting Basey *banig* geometric weaves, natural *tikog* grass textures, and Calbayog waterfall fluid mechanics—into perimeter acoustic wall claddings and suspended ceiling baffles to mitigate flutter echoes while cultivating regional identity.
* Proposing a non-structural overhead ceiling interface combining acoustic finish panels with an adjustable utility track-and-rigging grid to accommodate dynamic changes in ambient lighting, audiovisual equipment, and temporary decorative drapery.

The physical application of this framework is strictly limited to the **840-square-meter interior envelope** of the facility, comprising the **711-square-meter Main Convention Hall** and the **129-square-meter Lobby / pre-function area**.

### Delimitations of the Study
To protect the methodological integrity and professional boundaries of an undergraduate interior design thesis, the study establishes the following explicit delimitations:

* **Non-Structural Scope:** This study is strictly a non-structural interior overlay. It completely excludes any alterations to the primary building shell, exterior facades, load-bearing columns, perimeter foundation footings, and structural roof trusses. No structural engineering revisions or civil demolition works are proposed.
* **Engineering Boundaries:** While the interior framework coordinates spatial clearances for lighting, ventilation, and sound absorption, the study does not provide detailed engineering schematics, circuit load calculations, or mechanical ducting rerouting for Mechanical, Electrical, and Plumbing (MEP) systems, fire sprinkler networks, or commercial theatrical rigging hardware. These systems are addressed strictly as non-structural interior finish interfaces.
* **Spatial Boundaries:** The investigation is strictly confined to the interior Main Hall and Lobby. All outdoor grounds, exterior facade renovations, landscaping, vehicular parking networks, and back-of-house service zones (administrative offices, commercial catering kitchens, public restrooms, and outdoor loading bays) fall outside the scope of this study.
* **Exclusion of Administrative Policy Guidebooks:** The study formulates interior spatial layout guidelines and clearance recommendations within Chapter 5. It explicitly excludes the publication of a separate municipal administrative policy manual, commercial leasing regulations, venue fee schedules, municipal budgetary appropriations, or facility human resource management policies, avoiding external administrative validation liabilities.
* **Pre-Occupancy Predictive Verification:** Because this thesis produces an applied, unbuilt interior design framework rather than a physically constructed renovation, conducting a longitudinal on-site Post-Occupancy Evaluation (POE) is outside the timeframe of this study. Design feasibility and regulatory compliance are instead established through evidence-based pre-occupancy predictive methods: geometric CADD clearance stress-testing under BP 344, acoustic Sabine reverberation calculations using published material NRC values, and a structured post-design evaluative Focus Group Discussion (FGD) with representative venue stakeholders.
