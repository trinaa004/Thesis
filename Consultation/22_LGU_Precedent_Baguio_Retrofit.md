# Philippine LGU Facility Retrofit Precedent: The Baguio Convention and Cultural Center (BCCC)

**Document Type:** Review of Related Literature (RRL) — Local Civic Precedent & Methodological Justification  
**Applicable Thesis Sections:** Chapter 2 (Review of Related Literature — Cultural Identity & Adaptable Civic Interiors) & Chapter 1 (Rationale / Background of the Study)  
**Target Locale Comparison:** Calbayog City Convention Center (Brgy. Capoocan, Calbayog City, Samar — 840 sqm Main Hall)

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Comparative Overview

A critical critique in the evaluation of civic interior frameworks is the reliance on massive, nationally funded flagship venues (e.g., the Philippine International Convention Center [PICC] in Pasay City, Seoul's Coex Center, or the Albuquerque Convention Center) as design benchmarks. While such mega-venues illustrate large-scale spatial zoning and national identity, they do not reflect the fiscal, structural, and operational constraints faced by provincial Local Government Units (LGUs).

The **Baguio Convention and Cultural Center (BCCC)** serves as a directly comparable, empirically grounded Philippine provincial LGU facility retrofit precedent. Managed and rehabilitated under the City Government of Baguio in collaboration with the Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) and a multi-disciplinary local Technical Working Group (TWG), the BCCC underwent a comprehensive multi-million-peso rehabilitation and interior modernization program. This project demonstrates how an existing, aging municipal civic envelope can be transformed into a high-performance, future-proof MICE and cultural venue through non-structural interior reconfiguration, acoustic remediation, and regional cultural integration.

## 2. Background and Context of the Baguio Precedent

The Baguio Convention Center was constructed in 1977 to serve as the premier assembly venue for Northern Luzon and the site of the 1978 World Chess Championship. Over four decades of continuous municipal use, however, the facility developed severe operational bottlenecks common to aging Philippine civic interiors:
1. **Acoustic Deterioration and Flutter Echoes**: Excessive reverberation times and lack of specialized acoustic wall/ceiling treatments hampered speech intelligibility during lectures and conferences.
2. **Spatial Inflexibility**: Fixed, monolithic seating and rigid perimeter partitions prevented rapid reconfiguration between plenary conferences, trade exhibitions, and community social events.
3. **Technological and Lighting Deficits**: Outdated audiovisual infrastructure, high-glare ambient lighting, and inadequate cable/rigging management created visual and physical clutter across the hall floor.
4. **Diluted Sense of Place**: Interior finishes degraded into generic painted surfaces that failed to reflect Baguio's status as a UNESCO Creative City for Crafts and Folk Arts.

To resolve these deficits without undertaking cost-prohibitive structural demolition, the Baguio City Government under Mayor Benjamin Magalong and the City Buildings and Architecture Office (CBAO), supported by a ₱180-million national and local allocation, executed a phased rehabilitation and interior retrofitting master plan (City Government of Baguio, 2019; DPWH, 2021; Filipina News Sentinel, 2022).

## 3. Core Interior Retrofit Interventions & Technical Solutions

The BCCC rehabilitation offers four key design strategies directly translatable to the interior design framework for the Calbayog City Convention Center:

### A. Non-Structural Interior Envelope & Acoustic Remediation
Rather than altering load-bearing pillars or the external roof geometry, the BCCC retrofit focused on a high-performance **interior wall and ceiling overlay**. 
- Perforated acoustic wall paneling, modular fabric-wrapped acoustic baffles, and tuned resonant absorbers were installed to optimize reverberation time ($RT_{60}$) for both speech intelligibility (conferences) and musical resonance (cultural events).
- This mirrors the proposed Calbayog intervention of deploying *tikog*-surfaced acoustic perimeter panels and suspended fluid ceiling baffles to control acoustics in the 840 sqm hall without structural wall modifications.

### B. Spatial Zoning, Modularity, and Multi-Use Flexibility
The BCCC interior was segmented into adaptable zones capable of simultaneous or rapid sequential use:
- The main auditorium floor was upgraded with flexible, high-density movable seating systems, allowing transitions from formal plenary theater seating to open floor exhibition grids and banquet layouts.
- Peripheral foyer and mezzanine areas were repurposed into modular creative art galleries, civic function rooms, and the city's integrated SMART Command Center.
- This demonstrates the viability of **Open Building Theory (Habraken, 1972)** in local governance: the municipal building serves as the permanent "support," while modular FF&E, mobile track partitions, and demountable staging act as the kinetic "infill" that accommodates dynamic civic needs.

### C. Integration of Regional Cultural Identity & Creative Placemaking
As a designated UNESCO Creative City for Crafts and Folk Arts, Baguio embedded indigenous Cordilleran heritage into the venue's architectural identity and interior programming:
- **Architectural Motifs & Portals**: Preserved the building's iconic Ifugao *bale* (native house) pyramidal silhouette and restored the four monumental entrance columns featuring the traditional *halipan* (cylindrical wooden rat guard) motif, rooting the venue in authentic Cordilleran building traditions.
- **Murals & Creative Arts Exhibition Hub**: Modernized the lobby with a minimalist neutral backdrop that highlights large Cordilleran cultural murals and dedicated permanent gallery spaces for local artisans and indigenous crafts, reinforcing civic identity and place attachment (Scannell & Gifford, 2010).
- **Justification for Calbayog's Weaving Abstraction**: While Baguio integrates cultural identity primarily through monumental architectural motifs (*halipan* / *bale*) and curated gallery installations, the Calbayog thesis advances this concept by translating Samar’s living craft heritage (Basey *banig* weaving and *tikog* grass textures) into a **computational, non-structural interior surface matrix** across modular acoustic wall panels and ceiling drapes.

### D. Universal Accessibility and Life Safety Upgrades
The BCCC modernized its circulation corridors in strict accordance with Batas Pambansa Bilang 344 (Accessibility Law) and fire safety codes:
- Incorporation of dedicated wheelchair seating stalls with clear sightline offsets ($C \ge 60\text{ mm}$), step-free access ramps (1:12 gradient), tactile ground indicators, and expanded 1500 mm turning radiuses in restrooms and vestibules.
- Installation of concealed overhead cable trays, motorized rigging lines, and glare-free dimmable LED architectural lighting to clear floor pathways and reduce setup/teardown turnaround hazards.

## 4. Methodological Value and Justification for the Calbayog Thesis

Integrating the Baguio Convention and Cultural Center retrofit into the thesis literature review strengthens the study's justification in four profound ways:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│               METHODOLOGICAL JUSTIFICATION & PRECEDENT BRIDGE               │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
                                       │
        ┌──────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────┐
        ▼                                                             ▼
[ MACRO BENCHMARKS ]                                          [ MICRO PRECEDENT ]
PICC / Coex / Albuquerque                                     Baguio Convention Center (BCCC)
  • National/Global Scale                                       • Municipal / Provincial LGU Scale
  • Billion-Peso Budgets                                        • LGU Capital Outlay & Phased Budgets
  • Permanent Structural Mandates                               • Non-Structural Adaptive Retrofit
        │                                                             │
        └──────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────┘
                                       ▼
                     [ CALBAYOG CITY CONVENTION CENTER ]
                        840 sqm Main Hall (Samar LGU)
          • Feasible, Non-Structural Interior Design Overlay
          • Rapid Turnaround via Modular FF&E & Overhead Rigging
          • Basey Banig / Tikog Weaving & Waterfall Cultural Identity
          • Empirically Grounded in Philippine Municipal Realities
```

1. **Bridges the Scale and Resource Gap**: It counters panel skepticism regarding whether municipal facilities can successfully adopt advanced MICE interior standards. The BCCC proves that an LGU can modernize an existing public hall without full structural reconstruction.
2. **Validates the "Non-Structural Overlay" Scope**: It confirms that interior designers, working within the interior envelope (FF&E, acoustic paneling, lighting, finishes, and mobile partitions), can resolve primary operational bottlenecks (turnaround speed, acoustic clarity, poor wayfinding) without encroaching on civil/structural engineering domains.
3. **Supports Open Building & Place Attachment Triangulation**: It provides tangible Philippine proof of the thesis's dual theoretical framework: Habraken's (1972) kinetic infill achieves functional longevity, while Scannell and Gifford's (2010) Person-Process-Place model is activated when regional architectural motifs and local creative heritage are celebrated in public civic interiors.
4. **Legitimizes LGU Adaptive Reuse**: Since the Calbayog venue originated as a converted municipal bus station (2009), citing a specialized municipal civic retrofit proves that continuous interior adaptation is an established, economically responsible paradigm for Philippine LGUs.
