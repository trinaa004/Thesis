# CATEGORY 5: METHODOLOGY, SAMPLING DEFESIBILITY, & INSTRUMENTS

### Q11. "Why do you have two separate quantitative respondent groups with different sample sizes ($n = 15–25$ for Planners vs. $n = 100–120$ for Attendees)?"
* **Underlying Academic Concern**: The adviser wants methodological justification for dual-stakeholder sampling and proof that the sample sizes are scientifically defensible.
* **Recommended Verbal Response / Defense Script**:
  > *"Convention centers have two fundamentally distinct user populations that require tailored sampling strategies:
  > 1. Event Planners and Venue Operators represent a small, highly specialized expert population in Calbayog City. In MICE facility research, operational workflows are evaluated through Purposive Expert Sampling of 15 to 25 domain experts (An, Kim, & Hur, 2021; Pembegül, 2009; Etikan et al., 2016). Attempting a mass sample for a small expert population would compromise data validity.
  > 2. General Event Attendees represent a large public user population ($N = 500 \text{ to } 1,000+$). Applying Slovin's Formula ($n = N / [1 + N(e)^2]$) with a 95% confidence level and an acceptable margin of error ($e = 0.08–0.09$) establishes a statistically reliable sample of 100 to 120 respondents (Cochran, 1977; Israel, 1992; Roa & Lagamon, 2018).
  > Combining both gives a total quantitative sample of $N = 115 \text{ to } 145$, which is methodologically robust for an undergraduate interior design thesis."*
* **Key Evidence & Literature to Cite**:
  * An, J., Kim, H., & Hur, D. (2021); Pembegül, M. (2009).
  * Cochran, W. G. (1977). *Sampling Techniques*; Israel, G. D. (1992). *Determining Sample Size*.
