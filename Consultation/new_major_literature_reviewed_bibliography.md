## 1. Executive Context & Purpose

This compendium catalogs, categorizes, and provides concise operational annotations for all **new Major Literature Reviewed (Primary and Secondary Sources)** incorporated into the thesis study over the past two weeks. 

In academic and professional interior design research:
- **Primary Sources** represent authoritative legal statutes, national building codes, official government administrative orders, international venue standards, municipal master development plans, and direct institutional thesis records.
- **Secondary Sources** represent peer-reviewed academic journal articles, scholarly books, doctoral dissertations, post-occupancy facility evaluations, and industry technical reports that analyze, evaluate, and provide empirical evidence for spatial modularity, turnaround logistics, acoustic physics, cultural craft abstraction, and design research methodology.

Each entry is mapped directly to the **Statement of the Problem (SPOP)** sub-problems:
- **SPOP Q1:** Baseline indoor environmental quality (IEQ) and operational turnaround bottlenecks in the 840 sqm envelope.
- **SPOP Q2:** Standardized modular layout typologies (Plenary, Classroom, Banquet, Exhibition) and anthropometric clearances under BP 344.
- **SPOP Q3:** Cultural translation of Samar Basey *banig* weaving (*tikog* grass) and Calbayog waterfall linework into acoustic surfaces.
- **SPOP Q3.1:** Technical future-proofing, modular acoustic wall/ceiling assemblies, and kinetic overhead utility track interfaces.

### 2.2 Categorized Primary Sources Bibliography & Concise Annotations

#### Statutory Building, Safety, and Accessibility Laws
- **Presidential Decree No. 1096 (PD 1096). (1977).** *Adopting a National Building Code of the Philippines (NBCP) thereby revising Republic Act Numbered Sixty-Five Hundred Forty-One*. Republic of the Philippines.
  - *Key Provision:* Rule XII Sec. 1207 & Table XII.1 sets legally enforceable occupant load factors for assembly spaces.
  - *Application:* Prevents overcrowding by capping maximum allowable occupants per configuration (Plenary: 500 max; Banquet: 300 max; Classroom: 250 max; Exhibition: 20–24 standard booths).
- **Batas Pambansa Bilang 344 (BP 344). (1983).** *An act to enhance the mobility of disabled persons by requiring certain buildings, institutions, establishments and public utilities to install facilities and other devices*. Republic of the Philippines.
  - *Key Provision:* Establishes universal design standards for persons with disabilities (PWD), including ramp gradients, accessible doors, and dedicated seating stalls.
  - *Application:* Guarantees barrier-free passage throughout the 840 sqm envelope, with integrated wheelchair positions in all 4 layout configurations.
- **Republic Act No. 9514 (RA 9514). (2008).** *An act establishing a comprehensive fire code of the Philippines, repealing Presidential Decree No. 1185 and for other purposes (Revised Fire Code of the Philippines of 2008)*. Republic of the Philippines.
  - *Key Provision:* Section 10.2.5.2 specifies exit widths, maximum dead-end corridors ($6.0\text{ m}$), and maximum egress travel distances.
  - *Application:* Mandates unobstructed clear corridors along both sides of the 711 sqm Main Hall and ensures mobile partition storage pockets do not encroach on exits.
- **Republic Act No. 10173. (2012).** *An act protecting individual personal information in information and communications systems in the government and the private sector, creating for this purpose a National Privacy Commission, and for other purposes (Data Privacy Act of 2012)*. Republic of the Philippines.
  - *Key Provision:* Governs ethical collection, transparency, data minimization, and confidentiality of personal identifying data.
  - *Application:* Protects respondents in the purposive expert ($n=15\text{–}25$) and quota ($n=100\text{–}120$) surveys, ensuring strict anonymity.

#### Tourism, MICE Venue, and Universal Design Standards
- **Department of Tourism (DOT). (2023).** *Memorandum Circular No. 2023-0003: Updated progressive accreditation system (PAS) for tourism enterprises (MICE venues and facilities)*. Republic of the Philippines.
  - *Key Provision:* Outlines physical venue requirements for Category MICE facilities: acoustic segregation, lighting adequacy, HVAC, and dedicated registration foyers.
  - *Application:* Provides the accreditation criteria that elevate the Calbayog Convention Center from an informal civic hall into a certified regional MICE destination.
- **Association of Southeast Asian Nations (ASEAN). (2018).** *ASEAN MICE venue standard (Category meeting room)*. ASEAN Secretariat.
  - *Key Provision:* Harmonizes regional standards across ASEAN member states for technical conference facilities, AV readiness, and indoor environmental performance.
  - *Application:* Informs the specification of Module 4 (overhead ceiling utility interface) and acoustic separation between the 711 sqm hall and 129 sqm foyer.
- **Department of Justice (DOJ). (2010).** *ADA standards for accessible design*. U.S. Department of Justice.
  - *Key Provision:* Sections 221 and 802 detail clear floor spaces, vertical sightline calculations, and companion seating placement.
  - *Application:* Supplements BP 344 by ensuring unobstructed sightlines over standing audiences for wheelchair users during plenary assemblies.

## 4. Part III: The "Provincial Reality Gap" Literature Synthesis

The newly reviewed literature collectively establishes the **"Provincial Reality Gap"**—the foundational intellectual problem addressed by this thesis:

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                               THE PROVINCIAL REALITY GAP                                │
├───────────────────────────────────────────┬────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│   Metropolitan Mega-Complex Realities    │     Provincial Municipal LGU Realities     │
│   (SMX, PICC, World Trade Center)         │     (Calbayog City Convention Center)      │
├───────────────────────────────────────────┼────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ • Massive footprints (> 10,000 sqm)       │ • Constrained envelope (840 sqm total)     │
│ • Multiple dedicated specialized halls    │ • Single indivisible hall (711 sqm)        │
│ • Highly capitalized corporate budgets    │ • Budget-constrained municipal funding     │
│ • Dedicated, multi-crew utility staff     │ • Lean utility crew (4–8 municipal staff)  │
│ • Proprietary mechanized wall partitions  │ • Manual setup/teardown turnaround         │
│ • Corporate, brand-neutral interiors      │ • Urgent need for local cultural identity  │
└───────────────────────────────────────────┴────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Synthesis & Research Gap Justification
1. **Existing MICE Literature Focuses Almost Exclusively on Flagship Mega-Venues:** Global and national studies (Rogers & Wynn-Moylan, 2022; An et al., 2021; Fulgar, 2024) analyze large-scale venues with multi-million-peso operational budgets and dedicated engineering divisions.
2. **Provincial Civic Venues are Functionally Ignored in Design Research:** There is a pronounced lack of peer-reviewed literature addressing how a mid-scale provincial envelope ($500\text{–}1,500\text{ m}^2$), adapted from non-civic origins (a municipal bus terminal), can achieve high-performance MICE functionality.
3. **The Thesis Contribution:** By synthesizing **Open Building Theory (Habraken, 1972)** with **Place Attachment (Scannell & Gifford, 2010)**, this study formulates an **Interior Design Framework** that delivers:
   - Zero-structural-impact modularity;
   - Rapid turnaround via pre-calculated anthropometric clearances (Panero & Zelnik; Neufert; BP 344);
   - Built-in cultural acoustic surfaces (Samar *banig* & Calbayog waterfall linework) that eliminate recurring decorating costs for local organizers; and
   - Future-proof ceiling utility tracks that eliminate floor hazards.
