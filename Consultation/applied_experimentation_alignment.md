# Applied Experimentation Alignment: Core Components and Thesis Mapping

To help you understand how your thesis aligns with an **Applied Experimentation Study**, we need to translate the traditional scientific definitions (which people often associate with biology or chemistry labs) into the discipline of **Interior Design and Built Environment Research** (guided by seminal research authorities like **Groat & Wang, 2013** and **Zeisel, 2006**).

You are **not** doing "basic research" (e.g., theorizing about abstract human perception in an imaginary vacuum). You are conducting **applied research** that takes a **real, dysfunctional local government venue** and tests whether a **specific spatial intervention** solves its operational and cultural bottlenecks.

### 2. Deep Breakdown of Each Core Component

#### A. Real-World Environment (Practice Conditions)
* **What it means:** An applied experiment does not happen in an abstract hypothetical world; it is grounded in actual operational constraints.
* **In your thesis:** Your testbed is the **Calbayog City Convention Center** in Brgy. Capoocan, Calbayog City, Samar. It has real-world operational baggage: it was converted in 2009 from a municipal bus terminal, meaning it possesses a static, echoing rectangular hall (711 sqm) and a constrained lobby (129 sqm) that regularly struggles to host civic assemblies, social banquets, lectures, and regional exhibitions.

#### B. Independent Variable (IV) — The Direct Intervention You Manipulate
* **What it means:** The specific tool, design, or system that **you as the researcher control and introduce** to solve the problem.
* **In your thesis:** Grounded in **Habraken’s (1972) Open Building Theory**, you treat the concrete building shell as fixed "Support," while your **intervention is the kinetic "Infill."** You manipulate four specific design variables:
  1. **$IV_1$ — Spatial Reconfigurability:** 4 standardized modular layout typologies (Plenary, Classroom, Banquet, Exhibition).
  2. **$IV_2$ — Demising & Rigging Systems:** Kinetic folding acoustic room partitions and an overhead non-structural track/rigging grid.
  3. **$IV_3$ — Cultural Acoustic Surface Assembly:** Wall claddings abstracting Basey *banig* weaving (*tikog* grass textures) rated at $\text{NRC} \ge 0.75$ and suspended ceiling baffles reflecting Calbayog waterfall fluid forms rated at $\text{NRC} \ge 0.85$.
  4. **$IV_4$ — Reconfigurable FF&E:** High-density, mobile, stackable furnishings with calculated storage nesting footprints.

#### C. Dependent Variable (DV) — The Practical Metrics Being Measured
* **What it means:** The concrete results you measure to see if your intervention actually resolved the problem.
* **In your thesis:** You measure four direct outcomes:
  1. **$DV_1$ — Turnaround Duration & Operational Speed:** Does the modular framework reduce the manual trial-and-error setup/teardown hours currently experienced by event planners?
  2. **$DV_2$ — Anthropometric & Regulatory Compliance:** Do the proposed layouts mathematically achieve the required clearance widths under **Batas Pambansa Bilang 344** (wheelchair corridors $\ge 1200\text{ mm}$, turning radius $\ge 1500\text{ mm}$) and **PD 1096 Sec. 1207** (exit passage clearances)?
  3. **$DV_3$ — Acoustic Reverberation Control:** Does the material assembly successfully lower the reverberation time ($RT_{60}$) toward the recommended MICE standard of $0.8 \text{ to } 1.2\text{ seconds}$ to eliminate flutter echo?
  4. **$DV_4$ — Place Attachment & Civic Identity:** Does the interior aesthetic significantly elevate local pride and sense of place based on Scannell & Gifford’s (2010) Person-Process-Place model?

#### D. Control vs. Treatment Groups (Baseline vs. Proposed Design)
* **The Common Confusion:** *"How can I have a control and treatment group if I am only studying one convention center?"*
* **How Design Research Solves This (Within-Subjects / Pre-Test vs. Post-Test Quasi-Experiment):**
  * In clinical trials, you might have Group A (Control) and Group B (Treatment).
  * In architecture and interior design (**Groat & Wang, 2013; Zeisel, 2006**), the **same physical space is tested across two distinct states**:
    * **The Control State (Pre-Test / Phase 1):** The existing, unmodified baseline. You measure the venue's current performance through diagnostic surveys ($N = 115\text{--}145$), spatial envelope audits, event lifecycle observations, and qualitative interviews ($n = 8\text{--}12$).
    * **The Treatment State (Post-Test / Phase 2 & 3):** The same facility experiencing the proposed modular interior overlay.
* **How Testing is Executed without Full Construction (Pre-Occupancy Predictive Verification):**
  * Because physically renovating an entire government convention center is impossible within an undergraduate academic calendar, testing is conducted through **Pre-Occupancy Predictive Simulation & Structured Expert Validation** (**Tremblay, Hevner, & Berndt, 2010**):
    1. **Digital Geometric Stress-Testing:** Testing the 4 typologies in CADD against BP 344 and fire egress clearance algorithms.
    2. **Acoustic Absorption Calculations:** Applying laboratory-certified NRC ratings of *tikog* and acoustic panels using Sabine/Eyring reverberation formulas.
    3. **Post-Design Stakeholder Focus Group Discussion (FGD, $n = 7\text{--}10$):** A nested panel of actual event planners, operators, and attendees who experienced the baseline (Control State) reviews the treatment drawings/renderings and scores them using a standardized rubric to verify if the intervention solves the real-world operational problems.

### 3. Summary Defense Talking Script

If your thesis panel or adviser asks you:  
**"Why is your thesis classified as an Applied Experimentation study?"**

You can explain it clearly in simple terms:

> *"Our study is classified as an **Applied Experimentation** because it is a hypothesis-driven investigation conducted in a real-world setting rather than an abstract concept. 
> 
> * **The Testbed:** The actual 840-square-meter interior envelope of the Calbayog City Convention Center.
> * **The Control / Baseline:** Phase 1 documents the existing status quo—rigid layouts, long setup turnaround times, and severe acoustic flutter echo.
> * **The Independent Variable (Intervention):** Our non-structural interior design framework—consisting of 4 modular layout typologies, kinetic acoustic dividers, an overhead rigging grid, and localized Basey banig and waterfall acoustic finishes.
> * **The Dependent Variables (Outcomes):** The measurable outcomes we evaluate: turnaround time reduction, BP 344 accessibility clearance compliance, acoustic NRC and reverberation control, and user place attachment.
> * **The Verification:** We test this intervention through geometric CADD code simulation, acoustic Sabine calculations, and a structured Post-Design Focus Group Discussion with venue stakeholders to verify that the design intervention resolves the baseline problems."*
