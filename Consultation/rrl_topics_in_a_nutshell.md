# Chapter 2 (Literature Review) Topics In a Nutshell

**Official Working Title:**  
*An Interior Design Framework for the Calbayog City Convention Center: Harmonizing Spatial Modularity, Local Samar Heritage, and Future-Proof Event Adaptability*  
**Degree Program:** Bachelor of Science in Interior Design  
**Target Audience:** Thesis Adviser & Panel Reviewers  
**Spatial Envelope:** 840 square meters (711 sqm Main Convention Hall + 129 sqm Foyer / Pre-function Area)  
**Structural Scope Boundary:** Strictly non-structural interior overlay  

---

## 1. Executive Summary & Adviser Presentation Pitch

> **The 2-Minute Adviser Pitch (How to Introduce Chapter 2):**  
> *"Good day, Ma'am/Sir. Chapter 2 is structured as a **logical funnel** that bridges broad global MICE trends down to the specific spatial and cultural realities of an LGU-operated convention center in Samar.  
> 
> The review is organized into **five thematic blocks**:  
> 1. **Block 1 (The Theoretical Foundation):** Establishes why multi-use venues require modularity and future-proofing, anchoring on Habraken’s (1972) Open Building Theory to decouple the fixed building shell from the adaptable interior infill.  
> 2. **Block 2 (Technical Standards & Philippine Codes):** Details the exact anthropometric clearance standards (Panero & Zelnik; Neufert) and Philippine building laws (National Building Code PD 1096 Table XII.1, Accessibility Law BP 344, Fire Code RA 9514, and DOT PAS/ASEAN MICE Standards) governing assembly spaces.  
> 3. **Block 3 (Operational Turnaround & Logistics):** Reviews foundational facilities literature (Lawson, 2000; Tompkins et al., 2010; Ayuba & Agah, 2018) proving that setup delays are caused by **trial-and-error space planning** and spatial friction.  
> 4. **Block 4 (Cultural Placemaking & Precedents):** Demonstrates how vernacular craft (Samar Basey banig weaving and Calbayog waterfall forms) can be abstracted into acoustic wall panels and ceiling baffles, benchmarking directly against the Baguio Convention Center provincial retrofit.  
> 5. **Block 5 (Synthesis & Research Gap):** Bridges Open Building Theory with Place Attachment, identifying the **'Provincial Reality Gap'**—the lack of standardized, non-structural interior frameworks for resource-constrained provincial municipal envelopes—which directly justifies our Statement of the Problem (SPOP Q1–Q3.1)."*

## 3. Topic-by-Topic Bulleted Summary (All 14 Sections of 20_RRL.md)

### BLOCK 1: Global MICE Context & Architectural Adaptability

#### 1. MICE Industry ([20_RRL.md: Line 11](file:///C:/Users/Trina/OneDrive/Desktop/thesis%201/New%20Thesis/20_RRL.md#L11))
- **Core Concept:** Examines the Meetings, Incentives, Conferences, and Exhibitions (MICE) sector as a high-yield economic driver, noting the contemporary global paradigm shift from generic, corporate "black-box" convention halls to experiential, culturally immersive civic venues.
- **Key Citations:** Rogers & Wynn-Moylan (2022); Allied Market Research (2019); Bhandari (2016); Anas et al. (2020); Wee et al. (2022).
- **Thesis Application:** Justifies transforming the Calbayog Convention Center into a regional MICE destination that merges functional business events with Samar cultural tourism.

#### 2. Modularity, Flexibility, and Adaptability ([20_RRL.md: Line 21](file:///C:/Users/Trina/OneDrive/Desktop/thesis%201/New%20Thesis/20_RRL.md#L21))
- **Core Concept:** Explores spatial modularity through John Habraken’s (1972) **Open Building Theory**, which distinguishes the permanent, unchangeable architectural shell (*Support*) from the adaptable interior fit-out (*Infill*). Reviews Design for Adaptability (DfA) methodologies.
- **Key Citations:** Habraken (1972); Hashemian (2005); Kronenburg (2007); Omrani & Iordanova (2024); Dong et al. (2024).
- **Thesis Application:** Provides the theoretical anchor to decouple Calbayog’s fixed 840 sqm concrete/glass shell from the kinetic interior overlay (modular FF&E, track interfaces), ensuring zero structural alterations.

#### 3. Future-Proof Design ([20_RRL.md: Line 43](file:///C:/Users/Trina/OneDrive/Desktop/thesis%201/New%20Thesis/20_RRL.md#L43))
- **Core Concept:** Evaluates future-proofing as the intentional capacity of an interior space to accommodate shifting functional needs, technological changes, and evolving safety regulations over time without costly structural obsolescence.
- **Key Citations:** Love et al. (2015); DesignHorizons (2024); Materials (2024).
- **Thesis Application:** Informs the selection of durable, multi-generational interior materials and reconfigurable ceiling track infrastructures for the facility.

#### 4. Adaptable & Flexible Design ([20_RRL.md: Line 55](file:///C:/Users/Trina/OneDrive/Desktop/thesis%201/New%20Thesis/20_RRL.md#L55))
- **Core Concept:** Analyzes multifunctional spatial zoning and multi-scale zoning typologies in civic architecture, identifying that spatial efficiency depends on standardized modular dimensions rather than custom one-off furniture.
- **Key Citations:** Gallagher (2024); Etteh (2016); Barr (2023); Taleghani & Naghdbishi (2022).
- **Thesis Application:** Sets the baseline principles for converting an indivisible 711 sqm Main Hall across contrasting event scales.

### BLOCK 2: Technical Ergonomics, Layout Typologies & Philippine Codes

#### 5. Anthropometric and Ergonomic Standards ([20_RRL.md: Line 67](file:///C:/Users/Trina/OneDrive/Desktop/thesis%201/New%20Thesis/20_RRL.md#L67))
- **Core Concept:** Synthesizes human dimension data, eye-level clearances, body clearance ellipses, and seating pitches across four primary layout typologies:
  - *Theater / Plenary:* Minimum 840–900 mm row pitch, 305 mm clear seat passage, sightline clearance ($C \ge 60\text{ mm}$ staggered / $120\text{ mm}$ unstaggered).
  - *Classroom / Lecture:* 740–760 mm table height, 450–500 mm chair pull-out depth, 400–450 mm service passage.
  - *Banquet / Dining:* 10-person round tables ($\varnothing 1800\text{ mm}$ / $3200\text{–}3500\text{ mm}$ occupied cluster footprint), $1620\text{–}1800\text{ mm}$ two-way catering corridors.
  - *Exhibition / Trade Fair:* $2 \times 2\text{ m}$ / $3 \times 3\text{ m}$ booth modules; 1500 mm secondary passing lanes; 2400 mm main crowd corridors.
  - *Universal Accessibility:* Compliance with **Batas Pambansa Bilang 344 (BP 344)** and 2010 ADA Standards (1200 mm clear path, 1500 mm turning bays, $760 \times 1220\text{ mm}$ wheelchair stations, 1:12 ramp slopes).
- **Key Citations:** Panero & Zelnik (1979); Neufert (2012); BP 344 (1983); DOJ ADA Standards (2010).
- **Thesis Application:** Forms the exact mathematical and dimensional foundation for Module 1 (Layout Typologies) and Module 2 (Dimensional Standards).

#### 6. Ceiling Design and Rigging Systems ([20_RRL.md: Line 118](file:///C:/Users/Trina/OneDrive/Desktop/thesis%201/New%20Thesis/20_RRL.md#L118))
- **Core Concept:** Evaluates overhead suspension grids, motorized and stationary utility tracks, acoustic sound baffles, and concealed AV/electrical cable management.
- **Key Citations:** Syma (2023); AV Alliance (2024); Cat (2023); Phantos (2025).
- **Thesis Application:** Justifies Module 4 (Overhead Ceiling Track Interface), dropping power and lighting from above to eliminate tripping hazards and floor-taped cords.

#### 7. Events in a Convention Center ([20_RRL.md: Line 130](file:///C:/Users/Trina/OneDrive/Desktop/thesis%201/New%20Thesis/20_RRL.md#L130))
- **Core Concept:** Classifies the operational spectrum of events held in convention centers—corporate meetings, association conventions, civic assemblies, social banquets, cultural exhibitions, and emergency community shelter operations.
- **Key Citations:** Van der Wagen & White (2017); Sharma (2025); Maslanka et al. (2022); Cailles (2024).
- **Thesis Application:** Ensures the Calbayog layout manual covers the full operational spectrum required by the local government and community.

#### 8. Seating Styles / Layouts Matrix ([20_RRL.md: Line 147](file:///C:/Users/Trina/OneDrive/Desktop/thesis%201/New%20Thesis/20_RRL.md#L147))
- **Core Concept:** Features an in-depth comparative matrix of 8 seating configurations (Theater, Classroom, Boardroom, U-Shaped, Banquet, Cabaret, Chevron, Hollow Square), detailing best use-cases and spatial trade-offs.
- **Key Citations:** Pembegül (2009); Jain (2015); Waida (2021).
- **Thesis Application:** Provides the comparative evidence base used to distill the four primary operational typologies selected for Calbayog.

#### 9. Mid-Scale Provincial Convention Facility Standards in the Philippines ([20_RRL.md: Line 164](file:///C:/Users/Trina/OneDrive/Desktop/thesis%201/New%20Thesis/20_RRL.md#L164))
- **Core Concept:** 
  - *The Provincial Reality Gap:* Contrasts metropolitan mega-complexes (PICC, SMX) with mid-scale provincial envelopes ($500\text{–}1,500\text{ m}^2$, 300–800 occupants, lean 4–8 utility staff, budget-constrained organizers).
  - *Philippine Statutory Matrix:* Synthesizes **DOT Progressive Accreditation System (PAS - MC No. 2023-0003)** and **ASEAN MICE Venue Standards (AMVS)** with the **National Building Code (PD 1096 Rule XII Sec. 1207 Table XII.1)** ($0.65\text{ m}^2$ plenary, $1.40\text{ m}^2$ banquet, $1.80\text{ m}^2$ classroom, $2.80\text{ m}^2$ exhibition), **BP 344**, and the **Revised Fire Code (RA 9514)** ($\le 45\text{ m}$ exit travel, outward panic doors).
  - *Interior Architectural Synthesis:* Demonstrates that meeting standards in provincial envelopes requires non-structural FF&E standardization, acoustic overlays, and built-in cultural placemaking.
- **Key Citations:** DOT (2023); ASEAN (2018); PD 1096 (1977); BP 344 (1983); RA 9514 (2008); Fulgar (2024); PIDS (2020).
- **Thesis Application:** Grounds the thesis in enforceable Philippine law and specialized tourism accreditation standards.

### BLOCK 3: Operational Turnaround & Empirical Facilities Literature

#### 10. Operational Turnaround and Spatial Logistics ([20_RRL.md: Line 206](file:///C:/Users/Trina/OneDrive/Desktop/thesis%201/New%20Thesis/20_RRL.md#L206))
- **Core Concept:** 
  - Analyzes room turnaround time as the primary operational constraint in multipurpose civic venues (Lawson, 2000).
  - Examines *transit tortuosity* (disorganized zigzag movement) and material handling friction resulting from unstandardized layouts (Tompkins et al., 2010; Muther, 1973).
- **Key Citations:** Lawson (2000); Tompkins et al. (2010); Muther (1973); Lee & Lee (2017); An et al. (2021).
- **Thesis Application:** Directly proves that layout standardization and pre-calculated anthropometric clearances eliminate trial-and-error setup delays for Calbayog's utility crew.

#### 11. Related Empirical Studies on Venue Flexibility ([20_RRL.md: Line 220](file:///C:/Users/Trina/OneDrive/Desktop/thesis%201/New%20Thesis/20_RRL.md#L220))
- **Core Concept:** Synthesizes empirical investigations into venue adaptability:
  - *Ike et al. (2023):* 4 core architectural strategies for venue flexibility.
  - *Ayuba & Agah (2018):* Empirical finding that over 80% of event centers suffer operational friction due to a lack of flexible interior features.
  - *Lee & Lee (2017):* Exhibition organizer criteria prioritizing loading ease and turnaround speed.
- **Key Citations:** Ike, Barnaby, Agu, & Igwegbe (2023); Ayuba & Agah (2018); Lee & Lee (2017).
- **Thesis Application:** Validates that the spatial bottlenecks observed in Calbayog are widely documented phenomena in convention facilities literature.

### BLOCK 4: Cultural Placemaking & Precedent Grounding

#### 12. Placemaking in Convention Centers ([20_RRL.md: Line 241](file:///C:/Users/Trina/OneDrive/Desktop/thesis%201/New%20Thesis/20_RRL.md#L241))
- **Core Concept:** 
  - Explores environmental psychology of place attachment and civic pride (Relph, 1976; Scannell & Gifford, 2010).
  - *The Dual-Scale Equity Bridge:* Explains that while mega-venues use grand public foyers for placemaking, mid-scale provincial facilities (129 sqm foyer) operate through **built-in cultural surface overlays** that provide a dignified backdrop for low-budget community events without requiring disposable decor or wall-damaging tape.
  - *Precedents:* Albuquerque Convention Center (Pueblo/Hispanic art integration); Seoul COEX reimagining (Heatherwick Studio); PICC (Leandro Locsin architecture and Arturo Luz sculptures).
  - *Provincial LGU Benchmark (Baguio BCCC Retrofit):* Detailed case study of modernizing an aging municipal shell through Cordillera woodcraft, smart acoustic upgrades, and non-structural cultural infill.
- **Key Citations:** Relph (1976); Scannell & Gifford (2010); Fulgar (2024); Pap (2015); Warrick (2025); ASM Global (2024); Fakharany (2025); Cailles (2024); City Gov of Baguio (2019); DPWH (2021); Filipina News Sentinel (2022).
- **Thesis Application:** Provides the primary operational and aesthetic precedent for retrofitting an aging municipal LGU venue in the Philippines.

#### 13. Samar Weaving Heritage and Interior Surface Translation ([20_RRL.md: Line 269](file:///C:/Users/Trina/OneDrive/Desktop/thesis%201/New%20Thesis/20_RRL.md#L269))
- **Core Concept:** 
  - Details the ethnobotanical and cultural heritage of Basey, Samar *banig* mat weaving (*Samat* and *Lara* weaves) and *tikog* grass (*Fimbristylis umbellaris*).
  - Reviews computational abstraction methodologies (Wagas, 2016; Baradas, 1968; Del Monte, 2021) for translating vernacular weaving into contemporary interior surfaces without literal imitation.
  - Synthesizes Calbayog's eco-tourism identity as the "City of Waterfalls" (Tarangban, Bangon, Mapaso falls).
  - Integrates sustainable wood and fabric acoustic composite panel literature (Chojnacki et al., 2023, 2024; Cairoli, 2020; Habibi, 2025).
- **Key Citations:** Monte et al. (2022); Garado et al. (2026); NCCA (2004); Wagas (2016); Baradas (1968); Del Monte (2021); Chojnacki et al. (2023, 2024); Cairoli (2020); Habibi (2025).
- **Thesis Application:** Establishes the exact material, aesthetic, and acoustic parameters for Module 3 (Cultural Acoustic Wall Panels and Waterfall Ceiling Baffles).
