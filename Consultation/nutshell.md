# THESIS IN A NUTSHELL: CALBAYOG CITY CONVENTION CENTER

> **Official Working Title:**  
> *An Interior Design Framework for the Calbayog City Convention Center: Harmonizing Spatial Modularity, Local Samar Heritage, and Future-Proof Event Adaptability*  
> **Degree Program:** Bachelor of Science in Interior Design (BSID)  
> **Venue & Locale:** Calbayog City Convention Center (Brgy. Capoocan, Calbayog City, Samar)  
> **Spatial Envelope:** **840 sq.m.** (*711 sq.m.* Main Convention Hall + *129 sq.m.* Lobby) | **Capacity:** Up to 500 occupants  

---

## 1. What is it about? *(The Core Concept & Scope)*

It is a **site-specific, non-structural interior design framework, 3D visualization (SketchUp & Enscape), and layout manual** that transforms the Calbayog City Convention Center from a rigid, echoey, and generic municipal hall into a high-efficiency, culturally expressive, multi-functional event venue.

* **Strict Non-Structural Interior Overlay:** It does **not** demolish or modify load-bearing columns, building facades, or roof trusses. Everything proposed operates strictly within the interior envelope.
* **Dual-Layer Intervention:**
  1. **Active Logistical Layer (For Event Planners & Crew):** Mobile, modular furniture (FF&E), overhead utility/rigging tracks, an unobstructed **1800–2400 mm interior lobby staging buffer**, and 4 pre-engineered layout configurations to make event set-up and tear-down fast and effortless.
  2. **Passive Experiential Layer (For Attendees & Guests):** Acoustic wall panels and suspended ceiling baffles wrapped in Samar *tikog* / *banig* weaving patterns and fluid Calbayog waterfall motifs to absorb echoes and immerse visitors in local cultural identity.

---

## 2. Why? *(The Problem & Urgent Need)*

The convention center was originally adapted in 2009 from a **former municipal bus terminal**, resulting in three critical interior design failures:

| The Core Problem | Why It Happens | How It Affects Users |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Severe Setup Turnaround Delays & Ingress Bottlenecks** | No on-site furniture storage, no standardized floor plan templates, and narrow/friction-prone exterior facade sliding doors. | Event organizers haul in mismatched rental furniture, get jammed at the exterior sliding doors, and use manual trial-and-error measuring, causing chaotic 8–12+ hour setups. |
| **2. Acoustic Reverberation & Noise** | Hard ceramic floor tiles, concrete walls, and high uninsulated ceilings. | Flutter echoes and muddy speech intelligibility make seminars and speeches hard to hear and understand. |
| **3. Placeless, Generic Aesthetic** | Plain drywall, bland commercial paint, and zero regional identity. | Does not showcase Calbayog’s pride as the *"City of Waterfalls"* or Samar’s heritage of *Basey banig* reed weaving. |

## 3. What supports it? *(Theoretical & Empirical Foundation)*

Your thesis is grounded in validated architectural theories, statutory standards, and real-world provincial precedents:

```
                            [ THEORETICAL PILLARS ]
                                       │
        ┌──────────────────────────────┴──────────────────────────────┐
        ▼                                                             ▼
 Open Building Theory (Habraken, 1972)           Theory of Place Attachment (Scannell & Gifford, 2010)
  ├─ Support: Fixed structural shell (840 sqm)     ├─ Person-Process-Place (PPP) framework
  └─ Infill: Kinetic, modular interior overlays    └─ Tactile & visual Waray cultural resonance
```

* **Theoretical Frameworks:**
  * **Open Building Theory (Habraken, 1972):** Separates the permanent architectural shell (*Support*) from the adaptable interior fit-out (*Kinetic Infill*), allowing rapid space reconfiguration without structural changes.
  * **Theory of Place Attachment (Scannell & Gifford, 2010):** Proves that incorporating localized craftsmanship and cultural symbols creates emotional connection, comfort, and civic pride for visitors.
* **Statutory Compliance & Technical Standards:**
  * **Ergonomics & Clearances:** Neufert (2012), Panero & Zelnik (1979) anthropometrics.
  * **Philippine Codes:** National Building Code (PD 1096 Sec. 1207), Fire Code of the Philippines (RA 9514), and Accessibility Law (BP 344) ensuring universal wheelchair access (1200 mm corridors, 1500 mm turning radius).
  * **Acoustics:** Egan (2007) and Cavanaugh et al. (2010) establishing sound absorption thresholds (NRC $\ge 0.75$ for wall panels; NRC $\ge 0.85$ for ceiling baffles).
* **Real-World Case Precedent:**
  * **Baguio Convention & Cultural Center Modernization (2020–2022):** Proves how a legacy provincial LGU facility was successfully revitalized through acoustic overlays, modular typologies, and Cordilleran indigenous weaving motifs without civil demolition.

## 4. This is how I'll do it *(Methodology & Workflow)*

You are using an **Applied Design Research** approach executed through a **Convergent Parallel Mixed-Methods Design ($\text{QUAN} + \text{QUAL}$)** across three distinct phases:

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐  ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ STRAND 1: QUANTITATIVE SURVEY (QUAN)         │  │ STRAND 2: QUALITATIVE INTERVIEWS (QUAL)      │
│ • Sample: N = 115 to 145 respondents         │  │ • Sample: n = 8 to 12 key informants         │
│   ├─ 15–25 Event Planners (Expert Sampling)  │  │   (Planners, Venue Staff, Artisans)          │
│   └─ 100–120 Attendees (Slovin's Quota)      │  │ • Focus: Setup root causes & cultural nuance │
│ • Analysis: Weighted means, frequencies      │  │ • Analysis: Braun & Clarke (2006) Thematic   │
└──────────────────────┬───────────────────────┘  └──────────────────────┬───────────────────────┘
                       │                                                 │
                       └────────────────────────┬────────────────────────┘
                                                ▼
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ PHASE 2: SPATIAL DESIGN SYNTHESIS                                                      │
│ • Triangulate survey numbers + interview themes into design specifications             │
│ • Develop 4 CADD Layout Typologies + 3D Renderings + Acoustic Material Specifications  │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────┘
                                               ▼
┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│ PHASE 3: PREDICTIVE VERIFICATION & STAKEHOLDER VALIDATION                              │
│ • Evaluative Focus Group Discussion (FGD) (n = 7–10 nested stakeholders; rubric test)  │
│ • 3-Pillar Predictive Verification: Clearance Audits (BP 344), Acoustic NRC Specs,    │
│   and Analog Precedent Triangulation (Baguio BCCC)                                     │
└────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

1. **Phase 1: Baseline Fieldwork & Data Collection**
   * Concurrently collect quantitative surveys ($N = 115\text{--}145$) on setup delays and comfort ratings alongside qualitative interviews ($n = 8\text{--}12$) on operational pain points and local Samar artisanal crafts.
2. **Phase 2: Evidence-Based Spatial Synthesis**
   * Translate empirical data into 4 standardized layout typologies (Plenary, Banquet, Classroom, Exhibition), ergonomic circulation paths, and custom acoustic finishes.
3. **Phase 3: Post-Design Stakeholder Validation & Technical Verification**
   * Because this is an unbuilt thesis, validate the design before construction using an **Evaluative Focus Group Discussion (FGD)** ($n = 7\text{--}10$ planners, staff, and attendees) using a rubric evaluation, coupled with mathematical clearance audits (BP 344) and lab-rated acoustic NRC calculations.

### Anticipated Real-World Impact:
1. **Dramatic Turnaround Time Reduction:** Cuts venue transition times from 8–12+ hours of guesswork down to an estimated **2 to 3 hours** via standardized FF&E and pre-calculated layout templates.
2. **Acoustic Speech Clarity:** Eliminates echoes and sound blur, making speech intelligible across all 711 sq.m. of the Main Hall.
3. **Regional Pride & Cultural Identity:** Creates an authentic Samar-Calbayog interior identity (Basey mat weaves and cascading waterfall forms) that elevates the facility from a plain municipal hall to a prime, competitive provincial MICE venue.
4. **Zero Structural Risk:** Provides the City Government of Calbayog with a cost-effective, high-impact blueprint that requires no demolition, structural retrofitting, or building closures.
