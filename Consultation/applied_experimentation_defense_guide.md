## 1. Why Your Thesis is Classified as "Applied Experimentation"

Under your university and department curriculum guidelines, theses are often classified into distinct research traditions (e.g., Basic/Descriptive, Historical, Correlational, or **Applied Experimentation**).

In the discipline of Interior Design and Architecture, **Applied Experimentation** does not mean mixing chemicals in test tubes or conducting clinical drug trials. As established by seminal design research authorities (**Groat & Wang, 2013**; **Zeisel, 2006**; **Cross, 2007**; **Zimmerman et al., 2007**), applied experimentation in spatial design is:

> *"The systematic formulation and testing of an environmental or physical design intervention within a specific, bounded spatial setting to determine its measurable effects on operational performance, human behavior, and indoor environmental quality against a documented baseline."*

Your study fulfills every scientific and operational requirement of an **Applied Quasi-Experimental and Simulation-Based Design Study**:

| Scientific Element of an Experiment | Traditional Laboratory Experiment | Your Thesis: Calbayog City Convention Center |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Experimental Testbed (Setting)** | Sterile, controlled laboratory room | The real-world **840 sqm interior envelope** (711 sqm Main Hall + 129 sqm Lobby) of the Calbayog City Convention Center. |
| **Baseline / Pre-Test (Control State)** | Untreated subject / status quo measurement | **Phase 1 Diagnostic Baseline:** Documented rigid envelope, long setup/teardown turnaround times, acoustic flutter echo, spatial bottlenecks, and generic aesthetic lacking civic identity. |
| **Experimental Treatment (Independent Variable - IV)** | Chemical agent, drug dose, or physical stimulus | **Phase 2 Interior Design Framework:** Four modular layout typologies, kinetic space dividers, overhead rigging grid, and Samar *banig* / waterfall acoustic surface assemblies. |
| **Measured Outcomes (Dependent Variables - DV)** | Physiological response or chemical rate | **Phase 3 Evaluative Verification:** Turnaround time reduction, anthropometric clearance compliance (BP 344 / PD 1096), acoustic absorption ($RT_{60}$ mitigation), and user satisfaction. |
| **Testing Mechanism** | Sensor readings, assays | **Pre-Occupancy Predictive Simulation & Expert Rubric Testing:** Digital CADD clearance stress-testing, acoustic NRC calculations, and structured nested stakeholder Focus Group Discussion ($n = 7\text{--}10$). |

## 2. The 4-Part Scientific Architecture of Your Study

### A. The Setting: Applied Spatial Testbed
The investigation is not a hypothetical site study. It is situated within an actual, historically constrained local government facility:
* **Locale:** Calbayog City Convention Center, Brgy. Capoocan, Calbayog City, Samar.
* **Envelope Dimensions:** 840 square meters total (711 sqm Main Convention Hall + 129 sqm Lobby / Pre-function zone).
* **Physical Origin:** Renovated on December 14, 2009 from a municipal bus terminal.
* **Maximum Assembly Load:** 500 occupants.

### B. Pre-Test: Documenting the Baseline (Status Quo)
In Phase 1, you establish the baseline through empirical mixed-methods diagnostic fieldwork:
* **Quantitative Baseline ($N = 115\text{--}145$):** Structured surveys capturing Likert-scale ratings on existing Indoor Environmental Quality (IEQ), acoustic dissatisfaction, seating discomfort, and setup turnaround delays.
* **Operational Audits & Lifecycle Observations:** Documenting the real-world ingress setup, in-session flow, and egress teardown bottlenecks across contrasting event types.
* **Qualitative Baseline ($n = 8\text{--}12$):** In-depth key informant interviews with event planners, venue operators, and cultural advocates diagnosing the root causes of operational friction and lack of civic pride.

### C. Experimental Intervention (Independent Variables)
In Phase 2, you formulate the design intervention grounded in **Habraken’s (1972) Open Building Theory** (treating the building shell as fixed "Support" and the interior fit-out as kinetic "Infill"):
1. **$IV_1$ — Standardized Modular Layout Typologies:** Four distinct configurations (Plenary, Classroom, Banquet, Exhibition) with pre-calculated clearances.
2. **$IV_2$ — Kinetic Spatial Infill:** Mobile folding acoustic partitions and an overhead non-structural track-and-rigging grid.
3. **$IV_3$ — Cultural Acoustic Surface Assemblies:** Perimeter acoustic wall claddings abstracting Samar Basey *banig* weaving (*tikog* grass textures) with laboratory-certified ratings ($\text{NRC} \ge 0.75$) and suspended ceiling baffles reflecting Calbayog waterfall fluid geometry ($\text{NRC} \ge 0.85$).

### D. Post-Test & Testing Mechanism (Dependent Variables)
In Phase 3, you evaluate whether the experimental intervention solves the baseline deficiencies:
1. **$DV_1$ — Operational Turnaround Efficiency:** Pre-calculated layouts and mobile FF&E reduce manual trial-and-error setup time and labor hours.
2. **$DV_2$ — Universal Accessibility & Egress Compliance:** Verification of wheelchair clear paths ($\ge 1200\text{ mm}$), turning bays ($\ge 1500\text{ mm}$), and accessible seating stalls under **Batas Pambansa Bilang 344 (BP 344)** and **PD 1096 (National Building Code of the Philippines, Sec. 1207)**.
3. **$DV_3$ — Acoustic Reverberation Control:** Predictive calculation of reverberation time reduction using Sabine/Eyring principles with published material NRC thresholds.
4. **$DV_4$ — Place Attachment & Civic Identity:** Measured via the **Theory of Place Attachment (Scannell & Gifford, 2010)** through post-design stakeholder evaluation.

## 3. Real, Verified Precedents and Sourced Studies

When your panel asks for precedents of applied experimentation or empirical design research in convention facilities, cite these verified academic and professional sources:

### 1. Direct Academic Parallel (Philippine BSID Thesis)
* **Study:** Estrada, Ryena M. (2013). *Uplifting Sustainability and Modernism to the Interiors of a Convention Centre* (JCX Convention Centre, Mandaue City, Cebu).
* **Institution:** Department of Interior Design, College of Fine Arts and Design, University of Santo Tomas (UST).
* **Methodological Parallel:** Estrada conducted an applied interior redesign of an existing Visayan convention venue (~1,700 sqm), testing spatial convertibility and substituting synthetic materials with local indigenous fibers (Coco Coir, Cogon Grass, Giant Bamboo, Water Hyacinth) across 4 zones via dual surveys (staff vs. attendees).
* **Precedent Value:** Directly validates that testing spatial reconfigurability and indigenous material substitution within an existing Visayan assembly envelope is an approved BSID undergraduate thesis model.

### 2. Empirical Flexibility & Turnaround Research
* **Authors:** Ayuba, P., & Agah, F. A. (2018). *"Assessment of Flexible Features in the Design of Event Centres in Minna, Nigeria."* *Nigerian Journal of Technology (NIJOTECH)*, 37(3), 587–599.
* **Methodological Parallel:** Evaluated 10 multipurpose event centres and empirically demonstrated that **80% suffered from spatial rigidity**, directly causing long turnaround delays, high labor costs, and congestion. They tested the necessity of folding partitions, demountable staging, and multipurpose FF&E.
* **Precedent Value:** Validates the scientific premise of your baseline: static envelopes inherently cause turnaround bottlenecks, justifying modular spatial experimentation.

### 3. MICE Venue Competitiveness & Spatial Flexibility
* **Authors:** An, J., Kim, H., & Hur, D. (2021). *"Keeping the Competitive Edge of a Convention and Exhibition Center in MICE Environment: The Case of Suwon Convention Center."* *Sustainability*, 13(9), 5030.
* **Methodological Parallel:** Used structural equation modeling across venue organizers and attendees to prove that **interior room flexibility, configuration speed, and acoustic comfort** are the leading statistical determinants of venue success, outweighing exterior building form.
* **Precedent Value:** Empirically grounds your independent variables (modular layout speed and acoustic quality) as peer-reviewed determinants of convention center viability.

### 4. Direct Philippine Municipal LGU Assembly Modernization
* **Project:** City Government of Baguio, City Buildings and Architecture Office (CBAO), & DPWH (2019–2022). *Rehabilitation and Interior Modernization of the Baguio Convention and Cultural Center (BCCC).*
* **Methodological Parallel:** Modernized an aging, rigid municipal hall (built 1977) without structural demolition. Deployed kinetic fabric-wrapped acoustic panels, high-density movable seating, overhead service grids, and Cordilleran cultural motifs (*halipan*).
* **Precedent Value:** Real-world LGU proof that a public municipal assembly envelope achieves high-performance MICE functionality and acoustic control strictly through non-structural interior overlays.

### 5. Seminal Epistemological & Methodological Authorities
* **Groat, L., & Wang, D. (2013).** *Architectural Research Methods* (2nd ed.). John Wiley & Sons.
  * *Authoritative Text:* Delineates the strategies of **Experimental and Quasi-Experimental Research** and **Simulation Research** in built environments, establishing that digital simulation and spatial testing within existing envelopes are valid experimental strategies.
* **Zeisel, J. (2006).** *Inquiry by Design: Environment/Behavior/Neuroscience in Architecture, Interiors, Landscape, and Planning.* W. W. Norton & Co.
  * *Authoritative Text:* Establishes that design proposals act as scientific hypotheses tested through spatial manipulation, environmental criteria, and structured user feedback.
* **Tremblay, M. C., Hevner, A. R., & Berndt, D. J. (2010).** *"Focus Groups for Artifact Evaluation in Design Research."* *Communications of the Association for Information Systems*, 26(1), 27.
  * *Authoritative Text:* Methodological foundation for Phase 3: proves that structured focus groups with domain experts are an established, rigorous method for evaluating unbuilt design artifacts.

## 4. Verbatim Defense Script: Answering Panel Questions

Use these exact talking points during your defense or consultation:

### Question 1: "Why is your thesis classified as 'Applied Experimentation'? Where is the experiment?"
> **Verbatim Speaking Script:**  
> *"Our department curriculum classifies this study as **Applied Experimentation** because it is an **Applied Quasi-Experimental Design Study** conducted within a real-world architectural envelope, grounded in the methodology of **Groat and Wang (2013)** and **Zeisel (2006)**.
> 
> In traditional science, an experiment manipulates variables in a laboratory to observe the outcome. In interior architecture, my 'testbed' is the **840-square-meter interior envelope of the Calbayog City Convention Center**.
> 
> * **Phase 1 serves as my Baseline / Control State:** I empirically document the current rigid conditions—measuring prolonged setup turnaround times, acoustic flutter echo, and spatial bottlenecks through diagnostic surveys ($N = 115\text{--}145$), spatial audits, and operational event observations.
> * **Phase 2 introduces the Experimental Intervention (Independent Variables):** Grounded in Habraken’s Open Building Theory, I introduce four kinetic modular layout typologies, mobile space partitions, an overhead track grid, and engineered acoustic surfaces abstracting Samar Basey banig weaving and Calbayog waterfall fluid dynamics.
> * **Phase 3 executes the Evaluative Testing (Dependent Variables):** Because full physical construction of a municipal convention center exceeds an undergraduate timeline, testing is performed through **pre-occupancy predictive simulation and expert panel verification**. I test whether the intervention resolves the baseline problems through geometric CADD clearance audits under BP 344, laboratory NRC acoustic calculations ($\ge 0.75$ and $\ge 0.85$), and a structured Post-Design Focus Group Discussion ($n = 7\text{--}10$) using a standardized verification rubric (Tremblay et al., 2010).
> 
> Thus, this study is a systematic, hypothesis-driven applied experiment testing whether a non-structural modular interior overlay can remediate operational turnaround bottlenecks and acoustic reverberation in an adaptive-reuse civic hall."*

### Question 2: "Can an unbuilt design proposal really be considered an experiment?"
> **Verbatim Speaking Script:**  
> *"Yes, absolutely. According to **Groat and Wang (2013)** in *Architectural Research Methods*, **Simulation Research** is one of the primary experimental strategies in architectural and interior design. 
> 
> In engineering and architecture, unbuilt prototypes are regularly tested through computational and geometric simulation prior to multi-million-peso construction. In my study, the 4 modular typologies are mathematically tested against Philippine accessibility laws (BP 344) and fire egress codes (PD 1096 Sec. 1207), while acoustic finishes are tested against established laboratory absorption coefficients. 
> 
> Furthermore, as established by **Tremblay, Hevner, and Berndt (2010)**, an unbuilt artifact is scientifically evaluated through structured expert focus groups where real event planners and venue operators stress-test the operational feasibility of the proposal against their baseline experience. This constitutes rigorous pre-construction experimental evaluation."*

### Question 3: "What are your controlled variables?"
> **Verbatim Speaking Script:**  
> *"To ensure scientific rigor, several critical parameters are held strictly constant:
> 1. **The Physical Envelope:** Fixed at 840 square meters (711 sqm Main Hall and 129 sqm Lobby), bounded by the existing structural perimeter of the former bus terminal.
> 2. **Structural Boundary:** Strictly non-structural. No load-bearing columns, exterior walls, or roof trusses are altered.
> 3. **Maximum Assembly Load:** Capped at 500 occupants.
> 4. **Regulatory Standard:** Clearance thresholds are held strictly to Batas Pambansa Bilang 344 (1200 mm corridors, 1500 mm turning bays) and PD 1096 Table XII.1.
> 
> By holding these environmental and structural variables constant, we can directly measure the impact of our independent variables—spatial modularity and acoustic surface overlays—on operational turnaround efficiency and acoustic comfort."*
