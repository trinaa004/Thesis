# Theoretical & Conceptual Framework

## 1. Theoretical Background

This study is anchored in the conceptual synthesis of two complementary theoretical paradigms: **Actor-Network Theory (ANT)** (Latour, 1996, 2005) paired with **Environmental Affordance Theory** (Gibson, 1979), and the **Theory of Place Attachment** (Scannell & Gifford, 2010). 

Together, these dual frameworks establish a comprehensive, evidence-based interior design methodology that addresses both the **socio-technical operational mechanics** of event coordination and the **psychological, sensory cultural experience** of event-goers within the Calbayog Cultural Convention Center. 

*(Note: The physical boundary of the non-structural interior overlay—confining all interventions strictly within the 840 sqm interior envelope without altering structural columns, facades, or roof trusses—is established as a statutory scope parameter in Chapter 1 under **Scope and Limitations of the Study**, in compliance with Republic Act No. 10350 [Philippine Interior Design Act of 2012] and the National Building Code of the Philippines [PD 1096]).*

### 1.1. Operational & Socio-Technical Foundation: Actor-Network Theory (Latour, 1996, 2005) & Affordance Theory (Gibson, 1979)

To diagnose and resolve the operational setup friction, layout guesswork, and sensory deficits uncovered during fieldwork, this study integrates **Actor-Network Theory (ANT)** (Latour, 1996, 2005) with **Gibson's (1979) Theory of Environmental Affordances**.

#### A. Actor-Network Theory (Latour, 1996, 2005)
ANT posits that social and operational situations are generated through heterogeneous networks composed equally of **Human Actors** and **Non-Human Actants**. In environmental design, physical artifacts, finishes, and architectural boundaries are not inert, passive backdrops; they possess "agency" that actively enables, constrains, or dictates human behavior.

Within the Calbayog Cultural Convention Center, live event operations represent a precarious network sustained between:
* **Human Actors:** Event organizers, facility custodial utility crews, banquet catering staff, audio-visual technicians, keynote speakers, and attendees.
* **Non-Human Actants:** Hard ceramic floor tiles (600 × 600 mm), heavy non-stackable wooden banquet tables, rented plastic monobloc chairs, loose electrical extension cords taped with gaffer tape, sliding glass lobby doors, high exposed steel roof trusses (6.20 m height), and temporary vinyl tarpaulins stapled to drywall.

*Operational Friction as Translation Breakdown:*  
ANT explains that prolonged setup turnaround delays (4 to 8+ hours) and attendee dissatisfaction are the direct result of **network friction** where non-human actants actively impose physical resistance on human actors:
1. When custodial crews (Human Actors) attempt a rapid room conversion, heavy non-wheeled tables and the absence of a dedicated furniture storage room (Non-Human Actants) resist them, forcing staff into hours of manual lifting, fatigue, and trial-and-error spatial guesswork.
2. When a speaker presents (Human Actor), acoustic sound waves strike hard, uninsulated ceramic tiles and parallel glass walls (Non-Human Actants), translating into severe reverberant flutter echo (RT60 > 1.8 s) that degrades speech intelligibility and causes ear fatigue in the audience.

By diagnosing the interior envelope through ANT, the proposed interior design interventions deliberately introduce **new, optimized non-human actants**—specifically lightweight nesting tables on transport dollies, flame-retardant Basey *banig* acoustic wall panels (NRC ≥ 0.75), and overhead ceiling utility raceways—that eliminate friction and actively empower human operational efficiency.

#### B. Environmental Affordance Theory (Gibson, 1979)
Complementing ANT, Gibson’s (1979) Theory of Affordances examines the functional "action possibilities" that an interior environment physically offers or denies to its users. In a multi-purpose assembly facility:
* An undivided, column-free hall *affords* high-density standing assembly, but *fails to afford* rapid furniture stowage, speech intelligibility, or simultaneous dual-event hosting.
* Narrow lobby double doors *fail to afford* simultaneous two-way pedestrian egress and catering cart delivery.

The design interventions systematically re-engineer the hall's spatial affordances, ensuring that the 840 sqm interior envelope intrinsically affords four standardized primary operational typologies (Plenary, Classroom, Banquet, Exhibition) along with adaptable configurations for documented compound and dual-zone hybrid setups, backed by pre-calculated anthropometric clearances. Crucially, the interventions operate across two complementary affordance dimensions: standardized layout templates afford cognitive visualization and shared spatial understanding during pre-event client consultations—enabling coordinators to substantiate statutory capacity ceilings (such as the 250–300 max banquet limit) and eliminate over-capacity client demands; while future-proofing technical interventions (overhead rigging, modular staging, mobile FF&E on transport dollies) afford physical floor space recovery and mechanical turnaround acceleration to under 90 minutes.

### 1.2. Cultural & Sensory Placemaking Foundation: Theory of Place Attachment (Scannell & Gifford, 2010)

While ANT and Affordance Theory satisfy the functional and operational mechanics of space planning, public assembly venues risk degenerating into sterile, generic "black boxes" if stripped of cultural meaning and civic identity. To ground the sensory, qualitative, and emotional dimensions of the interior, this study integrates the **Theory of Place Attachment** (Scannell & Gifford, 2010).

Scannell and Gifford’s tripartite **Person-Process-Place (PPP)** framework conceptualizes place bonding across three interconnected dimensions:
1. **Person:** The individual and collective cultural identity of the Samar-Calbayog community, who view the convention center as their primary civic living room for institutional assemblies, cultural convocations, and milestone life celebrations.
2. **Process:** The psychological, emotional, and cognitive bonding mechanisms that occur when occupants experience familiar, dignified cultural cues within their civic environment.
3. **Place:** The tangible, physical interior characteristics—materials, textures, acoustics, and forms—that embody local heritage and geographical identity.

*Cultural Translation into Interior Finishes:*  
For event-goers and visiting delegations, place attachment is cultivated not through superficial historicist ornament, but through **contemporary, functional design abstractions**:
* **Regional Samar Craft:** Traditional Basey *banig* reed mat weaving linework (*Samat* and *Lara* weaves) and natural *tikog* grass textures (*Fimbristylis umbellaris*) are integrated into perimeter acoustic wall panels, solving reverberation while celebrating provincial artisanal heritage.
* **Calbayog Municipal Identity:** The cascading fluid forms of Calbayog's famed waterfalls (Tarangban, Bangon, Mapaso falls) are translated into suspended acoustic ceiling baffles that diffuse speech frequencies.
* **Civic Pride & Ritual:** The kinetic movements of the local **Hadang / Sarakiki-Hadang Festival** inform dynamic accent lighting and feature wall geometry, transforming an aging municipal hall into a culturally resonant venue that fosters civic pride and eliminates the need for single-use plastic tarpaulins.

### Synthesis of the Conceptual Framework

The conceptual framework operates through an integrated synthesis that bridges socio-technical operational efficiency and human-centered cultural placemaking within the Calbayog Cultural Convention Center. At its theoretical core, the framework unites Actor-Network Theory (Latour, 1996, 2005) and Environmental Affordance Theory (Gibson, 1979) with the Theory of Place Attachment (Scannell & Gifford, 2010). Rather than treating functional logistics and spatial aesthetics as competing priorities, this tripartite epistemological foundation recognizes that a civic assembly facility succeeds only when its physical envelope simultaneously enables seamless human-object operational workflows and fosters deep emotional place-bonding. In accordance with statutory professional boundaries governed by Republic Act No. 10350 (Philippine Interior Design Act of 2012) and the National Building Code of the Philippines (PD 1096), all conceptual mechanisms and proposed physical interventions operate strictly within the non-structural 840-square-meter interior fit-out envelope (711 sqm Main Plenary Hall and 129 sqm Pre-Function Lobby), leaving structural columns, external building facades, and load-bearing roof trusses unaltered.

This theoretical convergence directly translates empirical user needs into evidence-based interior design solutions. On one hand, addressing the baseline spatial and logistical challenges experienced by event planners and custodial staff (SOP Q1.2)—namely prolonged 4-to-8-hour setup turnovers, physical fatigue from heavy non-stackable furniture, and hazardous temporary floor cabling—the proposed interventions re-engineer the facility’s non-human actants. By introducing mobile nesting furniture on transport dollies, dedicated overhead utility and lighting raceways, pre-calculated 1620–1800 mm catering circulation lanes, and repeatable layout configurations, the interior envelope is endowed with active spatial affordances that streamline operational setup turnaround to under 90 minutes. Concurrently, addressing the baseline experiential deficits identified by event attendees and cultural stakeholders (SOP Q1.1)—specifically severe reverberant flutter echo ($RT_{60} = 2.53\text{ s}$), obstructed sightlines, and generic civic placelessness—the proposed design interventions introduce culturally responsive acoustic infill. By abstracting Basey *banig* weaving patterns and *tikog* grass textures (*Fimbristylis umbellaris*) into high-performance perimeter wall panels ($NRC \ge 0.75$) and translating Calbayog’s cascading waterfall fluid forms into suspended sound baffles ($NRC \ge 0.85$), acoustic speech remediation doubles as an instrument of local heritage preservation and civic pride without compromising multi-event versatility.

Ultimately, this synthesis culminates in the study’s overarching research outcome: comprehensive **Evidence-Based Interior Design Interventions for the Calbayog Cultural Convention Center**. In direct fulfillment of the Statement of the Problem, the resulting interior scheme synthesizes modular spatial layout interventions across four primary operational typologies (Plenary, Classroom, Banquet, and Exhibition/Trade Fair) while providing adaptable configurations for documented compound and dual-zone hybrid setups to optimize multi-scale adaptability from 50 to 500 occupants (SOP Q2), integrates a future-proof non-structural overhead track-and-rigging interface to support diverse event staging (SOP Q3.1), and embeds localized Samar-Calbayog cultural motifs into high-performance commercial interior finishes to cultivate enduring place attachment (SOP Q3.2). By substituting empirical evidence, normative anthropometrics, and statutory life-safety mandates for subjective guesswork, the proposed interventions systematically resolve the historical mismatch between the building’s rigid former bus station shell and its diverse municipal event trajectory, repositioning the convention center as an adaptable, future-proof, and culturally authentic regional civic asset.
