heoretical Background
This study is anchored in the conceptual synthesis of Open Building Theory (Habraken, 1972) and the Theory of Place Attachment (Scannell & Gifford, 2010). Together, these dual frameworks establish a comprehensive interior design methodology that addresses both the quantitative, logistical demands of event planners and the qualitative, sensory experiences of event-goers within the Calbayog City Convention Center.
In the context of the Calbayog City Convention Center, the existing architectural shell represents the fixed Support. This study operates strictly within the domain of the Infill, defining a non-structural interior overlay for the 840 sqm interior envelope (comprising the 711 sqm Main Hall and 129 sqm Lobby). By treating the interior space as a kinetic, modular system separate from the primary structure, the design framework introduces adaptable layout standards, mobile furniture, fixtures, and equipment (FF&E), and ceiling-suspended track interfaces. This theoretical decoupling provides a structured mechanism for event planners and venue coordinators, enabling rapid setup, teardown, and reconfiguration across diverse event formats (e.g., conferences, exhibitions, banquets) without requiring costly, disruptive civil or structural modifications to the building shell.
While Open Building Theory satisfies physical adaptability, large-scale assembly halls risk becoming sterile, placeless "boxes" if devoid of local context. To ground the qualitative and cultural dimensions of the space, this study integrates the Theory of Place Attachment (Scannell & Gifford, 2010), an environmental psychology framework that outlines how human bonding, identity, and spatial memory are developed through specific environmental interactions. Scannell and Gifford’s tripartite model organizes place attachment across three dimensions: Person (the community and individual identity), Process (the affective, cognitive, and behavioral bonding mechanism), and Place (the spatial and physical characteristics that embody local meaning).
For the event-goers, cultural resonance cannot remain an abstract concept; it must be translated into tangible interior interfaces. This framework directs how localized Samar heritage motifs, such as traditional weaving linework and references to Calbayog’s natural topographies ("The City of Waterfalls"), are abstracted into non-structural interior elements. By embedding these culturally responsive narratives into tactile and visual interior surfaces, including custom laser-cut acoustic panels, woven textile interface screens, and programmed ambient overhead illumination, the design framework transforms the reconfigurable hall from a generic functional shell into a culturally resonant venue that fosters identity, spatial memory, and emotional connection for event-goers across diverse event formats.







Conceptual Framework
Figure 2-18. Conceptual Framework
	The framework synthesizes Open Building Theory (Habraken, 1972) and the Theory of Place Attachment (Scannell & Gifford, 2010). Habraken’s theory establishes the structural building envelope of the convention center as a fixed, immovable "Support," while isolating the 840 sqm interior proposal (711 sqm Main Hall and 129 sqm Lobby) as an adaptable, kinetic "Infill". This directly addresses the active, logistical drivers of the Event Planner, who requires rapid turnaround times, spatial reconfigurability, and ergonomic material handling paths. Concurrently, Scannell and Gifford’s Person-Process-Place (PPP) model grounds the psychological and qualitative dimensions of the space. By linking place attachment to environmental cues, it serves the passive, experiential drivers of the Event-Goer, who seeks acoustic comfort, clear sightlines, intuitive wayfinding, and a cultural connection to Samar heritage.
The convergence of these theoretical pillars and user drivers generates the design intervention. To satisfy the operational requirements of event planners, the framework establishes Modular Interior Layout Standards & Adaptive Space-Planning Typologies alongside Flexible FF&E Configurations & Operational Zoning. These systems provide anthropometrically validated rulesets for furniture mobility, loading ingress/egress staging, and rapid spatial transformations without altering civil or structural components. Simultaneously, to enrich the sensory journey of event-goers, the framework incorporates Tactile & Acoustic Cultural Surface Overlays, which translate local Samar motifs (such as weaving patterns) into acoustic wall treatments and surface finishes. This is supplemented by Adjustable Overhead Lighting & Environmental Track Interfaces, providing adaptive illumination and acoustic baffling that dynamically adjusts to varying event scales.
Ultimately, these core interior interventions converge into a single Unified Design Outcome: a site-specific interior design framework for the Calbayog City Convention Center. By integrating modularity with cultural responsiveness, this framework resolves the operational friction between setup logistics and spatial identity. It transforms the 840 sqm interior envelope (711 sqm Main Hall & 129 sqm Lobby) into a future-proof, highly efficient, and culturally resonant assembly space that maximizes operational efficiency for venue operators while elevating the qualitative, sensory experience for event-goers.
