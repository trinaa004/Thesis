# Research Design

A research design serves as the overarching operational framework that connects research questions to the methodology and analytical strategies executed throughout a study (Creswell & Creswell, 2018; Groat & Wang, 2013). This study adopts an **Applied Design Research** approach executed through a **Convergent Parallel Mixed-Methods Design**, structured across three sequential phases:

### Phase 1: Baseline Fieldwork & Data Collection (QUAN + QUAL)
The investigation concurrently executes two data collection components within the same fieldwork timeframe:

* **Quantitative & Empirical Spatial Component (Surveys, Spatial Audits, & Dual-Modality Event Observations):** Structured survey questionnaires, physical architectural envelope audits, calibrated sound level meter logging (Benetech GM1352), and a **Dual-Modality Spatial Observation Protocol** evaluate baseline indoor environmental quality (IEQ)—specifically attendee satisfaction with lighting, acoustics, and thermal comfort (**Addressing SOP Q1.1 / Objective 1.1**)—alongside spatial usage efficiency, setup turnaround delays, and anthropometric clearance requirements across four primary layout typologies (Plenary, Classroom, Banquet, and Exhibition) and documented hybrid configurations based on ergonomic standards (Panero & Zelnik, 1979; Neufert, 2012) and accessibility mandates (BP 344; ADA, 2010) (**Addressing SOP Q1.2 & SOP Q2 / Objectives 1.2 & 2**). Spatial observations are executed across two complementary streams:
  1. *Primary Direct On-Site Live Event Lifecycle Shadowing (n = 5 contemporary events, October 2026):* Shadowing real-time Ingress setup, In-Session operations, and Egress teardown across contrasting event typologies (*PAGPASAMWAK Caravan*, *Samar Investment Forum & Gala*, *TESDA Graduation*, *Stakeholders' Night*, and *Wedding Banquet*) to document exact clock turnaround durations, physical waiter/attendee spatial collisions, and live acoustic/thermal comfort variations (**Addressing SOP Q1.2 / Objective 1.2**).
  2. *Secondary Archival Photographic & Contextual Content Trace Observation (Zeisel, 2006; Groat & Wang, 2013):* Systematic visual audits of high-resolution interior event photography and published municipal press releases, official posts, and article captions for representative event types across the four operational categories and occupancy tiers in the facility's 12-year longitudinal utilization ledger (N = 127 events, 2014–2026). This protocol audits physical trace evidence of furniture arrays, Monobloc chair grid spacing, unbaffled specular reflections, trailing floor cables, temporary stage backdrops, ground truss footprints, and entrance door obstructions during past events where direct physical attendance was impossible (**Addressing SOP Q2 / Objective 2**).
* **Qualitative Component (Key Informant Interviews):** Semi-structured interviews with event planners, operators, and cultural advocates uncover the operational root causes of setup bottlenecks and logistical friction (**Addressing SOP Q1.2 / Objective 1.2**). Concurrently, this component documents regional Samar cultural and natural heritage, abstracting design motifs from Basey *banig* weaving, *tikog* grass textures, Calbayog waterfall fluid forms, and Hadang festival movements, to guide the development of acoustic wall and ceiling finishes that resonate to the regional Samar heritage (**Addressing SOP Q3.2 / Objective 3.2**). 

### Phase 2: Data Triangulation & Interior Design Synthesis
In this stage, empirical survey metrics, qualitative interview themes, and technical standards are systematically triangulated and translated into an interior proposal. The design process directly integrates statutory accessibility mandates (BP 344; PD 1096 Sec. 1207), anthropometric clearance standards (Panero & Zelnik, 1979; Neufert, 2012), and laboratory-certified acoustic performance criteria (NRC thresholds; Egan, 2007; Cavanaugh et al., 2010) to mathematically govern spatial layouts.

The tangible design deliverables comprise evidence-based interior design interventions featuring:
1. **Modular Layout Typologies & Adaptable Configurations:** 2D CADD drawings detailing the four primary operational typologies (Plenary, Classroom, Banquet, and Exhibition configurations) alongside adaptable configurations for documented compound and dual-zone hybrid setups, with verified aisle widths, wheelchair turning bays, and service corridors (**Addressing SOP Q2 / Objective 2**).
2. **3D Perspective Digital Renderings & Spatial Models:** Visualizing interior volume, sightlines, circulation flow, lighting quality, and aesthetic ambiance across each layout typology (**Addressing SOP Q2 & SOP Q3 / Objectives 2 & 3**).
3. **Acoustic Wall Claddings & Ceiling Baffles:** Detail drawings and material specifications integrating abstracted Samar cultural motifs (Basey *banig* weaving, *tikog* grass textures, Hadang festival kinetic motifs) and Calbayog waterfall fluid forms (**Addressing SOP Q1.1 & SOP Q3.2 / Objectives 1.1 & 3.2**).
4. **Overhead Modular Track-and-Rigging Grid Schematic:** A non-structural ceiling interface supporting multi-scale event lighting and drapery flexibility (**Addressing SOP Q3.1 / Objective 3.1**).

### Phase 3: Post-Design Stakeholder Validation (Evaluative Focus Group)
Because the thesis produces an applied, unbuilt interior design proposal rather than a physically constructed facility, conducting a real-world, on-site Post-Occupancy Evaluation (POE) during the undergraduate research timeframe is beyond the practical and physical scope of this study (Preiser & Vischer, 2005). Pre-construction validity is instead established through a structured **Post-Design Stakeholder Focus Group Discussion (FGD)** (Tremblay, Hevner, & Berndt, 2010; Zeisel, 2006; Sanoff, 2000) (**Addressing & Validating SOP Q2, SOP Q3.1, and SOP Q3.2 / Objectives 2, 3.1, and 3.2**).

A purposive nested panel of key venue stakeholders (7 to 10, comprising professional event planners, venue facility managers, frequent event attendees, and cultural advocates) reviews the synthesized 2D layout drawings, material specifications, and 3D digital models during a synchronous online session hosted via Google Meet. Utilizing a structured Design Verification Rubric and a moderated questioning route (Krueger & Casey, 2014), the panel critically evaluates and validates the proposed interior design interventions' operational turnaround feasibility, anthropometric clearances, acoustic practicality, and cultural resonance across twelve tri-scale operational scenarios (Option A vs. Option B). Stakeholder feedback directly informs final design refinements through a problem-to-design traceability matrix. Formal, longitudinal on-site POE is systematically designated as a future post-construction recommendation for the City Government of Calbayog.
