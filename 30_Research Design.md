# Research Design

A research design serves as the overarching operational framework that connects research questions to the methodology and analytical strategies executed throughout a study (Creswell & Creswell, 2018; Groat & Wang, 2013). This study adopts an **Applied Design Research & Multi-Phase Spatial Synthesis Framework**, integrating quantitative ergonomic/dimensional modeling with qualitative cultural design abstraction to formulate and validate a site-specific, non-structural interior design framework for the 840 square meter Main Hall of the Calbayog City Convention Center.

This study employs an established design synthesis approach (Archer, 1995; Groat & Wang, 2013). The research methodology systematically addresses the study's research objectives through **four interconnected operational phases**.

---

## Phase 1: Quantitative Ergonomic & Dimensional Modeling

The quantitative phase establishes spatial efficiency, occupant capacity, empirical user preferences, and physical clearance parameters derived from venue surveys, international ergonomic standards, spatial planning metrics, and local regulatory mandates. 

1. **Empirical Venue & User Perception Survey**: Distribution of a researcher-developed structured survey questionnaire synthesized from benchmark MICE venue studies (An, Kim, & Hur, 2021; Pembegül, 2009) to 115–145 target respondents (comprising a specialized expert sample of 15–25 event planners/operators and a representative public sample of 100–120 venue attendees) to quantify existing spatial bottlenecks, layout turnaround challenges, and preferences for modular FF&E adaptability.
2. **Dimensional & Envelope Mapping**: Mathematical evaluation of the 840 square meter Main Hall interior footprint, establishing baseline circulation patterns, entrance/egress capacities, and spatial boundary constraints.
3. **Anthropometric Clearance Calculations**: Rigorous application of static and dynamic anthropometric standards (Panero & Zelnik, 1979; Neufert, 2012) to calculate:
   * *Theater (Plenary) Typology*: Seat pitch (900 mm / 35.4 in), clear seat passage (305 mm / 12 in), and vertical sightline offsets (C-values of 60 mm for staggered layouts and 120 mm for un-staggered layouts).
   * *Classroom (Lecture) Typology*: Writing desk surface height (740–760 mm), chair clearance depth (450–500 mm), and service corridor clearance (400–450 mm).
   * *Banquet (Social/Dining) Typology*: 10-person round table circular footprints (3200–3500 mm) and table-to-table service lane clearances (1620–1800 mm).
   * *Exhibition Typology*: Bi-directional multi-lane pedestrian circulation corridors in shoulder-width multiples (750–900 mm single-file, 1500–1800 mm two-person passing, 2400 mm crowd capacity).
4. **Universal Accessibility Compliance**: Integration of Batas Pambansa Bilang 344 (BP 344) and ADA Standards for Accessible Design (2010), verifying wheelchair clear paths (1200 mm), turning radii (1500 mm), and integrated wheelchair assembly seating stalls (760 mm x 1220 mm).

---

## Phase 2: Qualitative Cultural Abstraction & Pattern Language

The qualitative phase investigates how regional Samar heritage can be woven into interior surface treatments to cultivate a distinct sense of place without hindering layout reconfigurability. Drawing on established indigenous pattern abstraction methodologies (Wagas, 2016; Baradas, 1968; Del Monte, 2021), this phase comprises:

1. **Heritage & Material Vocabulary Analysis**: Documentation of traditional Samar *banig* (mat) weaving practices, *tikog* grass textures, regional Waray geometric motifs, and Calbayog’s cascading waterfall imagery—abstracting these elements into a non-structural interior pattern matrix (Garado et al., 2026; Del Monte et al., 2022).
2. **Systematic Pattern Language Abstraction**: Abstracting traditional motif geometries into a digital pattern matrix, converting cultural symbols into reproducible surface datasets for application across upgraded perimeter acoustic drapery and acoustic ceiling overlays.
3. **Non-Structural Surface Application**: Defining material and spatial parameters for integrating abstracted motifs into upgraded perimeter acoustic drapery and ceiling acoustic overlays.

---

## Phase 3: Spatial Synthesis & Framework Formulation

The synthesis phase combines the quantitative spatial parameters from Phase 1 and the qualitative cultural surface modules from Phase 2 into a comprehensive, future-proof interior design framework and layout manual. This synthesis delivers:

1. **Overhead Ceiling & Rigging Interface**: Spatial and functional specifications for a non-structural overhead ceiling grid that combines modular acoustic finish tiles with an adjustable track-and-rigging interface to support flexible lighting, sound distribution, and decorative draping (Phantos, 2025; Cairoli, 2020).
2. **Reconfigurable FF&E Layout Typologies**: A standardized menu of modular, repeatable furniture configurations optimized to maximize configuration speed, spatial flexibility, and setup/teardown efficiency for venue operators.
3. **Future-Proof Material Specifications**: Selection guidelines for sustainable, low-VOC, high-durability acoustic materials, FSC-certified hybrid absorptive-diffusive panels (Chojnacki et al., 2023), and modular furniture components designed for long-term operational adaptability.

---

## Phase 4: Framework Validation & Simulation Verification

To ensure that the synthesized interior design framework and layout manual achieve their operational, ergonomic, and aesthetic objectives, this study executes a two-tier digital simulation and workflow modeling protocol:

1. **Digital Spatial & Accessibility Simulation (CAD Desk Verification)**:
   * Digital 2D & 3D modeling and spatial simulation of all four layout typologies (Plenary, Classroom, Banquet, Exhibition) inside the 840 square meter architectural shell using Computer-Aided Design (CAD) software.
   * Geometric verification of continuous 1200 mm wheelchair travel paths and 1500 mm turning radiuses in strict compliance with Batas Pambansa Bilang 344 (BP 344) and ADA Standards (2010).
   * Vertical sightline offset simulation verifying C-value clearances (60 mm staggered, 120 mm un-staggered) from the rear and side seating boundaries toward the presentation stage.

2. **Logistical Turnaround Workflow Modeling (Efficiency Comparative Simulation)**:
   * Step-by-step comparative workflow simulation mapping the transition sequence between sequential event formats (e.g., Plenary to Banquet).
   * Compares the baseline unstandardized setup process against the standardized modular layout manual, tracking handling distances, equipment staging zones, and crew deployment requirements to substantiate turnaround time optimization.
