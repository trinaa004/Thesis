# Research Design

A research design serves as the overarching operational framework that connects research questions to the methodology and analytical strategies executed throughout a study (Creswell & Creswell, 2018; Groat & Wang, 2013). This study adopts an **Applied Design Research & Multi-Phase Spatial Synthesis Framework** executed through a **Sequential Explanatory Mixed-Methods Design**. This approach combines quantitative spatial and ergonomic modeling with subsequent qualitative inquiry and cultural abstraction to formulate a site-specific, non-structural interior design framework for the 840-square-meter interior envelope (comprising the 711-square-meter Main Convention Hall and 129-square-meter Foyer) of the Calbayog City Convention Center. The research is structured across four interconnected phases that systematically transition from quantitative diagnosis to qualitative elaboration, spatial synthesis, and simulation validation.

The investigation begins with a **quantitative diagnostic phase**, where structured survey questionnaires and physical envelope mapping evaluate baseline indoor environmental quality (IEQ), specifically attendee satisfaction with lighting, acoustics, and thermal comfort, alongside spatial usage efficiency, setup turnaround delays, and anthropometric clearance requirements across four layout typologies (Plenary, Classroom, Banquet, and Exhibition) based on ergonomic standards (Panero & Zelnik, 1979; Neufert, 2012) and accessibility mandates (BP 344; ADA, 2010). Building upon these quantitative findings, the **subsequent qualitative phase** employs semi-structured key informant interviews to explain the operational root causes of these spatial and environmental challenges, while investigating regional Samar heritage, extracting design motifs from Basey *banig* weaving, *tikog* grass textures, and Calbayog waterfall fluid geometry through established pattern abstraction methods to inform surface acoustic finishes.

In the subsequent design synthesis and validation stages, these empirical findings are synthesized into a comprehensive interior design framework and layout manual. This framework integrates standardized modular furniture (FF&E) configurations, sustainable acoustic wall and ceiling treatments, and an adjustable overhead track-and-rigging grid. Finally, the framework is validated through a two-tier protocol: a Computer-Aided Design (CAD) desk simulation verifying circulation, accessibility, and stage sightlines, followed by a time-and-motion comparative workflow simulation demonstrating setup and teardown turnaround efficiency.
