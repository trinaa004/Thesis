# Research Design

A research design serves as the overarching operational framework that connects research questions to the methodology and analytical strategies executed throughout a study (Creswell & Creswell, 2018; Groat & Wang, 2013). This study adopts an **Applied Design Research & Multi-Phase Spatial Synthesis Framework** executed through a **Sequential Explanatory Mixed-Methods Design**. This approach combines quantitative spatial and ergonomic evaluation with subsequent qualitative inquiry and cultural abstraction to formulate a site-specific, non-structural interior design framework for the 840-square-meter interior envelope (comprising the 711-square-meter Main Convention Hall and 129-square-meter Foyer) of the Calbayog City Convention Center. The research is structured across interconnected phases that systematically transition from quantitative diagnosis to qualitative elaboration and spatial synthesis.

The investigation begins with a **quantitative diagnostic phase**, where structured survey questionnaires and physical envelope mapping evaluate baseline indoor environmental quality (IEQ), specifically attendee satisfaction with lighting, acoustics, and thermal comfort, alongside spatial usage efficiency, setup turnaround delays, and anthropometric clearance requirements across four layout typologies (Plenary, Classroom, Banquet, and Exhibition) based on ergonomic standards (Panero & Zelnik, 1979; Neufert, 2012) and accessibility mandates (BP 344; ADA, 2010). Building upon these quantitative findings, the **subsequent qualitative phase** employs semi-structured key informant interviews to explain the operational root causes of these spatial and environmental challenges, while investigating regional Samar heritage, extracting design motifs from Basey *banig* weaving, *tikog* grass textures, and Calbayog waterfall fluid geometry through established pattern abstraction methods to inform surface acoustic finishes.

In the subsequent **design synthesis stage**, these empirical findings are synthesized into a comprehensive interior design framework and layout manual. This framework integrates standardized modular furniture (FF&E) configurations, sustainable acoustic wall and ceiling treatments, and an adjustable overhead track-and-rigging grid to guide the operational modernization and cultural enrichment of the venue.

Because the thesis produces an applied, unbuilt interior design proposal rather than a physically constructed facility, conducting a real-world, on site Post-Occupancy Evaluation (POE) during the undergraduate research timeframe is beyond the practical and physical scope of this study (Preiser & Vischer, 2005). Pre-construction design validity is instead systematically established through a **Pre-Occupancy Predictive Verification Framework** structured across three pillars: (1) **Analog POE & Empirical Precedent Transfer**, drawing operational benchmarks from published post-occupancy studies of analogous convention facilities (Ayuba & Agah, 2018; Baguio BCCC Retrofit, 2022; An et al., 2021); (2) **Spatial & Geometric Clearance Audits**, verifying circulation feasibility, egress capacities, and barrier-free routes across the four layout typologies against statutory accessibility mandates (Batas Pambansa Blg. 344) and anthropometric standards (Neufert, 2012; Panero & Zelnik, 1979); and (3) **Acoustic Material Performance Specifications**, establishing laboratory-certified Noise Reduction Coefficient (NRC) thresholds (Egan, 2007; Cavanaugh et al., 2010) for specified interior wall claddings and ceiling baffles. Formal, longitudinal on site POE is systematically designated as a future post-construction implementation protocol under Chapter 5 recommendations for the City Government of Calbayog.
