## 1. Defending the Four-Layer Interior Framework

### Panel Question:
> *"Why did you break down your interior framework into these four specific layers (Floor, Wall, Ceiling, Materials)? Isn't that tackling too many things at once?"*

### 60-Second Verbal Defense Script:
> **"Thank you for that question, Panel.**
> 
> **I structured my study around these four specific layers because an interior space is a 3-dimensional envelope bounded by three physical planes—the Floor, Wall, and Ceiling—plus the Material Specification layer that coats them.**
> 
> **Each layer directly solves a specific operational problem in the 840 square meter Main Hall:**
> 1. **The Floor Plane** solves **Logistical & Turnaround Efficiency** through reconfigurable FF&E layouts and ergonomic/BP 344 clearances.
> 2. **The Wall Plane** solves **Cultural Identity & Acoustic Comfort** by integrating non-structural Samar heritage acoustic panels.
> 3. **The Ceiling Plane** solves **Volumetric & Spatial Versatility** by using an overhead track-and-rigging grid for mobile drapes and lighting without cluttering the floor plane.
> 4. **The Material Specification Layer** solves **Future-Proofing & Durability** through low-VOC, high-wear acoustic materials.
> 
> **If I omitted any one of these layers, the framework would be incomplete. For example, solving floor layouts without addressing wall acoustics or overhead rigging would leave the venue visually flexible but acoustically unusable. These four layers work together as a single, holistic non-structural toolkit for venue operators."**

### Layer Justification Matrix:
| Interior Layer | Spatial / Functional Problem Solved | Theoretical Anchor & Citations | Primary Output |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Layer 1: Floor Plane** *(FF&E Layouts)* | Setup turnaround delays; circulation bottlenecks; BP 344/ADA accessibility. | **Modular Spatial Theory** (Hashemian, 2005; Ike et al., 2023); **Anthropometrics** (Panero & Zelnik). | Standardized menu of 4 event typologies (Plenary, Classroom, Banquet, Exhibition). |
| **Layer 2: Wall Plane** *(Heritage Acoustic Surfaces)* | Sterile aesthetic lacking regional identity; flutter echo and reverberation. | **Place Attachment** (Kockler, 1989; Garado et al., 2026); **Acoustic Panels** (Chojnacki et al., 2023). | Non-structural acoustic wall modules abstracting Basey *banig*, *tikog* grass, and Waray motifs. |
| **Layer 3: Ceiling Plane** *(Overhead Track & Rigging Grid)* | Rigid lighting; inability to temporarily subdivide the large 840 sqm hall volume. | **Overhead Flexibility** (Phantos, 2025; Cairoli, 2020). | Conceptual overhead track grid for mobile drapes, acoustic baffles, and lighting setups. |
| **Layer 4: Material Layer** *(Future-Proof Material Specs)* | Rapid material wear-and-tear; high VOCs; frequent municipal maintenance costs. | **Future-Proof Design** (DesignHorizons, 2024; Dong et al., 2024). | Low-VOC, high-durability, FSC-certified acoustic and modular FF&E material specifications catalog. |

## 2. Defending Acoustic Reverberation Strategy

### Panel Question:
> *"Did you mention acoustic reverberation in your study? Are you doing acoustic engineering or calculating RT60 decay formulas?"*

### 60-Second Verbal Defense Script:
> **"Thank you, Panel. Yes, uncontrolled acoustic reverberation and echo are explicitly identified in my survey (Items B4 & B5) and literature review as major operational drawbacks of the Main Hall.**
> 
> **However, in strict accordance with the Limitations established in Chapter 1, this study treats acoustics from an Interior Design Passive Strategy standpoint—NOT an acoustic engineering computation.**
> 
> **I do not perform RT60 mathematical decay formulas or STC laboratory testing. Instead, I address sound control spatially by specifying sound-absorptive interior surface treatments—such as porous tikog grass wall paneling, acoustic felt ceiling baffles, and mobile drapery—to absorb sound reflections and improve speech clarity without requiring structural demolition."**

### Acoustic Scope Boundary Matrix:
| In Scope: Interior Design Passive Strategy | Out of Scope: Acoustic Engineering (Explicit Limitations) |
| :--- | :--- |
| • Specifying sound-absorptive materials (felt, *tikog* grass, fabric wall panels). | • Mathematical RT60 reverberation decay time formulas (Sabine equation). |
| • Spatial placement of acoustic panels on walls and ceiling baffles. | • Laboratory STC wall assembly testing or decibel meter sound mapping. |
| • Citing peer-reviewed acoustic studies (*Chojnacki et al., 2023*; *Cairoli, 2020*). | • Active electronic sound-masking or HVAC duct silencer engineering. |

## 3. Defending Dual Cultural Synthesis (Samar Craft vs. Calbayog Waterfall Imagery)

### Panel Question:
> *"Why did you include both Samar and Calbayog in your cultural title? What does 'cascading waterfall imagery' mean inside an interior hall?"*

### 60-Second Verbal Defense Script:
> **"Thank you for that question, Panel.**
> 
> **My study synthesizes two distinct levels of local identity:**
> 1. **Regional Samar Heritage** supplies the physical craft vocabulary—specifically traditional Basey *banig* weaving patterns, *tikog* grass textures, and Waray geometric motifs.
> 2. **Local Calbayog Identity** supplies the municipal natural imagery—specifically referencing Calbayog’s official identity as the 'City of Waterfalls.'
> 
> **'Cascading waterfall imagery' does NOT mean installing physical water fountains inside the hall. It refers to a non-structural visual and acoustic abstraction: translating the fluid, vertical rhythm of waterfalls into undulating overhead acoustic ceiling baffles, suspended drapes, and vertical wall paneling. This balances the structured geometric weaves on the lower walls with fluid overhead movement, improving acoustics while honoring local civic pride."**

## 5. Defending Universal Accessibility & Accessible Seating Stalls in an Empty Hall

### Panel Question:
> *"Why do you mention 'integrated accessible seating stalls' when the Main Hall is just an open, empty floor space without fixed built-in seating?"*

### 60-Second Verbal Defense Script:
> **"Thank you for that question, Panel.**
> 
> **In spatial planning and universal accessibility standards (BP 344 and ADA 2010), an 'accessible seating stall' does NOT mean a built-in booth, cubicle, or permanent physical enclosure.**
> 
> **It refers to a designated 760 mm x 1220 mm clear floor footprint reserved within a temporary furniture layout—such as rows of loose chairs in a Plenary setup or tables in a Classroom setup—so a wheelchair user can park comfortably alongside general attendees, with an adjacent companion seat and direct aisle access for safe egress.**
> 
> **Because the 840 square meter Main Hall is a flexible open shell, venue operators arrange loose furniture per event format. My interior design framework provides pre-calculated layout templates that automatically integrate these 760 mm x 1220 mm spaces into the temporary seating plans, ensuring full BP 344 and ADA regulatory compliance whenever the hall is configured."**

### Accessibility Metric Matrix (Flexible Main Hall Context):
| Accessibility Metric | Spatial Meaning in an Open / Flexible Hall | Standard Specification (BP 344 / ADA 2010) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Accessible Seating Stall** | Reserved 760 mm x 1220 mm clear floor footprint integrated into loose chair/table layout templates next to companion seats. | **760 mm x 1220 mm** ($30\text{ in} \times 48\text{ in}$) clear floor spot + adjacent companion chair. |
| **Wheelchair Corridor Width** | Unobstructed main and cross-aisle walking paths between temporary furniture setups. | Minimum **1200 mm** ($47.2\text{ in}$) clear aisle width. |
| **Wheelchair Turning Radii** | Clear floor clearance at aisle intersections and dead ends for $360^\circ$ turns. | Minimum **1500 mm** ($59.0\text{ in}$) diameter turning circle. |

## 6. Defending Respondent Sampling Methodology & Slovin's Formula Calculation

### Panel Question:
> *"How did you determine your sample size of 115 to 145 respondents? Can you explain how Slovin's Formula calculation was done for the general public, and why you used purposive sampling for experts?"*

### 60-Second Verbal Defense Script:
> **"Thank you for that question, Panel.**
> 
> **My study uses a dual-track mixed sampling strategy targeting two distinct stakeholder groups because operational venue design requires both expert domain knowledge and public user evaluation:**
> 
> **1. For Group 1 (Event Planners & Operators, N = 15–25):** I used **Purposive Expert Sampling**. Because professional event coordinators managing events in this specific venue are a small, specialized population, a large random sample is methodologically inappropriate. Literature (An et al., 2021; Palinkas et al., 2015) confirms that 15 to 25 expert respondents provide high content validity for venue logistics and setup/teardown evaluation.
> 
> **2. For Group 2 (General Public & Event Attendees, N = 100–120):** I used **Purposive Quota Sampling** and applied **Slovin’s Sample Size Formula** ($n = \frac{N}{1 + N(e)^2}$). For an estimated active attendee population of 500 to 1,000+ venue visitors, a sample of 100 to 120 respondents achieves statistical reliability at a 95% confidence level with an acceptable margin of error of $e = 0.08$ to $0.09$ (8%–9%), which is standard for descriptive public architectural perception studies (Creswell & Creswell, 2018; Israel, 1992).
> 
> **3. For Qualitative Interviews (n = 8–12):** 8 to 12 key informants selected from the survey pool achieve **thematic saturation** (Braun & Clarke, 2006).
> 
> **Combined, the 115 to 145 quantitative sample and 8 to 12 qualitative interviews provide a statistically defensible and operationally grounded foundation for the design framework."**

---

### Step-by-Step Mathematical Calculation (Slovin's Formula)

#### **Formula:**
$$n = \frac{N}{1 + N(e)^2}$$

* **$n$** = Required Sample Size
* **$N$** = Total Estimated Population (Active venue visitors over a 5-year recall window, estimated at $N = 500 \text{ to } 1,000$)
* **$e$** = Margin of Error ($0.08 \text{ to } 0.09$, or $8\% \text{ to } 9\%$)
* **Confidence Level** = $95\%$

---

#### **Sample Calculation 1 (Population $N = 500$, Margin of Error $e = 0.085$ / $8.5\%$):**
1. **Square the margin of error ($e^2$):**
   $$e^2 = (0.085)^2 = 0.007225$$
2. **Multiply by population ($N \times e^2$):**
   $$N(e)^2 = 500 \times 0.007225 = 3.6125$$
3. **Add 1 to the denominator ($1 + N(e)^2$):**
   $$1 + 3.6125 = 4.6125$$
4. **Divide total population by denominator ($n = \frac{N}{\text{denominator}}$):**
   $$n = \frac{500}{4.6125} = 108.4 \approx \mathbf{109 \text{ respondents}}$$

---

#### **Sample Calculation 2 (Population $N = 1,000$, Margin of Error $e = 0.09$ / $9\%$):**
1. **Square the margin of error ($e^2$):**
   $$e^2 = (0.09)^2 = 0.0081$$
2. **Multiply by population ($N \times e^2$):**
   $$N(e)^2 = 1,000 \times 0.0081 = 8.1$$
3. **Add 1 to the denominator ($1 + N(e)^2$):**
   $$1 + 8.1 = 9.1$$
4. **Divide total population by denominator ($n = \frac{N}{\text{denominator}}$):**
   $$n = \frac{1000}{9.1} = 109.89 \approx \mathbf{110 \text{ respondents}}$$

### Follow-Up Panel Question:
> *"Where did you get the 500 to 1,000 estimated population figure for the general public?"*

### 60-Second Verbal Defense Script:
> **"Thank you for that follow-up question, Panel.**
> 
> **I derived the 500 to 1,000 estimated population figure based on two operational and physical parameters:**
> 
> **1. Venue Capacity Anchor:** The 840 square meter Main Hall of the Calbayog City Convention Center has a maximum single-event capacity of **500 occupants** (for assembly/plenary layouts).
> **2. Target Inclusion Filter (5-Year Recall Window):** Because the study filters respondents to adults (18+) who have attended or organized an event inside the Main Hall within the past five years (2021–2026), 500 to 1,000 is a conservative estimate of the *accessible target population of active, recurring venue visitors* in Calbayog City across civic, corporate, trade, and social event formats.
> 
> **3. Mathematical Plateau (The 'Safety Net' Argument):** Mathematically, Slovin’s formula reaches a stability plateau around 100 to 120 respondents. Even if the population estimate were assumed to be much larger—such as $N = 5,000$ or even $10,000$—Slovin’s formula at $e = 0.09$ yields $n = 120.4 \approx 121$ respondents. Therefore, our sample size of **100 to 120 public respondents** mathematically guarantees a 95% confidence level and 8%–9% margin of error regardless of minor population size variations."**
