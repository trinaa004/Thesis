## 1. Defending the Four-Layer Interior Framework

### Panel Question:
> *"Why did you break down your interior framework into these four specific layers (Floor, Wall, Ceiling, Materials)? Isn't that tackling too many things at once?"*

### 60-Second Verbal Defense Script:
> **"Thank you for that question, Panel.**
> 
> **I structured my study around these four specific layers because an interior space is a 3-dimensional envelope bounded by three physical planes—the Floor, Wall, and Ceiling—plus the Material Specification layer that coats them.**
> 
> **Each layer directly solves a specific operational problem in the 840 square meter Main Hall:**
> 1. **The Floor Plane** solves **Logistical & Turnaround Efficiency** through reconfigurable FF&E layouts and ergonomic/BP 344 clearances.
> 2. **The Wall Plane** solves **Cultural Identity & Acoustic Comfort** by integrating non-structural Samar heritage acoustic panels.
> 3. **The Ceiling Plane** solves **Volumetric & Spatial Versatility** by using an overhead track-and-rigging grid for mobile drapes and lighting without cluttering the floor plane.
> 4. **The Material Specification Layer** solves **Future-Proofing & Durability** through low-VOC, high-wear acoustic materials.
> 
> **If I omitted any one of these layers, the framework would be incomplete. For example, solving floor layouts without addressing wall acoustics or overhead rigging would leave the venue visually flexible but acoustically unusable. These four layers work together as a single, holistic non-structural toolkit for venue operators."**

### Layer Justification Matrix:
| Interior Layer | Spatial / Functional Problem Solved | Theoretical Anchor & Citations | Primary Output |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Layer 1: Floor Plane** *(FF&E Layouts)* | Setup turnaround delays; circulation bottlenecks; BP 344/ADA accessibility. | **Modular Spatial Theory** (Hashemian, 2005; Ike et al., 2023); **Anthropometrics** (Panero & Zelnik). | Standardized menu of 4 event typologies (Plenary, Classroom, Banquet, Exhibition). |
| **Layer 2: Wall Plane** *(Heritage Acoustic Surfaces)* | Sterile aesthetic lacking regional identity; flutter echo and reverberation. | **Place Attachment** (Kockler, 1989; Garado et al., 2026); **Acoustic Panels** (Chojnacki et al., 2023). | Non-structural acoustic wall modules abstracting Basey *banig*, *tikog* grass, and Waray motifs. |
| **Layer 3: Ceiling Plane** *(Overhead Track & Rigging Grid)* | Rigid lighting; inability to temporarily subdivide the large 840 sqm hall volume. | **Overhead Flexibility** (Phantos, 2025; Cairoli, 2020). | Conceptual overhead track grid for mobile drapes, acoustic baffles, and lighting setups. |
| **Layer 4: Material Layer** *(Future-Proof Material Specs)* | Rapid material wear-and-tear; high VOCs; frequent municipal maintenance costs. | **Future-Proof Design** (DesignHorizons, 2024; Dong et al., 2024). | Low-VOC, high-durability, FSC-certified acoustic and modular FF&E material specifications catalog. |

## 2. Defending Acoustic Reverberation Strategy

### Panel Question:
> *"Did you mention acoustic reverberation in your study? Are you doing acoustic engineering or calculating RT60 decay formulas?"*

### 60-Second Verbal Defense Script:
> **"Thank you, Panel. Yes, uncontrolled acoustic reverberation and echo are explicitly identified in my survey (Items B4 & B5) and literature review as major operational drawbacks of the Main Hall.**
> 
> **However, in strict accordance with the Limitations established in Chapter 1, this study treats acoustics from an Interior Design Passive Strategy standpoint—NOT an acoustic engineering computation.**
> 
> **I do not perform RT60 mathematical decay formulas or STC laboratory testing. Instead, I address sound control spatially by specifying sound-absorptive interior surface treatments—such as porous tikog grass wall paneling, acoustic felt ceiling baffles, and mobile drapery—to absorb sound reflections and improve speech clarity without requiring structural demolition."**

### Acoustic Scope Boundary Matrix:
| In Scope: Interior Design Passive Strategy | Out of Scope: Acoustic Engineering (Explicit Limitations) |
| :--- | :--- |
| • Specifying sound-absorptive materials (felt, *tikog* grass, fabric wall panels). | • Mathematical RT60 reverberation decay time formulas (Sabine equation). |
| • Spatial placement of acoustic panels on walls and ceiling baffles. | • Laboratory STC wall assembly testing or decibel meter sound mapping. |
| • Citing peer-reviewed acoustic studies (*Chojnacki et al., 2023*; *Cairoli, 2020*). | • Active electronic sound-masking or HVAC duct silencer engineering. |

## 3. Defending Dual Cultural Synthesis (Samar Craft vs. Calbayog Waterfall Imagery)

### Panel Question:
> *"Why did you include both Samar and Calbayog in your cultural title? What does 'cascading waterfall imagery' mean inside an interior hall?"*

### 60-Second Verbal Defense Script:
> **"Thank you for that question, Panel.**
> 
> **My study synthesizes two distinct levels of local identity:**
> 1. **Regional Samar Heritage** supplies the physical craft vocabulary—specifically traditional Basey *banig* weaving patterns, *tikog* grass textures, and Waray geometric motifs.
> 2. **Local Calbayog Identity** supplies the municipal natural imagery—specifically referencing Calbayog’s official identity as the 'City of Waterfalls.'
> 
> **'Cascading waterfall imagery' does NOT mean installing physical water fountains inside the hall. It refers to a non-structural visual and acoustic abstraction: translating the fluid, vertical rhythm of waterfalls into undulating overhead acoustic ceiling baffles, suspended drapes, and vertical wall paneling. This balances the structured geometric weaves on the lower walls with fluid overhead movement, improving acoustics while honoring local civic pride."**

## 4. Defending Scope & Limitations

### Panel Question:
> *"Why aren't you proposing structural modifications, MEP ducting rerouting, or venue business management models?"*

### 60-Second Verbal Defense Script:
> **"Panel, to maintain a focused research scope within Interior Design professional boundaries, this study is strictly a non-structural interior overlay.**
> 
> **Structural modifications to load-bearing columns or roof trusses are excluded because municipal facilities require solutions that minimize capital expenditure and structural risk. MEP schematics, HVAC rerouting, and financial budgeting are intentionally excluded to keep the focus strictly on physical spatial planning, furniture ergonomics, and material surface design."**
