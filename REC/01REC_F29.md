## 1. Scientific Background / Context and Literature Review Summary

The global expansion of the Meetings, Incentives, Conventions, and Exhibitions (MICE) industry has driven a paradigm shift in civic venue design, prioritizing spatial adaptability, configuration speed, and experiential user engagement over traditional rigid corporate layouts (An, Kim, & Hur, 2021; Ike et al., 2023). However, municipal facilities such as the Calbayog City Convention Center frequently operate within static interior envelopes that result in prolonged setup/teardown turnaround times, service circulation bottlenecks, acoustic reverberation issues, and sterile aesthetics that lack regional civic identity (Etteh, 2016; Fakharany, 2025). Grounded in modular spatial theory (Hashemian, 2005), future-proof design principles (DesignHorizons, 2024), overhead utility track/rigging interfaces (Phantos, 2025), universal accessibility mandates (BP 344; ADA), and culturally responsive design frameworks (Kockler, 1989; Krstić et al., 2024), this study addresses these operational and aesthetic deficiencies by formulating a site-specific, non-structural interior design framework and layout manual for the venue's 840 square meter interior envelope (comprising the 711 square meter Main Hall and 129 square meter Foyer). Adopting an Applied Design Research approach, the study utilizes a convergent parallel mixed-methods design combining a researcher-developed survey questionnaire (N = 115 – 145 event planners and attendees, synthesized from An et al., 2021, and Pembegül, 2009, with a 10-individual pilot test), semi-structured qualitative interviews (n = 8 – 12), and non-participatory architectural spatial mapping. By integrating modular furniture typologies (Plenary, Classroom, Banquet, Exhibition) with localized pattern surface  treatments abstracting Samar Basey *banig* weaving, *tikog* grass, and Calbayog waterfall motifs, this research establishes an empirically validated, non-structural design framework that optimizes operational turnaround efficiency, acoustic comfort, and cultural sense of place without incurring structural risks or invasive physical alterations.

---

## 2. Rationale / Significance

While existing MICE literature heavily emphasizes mega-scale international venues and structural architectural expansion (An et al., 2021; Lee & Fenich, 2016), a critical research gap persists regarding non-structural interior adaptive strategies for regional municipal convention facilities. Furthermore, current interior literature frequently separates functional spatial modularity from cultural heritage preservation, failing to integrate localized identity into operational venue infrastructure (Fakharany, 2025; Roa & Lagamon, 2018). This study addresses these literature gaps by formulating a site-specific, non-structural interior design framework consisting of modular interior typologies, ergonomic layout standards, and overhead ceiling interfaces for the 840 square meter interior envelope (711 sqm Main Hall and 129 sqm Foyer) of the Calbayog City Convention Center. By harmonizing flexible spatial configurations with Samar and Calbayog's identity and sustainable material specifications without requiring invasive structural modifications, the generated framework offers direct operational utility to **event planners and facility operators** by providing pre-calculated layout standards that eliminate trial-and-error space planning and optimize setup/teardown turnaround times; enhances spatial accessibility (BP 344/ADA), acoustic comfort, and civic identity for **event-goers and the local community**; equips the **local government** with a high-performance civic venue that supports eco-tourism and regional economic growth; and provides **interior design professionals and researchers** with a replicable model for non-structural civic interior revitalization.

## 3. Research Question(s) / General Objective(s)

The general objective of this study is to formulate a site-specific, non-structural interior design framework and layout manual for the 840 square meter interior envelope (comprising the 711 square meter Main Hall and 129 square meter Foyer) of the Calbayog City Convention Center by integrating spatial modularity, local Samar heritage, and future-proof event adaptability. Specifically, the study seeks to investigate current spatial and operational bottlenecks within the venue's interior envelope; determine ergonomic furniture, fixtures, and equipment (FF&E) density typologies (Plenary, Classroom, Banquet, Exhibition) and circulation paths that maximize setup/teardown turnaround efficiency and universal accessibility (BP 344/ADA); synthesize traditional Samar Basey *banig* weaving patterns, *tikog* grass textures, and Calbayog waterfall motifs into non-structural acoustic surface treatments to cultivate civic identity; propose an adaptable overhead ceiling track-and-rigging interface for dynamic lighting and drapery control; and specify sustainable, low-VOC acoustic materials to ensure long-term operational resilience.

---

## 4. Sub-question(s) / Specific Objective(s)

Specifically, this study seeks to address the following specific objectives:

*  Evaluate existing layout configurations, user circulation bottlenecks, and physical material conditions within the Calbayog City Convention Center's 840 square meter interior envelope (711 sqm Main Hall and 129 sqm Foyer).
*  Formulate reconfigurable furniture layouts and interior zoning strategies (Plenary, Classroom, Banquet, Exhibition) that maximize spatial capacity, enforce universal accessibility (BP 344/ADA), and optimize setup/teardown turnaround speed.
*  Synthesize culturally-responsive design elements—abstracting traditional Samar Basey *banig* weaving patterns, *tikog* grass textures, and regional Waray geometric motifs—into non-structural acoustic paneling to cultivate civic identity and mitigate reverberation.
* Propose an adaptable overhead ceiling system combining modular acoustic ceiling tiles with a flexible track-and-rigging interface to facilitate dynamic alterations in ambient lighting, acoustic drapes, and spatial dividers.
* Apply future-proof interior specifications regarding sustainable, low-VOC, high-durability, and acoustic materials to maintain long-term operational resilience.

## 5. Justification for the Need to Involve Human Participant(s)

Involving human participants is essential to this study because evaluating operational venue adaptability and experiential interior design cannot be achieved through theoretical layout modeling alone; it requires empirical insights from the primary human agents who operate within and experience the physical envelope of the Calbayog City Convention Center. The study recruits two targeted stakeholder groups to capture distinct dimensions of the venue: specialized event planners, coordinators, and facility operators (N = 15–25 expert sample; n = 8–12 interview key informants) provide indispensable domain expertise regarding real-world setup and teardown turnaround bottlenecks, furniture reconfigurability, spatial circulation clearance, and overhead utility track requirements; while general event-goers and community members (N = 100–120 public sample) evaluate the human experience of the space, including interior acoustic comfort, seating sightlines, intuitive wayfinding, and the subjective cultural resonance of abstracting traditional Samar Basey *banig* weaving patterns and Calbayog waterfall motifs into interior surface finishes. Involving these participants through a convergent parallel mixed-methods approach (concurrent quantitative surveys and qualitative semi-structured interviews) ensures that the resulting non-structural interior design framework is empirically grounded in actual operational workflows, human ergonomic needs, and community civic identity.

## METHODOLOGY

### 7. Research Design, Philosophical Paradigm, and Theoretical Framework

This study adopts a **Convergent Parallel Mixed-Methods Applied Design Research** design grounded in a **Pragmatic Paradigm (Pragmatism)**, which prioritizes real-world problem-solving, functional venue utility, and the synthesis of empirical quantitative metrics with qualitative human experiences to generate an actionable design manual for the 840 square meter interior envelope (711 sqm Main Hall and 129 sqm Foyer) of the Calbayog City Convention Center. The research design executes two concurrent data gathering strands: a **Quantitative Diagnostic Strand** evaluating spatial envelope dimensions, anthropometric seat pitch and clearance standards (Panero & Zelnik, 1979; Neufert, 2012), universal accessibility mandates (BP 344; ADA Standards 2010), and venue turnaround delays via a structured survey questionnaire ($N = 115–145$ event planners and attendees); and a concurrent **Qualitative Inquiry Strand** gathering semi-structured key informant interview feedback ($n = 8–12$) and conducting spatial/pattern mapping to abstract local Samar Basey *banig* weaving heritage, *tikog* grass textures, and Calbayog waterfall motifs into non-structural acoustic surface panels. This methodology is theoretically anchored in the conceptual synthesis of **Open Building Theory** (Habraken, 1972) and the **Theory of Place Attachment** (Scannell & Gifford, 2010). Habraken’s theory establishes the fixed building shell as a "Support" and the 840 sqm interior envelope (711 sqm Main Hall + 129 sqm Foyer) as an adaptable "Infill," providing the rationale for non-structural modular spatial layouts, flexible FF&E typologies, and future-proof overhead track interfaces. Concurrently, Scannell and Gifford’s Person-Process-Place (PPP) model provides the psychological foundation for translating Samar Basey *banig* weaving heritage and Calbayog waterfall motifs into tactile acoustic surface treatments that cultivate civic identity and spatial memory without incurring structural alterations.

### 8. Research Setting / Environment

The study will be conducted within the 840 square meter interior envelope (comprising the 711 square meter Main Hall and 129 square meter Foyer) of the Calbayog City Convention Center, located in Brgy. Capoocan, Calbayog City, Samar, it is one of the city's municipal MICE facility servicing high-density gatherings. This centralized municipal locale provides an accessible, physically safe, and highly convenient site for administering structured public surveys and conducting non-participatory spatial observations without interrupting ongoing venue operations. To ensure participant privacy and data confidentiality, qualitative key informant interviews will take place in private administrative spaces on-site or via secure digital teleconferencing platforms, with all responses anonymized using alphanumeric codes in accordance with Republic Act No. 10173 (Data Privacy Act of 2012).

---

### 9. Study Population

The study target population comprises a total quantitative sample of 115 to 145 human participants alongside a qualitative subset of 8 to 12 key informants, all of whom are at least 18 years of age and have organized, managed, or attended events inside the Calbayog City Convention Center within the past five years. The first participant group consists of 15 to 25 professional event planners, coordinators, venue managers, and logistical operators recruited via purposive expert sampling to evaluate operational logistics, setup/teardown turnaround bottlenecks, FF&E reconfigurability, and ceiling rigging utility. The second participant group comprises 100 to 120 general community members and event attendees selected through purposive quota sampling (calculated using Slovin’s formula at a 95% confidence level) to evaluate interior comfort, circulation efficiency, seating sightlines, acoustic quality, and cultural ambiance. Additionally, a qualitative sample of 8 to 12 key informants will be purposively selected from the expert survey pool to participate in 20-to-30-minute semi-structured interviews until thematic saturation is achieved. Data collection is further complemented by non-participatory spatial observations and envelope mapping of the 840 square meter interior envelope (711 sqm Main Hall and 129 sqm Foyer) to document physical dimensions, circulation flow, and existing interior finish conditions.

---

### 10. Inclusion Criteria

To be eligible for inclusion in this study, potential participants must satisfy three explicit criteria: (1) be at least 18 years of age at the time of data collection, (2) have organized, managed, facilitated, or attended at least one event inside the 840 square meter interior envelope (Main Hall or Foyer) of the Calbayog City Convention Center within the past five years, and (3) possess direct familiarity with the venue's interior layout or the regional cultural context of Samar. Restricting participation to adults aged 18 years and older is ethically and methodologically justified as it ensures participants possess full legal capacity to independently grant informed consent without requiring parental or guardian assent, while guaranteeing that respondents have reached the legal age of maturity, cognitive development, and civic/professional standing necessary to evaluate complex operational venue logistics, spatial circulation clearance, acoustic comfort, and cultural interior design identity. Furthermore, enforcing the five-year recall window ensures that participant feedback reflects contemporary event formats, current facility conditions, and recent operational turnaround challenges inside the convention center.

*Inclusivity and Physical Accessibility Accommodations:* Participant recruitment actively accommodates and encourages the inclusion of individuals with diverse physical accessibility needs, specifically persons with mobility impairments (such as wheelchair users and individuals utilizing assistive mobility devices). The inclusion of participants with physical mobility limitations is essential to critically evaluate the venue's spatial clearances, corridor widths, ramp transitions, integrated accessible seating stalls, and barrier-free circulation against Batas Pambansa Bilang 344 (BP 344 - Accessibility Law) and universal design criteria. To facilitate unhindered participation, research instruments will be administered in physically accessible venue zones, offered via digital formats (Google Forms), or completed with researcher assistance upon request.

---

### 11. Exclusion Criteria

Despite satisfying the general inclusion criteria, potential participants will be explicitly excluded from the study sample under the following conditions: (1) individuals who participated in the pilot pre-testing phase ($n = 10$) of the survey questionnaire, to prevent familiarity bias and data contamination; (2) individuals whose experience with the venue is strictly confined to ancillary, exterior, or administrative zones without direct operational or event-going experience inside the 840 square meter interior envelope (Main Hall or Foyer); (3) individuals with cognitive, sensory, or communication barriers that preclude independent or assisted completion of the research instruments (noting that physical mobility impairments are fully accommodated and not excluded); and (4) individuals who decline to sign the Informed Consent Form or exercise their right to withdraw prior to survey completion or interview transcription.

### 12. Sampling Procedure

Participant recruitment and spatial sampling will follow a systematic, phased protocol beginning with official written authorization from Calbayog City Convention Center management and local municipal authorities. Prior to main field sampling, the researcher-developed survey instrument will undergo pilot pre-testing with 10 individuals (event coordinators and attendees excluded from the final sample) to establish item readability and scale reliability (Cronbach's alpha). Following instrument refinement, prospective main participants—comprising 15 to 25 event planners and venue operators recruited via purposive expert sampling, 100 to 120 event attendees selected through purposive quota sampling, and 8 to 12 qualitative key informants—will be formally invited through direct outreach or on-site contact. Prior to making any decision regarding participation, candidates will be provided with an Informed Consent Form (in English and Waray/Filipino) detailing the study's scope, voluntary participation, non-penalized withdrawal, and confidentiality protections under Republic Act No. 10173 (Data Privacy Act of 2012). Executing signed or digital opt-in consent is mandatory *before* participants begin completing self-administered questionnaires (printed or Google Forms) or participating in 20-to-30-minute semi-structured interviews (audio-recorded with explicit consent for verbatim transcription), which is integrated with non-participatory architectural mapping of the 840 square meter interior envelope (711 sqm Main Hall & 129 sqm Foyer) conducted during active event setups and non-event periods.

### 13. Pre-Data Gathering Coordinative Actions

Prior to initiating data collection, formal coordinative actions will be executed across all institutional, operational, and community channels following approval by the University of San Carlos Research Ethics Committee (USC REC). First, official transmittal and permission letters will be submitted to the Calbayog City Convention Center management and local municipal authorities to secure administrative site access, access to venue event logs, and authorization for on-site mapping of the 840 square meter interior envelope (711 sqm Main Hall and 129 sqm Foyer). Second, formal invitation letters detailing the study's scope, voluntary nature, and time commitment will be transmitted directly to identified event planners, venue operators, and production managers for expert survey and interview recruitment. Third, to recruit general venue attendees, call-for-participation posters will be displayed on venue bulletin boards during scheduled public events, supplemented by digital call-for-participation announcements posted on verified local community and civic social media pages. All promotional materials, invitation letters, and transmittal documents will feature official university credentials, researcher contact information, and explicit notifications regarding voluntary participation and Data Privacy Act (RA 10173) safeguards prior to participant engagement.

### 14. Sampling Design / Strategy

This study employs a dual-track mixed sampling strategy combining non-probability **Purposive Expert Sampling** for operational stakeholders and **Purposive Quota Sampling** (calculated via Slovin’s formula at a 95% confidence level, $e = 0.08–0.09$) for public venue attendees. Participant recruitment will be executed in two targeted streams: first, professional event planners, coordinators, venue managers, and production operators ($N = 15–25$) will be directly recruited through formal transmittal and invitation letters to evaluate venue turnaround logistics, FF&E reconfigurability, and ceiling track utility; second, general community members and event attendees ($N = 100–120$) will be recruited via physical call-for-participation posters on venue bulletin boards during scheduled public events and digital announcements on local civic social media pages to evaluate interior comfort, sightlines, acoustics, and cultural identity. Following quantitative survey collection, a qualitative subset of 8 to 12 key informants will be purposively selected from the expert survey pool for 20-to-30-minute semi-structured interviews to achieve thematic saturation regarding spatial layouts and cultural surface abstractions.

### 15. Sample Size

This convergent parallel mixed-methods study targets a total quantitative sample size of **115 to 145 human participants** alongside a distinct qualitative subset of **8 to 12 key informants** derived from the 840 square meter interior envelope (711 sqm Main Hall and 129 sqm Foyer) of the Calbayog City Convention Center. For the quantitative Phase 1, the sample size is divided into two distinct stakeholder groups: Group 1 comprises **15 to 25 professional event planners, coordinators, and facility operators** selected to provide high content validity regarding operational setup/teardown turnaround bottlenecks and ceiling rigging loads, aligned with expert panel standards in specialized facility literature (An et al., 2021; Pembegül, 2009); Group 2 comprises **100 to 120 general event attendees and community members**, computed using Slovin’s sample size formula ($n = \frac{N}{1 + N(e)^2}$) based on an estimated active attendee population of $N = 500 \text{ to } 1,000$, achieving statistical power and reliability at a 95% confidence level with an acceptable margin of error ranging from $e = 0.08 \text{ to } 0.09$ (Israel, 1992; Creswell & Creswell, 2018). For the qualitative Phase 2, a purposively selected sample of **8 to 12 key informants** will participate in 20-to-30-minute semi-structured interviews, a sample size empirically established in methodological literature to reach thematic saturation when evaluating complex operational workflows and cultural surface pattern abstractions (Braun & Clarke, 2006; Guest et al., 2006).

### 16. Data Collection Tools, Technologies, and Techniques

Data collection utilizes researcher-developed structured survey questionnaires (printed forms and Google Forms) alongside an open-ended semi-structured interview guide, supported by encrypted digital voice recorders for audio transcription and spatial measuring/photographic tools for envelope mapping. The survey questionnaires comprise close-ended 5-point Likert scale and multiple-choice items structured across six core functional domains: demographic/professional profiles, baseline indoor environmental quality and spatial operational challenges (SPOP Q1), modular zoning and FF&E flexibility (SPOP Q2), Samar-Calbayog cultural material integration (SPOP Q3), overhead ceiling track adaptability, and universal accessibility and future-proof material specifications (SPOP Q2 & Q4). Concurrently, the qualitative interview protocol employs open-ended questions to gather in-depth operational and cultural insights from key informants ($n = 8–12$), complemented by an observation checklist to systematically evaluate circulation clearances and physical finish conditions inside the 840 square meter interior envelope (711 sqm Main Hall and 129 sqm Foyer).

### 17. Validation and Pre-Testing Procedure

To ensure data reliability, structural clarity, and participant comprehension, the researcher-developed data collection instruments will undergo pilot pre-testing prior to main field deployment. First, the quantitative survey instrument will undergo pilot pre-testing with 10 individuals (event coordinators and frequent venue attendees excluded from the final sample) to evaluate item readability, contextual flow, and scale reliability (Cronbach's alpha), ensuring internal consistency before final field deployment. Second, to ensure complete participant comprehension across diverse demographic backgrounds in Calbayog City and Samar, research instruments will feature contextualized phrasing in English, supported by bilingual (Waray/Filipino) translations and on-site assistance as needed.

### 18. Data Collection and Analysis Procedure

The execution of data collection and subsequent analysis will follow a structured convergent parallel timeline operating across a designated fieldwork period. Participants completing the self-administered quantitative survey questionnaire (printed hard copies or Google Forms) will require approximately 10 to 15 minutes, with physical forms retrieved immediately in sealed envelopes and digital entries auto-encrypted in password-protected databases; concurrently, key informants participating in qualitative semi-structured interviews will engage in 20-to-30-minute audio-recorded sessions, which are transcribed verbatim for thematic analysis. Grounded in a convergent parallel mixed-methods design, quantitative survey findings and qualitative interview narratives are gathered concurrently, analyzed independently, and subsequently triangulated during spatial synthesis to inform layout manual specifications. Finally, data validity is reinforced through methodological and data source triangulation, cross-verifying quantitative Likert perception scores from event planners and attendees, qualitative interview themes from venue administrators, and empirical field mapping notes from on-site spatial observations to synthesize a defensible non-structural interior design framework for the 840 square meter interior envelope (711 sqm Main Hall & 129 sqm Foyer).

### 19. Analytical Treatment of Data

The analytical treatment of data combines quantitative descriptive statistics, qualitative thematic analysis, and data management protocols tailored to the study's convergent parallel mixed-methods applied design framework:

* **Data Processing & Management:** Prior to analysis, quantitative survey responses from printed hard copies and Google Forms will undergo double-entry verification, data cleaning, and systematic coding using Microsoft Excel. Qualitative interview audio recordings will be transcribed verbatim into text transcripts, anonymized using alphanumeric codes (e.g., Key Informant P-01), and secured in encrypted, password-protected cloud storage.
* **Quantitative Descriptive Statistical Treatment:** As a descriptive applied design study, quantitative survey indicators across Form A and Form B will be analyzed using descriptive statistical techniques in Microsoft Excel, specifically calculating frequencies, percentage distributions, measures of central tendency (weighted means), and measures of dispersion (standard deviations). Results will be visually synthesized using structured frequency tables, bar charts, and evaluation matrices to establish spatial baseline priorities for setup turnaround efficiency, FF&E modularity, overhead track utility, and cultural surface preferences.
* **Qualitative Thematic Analysis:** Qualitative interview transcriptions will be analyzed following Braun and Clarke’s (2006) six-phase thematic analysis framework, grounded in a Pragmatic research paradigm. The analysis will execute an iterative coding process—combining inductive coding of operational bottlenecks with deductive thematic mapping anchored in Open Building Theory (Habraken, 1972) and Theory of Place Attachment (Scannell & Gifford, 2010)—to identify recurring themes, spatial requirements, and heritage pattern abstractions.
* **Analysis Reliability & Trustworthiness:** To ensure analytical rigor, trustworthiness, and reliability, the study employs a comprehensive audit trail documenting raw survey entries, coding schemas, and thematic matrices. Furthermore, methodological and data source triangulation will cross-verify quantitative weighted means, qualitative thematic insights, and empirical spatial observation notes to ensure that the final non-structural interior design manual is empirically validated and operationally sound.

### 20. Ethical Soundness

To uphold the highest standards of research ethics and human participant protection throughout the study, the researcher adheres to five fundamental ethical protocols:

* **Declaration of Conflict of Interest:** The researcher declares no financial, institutional, political, or personal conflicts of interest regarding the Calbayog City Convention Center or its operational management. As an independent academic investigation conducted in partial fulfillment of the Bachelor of Science in Interior Design degree at the University of San Carlos, the study receives no external commercial funding or institutional co-sponsorship.
* **Ethics Clearance & Operational Approvals:** Formal data collection will commence only after securing official research ethics clearance from the University of San Carlos Research Ethics Committee (USC REC) and written administrative authorization/transmittal approval from the Calbayog City Convention Center management and local municipal authorities of Calbayog City, Samar.
* **Informed Consent & Debriefing Protocols:** Prior to engaging in survey or interview administration, all prospective participants will be provided with an Informed Consent Form presented in English with Waray/Filipino language support. The document explicitly outlines the study's objectives, voluntary participation, non-penalized right of withdrawal at any point, and data protection safeguards. Signed or digital opt-in consent must be granted before data collection starts. Upon survey or interview completion, respondents will receive a brief verbal debriefing explaining how their spatial feedback contributes to formulating the site-specific interior design framework.
* **Participant Safety, Convenience, & Privacy Protection:** Data collection procedures are designed to ensure maximum physical safety and convenience, offering flexible on-site paper administration during public events or digital self-administration via Google Forms, alongside private or online teleconference options for key informant interviews. Participant privacy is strictly safeguarded through complete de-identification and alphanumeric coding (e.g., Respondent A-01, Key Informant P-01), guaranteeing that no direct personal identifiers (such as names or contact info) are attached to reported survey trends or interview quotes.
* **Data Protection, Storage, & Disposal Lifecycle:** In compliance with Republic Act No. 10173 (Data Privacy Act of 2012), rigorous data security protocols govern all stages of data handling. Physical paper survey questionnaires will be stored in locked security cabinets accessible exclusively by the researcher; while digital survey spreadsheets, audio recordings, and verbatim transcriptions will be encrypted and stored on password-protected cloud servers with two-factor authentication. To eliminate re-identification risk, a master linking key (pairing participant real names with assigned alphanumeric codes) will be maintained in an isolated, encrypted directory and permanently destroyed via digital file shredding within thirty (30) calendar days after the final thesis defense and manuscript approval, rendering the remaining analytical dataset permanently anonymized. Upon expiration of the subsequent one-year institutional retention window, all physical survey sheets will be shredded, and electronic files permanently purged from digital storage.
