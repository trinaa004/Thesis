## 1. Scientific Background / Context and Literature Review Summary

The global expansion of the Meetings, Incentives, Conventions, and Exhibitions (MICE) industry has driven a paradigm shift in civic venue design, prioritizing spatial adaptability, configuration speed, and experiential user engagement over traditional rigid corporate layouts (An, Kim, & Hur, 2021; Ike et al., 2023). However, municipal facilities such as the Calbayog City Convention Center frequently operate within static interior envelopes that result in prolonged setup/teardown turnaround times, service circulation bottlenecks, acoustic reverberation issues, and sterile aesthetics that lack regional civic identity (Etteh, 2016; Fakharany, 2025). Grounded in modular spatial theory (Hashemian, 2005), future-proof design principles (DesignHorizons, 2024), overhead utility track/rigging interfaces (Phantos, 2025), universal accessibility mandates (BP 344; ADA), and culturally responsive design frameworks (Kockler, 1989; Krstić et al., 2024), this study addresses these operational and aesthetic deficiencies by formulating a site-specific, non-structural interior design framework and layout manual for the venue's 840 square meter Main Hall. Adopting an Applied Design Research approach, the study utilizes a sequential explanatory mixed-methods design combining a researcher-developed survey questionnaire (N = 115 – 145 event planners and attendees, synthesized from An et al., 2021, and Pembegül, 2009, with a 10-individual pilot test), semi-structured qualitative interviews (n = 8 – 12), and non-participatory architectural spatial mapping. By integrating modular furniture typologies (Plenary, Classroom, Banquet, Exhibition) with localized surface pattern matrices abstracting Samar Basey *banig* weaving, *tikog* grass, and Calbayog waterfall motifs, this research establishes an empirically validated, non-structural design framework that optimizes operational turnaround efficiency, acoustic comfort, and cultural sense of place without incurring structural risks or invasive physical alterations.

## 2. Rationale / Significance

While existing MICE literature heavily emphasizes mega-scale international venues and structural architectural expansion (An et al., 2021; Lee & Fenich, 2016), a critical research gap persists regarding non-structural interior adaptive strategies for regional municipal convention facilities. Furthermore, current interior literature frequently separates functional spatial modularity from cultural heritage preservation, failing to integrate localized identity into operational venue infrastructure (Fakharany, 2025; Roa & Lagamon, 2018). This study addresses these literature gaps by formulating a site-specific, non-structural interior design framework consisting of modular interior typologies, ergonomic layout standards, and overhead ceiling interfaces for the 840 square meter Main Hall of the Calbayog City Convention Center. By harmonizing flexible spatial configurations with Samar and Calbayog's identity and sustainable material specifications without requiring invasive structural modifications, the generated framework offers direct operational utility to **event planners and facility operators** by providing pre-calculated layout standards that eliminate trial-and-error space planning and optimize setup/teardown turnaround times; enhances spatial accessibility (BP 344/ADA), acoustic comfort, and civic identity for **event-goers and the local community**; equips the **local government** with a high-performance civic venue that supports eco-tourism and regional economic growth; and provides **interior design professionals and researchers** with a replicable model for non-structural civic interior revitalization.

## 3. Research Question(s) / General Objective(s)

The general objective of this study is to formulate a site-specific, non-structural interior design framework and layout manual for the 840 square meter Main Hall of the Calbayog City Convention Center by integrating spatial modularity, local Samar heritage, and future-proof event adaptability. Specifically, the study seeks to investigate current spatial and operational bottlenecks within the venue's interior envelope; determine ergonomic furniture, fixtures, and equipment (FF&E) density typologies (Plenary, Classroom, Banquet, Exhibition) and circulation paths that maximize setup/teardown turnaround efficiency and universal accessibility (BP 344/ADA); synthesize traditional Samar Basey *banig* weaving patterns, *tikog* grass textures, and Calbayog waterfall motifs into non-structural acoustic surface matrices to cultivate civic identity; propose an adaptable overhead ceiling track-and-rigging interface for dynamic lighting and drapery control; and specify sustainable, low-VOC acoustic materials to ensure long-term operational resilience.

---

## 4. Sub-question(s) / Specific Objective(s)

Specifically, this study seeks to address the following specific objectives:

*  Evaluate existing layout configurations, user circulation bottlenecks, and physical material conditions within the Calbayog City Convention Center's 840 square meter Main Hall.
*  Formulate reconfigurable furniture layouts and interior zoning strategies (Plenary, Classroom, Banquet, Exhibition) that maximize spatial capacity, enforce universal accessibility (BP 344/ADA), and optimize setup/teardown turnaround speed.
*  Synthesize culturally-responsive design elements—abstracting traditional Samar Basey *banig* weaving patterns, *tikog* grass textures, and regional Waray geometric motifs—into non-structural acoustic paneling to cultivate civic identity and mitigate reverberation.
* Propose an adaptable overhead ceiling system combining modular acoustic ceiling tiles with a flexible track-and-rigging interface to facilitate dynamic alterations in ambient lighting, acoustic drapes, and spatial dividers.
* Apply future-proof interior specifications regarding sustainable, low-VOC, high-durability, and acoustic materials to maintain long-term operational resilience.

## 5. Justification for the Need to Involve Human Participant(s)

Involving human participants is essential to this study because evaluating operational venue adaptability and experiential interior design cannot be achieved through theoretical layout modeling alone; it requires empirical insights from the primary human agents who operate within and experience the physical envelope of the Calbayog City Convention Center. The study recruits two targeted stakeholder groups to capture distinct dimensions of the venue: specialized event planners, coordinators, and facility operators (N = 15–25 expert sample; n = 8–12 interview key informants) provide indispensable domain expertise regarding real-world setup and teardown turnaround bottlenecks, furniture reconfigurability, spatial circulation clearance, and overhead utility track requirements; while general event-goers and community members (N = 100–120 public sample) evaluate the human experience of the space, including interior acoustic comfort, seating sightlines, intuitive wayfinding, and the subjective cultural resonance of abstracting traditional Samar Basey *banig* weaving patterns and Calbayog waterfall motifs into interior surface finishes. Involving these participants through a sequential mixed-methods approach (quantitative survey and qualitative semi-structured interviews) ensures that the resulting non-structural interior design framework is empirically grounded in actual operational workflows, human ergonomic needs, and community civic identity.

## 6. Study Outcomes and Variable Operationalization

The study evaluates five empirical variables and delivers one primary design output:

* **Setup & Turnaround Efficiency**: Measures setup/teardown speed and spatial bottleneck reduction within the 840 sqm Main Hall. *Measurement*: 5-point Likert survey ratings (SPOP Q1) and qualitative interview feedback from event planners and operators.
* **Spatial Modularity & FF&E Adaptability**: Measures layout transition speed and furniture reconfigurability across Plenary, Classroom, Banquet, and Exhibition zoning typologies. *Measurement*: Quantitative survey flexibility scores (SPOP Q2) and expert operator interviews.
* **Overhead Infrastructure Utility**: Measures the functional adaptability of flexible ceiling tracks, motorized hoists, lighting controls, and acoustic draping. *Measurement*: Perceived utility ratings (SPOP Q4) evaluated by event coordinators and venue staff.
* **Cultural Identity & Acoustic Comfort**: Measures perceived civic pride, aesthetic appeal, sense of place, and acoustic reverberation control resulting from abstracted Samar Basey *banig* weaving and Calbayog waterfall motifs. *Measurement*: Attendee Likert survey scores (SPOP Q3) and qualitative interview themes.
* **Universal Accessibility Compliance**: Measures physical spatial inclusivity and regulatory adherence for all venue occupants. *Measurement*: Audit compliance with BP 344 and ADA standards (1200 mm wheelchair corridors, 1500 mm turning radii, 760 mm x 1220 mm seating stalls).
* **Applied Output (Interior Design Framework & Layout Manual)**: A site-specific manual synthesizing pre-calculated layout standards, overhead rigging specifications, sustainable acoustic materials, and cultural surface patterns for the Calbayog City Convention Center.
