# RESEARCH ETHICS COMMITTEE (REC) FORM — 01REC_F29


## 1. Scientific Background / Context and Literature Review Summary                                                                                  
                                                                                                                                                       
  │ The global expansion of the Meetings, Incentives, Conventions, and Exhibitions (MICE) industry has driven a paradigm shift in civic venue design,  
  │ prioritizing spatial adaptability, configuration speed, and experiential user engagement over traditional rigid corporate layouts (An, Kim, & Hur, 
  │ 2021; Ike et al., 2023). However, municipal facilities such as the Calbayog City Convention Center frequently operate within static interior       
  │ envelopes that result in prolonged setup/teardown turnaround times, service circulation bottlenecks, acoustic reverberation issues, and sterile    
  │ aesthetics that lack regional civic identity (Etteh, 2016; Fakharany, 2025). Grounded in modular spatial theory (Hashemian, 2005), future-proof    
  │ design principles (DesignHorizons, 2024), overhead utility track/rigging interfaces (Phantos, 2025), universal accessibility mandates (BP 344; ADA),
  │ and culturally responsive design frameworks (Kockler, 1989; Krstić et al., 2024), this study addresses these operational and aesthetic deficiencies
  │ by formulating a site-specific, non-structural interior design framework and layout manual for the venue's 840 square meter Main Hall. Adopting an 
  │ Applied Design Research approach, the study utilizes a sequential explanatory mixed-methods design combining a researcher-developed survey         
  │ questionnaire (N = 115 -- 145 event planners and attendees, synthesized from An et al., 2021, and Pembegül, 2009, with a 10-individual pilot test),
  │ semi-structured qualitative interviews (n = 8 -- 12), and non-participatory architectural spatial mapping. By integrating modular furniture        
  │ typologies (Plenary, Classroom, Banquet, Exhibition) with localized surface pattern matrices abstracting Samar Basey banig weaving, tikog grass,   
  and                                                                                                                                                  
  │ Calbayog waterfall motifs, this research establishes an empirically validated, non-structural design framework that optimizes operational          
  turnaround                                                                                                                                           
  │ efficiency, acoustic comfort, and cultural sense of place without incurring structural risks or invasive physical alterations.
