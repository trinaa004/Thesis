The **Calbayog Cultural Convention Center** (officially chartered by the City Government of Calbayog, and locally referenced as the Calbayog City Convention Center or "the convention center") is the premier city-owned indoor convention facility in Calbayog City. Located in Brgy. Capoocan, Calbayog City, Samar, the facility was originally constructed as a municipal central bus station before being officially renovated and inaugurated into a civic convention space on December 14, 2009. According to the official portal of the City Government of Calbayog (n.d.), the facility is formally envisioned to serve as a vital hub for promoting Calbayog City’s rich culture, arts, and tourism—featuring spaces for performances, art displays, cultural training, community gatherings, and local product showcases, while actively preserving regional traditions.

To contextualize the facility's specific operational role within Calbayog City's broader public event infrastructure, the municipal venue network is delineated across three distinct facilities:
1. **Calbayog City Sports Center**: A high-capacity sports and assembly arena designed for mass spectatorship, which officially accommodates large-scale athletic meets, city-wide assemblies, and the major outdoor street dancing competitions and grand showdowns of the **Hadang Festival (historically and popularly recognized as the Sarakiki-Hadang Festival)**.
2. **Handumanan Museum / Arts Gallery**: The city's dedicated municipal facility for preserving permanent historical artifacts, archival exhibits, and curated visual arts collections.
3. **Calbayog Cultural Convention Center**: Operates as an intimate, fully air-conditioned, and cost-effective 840-square-meter civic convention venue capable of accommodating up to 500 occupants. Because it provides a lower-overhead, fully enclosed rental tier compared to the sports arena, it functions as the city's primary venue for government assemblies, legislative caravans (such as the *PAGPASAMWAK Sangguniang Panlungsod Caravan*), regional economic summits (such as the *Samar Investment Forum*), academic conferences, private social banquets, and curated indoor cultural presentations and convocations (such as the landmark Samar-wide *Artists Kolektib* assembly).

The physical application of this study focuses strictly on the **840-square-meter interior envelope** of the venue, systematically delineated across four functional zones (**CCCC plans and physical site context/01_existing_physical_site_context_and_spatial_audit.md**):
* **Zone 1: Arrival & Lobby / Pre-Function Concourse (129 m² | Grid Lines A to B):** Serves as the primary registration threshold and circulation buffer. It is completely non-air-conditioned, resulting in high thermal stuffiness during events. Although nominal exterior entry doors measure 2000 mm in width, only one leaf opens, restricting usable pedestrian entry to just **1000 mm**. Street traffic noise from Hinay-La Street penetrates directly through frameless glass facades.
* **Zone 2: Main Plenary Assembly Hall (711 m² | Grid Lines B to E):** An open, column-free volume designed for up to 500 occupants, featuring a clear ceiling height of **5390 mm** (5.39 m) to the flat plasterboard plane and 6.20 m to the structural truss chord. Perimeter boundaries feature **glass walls between columns 1B–2B and 3B–4B with a height of 3,540 mm** (topped by a 1,850 mm painted gypsum bulkhead to the ceiling), while exterior walls along Grids 1 & 4 incorporate 2,040 mm sliding doors and a 1,500 mm clerestory window band covered by curtains hung at **+3,640 mm AFFL**. The remaining **1,750 mm painted gypsum board bulkhead** up to the 5,390 mm ceiling plane remains completely unpadded, generating severe specular flutter echoes across the upper room volume. Logistically, **Column Grid Line 4 represents the primary loading axis**, where tight door widths cause flight cases to collide with door frames. Furthermore, the ceiling exhibits physical panel damage near **Column 3B** with an exposed plenum, upper columns feature puncture scars from temporary wire rigging, and the hall completely lacks illuminated Fire Exit signage (RA 9514) and restroom wayfinding.
* **Zone 3: Elevated Stage Platform (52.5 m² | Grid Lines E to F):** Measures **11.11 m in clear width × 4.20 m in depth**, elevated **940 mm AFFL** (accessed via 3 riser steps) above the hall floor with a painted concrete deck in its baseline condition. Its plain concrete backdrop currently necessitates rented LED walls, while its static elevation wastes floor space during flat-floor expos. In the proposed interior framework, this fixed concrete dais is replaced with an on-demand, venue-provided demountable modular staging system that can be configured to variable heights (300/600/900 mm) or completely stowed away in back-of-house storage ("Zero-Stage Flat-Floor Mode") to reclaim 52.5 m² of continuous floor plate for trade fairs, banquets, and sports/dance arenas.
* **Zone 4: Back-of-House & Restroom Interface (Grid Lines E to G | Contextual Scope):** Encompasses storage, electrical room (AV console stationed between Grids D4 and E4), and municipal restrooms. Constricted by a **955 mm backstage door**, attendees must cross directly in front of the active stage to access sanitary facilities, navigating a narrow corridor congested with AV cables.

Focusing on this specific interior envelope allows for the formulation of an empirically grounded, non-structural interior design framework and layout manual that optimizes operational turnaround efficiency, acoustic speech intelligibility, universal accessibility (BP 344), and civic cultural identity.
