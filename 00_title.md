## 2. Defense Scripts: Answering Panel Questions on the Title

### 1. Panel Question on Epistemological Grounding: "Spatial Utilization Analysis" and "Evidence-Based Design"
> **Panel Question**: *"Why did you title your thesis 'Spatial Utilization Analysis and Evidence-Based Interior Design Interventions' instead of simply 'Proposed Interior Design for the Calbayog Convention Center'?"*
> 
> **Prepared Student Response**: 
> *"Thank you for that essential question, esteemed members of the panel. A title such as 'Proposed Interior Design' typically denotes a conventional, project-based design exercise driven by subjective aesthetic intuition. In contrast, our study is an **Applied Design Research** investigation grounded in **Evidence-Based Design (Hamilton & Watkins, 2009; Groat & Wang, 2013)**. 
> 
> The first half of our title—**Spatial Utilization Analysis**—reflects our rigorous empirical baseline: auditing twelve years of municipal event history across 127 verified events, conducting in-situ acoustic reverberation decay testing ($RT_{60} = 2.53\text{ seconds}$), logging diurnal noise and thermal loads, and shadowing live event lifecycles. 
> 
> The second half—**Evidence-Based Interior Design Interventions**—proves that every single spatial proposal we present—from our 1,500 mm V-shaped chevron egress corridors and demountable modular staging to our perimeter dual-service dropped soffits, suspended waterfall baffles, and Basey *banig* acoustic wall panels—is directly dictated by empirical data, statutory life-safety mandates (PD 1096, BP 344, RA 9514), and stakeholder validation. The title accurately states our scientific methodology and its tangible interior architectural deliverables."*

### 2. Panel Question on Geographic Precision: Hinay-la St., Brgy. Capoocan
> **Panel Question**: *"Why is the specific street and barangay address included in the main title?"*
> 
> **Prepared Student Response**: 
> *"Specifying **'Hinay-la St., Brgy. Capoocan, Calbayog City, Samar'** adheres to the formal thesis documentation standards of the University of San Carlos Department of Interior Design. Provincial LGUs frequently own multiple civic venues with colloquial or overlapping titles—such as the Calbayog City Sports Center or the Handumanan Museum. Specifying the exact street and barangay address permanently anchors our research to the specific 840-square-meter civic building envelope (the former central bus terminal renovated in 2009), ensuring unequivocal legal, municipal, and institutional demarcation."*

### 3. Panel Question on Balancing Spatial Adaptability vs. Samar Heritage
> **Panel Question**: *"Your title pairs 'Spatial Adaptability' with 'Samar Heritage Integration'. Are these two objectives in conflict—does strong cultural decoration hinder corporate event flexibility?"*
> 
> **Prepared Student Response**: 
> *"That was precisely the central research inquiry of our study (SPOP Q3). Historically, cultural integration in local civic facilities has relied on literal, ad-hoc decorations—such as hanging single-use plastic tarpaulins or pinning crude straw mats to gypsum boards, which damages walls, clutters the space, and clashes with corporate conferences. 
> 
> Grounded in the **Theory of Place Attachment (Scannell & Gifford, 2010)** and **Vernacular Abstraction (Del Monte, 2024)**, our framework proves that adaptability and heritage reinforce each other when executed through technical, high-performance interior materials. We abstract Basey *banig* geometric weaves into Class A flame-retardant fabric acoustic panels ($NRC \ge 0.75$), embed *tikog* linear textures into durable commercial wallcoverings, suspend undulating Calbayog waterfall baffles ($NRC \ge 0.85$) that optimize speech clarity, and carve Hadang festival relief motifs into permanent lobby partition features. The result is a corporate-neutral, camera-ready acoustic environment that actively cultivates civic pride without compromising multi-scale MICE versatility."*
